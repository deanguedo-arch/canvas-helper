Real-Life Inspiration for Research

Many times real-life events spark the interest of researchers in the area of human behaviour.  One such occurrence was in 1964 when a 28 year-old woman, Catherine (Kitty) Genovese, was murdered.

Kitty and her friend Mary Ann shared a second floor apartment in a quiet and mostly residential area of Queens, New York. Kitty had a job as a bar manager and often worked into the early morning hours.  March 13, 1964 was one such morning.  At 3:15 a.m. Kitty parked her car and proceeded to walk the short distance (about 30 metres) to her apartment door.  She noticed a strange man lurking in the shadows and began to run.  The man, however, ran faster than Kitty and was easily able to overpower the 5'1" woman who weighed 105 pounds.  They struggled, ending up near a street light at the end of the parking lot.  Here the assailant stabbed Kitty several times.

" Oh my God!  He stabbed me!" she screamed.   " Please help me!  Please help me!"

Some apartment lights went on in nearby buildings.  One woman, Irene Frost, clearly heard Kitty's screams.  In court she testified that "There was another shriek and she was lying down crying out."  On the seventh floor of the same building, Robert Mozer slid open his window and yelled "Hey, let that girl alone!"  The attacker immediately walked away.  After that, the only sound remaining was Kitty sobbing.

As Kitty struggled to her feet, the lights in the apartments went out.  Bleeding severely from the stab wounds, Kitty managed to reach the side of her building, staggered over to a locked door, and tried to stay conscious.  Within five minutes, the assailant returned.  He stabbed her again.

" I'm dying!  I'm dying!" she cried out.  Several people in her building heard her screams.  Once again lights came on, windows opened -- people witnessed the attack.  No one came to her assistance.  No one phoned the police.  At this point the killer left Kitty and moved his car from view.  

By this time, about 3:25 a.m., Kitty had stumbled to the back of the apartment and to a hallway leading to the second floor.  The man, who returned after moving his car, was easily able to locate Kitty by her blood trail.  As she lay defenseless and semi-conscious, the killer sexually assaulted her, took $49 in cash from her wallet, and stabbed her again.

The killer spent a total of 32 minutes torturing Kitty.  Three minutes after he left her for dead, Karl Ross, who lived on the same floor as Kitty, finally called the police.  The police arrived two minutes later.

After interviewing the immediate neighbourhood, police discovered at least 38 people who had heard or observed some part of the attack on Kitty Genovese. Why didn't anyone help her?  Why did they watch and do nothing?  Why did they ignore the terrified screams for help?   There were plenty of excuses.  Psychologists wondered if Kitty would have received the help she needed had she been attacked in the presence of only one witness.
