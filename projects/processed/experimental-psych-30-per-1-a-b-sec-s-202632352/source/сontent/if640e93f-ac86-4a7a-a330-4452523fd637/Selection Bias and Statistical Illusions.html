Selection Bias and Statistical Illusions


Selection bias relates to the reason researchers must randomly assign participants to the experimental and control (placebo) groups in double-blind studies.  If researchers were allowed to choose who receives the legitimate treatment and who receives the placebo, a significant risk would occur that the researchers would subconsciously choose people in such a manner that the outcome of the experiment would be more favourable.  Please note that researchers do not divide participants in this way purposefully, but the subconscious assignment of participants can affect experimental results significantly.


Statistical illusions are used a great deal in the advertising industry.  If you have ever stayed up late and watched infomercials (informational commercials), you might know what this means.  For example, you might decide to invent a new shampoo that is supposed to reduce dandruff.   The shampoo, however, fails to reduce dandruff in almost all of the trials.  The shampoo, in fact, is effective in only one of a hundred trials.  If you use the almost totally ineffective shampoo on 100 000 people, however, you will have a thousand testimonials that the shampoo works wonders!  You bottle your product, have a few of the thousand successful stories told in your infomercial, and you make a lot of money selling your shampoo to gullible viewers at home!

To some scientists, the most problematic statistical illusion relates to observational studies in which correlation is often confused with causation.  For example, you may have heard the statement that people who consume a moderate amount of alcohol have less heart disease than people who consume either no alcohol or too much alcohol.  People who report the news might inadvertently present this information in such a way that the public is led to believe that alcohol prevents heart disease.  In fact, this claim cannot be made.  Correlation is not causation. 

Quite possibly, people who are moderate in their alcohol consumption are different in a various ways from people who either abuse alcohol or abstain from alcohol.  Perhaps the people who consume moderate amounts of alcohol are also moderate in general, and that is what makes them healthier.  The fact is, scientists do not know the actual reason for a lower incidence of heart disease and cannot make any statement of causation.
