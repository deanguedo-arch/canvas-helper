In some studies, researchers want to know how close each individual score is to the middle of the distribution.  Using the mean as the measure of the middle of a distribution, researchers calculate the average squared difference of the scores from the mean.  The result of this calculation is called the variance.  The variance is used to determine the standard deviation.  The standard deviation is the square root of the variance. 

Wow... that sounds complicated!

Don't worry, you won't be required to perform these types of calculations in the final exam (or project).  However, be familiar with the terms mean, mode, median, range, variance, and standard deviation.

A simple example of how to calculate variance and standard deviation involves a dart game.  Six friends each throw one dart at a dart board much like the one in the photo above.  Three of the six friends get a "bulls-eye" (dead centre) worth 10 points.  Of the other three friends, one lands his dart in the first ring closest the centre and earns 9 points.  The second friend lands her dart in the third ring closest the centre and earns 7 points.  The last friend lands his dart in the fifth ring closest to the centre and earns 5 points.
