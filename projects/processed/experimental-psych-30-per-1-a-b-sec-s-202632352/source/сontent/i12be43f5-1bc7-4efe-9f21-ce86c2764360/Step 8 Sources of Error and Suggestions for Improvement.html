Step 8: Sources of Error and Suggestions for Improvement

Sometimes measurement devices do not operate with 100% accuracy.  Sometimes the experimental design has flaws evident only after starting an experiment.  These sources of error and others can affect the data compiled by the researcher and they may make any conclusion invalid.  For example, if the sandwiches consumed by participants in Group A inadvertently contained a small amount of botulinum toxin, participants may have begun to feel ill (headaches, nausea, upset stomachs), and this illness may be the cause of their slower reaction times, not the feeling of being extremely full.  As a researcher, you would repeat the experiment using food confirmed to be safe for human consumption.

Another suggestion for improvement may have the researcher using the same test subjects in both the experimental group and the control group.  For example, the researcher would feed 20 and have them run the simulation.  On the next day the researcher would use the same 20 people but not feed them before running the simulation.
