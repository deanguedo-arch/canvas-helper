Use the right method for the information

Genotype counts permit direct allele counting over 2N. Recessive phenotype information alone leaves some genotypes unknown, so a Hardy–Weinberg inference needs explicit assumptions. Expected genotype frequencies are p², 2pq and q² only within the model.

Keep frequencies distinct from counts. Multiply by population size only when an expected number is requested, and check that the complete categories total 1 or N as appropriate.

Interpret change without choosing a cause too soon

Selection links heritable variation with reproductive contribution; drift reflects chance; gene flow moves existing alleles between populations; mutation creates sequence variants. Non-random pairing can change genotype proportions without necessarily changing allele totals by itself.

A good population explanation uses the given evidence to distinguish these mechanisms. A small sample, an unexplained phenotype change or numerical recovery alone supports less than a complete claim about evolution or genetic diversity.

Worked example

Compare counts across population growth

Generation one has 16 aa individuals of 100 and generation two has 64 aa of 400. Assume the same complete-dominance Hardy–Weinberg model for both.

  1. The aa fractions are both 0.16.
  2. Under the stated model, q = 0.40 and p = 0.60 in both generations.
  3. Counts increased while inferred allele frequencies stayed equal.
  4. This supports no detected change at the studied locus under the assumptions.
  5. Without those assumptions, the recessive fractions alone would not determine every allele frequency.

Written explanations

Written explanation 1

Maximum 3200 characters. Explain the biology in your own words.

Compare after saving your own response

Known genotype counts allow direct summing of two copies per homozygote and one of each per heterozygote over 2N. A recessive phenotype count identifies aa but does not reveal the split between AA and Aa. Inferring q by √q² then requires the stated equilibrium and phenotype assumptions.
