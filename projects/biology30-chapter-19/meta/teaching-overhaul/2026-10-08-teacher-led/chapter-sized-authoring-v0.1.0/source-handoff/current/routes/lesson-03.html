Start with the allele pool

At a locus with two alleles, let p be the frequency of A and q the frequency of a. Because these are the complete allele categories, p + q = 1. This identity does not require equilibrium.

If gametes combine randomly with those frequencies, AA has expected frequency p × p = p² and aa has q × q = q². A heterozygote can arise as A from one gamete and a from the other, or the reverse. Its total expected frequency is pq + qp = .

Name the conditions rather than hiding them

The Hardy–Weinberg reference model assumes a very large , with respect to the locus, no mutation, no migration and no natural selection affecting the alleles or genotypes. A very large population minimizes chance fluctuations from sampling.

Under these idealized conditions, allele frequencies remain constant across generations and genotype proportions follow p² + 2pq + q² = 1. The model gives a baseline for asking whether observed data fit the prediction.

A real population need not satisfy every condition perfectly. A mismatch can suggest that assumptions need investigation, but the mismatch alone does not identify which mechanism caused it.

Dominance is not the cause of frequency

The letters p and q can represent the two alleles whether or not one is dominant. In familiar complete-dominance problems, p often names the dominant allele and q the recessive allele. This notation does not make p larger than q.

Dominance determines the heterozygote’s phenotype. It does not determine the allele’s frequency or guarantee that the dominant allele is favoured by selection.

When only the recessive phenotype count is given, identifying q² and taking its square root requires the stated equilibrium and phenotype-model assumptions. Without them, do not silently infer the unobserved genotypes.

Worked example

Derive a heterozygote prediction

At a two-allele locus, p = 0.70 and q = 0.30. Assume Hardy–Weinberg conditions.

  1. AA expectation is 0.70 × 0.70 = 0.49.
  2. aa expectation is 0.30 × 0.30 = 0.09.
  3. Aa expectation is 2 × 0.70 × 0.30 = 0.42.
  4. Check: 0.49 + 0.42 + 0.09 = 1.
  5. These genotype predictions depend on the model, unlike the allele total p + q = 1.
