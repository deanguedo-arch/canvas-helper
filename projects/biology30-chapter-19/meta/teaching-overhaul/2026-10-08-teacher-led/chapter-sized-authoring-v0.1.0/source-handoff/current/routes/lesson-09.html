Genetic diversity and conservation evidence

Why should population size and genetic variation be measured separately?

Learning goal

Use population-genetic mechanisms to evaluate conservation evidence without assuming a universal outcome.

Before you begin

Drift, selection, mutation and gene flow can act together. No single measurement describes every aspect of a population.

A head count is only one measurement

A can recover numerically after a bottleneck while retaining reduced . Descendants multiply surviving allele copies; they do not automatically restore every lost variant. Population size and therefore answer different questions.

Small isolated populations may experience strong drift and increased mating among relatives. can increase homozygosity and expose effects of recessive alleles already present. It does not mean related individuals deliberately create new harmful alleles.

Connect a proposed action with its mechanism

A connection between habitats may permit movement and . If migrants reproduce, new alleles can enter a population and genetic differentiation may decline. Whether this happens depends on actual movement, survival and reproduction.

A population-management proposal should specify its biological mechanism and the evidence needed to evaluate it. Adding individuals, improving habitat and connecting populations are different interventions with potentially different effects.

The effects can include trade-offs. For example, introduced individuals may carry genetic variants suited to a different environment. An evidence-based evaluation considers both genetic contribution and the ecological setting rather than assuming that every transfer is beneficial.

Use cautious conclusions from limited data

Suppose a hypothetical monitoring report records more animals using a crossing structure and a rise in population size. That supports use of the structure and a demographic change. It does not alone establish increased gene flow or restored diversity at every locus.

Useful additional evidence might include successful breeding between populations, marker diversity, survival and the persistence of changes over generations. Sampling should be comparable across time.

Genetic diversity can support , but it is not a guarantee against extinction. Habitat, environmental change, population size and many other factors also matter.

Worked example

Evaluate a recovery claim

A population rises from 30 to 300 after a bottleneck. A report calls its original genetic diversity fully restored without genetic measurements.

  1. The head count supports numerical recovery.
  2. A bottleneck may have removed alleles that population growth alone cannot recover.
  3. Genetic measurements are needed to assess current diversity relative to earlier or comparison samples.
  4. The report’s genetic conclusion exceeds its demographic evidence.

Size describes abundance, breeding success concerns contribution to future generations, and genetic diversity describes represented variation. A change in one does not guarantee the same change in the others. Together they provide a more informative picture of population condition.
