Calculating expected genotype frequencies

How do you move from a recessive phenotype count to expected carrier numbers?

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

The expected aa genotype frequency under a two-allele Hardy–Weinberg model.
The proportion of heterozygotes carrying a recessive allele in the stated model.
A model-predicted number obtained by multiplying a frequency by population size.
Recovering q from q² when the Hardy–Weinberg assumptions apply.

Identify the given quantity

Suppose a recessive phenotype occurs in 32 of 200 individuals and the problem explicitly assumes with complete dominance. The observed aa fraction is 32/200 = 0.16, which is used as q².

Take the square root to obtain the recessive : q = √0.16 = 0.40. Then p = 1 − q = 0.60. Do not call 0.16 the allele frequency; it describes individuals with two recessive copies.

Predict genotypes and convert to counts

Use the allele frequencies to calculate the expected genotype frequencies: p² = 0.36, = 0.48 and q² = 0.16. These are the expected fractions of AA, Aa and aa individuals.

To obtain counts, multiply each frequency by N = 200. The expected numbers are 72 AA, 96 Aa and 32 aa. Check that the counts total 200 and the frequencies total 1.

The heterozygotes are carriers in this simple recessive model. The dominant-phenotype fraction includes AA and Aa together, so it is 0.36 + 0.48 = 0.84, not p alone.

Keep precision until the final answer

Carry sufficient decimal precision through square roots and multiplication. Round the final expected number to an appropriate whole individual only after completing the calculation. Rounding q too early can noticeably change a carrier estimate.

If allele frequency is already given, start with that value instead of taking another square root. If all genotype counts are given, direct allele counting may be the appropriate first method.

A strong answer states the model, labels each quantity, shows the calculation and interprets the result. A bare number makes it difficult to see whether the correct denominator and assumption were used.

Worked example

Estimate carriers for a rare recessive condition

In a hypothetical population at Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, 1 in 10 000 individuals has a fully recessive phenotype.

  1. q² = 1/10 000 = 0.0001.
  2. q = √0.0001 = 0.01, so p = 0.99.
  3. Carrier frequency = 2pq = 2(0.99)(0.01) = 0.0198.
  4. As a percentage, this is 1.98%, not 0.0198%.
  5. The inference depends on the stated model; it is not a personal medical-risk estimate.
