Allele frequencies and genotype frequencies

Why do allele calculations usually use twice as many copies as individuals?

Learning goal

Calculate genotype and allele frequencies directly from diploid genotype counts.

Before you begin

At a diploid autosomal locus, an individual carries two allele copies, even when both are the same.

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

The proportion of individuals with a specified genotype.
The proportion of allele copies represented by a particular allele at a locus.
The contribution of one copy of each allele from a heterozygous individual.
The requirement that mutually exclusive complete categories account for the whole sample.

Count individuals for genotype frequencies

Let N be the number of diploid individuals sampled at a two-allele autosomal locus. The possible genotypes are AA, Aa and aa. is the number with one genotype divided by N.

For 30 AA, 50 Aa and 20 aa individuals, N = 100. The frequencies are 0.30, 0.50 and 0.20. They add to 1 because every sampled individual belongs to exactly one of these genotype categories.

Count copies for allele frequencies

The same sample contains 2N = 200 allele copies. Each AA contributes two A copies, each Aa contributes one A and one a, and each aa contributes two a copies.

The A count is 2(30) + 50 = 110, so p = 110/200 = 0.55. The a count is 2(20) + 50 = 90, so q = 90/200 = 0.45. The allele frequencies also add to 1.

The general relationships are p = [2(AA) + Aa]/2N and q = [2(aa) + Aa]/2N, where the genotype symbols inside the brackets represent counts. Write units or category names beside the numbers so that individuals are not mistaken for allele copies.

Direct counting does not need equilibrium

When all genotype counts are known, allele frequencies can be calculated directly. You do not need to assume Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium to count copies.

A recessive phenotype alone provides different information. Under a simple complete-dominance model, it identifies aa individuals but does not separate AA from Aa among dominant-phenotype individuals. Additional assumptions would be needed to infer all allele frequencies from that phenotype count alone.

Check every calculation: genotype counts total N, allele counts total 2N, and both sets of frequencies separately sum to 1.

Worked example

Use all genotype counts

A sample has 40 AA, 40 Aa and 20 aa individuals. Find p and q.

  1. Total individuals N = 100; total allele copies = 200.
  2. A copies = 2(40) + 40 = 120, so p = 120/200 = 0.60.
  3. a copies = 2(20) + 40 = 80, so q = 80/200 = 0.40.
  4. Check: 0.60 + 0.40 = 1.
  5. These are direct counts, not an inference from equilibrium.
