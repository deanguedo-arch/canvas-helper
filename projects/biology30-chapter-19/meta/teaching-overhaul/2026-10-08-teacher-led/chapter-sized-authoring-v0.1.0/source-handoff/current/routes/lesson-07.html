How can an allele become common without providing an advantage?

Chance can change the sample

Not every individual leaves the same number of descendants. In a finite , chance differences can change allele frequencies even when the alleles have no effect on survival or reproduction. This is .

Drift has a stronger relative effect in small populations because a small number of births, deaths or reproductive events can represent a large fraction of the . An allele may be lost or become fixed by chance.

A useful distinction is whether the case links reproductive success with a heritable trait. Trait-related differences support selection; random sampling independent of the trait supports drift. Both processes can operate together.

A bottleneck samples the original population

A bottleneck occurs when population size falls sharply. The survivors may carry a smaller and unrepresentative sample of the earlier alleles. If the reduction is random with respect to a studied allele, drift can strongly change that allele’s frequency.

If the population later increases, descendants reproduce the variation that survived. Recovery in numbers does not automatically bring back alleles that were lost. Mutation or gene flow might introduce variation later, but population growth alone does not guarantee restoration.

Founders establish a new gene pool

A occurs when a small group starts a new population. The founders may carry allele frequencies different from those of the source population simply because they are a small sample.

The source population can remain large and intact. That separates the founder case from a bottleneck that reduces an existing population. Both involve sampling, but the demographic history differs.

Do not infer that a newly common allele must be adaptive. A small island population can have unusual allele frequencies because of its founders even before evidence of local selection is available.

Worked example

Name the demographic event

Five individuals leave a large population to establish an isolated colony. By chance, they carry an uncommon allele at a high frequency.

  1. The original population remains large.
  2. The new population begins from a small sample of its allele pool.
  3. This is a founder effect, a form of genetic drift.
  4. No selective advantage is needed to explain the initial frequency difference.
