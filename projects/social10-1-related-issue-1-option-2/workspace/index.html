Globalization and Identity Overview

To what extent should we embrace globalization?

In this Unit we will be exploring the meaning of globalization and how it affects you. Understanding the role globalization plays in your life today, will help you to form opinions about the extent to which you feel we should embrace it.

Globalization is a key concept that permeates our lives. Look at your life and where the things you are wearing or using come from. You will notice that very few things around you were created in Canada. Most of the things you wear, eat, and use come from a variety of different places where other people live, work, love, and dream just as you do.

To better understand both your world and how it interconnects with the rest of the globe we are going to explore the concept of globalization, what it means, how it has evolved, and how it impacts our lives and world.

How is economic globalization at work in your life?

Global economic connections play a big role in our daily lives. How many of the products you use are made or sold outside of Canada? Does your family income have any connection with the global economy? Goods and services created today are easily shipped from one country to another, and this gives many people and companies an opportunity to prosper.

Many of the items we buy every day are the products of transnational corporations. A transnational corporation is a corporation that operates in two or more countries. Most large transnationals operate in several countries and the largest of these have more money than many entire countries. For example, Wal-Mart is based in the United States, but it has stores all around the world. Many of the products sold in Wal-Mart are made in factories in China, where pay is much lower than in North America.

How does globalization affect political aspects of your life?

Globalization also has a big role to play in political aspects of your life. When events take place far away, they may have an impact on your life. For example, if Canada decides to go to war, this affects how Canadian soldiers and their families live. Their contributions may make Canada and the world a better, safer place. However, when tax dollars go to support that war, it means there is less money for other government programs. If the Canadian government chooses to take measures to protect the environment from climate change, there are costs associated with these actions. Of course, there are also costs associated with not acting to prevent climate change as well. The federal government may send aid to countries in need if there is a disaster. It may try to convince other countries to treat their citizens more equally. When global events occur, your local politicians may be forced to act. For example, your mayor and town or city council may have to make decisions about the environment. Your provincial government may find that it has to make deals with transnational oil companies to encourage economic growth and create jobs in Alberta. All of these decisions have an impact on you.

Tiananmen Square, G8 protests in Quebec City, 2004 tsunami (pictured), Afghanistan, Caledonia, Ontario and Hurricane Katrina are examples of issues that illustrate political globalization.

How does globalization affect social aspects of your life?

Social Globalization refers to global access of popular culture. Avril Lavigne recorded the song "Girlfriend", released in 2007 in French, Portuguese, German, Italian, Spanish, Japanese, and Mandarin.

Globalization has an impact on social aspects of your life. By looking at the historical background to globalization, you learned that your ancestors were connected with people from other parts of the world. Today you are even more connected. For instance, you can connect with people all around the world through the Internet, whether it’s through social software, gaming, or e-mail. You may have friends and family all over the world.

The nature of work also affects social aspects of your life. Many globalizing forces have an impact on who you are and how you interact with others. If people all over the world start wearing the same clothing, drinking the same kind of pop, and eating the same brand of food in the same style of restaurant, how would this affect how they live? How would their lifestyle affect who they are? You may not notice the impact of big companies such as Coke, Disney, and McDonald’s because they represent the mainstream culture of which you are a part. Think about their impact a little more. In your grandparents’ day, women usually worked in the home. Now most women work out of the home, partly due to global social changes. This affects how families live. In your parents’ day, chances are that families all ate together, cooking and eating food that grew nearby. Now that there are global shipping networks and cheap fast-food outlets, many families order takeout or go to fast-food restaurants, spending less time together. Globalization may be changing your lifestyle and the lifestyles of people all over the world in ways that you have not considered.

Environmental Globalization involves the ecological issues that concern the world. Individuals are responding to their immediate environment while keeping the consequences on a global scale in mind.

Technology and Promotion of Culture

Technology and the Promotion of Culture

Access to new media and communication technologies is very evident in entertainment, quick global contact at your fingertips, and in the pop culture side of the everyday lives of many people. This aspect of globalization influences economic, social, and political dimensions of society. These technologies can unify a community across great distances and geographical obstacles. With these tools, people can instantly be informed by a click of a mouse or a tap of a finger. These tools also create opportunities and challenges to maintaining unique cultures and languages.

Some who view globalization as limiting predict that these technologies will develop a homogenized pop culture that will dominate diverse cultures. Some would argue that this may even lead to a complete erosion of those cultures in favor of a single one, especially as people use these technologies more and more often. Those who recognize the potentials of globalization use these technologies to establish a space for the maintenance of their culture, language, and identity in a globalizing world. They may make the case that changes in technology and media gives a new voice to marginalized and oppressed cultures, and a way to stand up for themselves.

Media, Technology and Cultural Exchange

Your individual identity can be easy to describe or it may be very complex. Who you are can be connected to your community, your language, your values and beliefs—even to how you dress and the activities you prefer. Often people think of their individual identity as a matter of personal choice. When the influences of globalization in the twenty-first century are examined, you may find that global contact and connections can also influence your personal choices about who you are. Understanding the forces of globalization and how they shape who you are, both individually and collectively, is the focus of this lesson.

The Olympics, World Cup, and student exchanges are some examples of cultural exchanges. How do such exchanges affect the identities of participants? What cultural exchanges have you been a part of (example: linguistic, athletic, musical, educational)? What did you learn from those exchanges?

 

 

 

Universalization - A Canadian Example

The majority of the popular culture we watch, listen to, and read, comes from media transnationals who own many smaller media outlets which create and broadcast their own materials. For example, the transnational Comcast owns NBC, Bravo, SportsNet, History Channel, Style, and 24 other TV and Radio channels.

Transnational media and communications corporations enable a large population of people around the world to gain access to the latest movies, music and pop culture. This universalization of culture through media transnationals has both advantages and concerns. 

Within Canada, we have the Canadian Radio and Television Commission (CRTC) whose purpose is to regulate and promote Canada culture, national identity and cultural sovereignty in the broadcasting media to “ensure that all Canadians have access to a wide variety of high quality Canadian programming” (Broadcasting Act of 1991). The CRTC regulations determine the minimum amount of Canadian Content (CanCon) that Canadian broadcasters must show.

Professor William Stanbury does not agree with the CRTC regulations regarding amount of Canadian Content: “The present Canadian content regulations have almost no redeeming social value. They are based on citizenship, not on the substantive content of TV programs or musical recordings. They alter the set of choices available to TV viewers and radio listeners by limiting the availability of foreign programs and musical recordings.” (as quoted in Atlantic Institute for Market Studies, 2008).  

With the rise of Internet streaming to watch TV shows, movies, and videos, and to listen to music and news, there is discussion regarding whether or not CRTC regulations should exist for all forms of media. In fact, the information we receive from the internet is very ‘personalized’ to give us more information that agrees with what we already think. Is this healthy? Does this promote thoughtful conversation?

You may have ideas about religion, spirituality, beliefs, and the connection to the land that you share with your family and community. Homogenization

Monoculture is a term used to refer to the uniformity of traditions, beliefs, customs, traditions, values and attitudes. Critics claim that globalization is leading to an increase in homogenization of culture.

If teens in Brazil are wearing the same jeans as the teens in Japan, how does that affect the local traditions?

Assimilation

Assimilation refers to a minority group adopting the traditions, language, values and beliefs of the dominant group to the extent that the identity of the minority group disappears. Assimilation can be a voluntary or non-voluntary process.

Marginalization

Marginalization occurs when individuals are excluded from groups, prevented from gaining power or influence, with identity and culture not respected. Although governments have acknowledged that these practices are unlawful, many individuals are facing these challenges today.

Black Lives Matter is a grassroots group within the United States demanding equality and respect for Black Americans. The movement has spread across North America as statistics on such issues as the large wage gap between black and white Americans, levels of police brutality, and the over-representation of African Americans in US prisons, has been publicized by the group.

Another example of a group which faces marginalization in modern society is the LGBTQ community. Though all members of the community are more likely to face discrimination, harassment, and violence, the transgender (someone whose internal gender identity does not align with their assigned sex at birth) community is especially vulnerable.

According to a survey of Canadian schools on the impact of this marginalization, LGBTQ youth experience higher rates of verbal harassment (insults and prejudiced comments), physical harassment (assault and physical aggression), and sexual harassment than their peers. Of all groups, transgender youth are most likely to experience all three. For example, almost half of transgender youth report that they have been sexually assaulted. You can see the report with additional statistics here. LGBTQ youth are also more likely to be truant or drop out of school, use drugs, or attempt suicide, according to this US report.

An important factor to note is that different kinds of marginalization can and do intersect. Someone who is both a visible minority and a part of the LGBTQ community may experience discrimination or prejudice based both on their race and their sexual orientation or gender identity. The recognition of this is known as intersectionality.

Traditions and Change Around the World

We know that collective identities and traditional ways of life are always changing.

In your grandparents’ day, your grandmother probably worked in the home, and families always sat down together for a meal. In your parents’ day, that began to change as more women worked outside the home, and more members of the household took responsibilities for meals. Today it is estimated that 20% of all meals are eaten in the car.

Changes in way of life is happening all over the world.

With the change in lifestyle come many changes to collective identity. Some possible examples include the following:

  • The role of women has changed, giving women more rights.

  • The family unit has become less important for many people.

  • More money might lead to possessions becoming more important.

There are many challenges to your collective identity. Today people are connected to each other in many ways. These connections influence how people live, what they believe, and how they act. While in the past, most aspects of cultural identity were locally created; many aspects of today’s culture in the form of food, music, art, and entertainment are mass produced and mass marketed. This mass production by large corporations also influences identity.

Transnational corporations can test the uniqueness of collective identities by providing products that everyone around the world uses, rather than the locally made food, clothing, and other items they have used in the past.

This brings changes to people’s traditional ways of producing, buying, and selling items. It can bring changes to daily life as well. For example, people who once grew their own food organically, or created their own artistic crafts, cannot compete financially with large chains. If their traditional crops are no longer grown, their local food is no longer produced or their handicrafts no longer made, the world is a less diverse place as these cultural groups lose their distinctiveness. They lose their income and also their some of their freedom to do and be who they are.

One World, One Taste? McDonald’s ran a campaign called “One World, One Taste.” Coca-Cola’s logo is a rainbow coloured map of the world flowing out of a Coke bottle. Coke and McDonald’s operate all around the world, encouraging people to eat and drink their North American-based products. Their campaigns are designed to encourage everyone around the world to eat and drink the same food and pop. Is that necessarily a good thing or a bad thing for collective identity?

This process can take place both at a local level, such as a single city or country, or at a global level, among different cultures across the globe. Examples of cultural homogenization include the development of a global culture, McDonaldization of industry, and global Americanization. As a result of cultural homogenization, local and indigenous cultures lose their authenticity in favor of the dominant Western, particularly American, norms and values (Pritchard, 2009), and global stratification is exacerbated. This concept is closely related to other sociological concepts such as globalization, cultural imperialism, and assimilation. HYBRIDIZATION: Cultural hybridization refers to the interaction between local cultures and global ones, which lead to the transformation of mainstream cultures (see also: glocalization and cultural blending). Hybridization is closely related to interactions between different cultures, and multiculturalism. An example of cultural homogenization would be the widespread consumption of American fast food in the Middle East, while an example of hybridization would be the popularity of Middle Eastern Doner Kebabs in Europe, as a result of migration from this region. Another way to think about hybridization is that it is the combining of the elements of two or more different cultures to create something new. For example, Sesame Street combines elements of American culture, and combines it with the culture of another country where it is produced. It is produced in many countries around the world.

Summary

What new understandings have you gathered at the end of this Unit to help you respond to the question: To what extent should globalization shape identity?

In a globalizing world, there may be many opportunities and challenges to expressing who you are and want to be. Aspects of your individual identity may be of personal choice or shaped by one of several collective identities that may be a part of your community. The future of your individual and collective identities may depend on the forces of globalization. Identities may strengthen as people use the forces of globalization to celebrate or affirm their identities. Media and communication technologies may be twenty-first-century tools that will complement efforts to maintain identity. The identities of some groups, however, may find limited opportunities if the forces of globalization lead to the assimilation and homogenization of their culture and language. These groups may need to rely on strategies to respond to the overwhelming influences of globalization. The response in favour of or against globalization is not always easy because of the many diverse experiences with globalization.

At its most basic, there is nothing mysterious about globalization. The term has come into common usage since the 1980s, reflecting technological advances that have made it easier and quicker to complete international transactions–both trade and financial flows. It refers to an extension beyond national borders of the same market forces that have operated for centuries at all levels of human economic activity–village markets, urban industries, or financial centers.

Markets promote efficiency through competition and the division of labor–the specialization that allows people and economies to focus on what they do best. Global markets offer greater opportunity for people to tap into more and larger markets around the world. It means that they can have access to more capital flows, technology, cheaper imports, and larger export markets.

Globalization

What does globalization mean in this issue?

Reveal unit answer

Globalization is the growing connection among people, economies, cultures, technologies, and political decisions around the world. In this unit, it is not only trade or travel; it is the way ideas, products, media, languages, and values move across borders and shape how people understand themselves.

A complete answer should explain both sides: globalization can connect people to new ideas and opportunities, but it can also pressure cultures and identities to become more alike.

Identity

How is identity shaped by globalization?

Reveal unit answer

Identity is the way people understand who they are as individuals and as members of communities. Globalization can shape identity through media, language, consumer choices, migration, technology, and exposure to other cultures.
