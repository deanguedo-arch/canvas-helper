Global Responses

In this lesson you will explore the organizations and actions of the global community in response to the challenges and opportunities of economic globalization. You will focus on this issue question: What has the global community done to encourage worldwide prosperity?

What has the global community done to encourage worldwide prosperity?

Think back to Evan, the boy you met in earlier lessons. Evan just turned 16, and with the money he made from his pop stand and later as a clerk in the transnationally owned convenience store, he has quite a bit of money in the bank—but not quite enough to buy a used car he has his eye on. With a car, Evan could drive to a nearby factory where he could get a higher-paying job. Later on, he would be able to drive to his apprenticeship shop in the industrial park and then to a full-time job. So, he and his mom go down to the bank.

Evan does not yet have a credit rating, but he has a steady job and money for a down payment. The bank checks his credit history to be sure he has no problems, checks his employment record, and gives him a small loan (co-signed by his mom) that he will pay back with interest over the next two years, establishing his credit rating at the same time.

Think again about Evan and his need for a loan that will help him create sustainable prosperity for himself. The loan will help him get on his feet and give him greater opportunities. What conditions would a Canadian bank place on Evan to make sure he paid back his loan? If you don’t know much about bank loans, talk to someone who has ever applied for a loan, or go to your local bank branch and ask for some information.

What if Evan was not a person but a nation? The imaginary nation of “Evania” wants to make a better life for its citizens. It wants to create sustainable prosperity for all its people. A loan would help the country improve its infrastructure. With a better infrastructure, it could educate its people, improve their health care, and set up roads to get its products to the sea, where they could be shipped to other countries and earn a good income for the citizens. It applies to the IMF for a loan.

Like people, countries turn to banks when they are having financial troubles. The International Monetary Fund is a kind of international bank that helps nations when they need money. Unlike the banks most people deal with, the IMF also imposes conditions.

If a nation wants money from the IMF, it usually must commit to what is called a “Structural Adjustment Program” or a “Poverty Reduction Program.” The IMF makes the assumption that all poor countries have not managed their money in the correct way. They assume that bad decisions are always responsible for creating debt. The IMF insists that borrowing nations must make major changes to the way they manage their affairs. These changes are supposed to promote economic growth by creating new income and paying their debts. If countries do not agree to make these changes, they will not get a new loan.

Following are conditionalities that may be imposed on countries by the IMF:

  • The country must open its borders to trade.
  • The country must cut its public spending even if that means requiring elementary-age children to pay tuition to attend school, or cutting salaries to doctors, or taking other measures.
  • The country must increase taxes on citizens.
  • The country must sell off its state-owned property.
  • The country must pay interest on the loan.

Would you like your bank to tell you how to live in order to get a loan? People and nations love their independence. They do not always want to live on someone else’s terms, and so they have to be desperate to give up part of their sovereignty to the IMF to borrow money. On the other hand, these nations may have made many mistakes in their money management in the past. They may have social programs that they can’t afford. The profits from natural resources may have gone to corrupt leaders. If these countries don’t get a loan, they could be in deep trouble. Many nations agree to these conditions because they want the money, and they believe that if they meet the conditions set by the IMF, their countries will be more prosperous. There are many perspectives about who really benefits from the actions of both the IMF and the World Bank.  

Creation of the United Nations, 1945

 

Today, there are 193 member states and two non-member observer states in the United Nations.

The United Nations was founded right after World War II to replace the League of Nations. When it was formed, its goals included co-operation in international law, international security, social progress, economic development, and human-rights issues. There were 51 initial members, and by June 2008, there were 192 member countries. The United Nations is active in peacekeeping and peacemaking. It tries to help nations resolve conflicts and works toward ending poverty around the world. Every nation has a voice in the United Nations.

 How have people responded to economic globalization?

You read that one of the demands of the World Bank and IMF is that countries that want loans must limit their trade barriers and privatize the ownership of government-run operations. This has especially been the case with former communist countries. Privatization has caused hardships in countries where people rely on the government to provide them with free education, pensions, health care, and employment. It has also been a problem in nations where people who have worked under a communist system for years don’t have a lot of money to invest in buying large factories, airlines, and other businesses. That means that people from outside the country, corrupt government officials, and criminals (who often have more money than others) are the ones who prosper when government assets are sold.

Free Trade Agreements

One way nations have responded to the idea that free trade will bring greater prosperity is by setting up regional trade agreements with others. These agreements set out rules for how the different member countries will do business. You’ll read more about trade agreements in Module 5.

 The Anti-Globalization Movement

People protest against injustice in many ways. They can use creative and sometimes violent means of protest when they feel that their voices are not being heard or when they feel powerless to act against a large and powerful organization, government, or corporation. Who is responsible for the injustices that some people see in the global economy? Who should act? What should people do if they believe the current system is promoting inequality?

The actions of the World Bank, the IMF, and the World Trade Organization have outraged people around the world who believe that their policies are designed to benefit the rich and punish the poor. There have been huge and violent protests at many international meetings of these organizations.

Vocabulary

infrastructure: large-scale systems in countries or regions that are needed for the economy to function, including roads, railways, power and water, telecommunications, and schools

conditionalities: something that will be done only if something else is done first. In terms of the IMF and the World Bank, conditionalities are actions that the governments of countries applying for loans must do before any money is given to them.

privatize: to transfer a business, resource, or other economic enterprise from government or state ownership to private ownership

 

Lesson Summary

In this lesson you examined how people around the world—on their own in small groups, in large international businesses and organizations, and in powerful nations—have responded to some of the challenges and opportunities of economic globalization. Some have tried to establish rules to protect their own interests, while others protest what they see as injustice and unfairness in the form of a new kind of imperialism in which the rich of the world try to control and exploit the poor and powerless.
