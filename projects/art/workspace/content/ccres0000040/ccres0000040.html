Lesson 6: Symbols & Meaning 

Turning Ideas into Images 

Artists often communicate ideas through images rather than words. One of the most powerful ways artists do this is through the use of symbols. A symbol is an image that represents something beyond its literal appearance. Instead of showing an idea directly, a symbol suggests meaning and invites viewers to interpret what they see.

Symbols can represent emotions, memories, identity, beliefs, or experiences. A drawing of a tree might represent growth or life. An empty road might represent change or uncertainty. These images become meaningful because viewers associate them with deeper ideas.

In this lesson you will begin learning how artists turn thoughts and experiences into visual symbols. You will explore how both widely recognized symbols and personal symbols can communicate meaning. You will also begin developing your own symbolic imagery based on your experiences, interests, and identity.

This lesson builds on the technical skills you have developed earlier in the course. You will continue using composition, colour, and value, but now you will use those tools to communicate meaning rather than simply describing objects.

This lesson is designed for students learning independently or at a distance. Your artwork does not need to explain everything literally. Instead, you are encouraged to make thoughtful choices that express ideas important to you.

What You Are Learning

In this lesson you will learn how images can represent ideas and emotions. You will explore the difference between universal symbols, which many people recognize, and personal symbols, which carry meaning connected to your own life experiences.

You will also examine how artists use symbolism to explore identity and tell stories about their lives and communities. Through sketchbook exercises you will experiment with translating your own ideas into visual form.

By the end of the lesson you will create a symbolic artwork that represents something meaningful about you. The goal is not realism but communication through visual ideas.

Technique Focus

Understanding Symbols

A symbol is an image that stands for something beyond its literal appearance. Some symbols are widely recognized across many cultures and are often referred to as universal symbols. For example, a heart shape is commonly associated with love, while a skull often represents danger or death.

Other symbols are much more personal. A specific object, place, or image may hold meaning for you because it is connected to your memories or experiences. For example, a hockey stick might represent childhood memories, teamwork, or dedication for someone who grew up playing hockey. A certain landscape might represent home.

Personal symbols are especially important in Art 10 because they allow students to express ideas that are unique to their own experiences.

Metaphor in Art

Artists often use visual metaphors to communicate complex ideas indirectly. A metaphor compares two different ideas so that one image represents another concept.

For example, an image of a cracked phone screen might represent broken communication or damaged relationships. A small plant growing through concrete could symbolize resilience or determination. An empty chair might suggest absence, loss, or memory.

Metaphors allow artists to communicate ideas without explaining them directly. Instead of stating the meaning clearly, artists present an image that encourages viewers to think and interpret.

Symbolic imagery often becomes more powerful when it invites multiple interpretations.

Identity and Narrative

Symbols are frequently used by artists to tell stories about who they are, where they come from, and what they believe. By combining different symbolic elements, artists can create images that communicate personal narratives.

An artwork might include symbols representing family, culture, personal challenges, or important experiences. These symbols do not always need to be explained clearly. Often the meaning becomes richer when viewers bring their own interpretations.

When artists combine symbolic imagery with thoughtful composition and colour choices, the artwork becomes a visual story.

Learning how to create symbolic imagery allows artists to move beyond simple observation and begin expressing deeper ideas.

 

Art History and Context

In the late nineteenth century a group of artists began focusing on imagery that represented emotions, dreams, and ideas rather than realistic scenes. This movement became known as Symbolism.

Symbolist artists believed that some ideas were too complex to describe directly. Instead of painting the visible world realistically, they used symbolic imagery to suggest emotional or spiritual meaning.

Symbolism remains important in contemporary art today. Many artists use symbolic imagery to explore identity, cultural experiences, and personal narratives.

Understanding symbolism allows artists to communicate ideas that may be difficult to express through words alone.

Artists Who Use Symbolism

Kent Monkman

Kent Monkman is a contemporary Indigenous artist known for using symbolism and storytelling to explore Indigenous identity and colonial history. His paintings often include recurring symbolic characters and references to historical artworks.

One of Monkman’s well-known figures is Miss Chief Eagle Testickle, a character who appears throughout many of his artworks. This figure acts as a symbolic storyteller who challenges historical narratives and reclaims Indigenous perspectives.

Monkman’s work demonstrates how symbols can carry layered meanings connected to culture, identity, and power.

Brian Jungen

Brian Jungen is a Canadian artist who transforms everyday objects into sculptures with new symbolic meaning. In one of his well-known works, Jungen created masks inspired by Northwest Coast Indigenous forms using pieces of Nike sneakers.

By changing how familiar objects are used, Jungen invites viewers to think about consumer culture, identity, and tradition.

His work shows that symbols do not always come from drawing or painting. Sometimes the materials themselves carry meaning.

Frida Kahlo

Frida Kahlo often painted self-portraits filled with symbolic imagery representing her emotions, cultural identity, and personal experiences. Her paintings frequently include animals, plants, and objects that represent aspects of her life.

Kahlo used symbolism to communicate pain, resilience, and personal history. Rather than hiding these experiences, she transformed them into powerful visual narratives.

Her work demonstrates that personal experiences can become meaningful artistic symbols.
