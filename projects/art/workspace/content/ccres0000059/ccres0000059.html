Technique Focus

When artists create a series of artworks, they are not simply repeating the same image several times. Instead, a series uses repetition with variation, meaning that each artwork is connected to the others through a shared idea or visual strategy while still showing changes and development. The artworks might explore the same theme or subject, but the artist may vary elements such as scale, composition, colour, or materials. These changes allow the artist to investigate the idea more deeply while still maintaining a clear connection between the pieces. Through repetition with variation, each artwork becomes part of a larger exploration rather than a duplicate of the others.

Another important aspect of a successful series is visual consistency. Visual consistency helps viewers recognize that the artworks belong together as part of the same body of work. Artists often create this connection by repeating certain visual elements throughout the series. For example, they may use the same symbols, a limited or consistent colour palette, similar compositional structures, or the same materials and artistic processes. These repeated visual characteristics act like a visual language that connects each artwork in the series and helps the viewer understand the relationship between the pieces.

Finally, creating a series allows for conceptual development over time. Instead of expressing an idea in a single image, artists can explore their ideas across multiple artworks. This approach allows them to ask deeper questions, examine different perspectives, and refine their thinking as the series develops. As each new artwork is created, the artist may discover new ideas or directions that strengthen the overall concept. Working in a series encourages artists to stay with an idea long enough to fully explore it, leading to more thoughtful and developed artwork.

 

 

 

How Edward Burtynsky Uses a Series to Create Meaning

Edward Burtynsky is a Canadian photographer known for creating large photographic series that explore the relationship between human activity and the environment. Rather than taking a single photograph of an industrial site, Burtynsky revisits similar types of locations across different places and times. By photographing quarries, mines, oil fields, factories, and waste sites around the world, he builds a body of work that examines how industry reshapes landscapes. Each photograph can stand on its own, but when viewed together as part of a series, the images reveal a much larger story about environmental impact and the scale of human influence on the planet.

Burtynsky creates cohesion in his series by maintaining a consistent photographic style. His photographs are typically large-scale, highly detailed, and carefully composed. The perspective, clarity, and visual structure remain similar from one image to the next, which helps viewers recognize that the photographs belong to the same exploration. Even though the locations may be different, the visual approach remains consistent. This consistency allows the viewer to focus on the subject matter and notice how different landscapes have been shaped by similar industrial processes.

Another important feature of Burtynsky’s work is the repetition of subject matter. Many of his photographs depict industrial sites such as mines, shipping yards, or oil extraction areas. While these locations may appear different at first, they share common characteristics related to human industry and environmental transformation. By repeatedly photographing these kinds of landscapes, Burtynsky encourages viewers to see patterns in how human activity affects the natural world.

Although the subjects repeat, there are always subtle variations between the images. Each photograph shows a different location, scale, pattern, or stage of industrial development. These differences are important because they prevent the series from becoming repetitive while still maintaining a clear connection between the works. When viewers see the images together, the small variations allow them to compare and reflect on the similarities and differences between each landscape.

Burtynsky’s series builds meaning through accumulation, meaning that the impact of the work becomes stronger as more images are seen together. A single photograph might show an industrial landscape, but a series of photographs reveals how widespread these changes are across the world. As viewers move from one image to the next, they begin to understand the larger environmental story the artist is communicating.

This approach demonstrates how a series can deepen meaning over time. By returning to a similar subject again and again, Burtynsky is able to explore his theme more thoroughly than a single image could. Each photograph contributes another piece to the larger idea, allowing the viewer to see how the concept develops across the series.

For your own work in Art 30, Burtynsky’s approach shows how a series can be built through repetition with variation. When you revisit a theme or subject multiple times, each artwork adds new information, perspective, or visual interest. Even small differences between the artworks can become meaningful when the pieces are viewed together. A strong series allows the viewer to see connections between the works while also recognizing how each piece contributes something unique to the overall idea.

Dana Claxton is an Indigenous artist who works in photography, film, and installation. Her work often appears in series, where multiple images are presented together to explore ideas about Indigenous identity, power, representation, and cultural presence. Rather than creating isolated artworks, Claxton builds bodies of work that develop a concept across several images. Each photograph contributes to the overall message, and when the works are viewed together, they communicate a stronger and more layered perspective about Indigenous culture and contemporary identity.

One of the ways Claxton creates cohesion in her series is through strong visual consistency. Her images often share similar lighting, composition, and stylistic choices, which helps viewers recognize that the artworks belong to the same exploration. The photographs are typically highly stylized, meaning that colour, clothing, pose, and environment are carefully controlled to create a specific visual impact. This level of visual control helps establish a clear artistic voice and ensures that each image contributes to the overall tone and meaning of the series.

Claxton’s work also emphasizes the intentional use of symbolism. Objects, clothing, gestures, and settings are often chosen carefully because they carry cultural meaning. These symbols may refer to Indigenous traditions, identity, resistance, or contemporary life. By repeating and varying symbolic elements across several artworks, Claxton builds layers of meaning that develop as viewers encounter each image in the series. The symbolism encourages viewers to think about how Indigenous identity has been represented historically and how it can be reclaimed and expressed in contemporary art.

Another defining characteristic of Claxton’s work is its thematic focus. Each series concentrates on a central idea, such as representation, cultural power, or the visibility of Indigenous peoples in modern society. Because all of the images in the series relate to the same theme, the works reinforce one another and deepen the viewer’s understanding of the concept. This approach allows Claxton to explore complex ideas from multiple perspectives while maintaining a strong conceptual connection between the pieces.

Claxton’s work demonstrates how a series can be used to assert identity and perspective. By presenting several related images together, the repetition becomes intentional and meaningful rather than repetitive. Each image strengthens the message of the others, and the accumulation of works allows the artist to communicate ideas with greater depth and clarity.

For your own Art 30 work, Claxton’s approach shows how a series can be used to explore an important theme or personal perspective. When artworks are connected through consistent style, symbolism, and concept, they create a powerful visual statement. Repeating certain ideas or visual elements across multiple pieces can help viewers understand the message you are communicating and see how your thinking develops across the series.

Cindy Sherman is an international photographer known for creating series of photographs in which she transforms herself into different characters. In many of her projects, Sherman appears in the images while wearing different costumes, makeup, and hairstyles that represent fictional identities. Her work often explores ideas about identity, gender roles, stereotypes, and how people are represented in media and culture. Rather than creating unrelated photographs, Sherman organizes her images into series so that each photograph contributes to a larger investigation of these ideas.

Sherman creates cohesion in her work by maintaining a consistent format across the series. Many of her photographs use similar framing, lighting, and presentation, which helps the viewer recognize that the images belong to the same body of work. Even though the visual structure remains consistent, Sherman changes the character, costume, pose, or setting in each photograph. These variations allow her to explore many different identities and social roles while still maintaining a clear connection between the images.

A key characteristic of Sherman’s work is repetition with variation. The photographs repeat certain visual structures, but each image introduces a new character or scenario. This balance between consistency and change allows the viewer to compare the images and notice how different identities are constructed. Because the format remains similar, the variations in character and costume become more noticeable and meaningful.

Sherman’s work is also strongly concept-driven, meaning that the central focus of the series is the idea being explored rather than simply the visual subject. Through her different characters, Sherman invites viewers to question assumptions about identity, gender, and the roles people perform in society. Each photograph presents a different possibility, encouraging viewers to think critically about how identity can be shaped by appearance, clothing, and social expectations.

Sherman’s approach demonstrates how a series can explore one idea from many different perspectives. By maintaining consistency in format while introducing variation in character and role, she is able to investigate the concept of identity in depth. The repetition helps connect the images, while the changes between them reveal new interpretations of the theme.

For your own work in Art 30, Sherman’s practice shows how a series can be built around a single concept while exploring that concept in different ways. When a series maintains some visual consistency—such as format, composition, or technique—the variations between the artworks become more meaningful. This allows each piece to contribute a new perspective while still belonging to the same investigation.

A series shows not just what you made — but how you think over time.
