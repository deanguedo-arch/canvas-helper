Art 30 Student Course Handbook

Welcome to Art 30

Art 30 is a studio-based, portfolio-driven course focused on helping you develop a personal artistic voice.

This course builds on the skills from Art 10 and Art 20, but at this level you are expected to work with greater independence, intention, and critical thinking.

You will explore drawing, composition, and artistic context while creating a body of work that reflects who you are, what you care about, and how you choose to communicate visually.

 

What Is Art 30 Really About?

Art 30 is not about making "perfect" artwork.

It is about:

  • Developing a recognizable personal style
  • Making intentional choices about materials, techniques, and subject matter
  • Taking creative risks and experimenting
  • Reflecting thoughtfully on your process
  • Connecting your work to artists, cultures, and ideas
 

Course Structure at a Glance

  • Lessons and videos to help guide your portfolio building
  • 6–8 Major Studio Projects
  • Artist Research & Reflection 

Your work across the entire course forms a portfolio, not a series of isolated assignments.

 

The Three Big Ideas in Art 30

Every lesson and project connects to all three of these areas:

1. Drawings

How you record ideas, explore techniques, and develop skill and personal style.

2. Compositions

How you organize visual elements (line, colour, value, space, texture, form) to create meaning.

3. Encounters with Art

How artists from different cultures and times influence ideas, styles, and ways of seeing.

 

Your Sketchbook 

Your sketchbook is evidence of thinking, experimenting, and growth.

What Goes in Your Sketchbook

  • Technique experiments
  • Idea sketches (even unfinished ones)
  • Composition thumbnails
  • Media trials
  • Written reflections
  • Artist studies
  • Planning pages for major projects

Remember: More exploration = stronger portfolio.

 

Sketchbook Reflections — What to Write About

You do not need to write in perfect paragraphs. Use bullet points if needed.

Good reflections answer questions like:

  • What did I try?
  • What worked? What didn’t?
  • What materials or techniques surprised me?
  • How did an artist influence this work?
  • What would I change or push further?

Honest thinking > polished writing.

 

Final Portfolio  

These are your finished artworks that demonstrate learning over time.

Project Expectations

Each major project should show:

  • Clear intent or concept
  • Thoughtful use of materials and techniques
  • Strong composition
  • Connection to personal experience, culture, or ideas
  • Evidence of planning in your sketchbook

Projects may include (but are not limited to):

  • Drawing
  • Painting
  • Mixed media
  • Sculpture or assemblage
  • Series of related works
 

Artistic Voice & Personal Style

By the end of Art 30, your work should:

  • Look intentionally connected
  • Show recurring themes, symbols, or approaches
  • Reflect your choices, not copied solutions

Personal style does not mean repeating the same thing — it means making consistent, thoughtful decisions.

 

Critique & Talking About Art

You will regularly:

  • Write self-evaluations
  • Compare your work to professional artists

You will learn to use words like:

  • composition
  • emphasis
  • contrast
  • balance
  • symbolism
  • originality
  • technique
  • clarity of meaning

This helps you explain why something works — not just whether you like it.

 

Assessment — How You Are Evaluated

Art 30 assessment focuses on growth, intention, and evidence.

You are assessed on:

  • Skill development
  • Creative risk-taking
  • Problem-solving
  • Reflection and self-evaluation
  • Portfolio quality
  • Engagement with artists and ideas

Effort alone is not enough — thinking and decision-making matter.

 

Final Portfolio

Your final submission includes:

  • Selected major artworks
  • Supporting sketchbook pages
  • Written artist statement
  • Self-evaluation

Your portfolio should answer the question:

Who am I as an artist right now?

Artist Statement

Your artist statement explains:

  • What themes or ideas you explore
  • Why you chose certain materials or processes
  • How your work connects to artists or culture
  • What you have learned about yourself as an artist

This is about clarity, not fancy language.

 

Tips for Success in Art 30

  • Take risks early
  • Don’t wait for “perfect” ideas
  • Use your sketchbook constantly
  • Ask questions
  • Look at lots of art
  • Reflect honestly
  • Push ideas further than feels comfortable

Art 30 rewards curiosity and commitment.

 

Final Thought

Art 30 is your chance to:

  • Explore who you are
  • Say something meaningful
  • Build a body of work you’re proud of

You don’t need to know exactly where you’re going — you just need to keep working, reflecting, and growing.
