Understanding What Makes a Series Cohesive

Before beginning an in-depth study, it is important to understand what makes a group of artworks feel connected to one another. In art, a series refers to several artworks that are intentionally related through a shared idea, visual approach, or artistic process. Instead of each piece standing alone as an unrelated image, the artworks work together to explore a larger concept. When a series is successful, viewers can easily recognize that the artworks belong together and are part of the same investigation.

Artists create this sense of connection by developing visual unity across multiple pieces. Visual unity can come from repeating certain elements throughout the series. For example, an artist might use a consistent colour palette across all of the artworks, or they may repeatedly explore the same subject matter. Symbols can also create cohesion if they appear throughout the series and carry meaning that develops from one piece to the next. Composition can also connect artworks if the artist organizes images in similar ways or repeats certain visual structures. In some cases, artists unify a series through technique by using the same materials, processes, or mark-making approaches in each artwork.

When studying artists who work in series, it becomes clear that these connections are usually created through small but intentional visual decisions. By repeating certain colours, shapes, textures, or themes, artists guide the viewer to see the artworks as part of a larger body of work rather than separate pieces. As you analyze examples of artists who create series, pay attention to the visual strategies they use to create cohesion. Recognizing these patterns will help you understand how artists build meaningful connections between artworks and will help you plan your own in-depth study series.
