Nondisjunction and chromosome abnormalities

How do segregation errors change gamete chromosome numbers?

Compare first-division and second-division errors

If a homologous pair fails to separate in I and meiosis II otherwise proceeds normally, all four final products have an abnormal number for that chromosome type. Two have an extra chromosome, n + 1, and two are missing one, n − 1.

If meiosis I is normal but sister chromatids fail to separate in one cell during meiosis II, two products can remain normal. The affected cell produces one n + 1 product and one n − 1 product. The other cell produces two normal n products.

These predictions assume one specified error and otherwise normal divisions. Multiple errors can produce other patterns, so do not apply the simplified pattern without checking the question.

Fertilization converts a gamete error into a zygote count

An n + 1 gamete joining a normal n gamete produces a 2n + 1 zygote, a trisomic complement for one chromosome. An n − 1 gamete joining a normal gamete produces 2n − 1, a monosomic complement.

An extra copy of one chromosome differs from an extra complete set. 21, for example, concerns chromosome 21, not three copies of every chromosome. Polyploidy refers to additional whole sets.

A karyotype can identify many count abnormalities. The detailed effects depend on the chromosome and biological context; a schematic should not be treated as a complete prediction of a person's abilities or health.

Worked example

Infer where the error occurred

Four meiotic products have counts n + 1, n − 1, n and n for one chromosome type.

  1. Two products are normal, so the simple meiosis-I nondisjunction pattern does not fit.
  2. A normal meiosis I can produce two cells with the correct homologues.
  3. An error in only one of those cells during meiosis II produces one extra and one missing chromosome.
  4. The unaffected cell produces two normal products.
  5. Under the stated single-error model, meiosis II nondisjunction fits.
