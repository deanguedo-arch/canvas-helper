One counting method across the chapter

Identify homologues, sister chromatids and chromosome sets separately. DNA replication increases DNA amount while chromatids remain associated. Chromatid separation changes the number of independently counted chromosomes within the still-undivided cell. Always state whether the count refers to a whole cell, a pole or a daughter nucleus.

The same approach works for a small 2n = 4 model and a human 2n = 46 cell. The model simplifies the number of structures, not the underlying rules.

Mechanisms explain outcomes

Mitosis distributes copied chromosomes and normally preserves ploidy. Meiosis separates homologues and then chromatids, reducing sets while creating new combinations. Nondisjunction changes the count of a chromosome type when segregation fails.

In a life cycle, follow the arrows where sets change. Meiosis and fertilization balance the reduction and restoration of ploidy. Mitosis can occur in haploid or diploid stages; the largest visible body is not automatically diploid.

Worked example

Compare three cell snapshots

A 2n = 6 model shows A: six replicated chromosomes; B: three replicated chromosomes per cell; C: three unreplicated chromosomes per product.

  1. A can represent a post-S diploid cell before reduction: six chromosomes and twelve chromatids.
  2. B fits the end of meiosis I: one complete haploid set, still replicated.
  3. C fits the end of meiosis II: one complete haploid set with separated chromosomes.
  4. The change from B to C separates copies, not another halving of the chromosome types.

Written explanation 1

Maximum 3200 characters. Explain the biology in your own words.

Compare after saving your own response

After replication the cell has four chromosomes and eight chromatids. Mitosis separates sister chromatids and produces two cells with four chromosomes each. Meiosis I separates homologues into cells with two replicated chromosomes; meiosis II separates chromatids, producing haploid products with two chromosomes each.
