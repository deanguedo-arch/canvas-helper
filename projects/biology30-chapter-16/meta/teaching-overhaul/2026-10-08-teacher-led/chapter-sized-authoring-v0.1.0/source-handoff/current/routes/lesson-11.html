In , offspring form without fusion of gametes. Budding in hydra and vegetative reproduction in plants are examples. Eukaryotic asexual reproduction often uses , maintaining the genetic complement of the parent lineage.

Different strategies have different trade-offs

Asexual reproduction can establish offspring without finding a mate and can preserve a successful genotype. However, genetically similar descendants may share vulnerabilities when conditions change. Mutation can still create differences, so clones are not permanently immune to variation.

Sexual reproduction can produce more varied allele combinations, potentially increasing the range of responses within offspring. It also involves costs such as gamete production and, in many organisms, finding or interacting with a mate. Neither strategy is universally best in every environment.

Parthenogenesis involves development from an unfertilized egg. Because different organisms use different cellular mechanisms, read the supplied life-cycle information before assigning ploidy or claiming all resulting offspring are identical.

Bacteria transfer DNA and divide by different processes

Bacteria reproduce by , copying genetic material and dividing the cell. They do not have a membrane-bound nucleus, so this is not nuclear mitosis with the same -stage sequence used for eukaryotes.

Bacteria can also exchange genetic material. During , direct contact can transfer DNA such as a plasmid. This changes genetic content but is not itself the production of an additional cell. Division and genetic exchange are different events.

Use the number of cells and the movement of DNA as separate observations. A diagram showing a connection between two cells may show exchange, not two gametes fusing.

Worked example

Read two bacterial diagrams

Diagram A shows one bacterium copying its chromosome and separating into two cells. Diagram B shows DNA passing between two cells that remain distinct.

  1. Diagram A increases cell number through binary fission.
  2. Diagram B changes genetic content through contact-mediated transfer, consistent with conjugation.
  3. Neither requires a eukaryotic nucleus or the PMAT stages of mitosis.
  4. Do not label both events reproduction merely because both involve DNA.
