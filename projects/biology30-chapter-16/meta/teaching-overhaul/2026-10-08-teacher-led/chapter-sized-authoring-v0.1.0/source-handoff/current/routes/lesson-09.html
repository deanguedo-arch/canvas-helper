Begin with the purpose of chromosome distribution

preserves the complement when a cell divides. It supports growth, replacement and asexual reproduction in many eukaryotes. A diploid cell normally makes diploid daughter nuclei; a haploid cell can make haploid daughter nuclei by mitosis.

reduces chromosome sets in sexual life cycles. The reduction allows fertilization to restore a diploid set without doubling at every generation. A diploid precursor undergoes one replication and two divisions.

Connect a changed mechanism to a changed outcome

If a question shows two products, do not automatically choose mitosis; it may show only the end of meiosis I. Likewise, four pictures may show four stages of one process rather than four final cells. Read what each image represents.

To compare fairly, use the same starting chromosome model. For 2n = 4, normal mitosis produces two 2n = 4 nuclei. Complete meiosis produces haploid products with n = 2. Explain how the separation events produce these outcomes.

“Identical” is a useful expected pattern in normal mitosis, but mutations can occur. “Different” in meiosis concerns genetic combinations, not an instruction to remove essential chromosome types.

Worked example

Identify a process from several clues

A diagram shows one DNA replication, homologous pairing, two successive divisions and products with half the starting chromosome sets.

  1. One replication alone fits both mitosis and meiosis, so it is not decisive.
  2. Homologous pairing and a reduction division point toward meiosis I.
  3. A second division separating chromatids completes meiosis.
  4. The haploid final products agree with that mechanism.
