How can you follow ploidy through an unfamiliar life cycle?

Learning goal

Before you begin

Use three rules through any diagram

normally preserves ploidy. reduces a diploid complement to haploid. Fertilization combines haploid gametic complements to form a diploid zygote. These rules help you interpret an unfamiliar life cycle without memorizing every species illustration.

Look for the arrows where sets change. A 2n-to-n transition suggests meiosis in the standard model. Fusion of two n cells to make 2n identifies fertilization. Growth from n to n or 2n to 2n commonly involves mitosis.

Follow a plant’s two multicellular stages

A diploid produces haploid spores through meiosis. A develops by mitosis into a haploid . The gametophyte then produces haploid gametes by mitosis. When gametes fuse, the diploid zygote grows by mitosis into a new sporophyte.

The surprising step for learners is gamete production by mitosis in the gametophyte. The parent stage is already haploid, so another is not needed. The meiotic products in this plant cycle are spores, not the final gametes.

Spores and gametes therefore have different roles. A spore can grow into a multicellular stage without fusion. A gamete contributes to the next diploid stage through fertilization.

The most visible stage depends on the organism

In mosses, the familiar green plant is usually the gametophyte, while the sporophyte remains attached to it. In conifers, the familiar tree is the sporophyte; the gametophytes are much smaller and develop in association with reproductive structures.

Do not label the largest structure diploid merely because it is large. Use the life-cycle arrows and the chromosome information. An unfamiliar organism may also alternate sexual and without following the same plant pattern.

When answering, state the evidence: “These two haploid cells fuse, so this arrow represents fertilization.” That is stronger than guessing from a familiar-looking shape.

Worked example

Identify three arrows

A cycle shows a 2n plant producing n spores, spores growing into an n plant, and two n gametes fusing.

  1. The 2n-to-n spore-producing step is meiosis.
  2. Growth from an n spore to an n multicellular stage preserves ploidy and uses mitosis.
  3. The fusion of two n gametes produces a 2n zygote through fertilization.
  4. The diploid multicellular stage is the sporophyte and the haploid one the gametophyte.
