Cytokinesis, growth and tissue maintenance

How does division produce separate cells and support a multicellular body?

Learning goal

Compare plant and animal cytokinesis, explain growth and repair, and distinguish normal cell-cycle control from uncontrolled division.

Before you begin

Mitosis produces two nuclear chromosome groups. Separate cells also require division of the cytoplasm.

Animal and plant cells divide differently

In an animal cell, a contractile ring helps constrict the cell surface. The resulting deepens until the cytoplasm is separated. Each daughter cell receives a nucleus and cytoplasmic components.

A plant cell has a rigid cell wall, so it cannot complete division by the same inward pinching process. Vesicles gather and fuse near the cell middle, forming a . This partition expands and contributes to the new membranes and wall separating the daughter cells.

In both cases, the goal is separation into cells. The physical method differs because of cell structure. The word identifies the cytoplasmic process, not one specific shape of a dividing cell.

Division supports growth and replacement

A multicellular body grows partly by increasing cell number. Mitosis and cytokinesis also replace cells lost through normal wear and support repair after injury. Different tissues vary in how frequently their cells divide and in their ability to regenerate.

Stem cells can retain a capacity for division and produce cells that become specialized. This does not mean every body cell can form every tissue. The developmental potential of a stem cell depends on the type and its context.

A cell culture or tissue sample can help investigate division, but a visible increase in cell number alone does not show that a complete functional organ has been repaired.

Control matters as much as the capacity to divide

Checkpoints and signalling systems help regulate the . They can delay division when conditions are unsuitable or DNA is damaged. Changes to genes involved in these controls can allow uncontrolled proliferation and contribute to cancer.

Telomeres are protective regions at ends. They shorten during many somatic-cell divisions and are involved in limitations on continued proliferation. Telomere length is not a simple personal countdown clock, and not every cell has the same maintenance mechanisms.

Compare outcomes using evidence: organized replacement supports tissue function; uncontrolled division can disrupt it. The rate of division by itself does not provide a complete diagnosis.

Worked example

Explain the shape of cytokinesis

Two images show an animal cell pinching inward and a plant cell developing a partition through its centre.

  1. Both show cytoplasmic separation after or during nuclear division.
  2. The animal cell can constrict its flexible boundary, forming a cleavage furrow.
  3. The plant cell builds a cell plate because a rigid wall prevents the same pinching mechanism.
  4. Different mechanisms accomplish the same broad task of separating daughter cells.
