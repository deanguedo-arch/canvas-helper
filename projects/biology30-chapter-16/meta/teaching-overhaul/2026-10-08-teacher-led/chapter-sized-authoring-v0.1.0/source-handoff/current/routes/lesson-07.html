The chromosome movements resemble mitosis, but the starting cells have one set of rather than two. Because crossing over may have occurred, the chromatids that separate are not necessarily genetically identical over their whole lengths.

Check the final counts

In a 2n = 4 starting model, each cell after meiosis I has two replicated chromosomes. After meiosis II, each product has two unreplicated chromosomes. It remains haploid, n = 2. Meiosis II does not halve the number of chromosome types again.

The complete meiotic process usually produces four haploid nuclear products from one diploid precursor. Each contains a chromosome of every type needed for a haploid set. Random assortment and recombination create genetic differences among those products.

Functional gametes are not always four equal cells

During , meiotic products can differentiate into four sperm. Their shape and cell contents are modified for their role in fertilization. A spermatid is not yet a fully mature sperm simply because meiosis has ended.

During oogenesis, cytoplasmic division is unequal. Most cytoplasm is retained in one large cell, while polar bodies receive other chromosome complements and little cytoplasm. This concentrates cellular resources in the future egg. In humans, the secondary oocyte normally completes meiosis II in connection with fertilization.

Distinguish four nuclear products from four equally functional gametes. The patterns of cytoplasm allocation and differentiation affect what the products become.

Worked example

Do not halve n twice

A species has 2n = 10. Follow one cell from the end of meiosis I to the end of meiosis II.

  1. After meiosis I the cell has five chromosomes, each with two chromatids.
  2. No full DNA replication occurs before meiosis II.
  3. Sister chromatids separate in meiosis II.
  4. Each final product has five unreplicated chromosomes: n = 5, not 2.5.
  5. Meiosis I reduced sets; meiosis II separated copies.
