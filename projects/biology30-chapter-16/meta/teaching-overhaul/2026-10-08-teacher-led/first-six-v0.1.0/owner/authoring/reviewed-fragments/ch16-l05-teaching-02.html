More cells must become useful tissue

Cell division supports three related functions. Growth increases cell number as an organism develops. Maintenance replaces cells lost through normal wear. Repair replaces damaged tissue, such as cells involved in closing a skin wound. Each new cell needs the genetic information required to carry out its role, and division must occur in the right place and amount.

Stem cells can keep dividing and produce descendants that become specialized. Their developmental possibilities depend on the stem-cell type and its surroundings; an adult tissue stem cell cannot automatically produce every kind of body cell. Different tissues also differ in their capacity for replacement. Many mature cells do not divide continuously.

Suppose a culture doubles its cell count after a growth signal. That shows an increase in number, but successful repair also requires the appropriate cell types, organization and function. For a damaged sensory tissue, for example, replacement cells would need the right specialized structures and connections to transmit useful signals. Evidence of those functions and controlled growth is needed before concluding that the tissue has been restored.
