Signals and checkpoints regulate the . A cell-cycle checkpoint is a control mechanism that can delay progression when conditions are unsuitable, including when DNA damage is detected. A pause gives the cell an opportunity to respond; the checkpoint is not itself a guarantee that every error will be repaired. If damaged DNA is copied and distributed despite failed control, the damage can pass to daughter cells.

Normal root-tip growth can involve rapid division while still responding to regulatory signals. Cancer involves abnormal loss of growth control; speed alone is insufficient evidence. Changes in genes that regulate division can allow damaged lineages to keep multiplying. A tissue's organization, function and response to signals matter alongside its cell count.

Telomeres are protective regions at ends. They shorten during many somatic-cell divisions and can contribute to limits on continued proliferation. Some cells maintain them differently, including by adding repeat sequences. This is a cell-biology relationship, not a simple clock that predicts a person's age or remaining lifespan.
