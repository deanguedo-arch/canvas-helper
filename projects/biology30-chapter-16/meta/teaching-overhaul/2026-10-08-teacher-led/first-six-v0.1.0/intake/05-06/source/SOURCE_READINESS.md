## Source contradictions and bounded decisions

1. **Cytokinesis timing.** Teacher23 says all four mitotic stages precede possible cytokinesis, while teacher31's original figure text and textbook557 telophase image show overlap. Native05 already allows during/after nuclear division. Preserve that distinction; do not introduce an absolute non-overlap rule. A primary experimental paper independently places initiation in anaphase. [Perchey et al.,2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30327204/)
2. **Division rate and cancer.** Textbook560–561 emphasizes rapid division/tumours. Native05 correctly distinguishes normal controlled growth from failed regulation, and increased number from functional repair. Keep these limits. The NCI describes normal need-based replacement and abnormal growth, including that tumours can be benign; NIGMS identifies pauses for DNA problems. Do not infer a diagnosis from a root-tip rate or a single culture observation. [NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/understanding/what-is-cancer), [NIGMS glossary, cell cycle](https://nigms.nih.gov/education/glossary)
3. **Stem-cell types.** Teacher35 broadly calls adult-tissue stem cells pluripotent/easy to obtain and declares only the zygote totipotent. NIH distinguishes adult tissue stem cells from embryonic/iPS pluripotent cells, and NIH developmental researchers study totipotent early-embryo cells. Preserve native05's limited type/context distinction; do not teach slide35's universal claims or impose its ethical judgment. No potency taxonomy or ethics assignment is added. [NIH stem-cell basics](https://stemcells.nih.gov/info/basics/stc-basics/), [NIDDK laboratory research](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/at-niddk/labs-branches/laboratory-cellular-developmental-biology/mammalian-developmental-biology/research)
4. **Telomeres.** Teacher36 universalizes shortening every division and inevitable immediate death. Native05 already limits shortening to many somatic divisions and rejects a personal countdown. NHGRI's narration explicitly describes telomerase restoring repeats in some cell types; NIA's own scientific report distinguishes senescence from apoptosis. Keep the modest cell-biology explanation; omit personal lifestyle/aging predictions and Dolly-causation inference. NIA was available through indexed official text but direct fetch failed, which limits that retrieval claim. [NHGRI telomere](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Telomere?id=194), [NIA scientific council report](https://www.nia.nih.gov/about/naca/council-minutes-january-2010)
5. **Homologue sets, not all-maternal/all-paternal daughters.** Teacher49's wording can imply that an entire parental set goes to a daughter, contrary to teacher45–47 and textbook566. One of each homologous pair goes to each pole; pair orientations are independent. Retain the native explanation. Stage the language around complete haploid sets, not a purported loss of half the gene types. [NHGRI meiosis](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Meiosis) confirms the single-set meaning; actual source565–566 supplies the mechanism.
6. **Exact source figure16.11.** The model has2n=4: a short blue/yellow pair and a long green/red pair. In its metaphaseI cell the poles are top/bottom, the short tetrad is at left and long tetrad at right. Upper and lower chromosome rows lie on opposite sides of the equator. The upper daughter receives blue+green, the lower yellow+red, each one short and one long. Each has2 chromosomes/4 chromatids. The four colours trace chromosome units, not a supplied two-colour parental legend. Exchanged colour tips already represent recombination. The circular layout is a sequence with two divisions, not repeated DNA replication.
7. **Synapsis versus metaphase alignment.** Native worked06 step1 says homologues pair at metaphaseI. Its words/models/sequence, teacher42 and textbook564 place synapsis in prophaseI. Preserve the original worked model in this packet; later teaching should explicitly identify homologues as already paired when they align at metaphaseI. Any change to the frozen worked/model fields requires an explicit disposition instead of incidental config edits.
8. **Sister identity and tetrads.** Teacher44/textbook565's simplified identity statements apply before exchange; recombination can make sisters genetically nonidentical. TextbookFigure16.12 caption's wording about two pairs of non-sisters is potentially confusing: its actual labels, p564 prose and native key identify a tetrad as two replicated homologues, each with a sister pair. Keep the native2-chromosome/4-chromatid key. The crossing-over figures support non-sister exchange, not four chromosome sets. [NHGRI crossing over](https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Crossing-Over)
9. **No extra S phase; no universal identical products.** Teacher50 says interphase does not occur and calls meiosis-II products identical. Keep the narrower no-DNA-replication-between-divisions statement; textbook565 allows variation in telophaseI/nuclear reformation, and its actual recombination figure shows nonidentical products. Detailed meiosisII is later07, except retained forward optional context. Do not imply all four products become functional ova.
10. **Model versus species.** Teacher42's two-pair wording belongs to its2n=4 model, not every meiotic cell. Native worked06 explicitly supplies that model; required writing independently supplies2n=8, and guided work2n=12. Preserve all three givens and always specify per daughter/per pole versus whole undivided cell.
