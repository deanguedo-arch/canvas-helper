Vocabulary help
Key terms
Words for this lesson
Use these definitions when you need help with a term.
cytokinesis
Division of the cytoplasm into separate daughter cells.
cleavage furrow
An inward constriction of an animal cell during cytokinesis.
cell plate
A developing partition that contributes to the new wall between dividing plant cells.
cell-cycle checkpoint
A control mechanism that helps determine whether a cell proceeds to a later stage.
Animal and plant cells divide differently
In an animal cell, a contractile ring helps constrict the cell surface. The resulting
cleavage furrow
deepens until the cytoplasm is separated. Each daughter cell receives a nucleus and cytoplasmic components.
A plant cell has a rigid cell wall, so it cannot complete division by the same inward pinching process. Vesicles gather and fuse near the cell middle, forming a
cell plate
. This partition expands and contributes to the new membranes and wall separating the daughter cells.
In both cases, the goal is separation into cells. The physical method differs because of cell structure. The word
cytokinesis
identifies the cytoplasmic process, not one specific shape of a dividing cell.
Cytokinesis in a plant cell
View larger
A cell plate develops between the daughter nuclei. Plant cytokinesis differs from an animal cell membrane pinching inward. Inquiry into Biology, Chapter 16, p. 559.
Division supports growth and replacement
A multicellular body grows partly by increasing cell number. Mitosis and cytokinesis also replace cells lost through normal wear and support repair after injury. Different tissues vary in how frequently their cells divide and in their ability to regenerate.
Stem cells can retain a capacity for division and produce cells that become specialized. This does not mean every body cell can form every tissue. The developmental potential of a stem cell depends on the type and its context.
A cell culture or tissue sample can help investigate division, but a visible increase in cell number alone does not show that a complete functional organ has been repaired.
Control matters as much as the capacity to divide
Checkpoints and signalling systems help regulate the
cell cycle
. They can delay division when conditions are unsuitable or DNA is damaged. Changes to genes involved in these controls can allow uncontrolled proliferation and contribute to cancer.
Telomeres are protective regions at
chromosome
ends. They shorten during many somatic-cell divisions and are involved in limitations on continued proliferation. Telomere length is not a simple personal countdown clock, and not every cell has the same maintenance mechanisms.
Compare outcomes using evidence: organized replacement supports tissue function; uncontrolled division can disrupt it. The rate of division by itself does not provide a complete diagnosis.
Worked example
Explain the shape of cytokinesis
Two images show an animal cell pinching inward and a plant cell developing a partition through its centre.
Both show cytoplasmic separation after or during nuclear division.
The animal cell can constrict its flexible boundary, forming a cleavage furrow.
The plant cell builds a cell plate because a rigid wall prevents the same pinching mechanism.
Different mechanisms accomplish the same broad task of separating daughter cells.
