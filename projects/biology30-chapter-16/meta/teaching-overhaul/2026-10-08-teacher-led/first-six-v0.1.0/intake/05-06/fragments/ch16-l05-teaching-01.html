Separate the cytoplasm, then compare the boundaries

Mitosis distributes nuclear chromosomes into two groups. Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm so those groups can belong to separate daughter cells. The processes can overlap near the end of mitosis; two nuclei inside one continuous cell do not yet show completed cytoplasmic separation. DNA copying happened earlier, during S phase.

A plant cell has a rigid outer wall. Small membrane-bound vesicles gather and fuse between the daughter nuclei, forming a that grows outward. Their membranes contribute to the new cell membranes, while deposited wall material forms the partition between the daughters. Both animal and onion cells use spindle microtubules to distribute chromosomes. Typical animal cells have centrioles associated with spindle organization; onion cells form a spindle without centrioles. Thus, a cell plate versus a furrow distinguishes the cytokinesis mechanism, while the centriole comparison concerns spindle organization. Both kinds of cell have cell membranes.
