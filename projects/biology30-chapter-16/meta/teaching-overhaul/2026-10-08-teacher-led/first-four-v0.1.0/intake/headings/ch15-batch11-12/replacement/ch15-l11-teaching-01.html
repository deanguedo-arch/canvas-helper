Locate the step that a technology changes

Reproduction requires several distinct events: an oocyte develops and is released, sperm reach it, occurs, and the resulting embryo develops and implants. A technology can change one step without completing every later step. Begin a comparison by naming what is placed where and which event still has to occur.

In vitro fertilization, or , brings eggs and sperm together outside the body in a laboratory. An embryo produced there may subsequently be transferred into the uterus.

Follow the difference: insemination places sperm in the reproductive tract before fertilization; embryo transfer places an already developing embryo in the uterus after laboratory fertilization. Placement is not attachment. After transfer, successful in the endometrium must still occur for a continuing uterine pregnancy.

This explains why a model with blocked oviducts is different from one with inadequate follicle stimulation. Laboratory fertilization can bypass the usual meeting place, whereas fertility medication can alter hormonal stimulation of follicles. Increasing that stimulation does not itself open a blocked duct. These examples locate mechanisms; they do not select treatment for an individual.
