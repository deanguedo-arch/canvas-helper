Worked example

Track the count through a changed cell state

Given: A model diploid G1 cell has six unreplicated chromosomes. It completes S phase normally, but no sister chromatids have separated. Question: What has changed, and what has remained the same?

  1. Record the starting state: six unreplicated chromosomes contain six DNA molecules. The diploid model has two sets of three chromosome types.
  2. Apply S phase: each chromosome's DNA is copied. Each now consists of two joined sisters, giving 6 × 2 = 12 chromatids and double the starting DNA amount.
  3. Use the stated boundary: the sisters have not separated. Count six replicated chromosomes, not twelve individual chromosomes. Ploidy remains 2n = 6.
  4. Follow normal division: mitosis distributes one copy of each chromosome to each daughter nucleus; cytokinesis separates the cells. Each daughter receives six chromosomes in this model.

Check a different condition: if mitosis completed but cytokinesis failed, the model could have two nuclei in one shared cytoplasm. Count nuclei and cells separately; two nuclei do not by themselves demonstrate that two daughter cells have formed.
