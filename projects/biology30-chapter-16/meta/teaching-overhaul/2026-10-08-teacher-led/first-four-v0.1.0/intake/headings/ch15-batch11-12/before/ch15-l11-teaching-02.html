Distinguish a transport route from a hormone route

interrupts the vasa deferentia, the ducts carrying sperm from the epididymides toward the ejaculatory pathway. Testicular hormone-producing cells release testosterone into blood, a different route. Blocking a sperm duct therefore does not by itself stop testosterone reaching its targets. Accessory glands can continue adding fluid to semen even when sperm transport is interrupted.

In tubal ligation, the oviduct pathway is interrupted so sperm and oocyte are less likely to meet. This acts on transport rather than removing the ovaries. Physical barriers, such as condoms, also reduce gamete contact without needing to stop ovarian hormone secretion. A change in passage is not evidence that every reproductive tissue has stopped working.

Hormonal contraception changes signalling or conditions for sperm passage. Combined estrogen-like and progesterone-like contraceptive signals can reduce the FSH/LH stimulation needed for follicular development and the LH surge that triggers ovulation. Some other hormonal methods rely substantially on thickening cervical mucus, making sperm passage more difficult even when ovulation continues. Name the particular mechanism rather than treating all methods as identical.

Two source clarifications matter here. An IUD is a device in the uterus: copper IUDs are non-hormonal, while hormonal IUDs can act through cervical mucus and other local effects without consistently stopping ovulation. Emergency contraceptive pills are contraception; levonorgestrel emergency contraception acts by preventing or delaying ovulation. The old textbook statement that it prevents implantation is not supported by current evidence.
