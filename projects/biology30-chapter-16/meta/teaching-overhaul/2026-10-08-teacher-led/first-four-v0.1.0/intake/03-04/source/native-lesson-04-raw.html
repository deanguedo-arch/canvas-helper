Mitosis: chromosome movement through the stages

How does mitosis give two nuclei a matching chromosome complement?

Learning goal

Before you begin

DNA replication has already occurred before normal mitosis. Sister chromatids are copies of one chromosome.

Key terms

Words for this lesson

A microtubule structure involved in chromosome attachment and movement during division.
The mitotic stage in which chromosome groups reach the poles and daughter nuclei form.

Prepare and align the chromosomes

During , replicated chromosomes condense and the spindle becomes organized. The nuclear envelope breaks down as division proceeds, allowing spindle microtubules to interact with attachment regions. Some accounts distinguish part of this interval as prometaphase; the four-stage model groups these events for learning.

During , replicated chromosomes align individually along the equator of the cell. The two are connected to opposite spindle poles. This arrangement makes it possible to separate one copy toward each future daughter nucleus.

Separate copies, not homologous pairs

During , sister chromatids separate. Each becomes an individual chromosome and moves toward its assigned pole. The important event is separation of the copies made during .

Mitosis does not normally pair maternal and paternal into tetrads and separate those pairs as meiosis I does. A diploid cell uses mitosis to preserve the chromosome complement in the two daughter nuclei.

If a diagram uses two colours for parental origin, each normal mitotic daughter receives both parental contributions. Do not place all maternal chromosomes in one daughter and all paternal chromosomes in the other.

Finish nuclear organization

During , chromosomes arrive at opposite poles, become less condensed and are enclosed by forming nuclear envelopes. The spindle breaks down. Cytokinesis then completes or overlaps the separation into daughter cells, depending on the cell type.

The sequence is often abbreviated PMAT: prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. The abbreviation is useful only when connected with the events. In a new image, locate condensed chromosomes, their alignment, their separation or the forming nuclei.

Normal mitosis produces daughter nuclei with matching chromosome complements. Mutations or division errors can introduce differences, so “identical” describes the expected genetic distribution, not a guarantee against every possible change.

Worked example

Identify a stage without relying on the caption

A model cell contains two groups of separated chromosomes moving toward opposite poles, and new nuclear envelopes have not formed.

  1. The chromosomes are no longer lined up at the equator, so this is not metaphase.
  2. Sister chromatids have separated and are moving apart.
  3. These observations identify anaphase.
  4. Count each separated chromatid as a chromosome, and state whether counting one pole or the whole cell.
