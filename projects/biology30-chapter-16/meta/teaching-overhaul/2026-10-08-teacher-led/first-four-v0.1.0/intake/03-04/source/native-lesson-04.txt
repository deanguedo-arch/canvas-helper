Mitosis: chromosome movement through the stages
How does mitosis give two nuclei a matching chromosome complement?
Learning goal
Trace prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase, connecting visible events with accurate chromosome distribution.
Before you begin
DNA replication has already occurred before normal mitosis. Sister chromatids are copies of one chromosome.

Vocabulary help
Key terms
Words for this lesson
Use these definitions when you need help with a term.
prophase
The mitotic stage in which chromosomes condense and the division machinery becomes organized.
metaphase
The mitotic stage in which replicated chromosomes align at the cell equator.
spindle fibre
A microtubule structure involved in chromosome attachment and movement during division.
telophase
The mitotic stage in which chromosome groups reach the poles and daughter nuclei form.
Prepare and align the chromosomes
During 
prophase
, replicated chromosomes condense and the spindle becomes organized. The nuclear envelope breaks down as division proceeds, allowing spindle microtubules to interact with 
chromosome
 attachment regions. Some accounts distinguish part of this interval as prometaphase; the four-stage model groups these events for learning.
During 
metaphase
, replicated chromosomes align individually along the equator of the cell. The two 
sister chromatids
 are connected to opposite spindle poles. This arrangement makes it possible to separate one copy toward each future daughter nucleus.
Mitosis in a model cell: 2n = 4
View larger
Four replicated chromosomes align individually; sister chromatids separate. Each daughter nucleus receives four chromosomes. Blue and orange identify parental origins, not different cell types. Schematic — not to scale.
Separate copies, not homologous pairs
During 
anaphase
, sister chromatids separate. Each becomes an individual chromosome and moves toward its assigned pole. The important event is separation of the copies made during 
S phase
.
Mitosis does not normally pair maternal and paternal 
homologues
 into tetrads and separate those pairs as meiosis I does. A diploid cell uses mitosis to preserve the chromosome complement in the two daughter nuclei.
If a diagram uses two colours for parental origin, each normal mitotic daughter receives both parental contributions. Do not place all maternal chromosomes in one daughter and all paternal chromosomes in the other.
Mitosis in diagrams and microscope images
View larger
Use chromosome condensation, alignment, separation and nuclear re-formation to identify stages. Interphase belongs to the cell cycle but is not a stage of mitosis. Inquiry into Biology, Chapter 16, p. 557.
Finish nuclear organization
During 
telophase
, chromosomes arrive at opposite poles, become less condensed and are enclosed by forming nuclear envelopes. The spindle breaks down. Cytokinesis then completes or overlaps the separation into daughter cells, depending on the cell type.
The sequence is often abbreviated PMAT: prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. The abbreviation is useful only when connected with the events. In a new image, locate condensed chromosomes, their alignment, their separation or the forming nuclei.
Normal mitosis produces daughter nuclei with matching chromosome complements. Mutations or division errors can introduce differences, so “identical” describes the expected genetic distribution, not a guarantee against every possible change.
Worked example
Identify a stage without relying on the caption
A model cell contains two groups of separated chromosomes moving toward opposite poles, and new nuclear envelopes have not formed.
The chromosomes are no longer lined up at the equator, so this is not metaphase.
Sister chromatids have separated and are moving apart.
These observations identify anaphase.
Count each separated chromatid as a chromosome, and state whether counting one pole or the whole cell.
