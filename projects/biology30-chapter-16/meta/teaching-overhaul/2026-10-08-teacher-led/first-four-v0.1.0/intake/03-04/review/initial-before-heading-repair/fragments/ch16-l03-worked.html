Worked example

Explain why the same number can describe different things

Given: A normal diploid cell has 2n = 6. Question: Count its chromosomes in G1, after S, during whole-cell mitotic anaphase and in each daughter cell. Explain the changes.

  1. G1: there are six unreplicated chromosomes, each with one DNA molecule. There are no sister pairs yet.
  2. After S, before separation: every chromosome has copied its DNA. Six joined sister pairs are still six chromosomes, now containing twelve chromatids. DNA amount is twice the G1 amount.
  3. Anaphase, whole undivided cell: the twelve sisters have separated and now count as twelve individual chromosomes. Six move toward each pole. No second replication occurred, so whole-cell DNA amount is unchanged from the post-S state.
  4. Each daughter: normal division distributes six chromosomes to each cell. Its chromosome number remains 2n = 6, and each has half the preceding undivided cell's DNA amount.

Check the wording: “Twelve” describes chromatids after S but whole-cell chromosomes after separation. Neither statement means twelve chromosomes arrive in each daughter. Always attach the number to its stage, quantity and boundary.
