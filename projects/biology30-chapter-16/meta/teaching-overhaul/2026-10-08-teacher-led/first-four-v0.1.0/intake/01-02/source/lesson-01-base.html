Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

A DNA molecule with associated proteins, organized as a distinct genetic unit.
DNA and its associated proteins in the nucleus, often less condensed between divisions.
A pair of chromosomes with the same genes at corresponding loci, one typically inherited from each parent.
One of each type of chromosome characteristic of a species.

The genetic material can be packaged differently

DNA associates with proteins to form . During much of a cell's life, this material is less condensed. As a cell prepares to divide, the DNA becomes more tightly organized into visible chromosomes. Condensation helps the cell move and separate its genetic material without leaving it as a tangled mass.

The is not created from nothing when it becomes visible. The same genetic material is being packaged differently. Likewise, an uncondensed nucleus has DNA even when you cannot identify individual chromosomes in a light-microscope image.

One type from each parent forms a homologous pair

A typical human body cell has 46 chromosomes arranged in 23 pairs. Most pairs are homologous: the two chromosomes have the same genes at corresponding positions. One typically comes from the mother and one from the father. The alleles, or versions of those genes, need not be identical.

The sex chromosomes require care. In an XX pair the chromosomes are homologous; X and Y share some corresponding regions but are not matching copies over their whole lengths. Autosomes are the chromosomes other than the sex chromosomes.

A karyotype arranges photographed chromosomes by size and other features. It helps examine chromosome number and larger structural differences; it does not directly reveal every DNA sequence or every possible genetic condition.

Sets and copies answer different questions

A haploid cell contains one , represented by n. A diploid cell contains two sets, represented by 2n. Human gametes are normally haploid with 23 chromosomes, while the zygote and most body cells are diploid with 46.

Ploidy refers to chromosome sets, not how tightly DNA is packed or whether a chromosome has replicated. A chromosome may be drawn as a single rod or as two joined chromatids. You need the stage and the relationship between the structures before deciding what the picture means.

For any diagram, first ask: how many chromosome types are shown, how many sets are present, and which structures are ? Only then consider replication.

Worked example

Identify two different relationships

A model diploid cell has a long maternal chromosome, a long paternal chromosome, a short maternal chromosome and a short paternal chromosome.

  1. Pair chromosomes by corresponding type: the two long chromosomes form one homologous pair, and the two short chromosomes form another.
  2. There are two chromosome types, so one complete set contains two chromosomes.
  3. The cell contains two sets: n = 2 and 2n = 4.
  4. A maternal and paternal homologue can have different alleles even though they carry the same genes at corresponding positions.
