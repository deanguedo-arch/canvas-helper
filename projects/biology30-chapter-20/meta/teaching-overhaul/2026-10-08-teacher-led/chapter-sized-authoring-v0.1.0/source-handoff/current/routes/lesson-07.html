Feeding relationships and predator–prey changes

How can a change in one population affect another?

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

An organism that builds organic material from inorganic sources using an energy source.
An organism that obtains organic material by feeding on other organisms or their products.
An interaction in which one organism consumes another as prey.
A delay between one change and a related response.

Transfer energy rather than creating it

Producers build organic material using energy from light or chemical sources. Photosynthetic organisms transform light energy into chemical energy stored in molecules. They do not create energy from nothing.

Consumers obtain organic material and energy through feeding. Herbivores depend on producers, and predators depend on prey. These relationships connect population processes because food availability can influence survival, growth and reproduction.

A response can follow after a delay

When prey become more abundant, predators may have greater feeding success. Improved survival and reproduction can increase predator numbers after a delay. More predators can then contribute to increased prey .

A prey decline can later reduce predator reproduction or survival. Such feedback can contribute to oscillating patterns, but real populations are also affected by resources, disease, weather, migration and other interactions.

Do not expect the peaks of both populations to occur at exactly the same time. A delay can arise because producing and raising offspring takes time.

Use graphs as evidence, not complete explanations

Read both axes, the and the scale used for each population. A graph with separate scales can make changes look visually similar even when their numerical magnitudes differ.

A repeated lagged association is consistent with a predator–prey hypothesis, but correlation alone does not establish that is the only cause. Additional data about feeding, mortality and resources can strengthen the explanation.

Defensive adaptations can influence predation, including camouflage, protective structures and chemical defences. Explain how a supplied trait changes detection or capture rather than memorizing a list as proof of adaptation in every case.

Worked example

Read a lagged pattern

A hypothetical prey series peaks in year 4, followed by a predator peak in year 5.

  1. The predator peak follows the prey peak by one year in the supplied data.
  2. Greater prey availability could support later predator survival and reproduction.
  3. Increased predation could then contribute to prey decline.
  4. Other environmental causes remain possible, so the graph alone does not prove a single mechanism.
