Why do species invest differently in offspring number and parental care?

Learning goal

Before you begin

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

The maximum reproductive increase possible under favourable conditions for the organism.
A pattern of growth, survival and reproduction across an organism’s life.
A teaching description of traits often associated with rapid reproduction and many offspring.
A teaching description of traits often associated with slower reproduction and greater investment per offspring.

Follow the allocation trade-off

describes how organisms grow, survive and reproduce. Energy invested in producing many offspring cannot simultaneously be invested without limit in each offspring’s protection, growth and care. Age at maturity and reproductive frequency also affect population growth.

describes potential increase under favourable conditions. means actual growth is often lower. A species capable of producing many young can still have a declining population if few survive and reproduce.

Use r and K as comparative patterns

The r-associated end of a teaching continuum often includes early reproduction, many small offspring, short generation times and less investment in each offspring. Such traits can support rapid increase when conditions permit.

The K-associated end often includes later reproduction, fewer offspring, longer lives and more investment per offspring. These are broad comparisons, not fixed laws that predict every trait or graph for every species.

Some organisms combine features from both ends. Sea turtles, for example, produce many eggs yet can be long-lived as adults. Interpret the actual traits supplied rather than forcing every organism into a perfect category.

Read survivorship as survival through age

A survivorship curve shows the fraction or number remaining alive as age increases. Type I patterns retain many individuals until later life; Type II shows a more nearly constant risk across ages; Type III has high early loss with greater survival among those reaching later stages.

Survivorship is not the same as offspring production or a population-growth curve. Read the axes carefully. A steep early decline on a survivorship plot indicates early , not necessarily that the whole population is shrinking.

Connect survival with reproduction to explain population consequences. Many offspring can compensate for high early mortality, but neither measurement alone guarantees a particular growth rate.

Worked example

Interpret a mixed strategy

An organism produces hundreds of eggs with little care, but survivors can live and reproduce for decades.

  1. Many eggs and low early investment resemble r-associated traits.
  2. Long adult life resembles a trait often associated with the other end of the continuum.
  3. The combination is not a contradiction: life histories can mix features.
  4. Actual population growth depends on survival and reproduction, not the category label alone.
