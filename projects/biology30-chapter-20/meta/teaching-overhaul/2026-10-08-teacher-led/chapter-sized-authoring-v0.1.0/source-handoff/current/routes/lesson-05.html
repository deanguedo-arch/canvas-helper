When does the strength of a limiting effect depend on crowding?

Learning goal

Before you begin

Ask whether crowding changes the effect

A has a per-capita effect that changes with density. When more individuals share a fixed food supply, competition can reduce growth or reproduction. Disease transmission can also become easier when contact rates rise.

A density-independent effect is not primarily set by crowding in the stated case. A severe weather event may affect organisms regardless of whether they are in a dense or sparse population. The distinction concerns the relationship with density, not merely “biotic versus abiotic.”

Several mechanisms can act together

A drought may reduce food availability, after which competition becomes stronger. The initial environmental event and the density-dependent response are connected but distinct parts of the explanation.

Why real populations fluctuate

can change as resources and habitat change. Responses may also be delayed: individuals can continue reproducing for a time before food shortages reduce survival or reproduction. This can produce and decline.

A fluctuation around K is not evidence that the concept is meaningless. It shows that an idealized smooth model leaves out delays and changing conditions. Likewise, a crash does not by itself reveal which resource or interaction caused it.

Use data about resources and demographic rates to connect the population pattern with a mechanism. Do not label every decline “competition” without supporting information.

Worked example

Separate the initiating event from the crowding response

A dry year reduces plant growth. Later, herbivores in the most crowded patches have the lowest per-capita reproduction.

  1. The dry year is an environmental change affecting resources.
  2. The stronger reduction in crowded patches links part of the response with density.
  3. Food competition is a plausible density-dependent mechanism if supported by resource measurements.
  4. The explanation can include both an external event and a density-related consequence.
