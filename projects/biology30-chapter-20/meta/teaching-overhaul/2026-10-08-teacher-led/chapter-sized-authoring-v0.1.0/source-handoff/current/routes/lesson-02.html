Births, deaths, immigration and emigration

How do you account for every addition and loss in a population?

Learning goal

Calculate net change and final population size while distinguishing open and closed populations.

Before you begin

Population size is a count within defined boundaries. Crossing the boundary and reproduction can both change that count.

Vocabulary help

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

Births adding individuals to a population during the interval considered.
Deaths removing individuals from a population during the interval considered.
Movement of individuals into the defined population.
Movement of individuals out of the defined population.

Add entries and subtract exits

Over a defined interval, births and add individuals. Deaths and remove them. Net change is ΔN = (births + immigration) − (deaths + emigration).

The symbols n, i, m and e may be used for these totals, giving ΔN = (n + i) − (m + e). In this calculation, use counts over the same interval. If a question supplies rates, first handle their time basis consistently.

A positive ΔN means increase; a negative value means decrease. Zero means additions and losses balance, not that no individual was born, died or moved.

Net change is not the final population

If the initial population is N₀, the final size is N₀ + ΔN. A change of +48 in an initial population of 205 gives 253, not 48. Keep the starting count and the change on separate lines.

For example, 240 births and 11 immigrants are 251 additions. If there are 195 deaths and 8 emigrants, losses total 203. Net change is 251 − 203 = +48, and 205 + 48 = 253.

Check that every event is counted once and that all events refer to the same population and time interval.

State whether the population is open or closed

An open population exchanges individuals with other populations through immigration or emigration. A closed-population model assumes neither movement term contributes. In that model, ΔN = births − deaths.

Closed does not mean no births or deaths. It also does not mean matter and energy never cross an ecosystem boundary. Here the term concerns movement of individuals into and out of the population.

The suitability of a closed model depends on the case. A fence, island or tank may reduce movement, but the model should be stated rather than assumed merely from a name.

Worked example

Track all four terms

A population starts at 120. Over one year there are 30 births, 12 immigrants, 20 deaths and 7 emigrants.

  1. Additions = 30 + 12 = 42.
  2. Losses = 20 + 7 = 27.
  3. ΔN = 42 − 27 = +15 individuals.
  4. Final N = 120 + 15 = 135.
  5. The calculation concerns an open population over the stated year.
