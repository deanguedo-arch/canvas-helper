Four related calculations answer different questions

Density compares N with area or volume. Net change balances births and immigration against deaths and emigration. Average growth rate divides net change by time, while the course’s interval per-capita measure divides it by initial N.

Keep units and time bases explicit. A percentage change over several years is not automatically an annual rate, and a large population does not necessarily have a steep growth slope.

Mechanisms connect populations and environments

Exponential and logistic models emphasize different assumptions about per-capita growth and resource limits. Density dependence, interactions and changing conditions can modify those patterns. Predator–prey correlations and competition experiments need appropriate evidence for causal interpretation.

Succession depends on substrate, biological legacy, colonization and disturbance. Human-resource cases depend on both population composition and patterns of use. The same habit applies throughout: describe the evidence, explain a mechanism and state the limits.

Worked example

Check a population report

A report says a population grew 20% over four years and therefore grows 20% every year.

  1. The stated percentage describes change relative to the initial size over the whole interval.
  2. An annual rate requires additional treatment of time and a specified growth model.
  3. Do not turn an interval proportion into a yearly exponential parameter.
  4. Use the actual starting and final counts to check the reported arithmetic.
  5. A numerical trend still needs ecological evidence to explain its cause.
