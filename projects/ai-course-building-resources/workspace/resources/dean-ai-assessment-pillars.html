ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY ARCHITECTURE

Product. Process. Defence. We are building assessments where learning is visible, valid, and defensible even when AI exists.

Start Here: The Assessment Framework

Use this short overview before moving into the shift from product-only assessment to product, process, and defence.

Why Assessment Has to Change

Traditional online assessment is vulnerable because final products alone no longer prove learning. AI makes polished work easy to produce, but polish is not proof.

To maintain assessment integrity in an online environment, we must shift our focus from merely evaluating a final submission to evaluating a complete body of evidence that proves independent understanding.

info Our Responsibility vs. Alberta Education

This framework does not replace Alberta Education diploma exams. Alberta Education owns provincial standardization. Our responsibility is to ensure that school-awarded course marks are valid, visible, and professionally defensible before students sit for their diploma exams.

The Old Paradigm

Uploading assignments and relying on trust-based grading. Highly vulnerable to generative AI.

The New Paradigm

Structured teacher interaction, process tracking, self-regulation, and mandatory oral/written defence.

Core Assessment Model

Every major summative assessment is evaluated across three distinct buckets. While the foundational model is 50/25/25, teachers use professional judgment to shift these weights based on course demands. We do not change weights based on AI level, but rather on pedagogical intent.

Product

The student can meet the learning outcome.

The traditional output. This is the final written response, exam, presentation, project, source analysis, or issue response.

  • check_circle Evaluates final performance
  • check_circle Aligns with curriculum standards

Process

The student can show how the work was created.

Evaluates perseverance, planning, and proper completion. Includes draft history, outlines, source organizers, and AI-use logs.

  • check_circle Makes learning visible
  • check_circle Proves disclosure & self-advocacy

Defence

The student can explain, defend, and apply the learning.

Evaluates self-reflection, metacognition, and the ability to articulate why specific choices were made. Includes oral conferences or transfer tasks.

  • check_circle Validates ownership of knowledge
  • check_circle The ultimate proof of learning

Friction vs. Integrity Layers

Layer 1 (Settings): Timed, randomized order/answers, large banks.
Layer 2 (Design): Source-based questions, political cartoons, Alberta contexts.
Layer 3 (Images): Using images for excerpts/questions to block simple copy-paste.

verified_user Layer 4: Verification

Images and settings add friction, but they do not create security. The Oral Defence is the actual integrity layer.

After the verified MC assessment (e.g., 60 questions), the teacher conducts a 5-8 minute highly targeted oral verification sampling 3-5 specific questions from the student's quiz. Verification is not a retake; it is a sampling to prove authorship and comprehension.

The Defence Probe (Filter Output) Cognitive Target
Why did you choose that answer? (1 correct item)Checks: Reasoning

The Absolute Rule:

If the student explains well, the quiz mark stands. If they show partial understanding, corrections are required. If they clearly cannot explain their answers, the mark is capped. If the student cannot defend the result, the result is not fully validated.

Blue Team: The Case for Defensibility and Pedagogical Integrity

The Blue Team evaluates the framework based on its alignment with educational research, risk mitigation, and systemic protections.

Eliminates the "AI Detective" Trap: By explicitly stating that the goal is not to police students or catch them cheating, the framework neutralizes the unwinnable arms race of AI detection. It safely assumes AI is present and shifts the burden of evidence directly onto the student.

Aligns with Modern Assessment Research: The 50/25/25 split perfectly operationalizes the urgent shift from product-based evaluation to process-based and metacognitive assessment. It recognizes that in a generative age, polished outputs alone are no longer secure proof of learning.

Empowers Student Agency: Allowing students to choose their route treats AI as a professional tool rather than a prohibited one. This aligns with the GENAI Assessment Scale and gives students clear rules of engagement.

Proactive Competency Gating: The AI Use Permit ensures students do not automatically get access to powerful tools without proving they understand the difference between AI supporting their learning and replacing it.

Standardized Validity Rules: Mark caps and validity gates give staff a unified, fair response system. It prevents arbitrary point docking and focuses on the inability to validate the mark.

Red Team: The Case for Friction and Logistical Failure

The Red Team hunts for bottlenecks, scaling issues, and predictable human behaviour from both students and teachers.

The Defence is a Mathematical Scaling Nightmare: If every summative requires a 5-minute teacher conference or live Q&A, an online teacher with 150 students completing 4 summatives a term faces roughly 50 hours of live conferencing before counting other verification chats.

The Competency Gate Bottleneck: If Level 1 and Level 2 mini-modules require teacher verification for every student, the first weeks of the semester can stall while teachers try to clear AI-use permits.

Evidence Burden May Incentivize Concealment: Prompt trails, annotated AI outputs, source verification tables, and revision rationales can become a large organizational load. Students who already struggle with time management may hide AI use and claim Level 0 to avoid the paperwork.

Multiple-Choice Friction: Multiple-choice assessments are used because they are efficient and scalable. If every test triggers a randomized follow-up defence, the format can become almost as time-consuming as written response grading.

Consensus and Strategic Next Steps

The Verdict: The philosophy, structure, and student-facing messaging are highly defensible. The critical point of failure is teacher time. If verification and defence are not optimized, the framework can collapse under its own weight.

Optimize and Automate the Defence: Diversify what counts as defence. Use 2-minute recorded explanations, screen-recorded draft walkthroughs, metacognitive forms, or automated reflection checks before requiring a live conference.

Streamline the Competency Gate: Make the AI Use Permit as frictionless as possible. Use auto-graded knowledge checks and portfolio evidence that is audited when later work raises questions.

Scaffold the Process Gathering: Provide rigid templates for prompt trails, verification tables, and revision notes so evidence collection is part of the workflow rather than a chore at the end.

Run a Controlled Pilot: Test the 50/25/25 model with one department first. Track actual teacher hours, evidence quality, student clarity, and confidence before a hard launch.

Invite teachers, students, and leaders to work through the three cards, then capture the decisions that make the model more usable.

What We Are Trying To Replicate

The repeatable move is this: present a strong model, invite the strongest critique, then build the consensus version that survives the classroom reality.

The conclusion is not that Product, Process, and Defence are wrong. The conclusion is that Defence must become a flexible evidence category, process evidence needs templates, and live meetings should be sampled, targeted, or triggered by risk.
