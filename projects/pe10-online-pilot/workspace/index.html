A simple course path

  1. Plan. Read the safety guidance and build a realistic activity plan.
  2. Move and document. Add one log entry after each continuous activity session.
  3. Pause every five hours. Use checkpoints to notice progress and adjust.
  4. Complete five assignments. Use your own activity evidence in each one.
  5. Review your portfolio. Export your work and submit through Brightspace when your teacher provides the locations.

    Course structure

    How PE10 Works

    Choose meaningful activities, collect honest evidence, and show what you learn. You can work at your own pace while staying in contact with your teacher.

    The 50/50 evidence model

    Activity evidence · 50%

    Complete 50 hours, document sessions accurately, show variety, and use checkpoints to explain growth.

    Five assignments · 50%

    Each assignment is worth 10 points. Your teacher evaluates the work in Brightspace using the visible rubrics.

    No grade is calculated here. This course shows evidence readiness only. Brightspace remains the official grade record.

    Activity evidence rules

    • 50 total hours: logged activity time must reach 3,000 minutes.
    • Five hours in each dimension: use this as a breadth check, or follow a teacher-approved alternate evidence plan.
    • No more than 30 hours in one dimension: this keeps one activity type from replacing the whole program.
    • Quality matters: identify a learning focus, the adjustment you made, and a short reflection.
    • Teacher approval comes first: ask before unfamiliar, higher-risk, or difficult-to-classify activity.

    Five movement dimensions

    Classify a session by its main learning purpose. The same activity can sometimes fit more than one dimension; choose the best match and stay consistent.

    Alternative environments

    Movement adapted to outdoor, land, ice, or water settings—for example walking a trail, snowshoeing, skating, cycling, or properly supervised aquatics.

    Dance

    Rhythmic, expressive, social, or cultural movement such as a learned routine, creative sequence, or community dance practice.

    Games

    Activities that use rules, tactics, spatial awareness, and interaction—for example basketball, badminton, soccer, or an adapted cooperative game.

    Gymnastics and body control

    Movement challenges focused on balance, strength, flexibility, coordination, shapes, safe landings, or controlled sequences.

    Individual activities

    Activities where you track personal technique or challenge, such as running, resistance training, yoga, martial arts, or target activities.

    Ways to build evidence

    Use the route that fits your access, needs, and community.

    • School or division programming: teacher-guided activity and observation.
    • Community activity: a team, club, class, recreation program, or cultural activity.
    • Self-directed activity: an agreed plan with appropriate equipment, location, and supervision.
    • Adapted or alternate evidence: a teacher-approved way to demonstrate the outcome when a standard pathway creates a barrier.

    Document learning, not private health details

    The course does not ask for body weight, calories, diagnoses, verifier contact information, or medical explanations. If an accommodation or safety concern affects participation, speak privately with your teacher and follow school procedures. In the course, record only the plan or evidence format you have agreed to use.

    Plan before you participate

    Safety and Activity Planning

    Every session begins with a decision: is this activity appropriate for me, in this place, with this equipment and supervision, today?

    Stop and get help

    Stop activity and tell a responsible adult if you have a possible head injury, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, sudden weakness, or pain that changes how you move. Call 9-1-1 for an emergency. This course cannot assess an injury or replace medical care.

    Before-session check

    1. Choose the right challenge. Match the activity to your experience, current readiness, and teacher-approved plan.
    2. Check the setting. Consider weather, visibility, traffic, surface, water, boundaries, and access to help.
    3. Check equipment. Use activity-appropriate clothing and protective equipment that fits and is in good condition.
    4. Plan supervision. Know who is present, where you are going, and how to get help. Do not attempt an unfamiliar higher-risk activity alone.
    5. Warm up and progress gradually. Start below your maximum, notice how you respond, and change one challenge at a time.
    6. Respect recovery. Hydrate, eat regularly, sleep, take rest when needed, and never use movement as punishment for eating.

    When to check with your teacher first

    Pause before logging an activity when any of these conditions apply. Your teacher will use current division and activity-specific guidance to decide what evidence is appropriate.

    Unfamiliar: you have not learned the technique or safe setup.

    Setting-dependent: the activity involves water, ice, traffic, heights, remote areas, or changing weather.

    Contact or speed: collision, combat, wheeled, downhill, or high-speed movement is involved.

    Specialized equipment: loads, machines, restraints, climbing systems, or protective equipment require instruction or supervision.

    Return after injury: you are returning after a suspected concussion, significant injury, or medical restriction.

    Suspected concussion

    A concussion can follow a blow to the head, face, neck, or body and can happen without loss of consciousness. Symptoms may affect the body, thinking, emotions, or sleep. If a concussion is suspected, stop participating, report the incident to an adult, and follow school and medical directions. Only a doctor or nurse practitioner diagnoses a concussion. After a diagnosis, EIPS requires medical direction before return to physical activity.

    Read EIPS Administrative Procedure 317: Concussions ↗

    Use the talk test

    Light
    You can talk and breathe comfortably. Useful for gentle movement, technique, warm-up, and recovery.
    Moderate
    Your breathing is quicker, but you can still speak in sentences.
    Vigorous
    Your breathing is much quicker and speaking more than a few words is difficult. Build toward this gradually.

    Intensity is personal. A familiar activity may feel different because of sleep, stress, weather, illness, access needs, or recovery.

    When to contact your teacher

    Ask early. A short planning conversation can prevent unusable evidence later.

    • Before an unfamiliar, higher-risk, or difficult-to-classify activity.
    • When a movement dimension creates an access, safety, cultural, financial, or accommodation barrier.
    • After a suspected concussion, injury, or change in safe participation.
    • When a health claim or source is confusing, outdated, or upsetting.
    • When saved work, backup, printing, or Brightspace submission does not work.
