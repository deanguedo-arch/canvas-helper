The Rocking Horse Winner

In "the Rocking Horse Winner" the protagonist, Paul, attempts to gain luck as his mother tells him, "If you’re lucky you have money. That’s why it’s better to be born lucky than rich. If you’re rich, you may lose all your money. But if you’re lucky you will always get more money."

Paul’s mother is presented as an unemotional, detached parent due to her obsession with obtaining more money. Paul hopes to obtain love from his mother by fulfilling her desire to be wealthy. Paul does fulfil his wishes and claims to be "lucky" but, ironically, he dies in the process. Paul’s luck of winning eighty thousand pounds only to die soon after is situational irony.

The irony develops the theme: When the desire for materialism replaces the fundamentals in life, such as love and nurturing, the outcome can be detrimental. It is imperative that Paul dies. Had Paul lived the author would be condoning the obsession of materialism.
