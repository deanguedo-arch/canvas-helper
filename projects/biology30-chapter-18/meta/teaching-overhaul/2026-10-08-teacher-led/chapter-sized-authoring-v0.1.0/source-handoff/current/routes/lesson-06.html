Read the message in triplets

A ribosome reads in successive three- codons. Each amino-acid specifies one amino acid in the standard code. Several different codons can specify the same amino acid; this redundancy does not mean one codon specifies several amino acids at once.

AUG commonly acts as a start codon and specifies methionine. Starting at the correct AUG establishes the . A sequence written for codon practice may instead be identified as an internal coding segment; follow that instruction rather than assuming every short fragment contains the beginning of a full gene.

Ribosomes contain and proteins. As matching tRNAs enter and the ribosome moves along the mRNA, amino acids are linked by peptide bonds. The growing chain is a polypeptide, which can later fold and undergo additional processing.

The mRNA is not physically converted into protein. It is read as information while amino acids are assembled from a separate supply.

Stop at the signal and use the right table

UAA, UAG and UGA are stop codons in the standard code. A release factor helps terminate translation and release the chain. A does not recruit a tRNA carrying a “stop amino acid.”

Always use the mRNA codon, written 5′ to 3′, when looking up an amino acid. Convert a DNA template or anticodon first. A protein chain is described by amino-acid order; it does not have DNA-style 5′ and 3′ ends.

Worked example

Translate a complete short message

A coding message is 5′-AUG UUU GGC UAA-3′. Translate from the first AUG.

  1. AUG establishes the frame and contributes methionine (Met).
  2. UUU specifies phenylalanine (Phe).
  3. GGC specifies glycine (Gly).
  4. UAA terminates translation and contributes no amino acid.
  5. The chain is Met–Phe–Gly: three amino acids, not four.
