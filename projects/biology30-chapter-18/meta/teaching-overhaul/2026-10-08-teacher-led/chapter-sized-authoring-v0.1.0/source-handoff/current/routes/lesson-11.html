Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

A method that amplifies a selected DNA region through repeated cycles.
Separation of molecules by movement through a gel under an electric field.
A set of DNA fragments of known sizes used as a reference in a gel.
A pattern based on selected variable DNA regions used for comparison.

Amplify a selected region

increases the amount of a selected DNA region. Repeated cycles separate the strands, allow primers to bind, and use a heat-stable to extend new strands. Primers define the region to be copied.

In an idealized model, each complete cycle doubles the number of target molecules. Starting from one target molecule, three ideal cycles produce eight. Real reactions may not double perfectly throughout, so the model is a calculation aid rather than a guarantee about every experiment.

Read a gel from wells toward the positive end

DNA has a negative charge because of its phosphate backbone. In a standard DNA gel, an electric field draws fragments toward the positive electrode. Smaller fragments usually travel farther through the gel than larger fragments under the same conditions.

Samples are loaded into separate lanes. A provides fragments of known sizes for comparison. Identify the wells, migration direction and ladder before interpreting a band position.

A band represents many molecules of similar length, not one whole cell. Two fragments can have the same length without necessarily having identical sequences.

Compare patterns without overstating identity

DNA profiling compares selected markers rather than reading every in the genome. In a simplified classroom matching problem, compare the complete supplied pattern, not just one shared band. Excluding a sample that lacks required bands differs from proving every circumstance associated with a compatible sample.

In a simplified parentage exercise, each child marker must be explainable by inheritance from the proposed parents. Account for the maternal contributions before comparing the remaining markers with possible paternal contributions.

Actual interpretation also considers laboratory quality, contamination, relatedness and statistical evidence. A classroom gel cannot by itself establish guilt, consent or the circumstances in which material was deposited.

Worked example

Read distance as fragment size

In one lane, band A remains near the wells and band B travels farther toward the positive electrode.

  1. Identify the wells as the loading end.
  2. Confirm that DNA moved toward the positive electrode.
  3. Under the same run conditions, the farther band B corresponds to smaller fragments than A.
  4. Use a ladder to estimate sizes; distance alone does not identify the full sequence.
