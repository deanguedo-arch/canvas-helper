Vocabulary help

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

A persistent change in a DNA sequence.
A coding substitution that leaves the specified amino acid unchanged.
A coding change that introduces a premature stop codon.
A shift in reading frame caused by an insertion or deletion not divisible by three in a coding region.

Locate the change in DNA

A is a persistent DNA sequence change. It may arise from replication errors or DNA damage that is not accurately repaired. An error during transcription or translation can alter an individual product without changing the DNA template itself.

A substitution replaces one base with another. In a coding region, its consequence depends on the resulting . A leaves the amino acid unchanged; a missense substitution changes an amino acid; a introduces a premature stop.

Check the code rather than guessing severity

Consider the internal mRNA CAG UUA CCC. It specifies Gln–Leu–Pro. Changing UUA to UUG still gives leucine, a silent substitution. Changing UUA to UCA gives serine, a missense change. Changing it to UAA creates a stop signal.

The size of a change in DNA does not by itself determine the effect on the organism. Protein location, function, remaining sequence and other biological factors matter. A missense change can be important or relatively minor; a premature stop can remove a critical region.

Track the frame and the lineage

An insertion or deletion of one shifts the downstream triplet grouping. Insertions or deletions not divisible by three can therefore cause a in a translated region. Removing exactly three bases may remove an amino acid without shifting later codons.

A somatic mutation occurs in a body-cell lineage. It can affect that organism and descendant cells without usually entering its gametes. A germ-line mutation can be transmitted if it is present in a gamete that contributes to an offspring. “Can be inherited” does not mean every offspring receives it.

Mutations create new sequence variants. Selection and other population processes determine how their frequencies change; organisms do not produce the exact mutation they need on demand.

Worked example

Classify the actual coding effect

An internal coding RNA sequence changes from 5′-CAG UUA CCC-3′ to 5′-CAG UUG CCC-3′ as a result of a DNA substitution.

  1. The changed codon is UUA → UUG.
  2. Use the mRNA table: both specify leucine.
  3. The peptide remains Gln–Leu–Pro.
  4. Classify the coding substitution as silent rather than assuming every base change alters the protein.

Does natural selection create a needed mutation when an organism is challenged?

Written explanation 1

Compare after saving your own response

A silent substitution changes a codon without changing its amino acid, such as UUA to UUG for leucine. A missense substitution changes the amino acid, such as UUA to UCA for serine. A nonsense change creates a premature stop, such as UUA to UAA. The biological effect depends on context.
