Why can only one side of a replication fork be copied continuously?

Learning goal

Before you begin

Use each parental strand as a template

Replication begins at an origin and opens a region of the DNA. Helicase separates the paired strands by disrupting their hydrogen-bond interactions. It does not cut each strand’s sugar-phosphate backbone into small pieces.

Each parental strand provides a template for a complementary new strand. The two resulting DNA molecules each contain one old strand and one new strand. This is .

Meselson and Stahl followed DNA density after cells moved from a heavy-nitrogen medium to a light-nitrogen medium. The first generation produced intermediate-density DNA. In the second, intermediate and light DNA appeared. This pattern supported semiconservative replication over the alternatives tested.

A primer provides a starting point

cannot begin a new strand from nothing. Primase makes a short RNA primer that supplies a starting 3′ end. DNA polymerase then extends from that end with complementary DNA nucleotides.

New DNA is always synthesized 5′ to 3′. Because the parental templates run in opposite directions, their new strands cannot both follow the moving replication fork continuously.

The leading strand is synthesized continuously toward the fork. The lagging strand is made in Okazaki fragments, each extending 5′ to 3′ away from the fork as additional template is exposed. Each fragment requires a primer.

Finish the backbone and check the result

RNA primers are removed and replaced with DNA through the replication machinery. seals the remaining backbone breaks between adjacent segments. Proofreading and repair reduce errors, although some DNA changes can persist.

Use the enzyme names to explain different jobs: helicase opens, primase starts, DNA polymerase extends, and ligase joins. Do not use the word “copies” as if one enzyme performs every part.

Replication increases DNA amount but does not by itself separate sister chromatids into daughter cells. Cell division is a later process.

Worked example

Interpret the second generation

Cells with fully heavy-labelled DNA replicate twice in light-nitrogen medium. Predict the DNA types under semiconservative replication.

  1. After the first replication, every molecule has one heavy parental strand and one light new strand.
  2. Each hybrid molecule then separates its strands for the second replication.
  3. The heavy strand produces another hybrid; the light strand produces a fully light molecule.
  4. The expected second-generation molecules are half hybrid and half light, with no fully heavy molecules.

The templates are antiparallel while all new DNA extends 5′ to 3′. One new strand follows the opening fork continuously. The other must be made as fragments extending away from the fork as new template is exposed. Those fragments are joined after primer replacement.
