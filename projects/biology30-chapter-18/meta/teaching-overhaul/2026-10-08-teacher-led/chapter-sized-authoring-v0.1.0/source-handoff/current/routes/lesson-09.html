Recombination and sources of variation

How can new allele combinations arise without a new mutation at every gene?

Learning goal

Distinguish mutation, recombination and transfer of existing genetic material.

Before you begin

Mutations change sequences. Meiosis can rearrange existing alleles among chromosomes and gametes.

Separate new sequences from new combinations

A can create a new allele. Recombination can bring existing alleles together in new arrangements. During meiosis, crossing over exchanges corresponding DNA between non-sister chromatids, and independent assortment distributes homologues into gametes.

Random fertilization adds another level of combination. An offspring can therefore differ from both parents without every difference requiring a new mutation. Keep the origin of a sequence variant separate from its reassortment.

Bacteria exchange genetic material in several ways

Bacteria normally reproduce by binary fission, but they can also acquire DNA from other sources. involves uptake of external DNA. involves direct contact and transfer, often including plasmids. involves a virus carrying bacterial genetic material.

These mechanisms can introduce traits into a bacterial lineage. They are not the same as mitosis or the production and fusion of animal gametes. The important question is how the DNA enters and how it can persist in descendants.

A transferred sequence must be maintained and expressed in an appropriate context to affect a phenotype. Detecting DNA outside a cell is not enough to establish a stable inherited change.

Mitochondrial DNA records a particular lineage

Mitochondria contain their own DNA, consistent with their evolutionary origin through endosymbiosis. In the usual human model, mitochondrial inheritance is predominantly maternal because the egg contributes the mitochondria retained in the embryo.

Comparing can inform relationships along maternal lineages. It does not represent the entire nuclear genome or all branches of ancestry. Similarity of a short sequence is evidence to interpret, not a complete family history.

Do not assume mitochondrial variants are always harmless. Like other sequence changes, their effects depend on the genes and biological processes involved.

Worked example

Identify the route of transfer

A bacterium acquires DNA carried by a bacteriophage, and its descendants retain an associated trait.

  1. The carrier is a virus, which identifies transduction.
  2. Retention in descendants supports a heritable genetic change rather than temporary external contamination.
  3. This is distinct from transformation by free DNA or conjugation through contact.
  4. The transferred allele may already have existed in another bacterium; transfer need not create it by mutation.

Mutation creates sequence changes that can produce new alleles. Recombination rearranges genetic material, including existing alleles, into new combinations. Both can generate variation, but the origin of an allele differs from reshuffling its combinations.
