Follow information without confusing the molecules

Replication copies DNA through complementary semiconservative synthesis. Transcription uses one template to make RNA, and translation reads mRNA codons to assemble a polypeptide. RNA processing modifies the message without normally deleting introns from the genomic DNA.

A sequence answer should identify the strand and direction, preserve antiparallel pairing and use the correct reading frame. A stop codon is a signal, not an amino acid.

Connect a change with the relevant measurement

A DNA mutation may alter a product, but its effect must be traced through the sequence and biological context. Recombination can create new combinations without a new mutation at every locus.

PCR, gels, sequencing and recombinant-DNA methods provide different kinds of evidence. A complete explanation identifies what each method measures and separates compatibility, expression and function from stronger unsupported claims.

Worked example

Trace a sequence change without overstating its effect

Coding DNA changes from 5′-ATG CAA TAA-3′ to 5′-ATG CAG TAA-3′.

  1. The corresponding RNA changes CAA to CAG in the second codon.
  2. Both CAA and CAG specify glutamine.
  3. Both simplified messages produce Met–Gln, then stop.
  4. Classify the substitution’s amino-acid effect as silent.
  5. Do not infer an organism-level effect without additional context or measurements.
