A shared genome does not require identical activity

Many nucleated somatic cells contain essentially the same inherited genome, yet muscle, nerve and other cells perform different tasks. They express different sets of genes and produce different amounts of particular products.

is not the same as changing the DNA sequence. A cell can change which genes are active without replacing every gene it contains. Some genes produce functional RNA rather than a protein.

Separate the molecules and their jobs

DNA generally stores information in a double-stranded molecule using deoxyribose and the bases A, T, C and G. RNA uses ribose and uracil, U, instead of thymine. RNA is usually single-stranded but can fold through base pairing within the molecule.

For a protein-coding gene, transcription produces RNA from a DNA template. Translation uses the sequence to assemble amino acids into a polypeptide. The commonly taught information pathway is DNA → RNA → protein.

Messenger RNA carries the sequence, brings matched amino acids, and forms part of the ribosome’s structure and catalytic machinery. These RNA types have different roles even though all are nucleic acids.

Locate the processes in a eukaryotic cell

For the nuclear genes considered here, transcription occurs in the nucleus. The RNA is processed, and mature mRNA can leave through nuclear pores. Translation occurs at ribosomes in the cytoplasm, including ribosomes associated with rough endoplasmic reticulum.

A protein’s amino-acid sequence influences how it folds and functions. A different sequence may change that function, but not every sequence difference has an equally large effect.

Do not describe non-protein-coding DNA as automatically useless. Some sequences regulate expression, and some genes produce functional RNAs. This chapter focuses on protein synthesis without claiming that it explains every DNA function.

Worked example

Same inherited instructions, different output

A muscle cell produces much more of a muscle-associated protein than another nucleated somatic cell.

  1. Both cells can carry the relevant DNA sequence.
  2. Different regulation changes which genes are transcribed and how much product is made.
  3. mRNA and translation connect the expressed gene with the protein output.
  4. The observation does not require one cell to have lost every unused gene.
