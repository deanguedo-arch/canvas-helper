Build a nucleotide before building a helix

DNA is a polymer: a long chain made from subunits. Each DNA nucleotide contains deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogenous base. The four bases are adenine, thymine, cytosine and guanine, abbreviated A, T, C and G.

Sugars and phosphates form the backbone of each strand. Bases project inward and pair with bases on the other strand. Covalent bonds hold the backbone together; hydrogen bonds connect complementary bases across the strands. These are different connections and perform different structural roles.

Pairing and direction are separate rules

In the standard double-stranded DNA model, A pairs with T and C pairs with G. A and G are double-ring purines; C and T are single-ring pyrimidines. A purine paired with a pyrimidine helps maintain a consistent width.

The strands are . If an upper strand is written 5′-ATGC-3′, its aligned partner is 3′-TACG-5′. If you must report that partner in the 5′ to 3′ direction, reverse the displayed order: 5′-GCAT-3′.

The numbers identify positions in the sugar and therefore strand orientation. They do not identify the first and third bases in a codon. Direction becomes essential when interpreting replication and transcription.

Use proportions without assuming equal amounts

Chargaff’s observations supported equality between A and T, and between C and G, in double-stranded DNA. If A accounts for 30% of all bases, T is 30%, leaving 40% for C and G together. Each is therefore 20%.

Franklin’s X-ray diffraction work provided important structural evidence. Watson and Crick developed a double-helix model consistent with that evidence and the base relationships. Structure, chemical composition and inherited function were connected by different lines of investigation.

Do not apply equal A/T proportions to every single strand by itself. The equalities arise from the complementary double-stranded molecule.

Worked example

Write a complement with its direction

An upper strand is 5′-AAGTC-3′. Write the aligned complementary strand and then write that partner 5′ to 3′.

  1. Pair A with T, A with T, G with C, T with A and C with G.
  2. The aligned partner is 3′-TTCAG-5′.
  3. To report the partner 5′ to 3′, read it from its 5′ end: 5′-GACTT-3′.
  4. Check that the strands are complementary when aligned antiparallel.
