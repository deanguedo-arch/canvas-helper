### 7. ch14-final-writing-1
Group: required final check; prompt rendered page: 7

Prompt: Compare the regulation of sperm production with the sequence leading to ovulation. Use GnRH, FSH, LH and the correct target cells or structures.

First response (1045 characters):
Both pathways begin when the hypothalamus releases GnRH, which stimulates the anterior pituitary to release FSH and LH. In the testis, FSH acts on Sertoli cells in the seminiferous tubules to support developing sperm. LH acts on interstitial or Leydig cells to stimulate testosterone production; testosterone helps support sperm development. Testosterone also inhibits upstream stimulation, while Sertoli-cell inhibin particularly restrains FSH. In the ovary, FSH supports follicular development and LH also contributes to ovarian hormone production. A growing follicle releases estrogen. Sustained high estrogen before ovulation switches to a temporary positive-feedback effect, producing a sharp pituitary LH surge. That surge helps release the secondary oocyte and form the corpus luteum from the remaining follicular tissue. The comparison is therefore a shared GnRH-pituitary start with different gonadal targets and outcomes: Sertoli/Leydig support of sperm development versus follicular development and an LH-triggered ovulation sequence.

Visible support: L-central, L-testis, L-feedback, L-ovulation
Judgment: Sufficient; no external or optional textbook material needed

### 8. ch14-final-writing-2
Group: required final check; prompt rendered page: 7

Prompt: In a hypothetical cycle, an LH surge is followed by ovulation, but the corpus luteum then fails to remain active. Explain the likely hormone and endometrial consequences. Distinguish this from an oviduct blockage.

First response (684 characters):
The LH surge and ovulation have already occurred in this case. If the corpus luteum does not remain active, its progesterone and estrogen output will fall. With less progesterone, the endometrium loses its normal luteal support, so the functional lining is likely to break down and be shed rather than remain maintained. This is a hormone-production/support failure after ovulation; it does not undo the earlier release of the oocyte. An oviduct blockage is instead a transport problem: it can stop or reduce gamete encounter or onward movement toward the uterus even while ovarian hormones and cycles continue. A blockage does not by itself demonstrate inactive corpus-luteum tissue.

### 12. ch14-transfer-writing

Explain why hormone concentration alone cannot establish that the lining will respond normally. Distinguish hormone production from target-cell response.

First response (659 characters):
A normal progesterone concentration means that the hormone is available to the tissue; it does not show that the target can use it. Endometrial cells need functioning progesterone receptors to recognize the signal and produce their normal response. The stated problem is at the target-cell response step, not evidence that the corpus luteum has failed to produce progesterone. The tissue model also does not itself measure where the supplied progesterone was produced. Increasing supply alone would not establish a restored receptor response.
