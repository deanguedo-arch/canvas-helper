### 03-4. Required written explanation 2

**Prompt:** Trace the main stages from a primary spermatocyte to sperm. Distinguish meiosis from differentiation.

**Answer:** The primary spermatocyte begins meiosis after its DNA has been copied. Meiosis I produces two secondary spermatocytes. Meiosis II divides both of those cells, producing four haploid spermatids in total. There is no extra DNA-copying stage between the two divisions. The four spermatids then differentiate: their form changes as they develop features such as a compact head and a flagellum. Differentiation produces specialised sperm without a third meiotic division, without doubling the four cells to eight and without halving the chromosome set again. Each haploid cell normally has one set of 23 chromosomes in humans.

**Visible-support mapping:**
1. Starting DNA-copy state: `ch14-l03-teaching-02-p1`, “Before a primary spermatocyte begins meiosis, its DNA has been copied.”
2. Primary → two secondary: p2, “Meiosis I takes one primary spermatocyte to two secondary spermatocytes.”
3. Two secondary → four spermatids: p2, “Meiosis II divides each secondary spermatocyte,” followed by the explicit 2 × 2 = 4 explanation.
4. No intervening DNA copying: p2 directly states it.
5. Haploid count: p3, “one set of 23 chromosomes in humans.”
6. Change of form, features, no third division/no additional halving/no eight cells: p4 explicitly supplies each distinction.
7. Sequence cross-check: p5 and the “Sperm development” video's local read-the-explanation summary state the same main sequence.

**Missing or ambiguous support:** None. I did not add chromosome counts for primary/secondary spermatocytes or detailed chromosome movements, which this lesson does not develop.

### 04-3. Required written explanation 1

**Prompt:** Trace two branches from GnRH to their testicular targets. Explain how the branches support sperm production.

**Answer:** The hypothalamus releases GnRH, which stimulates the anterior pituitary to release FSH and LH. FSH travels in the blood to Sertoli cells inside seminiferous tubules and supports the functions that help developing sperm. LH travels to interstitial cells between the tubules and stimulates them to release testosterone. Testosterone and FSH-supported Sertoli-cell activity work together to support sperm production. The branches therefore contribute to the same process through different direct cell targets; neither hormone turns its target cell into a sperm.

**Visible-support mapping:**
1. Hypothalamus → GnRH → anterior pituitary → FSH/LH: `ch14-l04-teaching-01-p2` states the source, signal, target and releases explicitly.
2. FSH → Sertoli cells within tubules → developmental support: p3.
3. LH → interstitial cells between tubules → testosterone: p3.
4. Shared support of sperm production: `ch14-l04-teaching-02-p1`, “testosterone and FSH-supported Sertoli-cell activity work together to support spermatogenesis.”
5. Target cells do not become sperm: `ch14-l04-teaching-01-p3` directly excludes both conversions.
6. Visual check: the new SVG's left branch ends at “Support sperm production”; the testosterone box on the right has a green arrow into that same support box.

**Missing or ambiguous support:** None. The lesson supplies a functional relationship without needing a molecular explanation of how testosterone acts locally.

### 04-4. Required written explanation 2

**Prompt:** In a functioning feedback model, explain what rising testosterone does to its upstream stimulation. Why is this negative feedback?

**Answer:** Rising testosterone increases the feedback that reduces stimulation from the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary. GnRH and especially LH stimulation decrease, tending to limit further testosterone production by interstitial cells. It is negative feedback because the response opposes the initial testosterone rise. “Negative” describes that opposing relationship, not harm or a rule that every hormone must fall.

**Visible-support mapping:**
1. Rise → reduced control-centre stimulation → less GnRH/LH: `ch14-l04-teaching-03-p1` directly explains the full sequence.
2. LH's target and output: `ch14-l04-teaching-01-p3` connects LH, interstitial cells and testosterone.
3. Opposing the initial change: `ch14-l04-teaching-03-p1` defines why the feedback is negative; p5 distinguishes this from harmfulness or all quantities falling.
4. Figure: red dashed returns begin at the testosterone box and terminate with bars at the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary. `ch14-l04-teaching-03-p2` explains those signs as inhibition, not hormone conversion or backward flow.

**Missing or ambiguous support:** None. No exact concentration, timing, FSH change or guaranteed restoration is claimed.
