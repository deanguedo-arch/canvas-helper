EXACT ORIGINAL PROMPT
Explain why a blocked vas deferens could prevent sperm from appearing in semen without stopping all semen production.

EXACT ORIGINAL MODEL
The duct blockage interrupts sperm transport. Seminal vesicles, the prostate and bulbourethral glands add secretions through other connections. Some gland fluid can still enter the downstream pathway.

EXACT ORIGINAL CRITERIA
Distinguishes transport from gland secretion.
Does not infer loss of testosterone solely from a blocked duct.

ISSUE
There are two testes and paired transport routes. A blockage in one vas deferens does not establish that the other side contributes no sperm. The prompt also does not settle the timing or whether sperm were already farther downstream. The learner reviewer answered with these qualifications using visible candidate teaching. The original model explains the mechanism but omits the one-side/both-side distinction. This finding is not a claim that the visible physiology explanation is wrong or that all possible responses fail.

PROPOSED MINIMAL CLARIFICATION, NOT APPLIED
Explain why completely blocked vasa deferentia on both sides could prevent newly produced sperm from reaching semen without stopping all seminal-fluid production. Assume the accessory glands and their downstream ducts function normally.

PROPOSED ALIGNED MODEL, NOT APPLIED
Both sperm-transport routes are blocked, so newly produced sperm cannot pass from the epididymides through the vasa deferentia to the downstream pathway. The seminal vesicles, prostate and bulbourethral glands still add fluid through their functioning downstream connections. Testosterone can still enter the blood from interstitial cells; its route is separate. This model does not establish whether sperm were already present downstream before the blockage.

PROPOSED CRITERIA IF THE CHANGE IS APPROVED
- Distinguishes blocked transport of newly produced sperm on both sides from functioning accessory-gland secretion
- Explains why this duct change alone does not establish loss of testosterone release into blood
- Does not claim immediate absence of all previously stored downstream sperm
