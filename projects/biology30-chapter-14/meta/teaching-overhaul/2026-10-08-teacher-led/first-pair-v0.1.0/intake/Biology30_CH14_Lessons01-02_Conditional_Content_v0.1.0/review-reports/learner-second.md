## Protected Lesson 2 writing 2: effect of the ambiguity

The prompt uses “a blocked vas deferens” while describing possible absence of sperm in semen. The candidate explicitly teaches that there are two testes and paired ducts and instructs the learner to check whether a case affects one side or both before claiming complete interruption.

That creates a real interpretive burden for a first-time learner: accepting an unstated bilateral assumption could yield a broad answer that conflicts with the lesson's caution. The teaching provides the tools to resist that inference, and my first response did so:

- A single affected side loses passage of sperm from that testis
- Fluid from independently connected accessory glands can remain
- Complete interruption of passage from both testes needs both relevant routes affected
- A blocked sperm duct does not establish stopped testosterone release

This qualified answer meets the supplied criteria and preserves the intended mechanism. It is reasonable to regard the **teaching** as adequate for that mechanism while retaining an **assessment wording limitation**. The visible caution is mitigation, not a repair. The first report's suggested prompt clarification was a content-review recommendation only. Following the later instruction that assessment wording is protected, I made no changes and do not treat that suggestion as authorized work.
