## Scientific claims and source/art reconciliation

### Pass: anatomy, routes and transport (lesson 05 sections 1–4)

Ovary, oocyte and supporting tissue are correctly distinguished. The oviduct opens near the ovary rather than forming a sealed connection. Fimbrial guidance, ciliary action and muscular contribution are appropriately described without claiming the cell swims. The sperm route vagina → cervix → uterus → oviduct is correct. Fertilization is correctly located normally in the oviduct and distinguished from subsequent implantation.

The source basis is textbook p.482/Figure14.4, pp.483–484/Table14.3 and slides22–23. The retained crop has label leaders, not movement arrows, and does not separately label the endometrium. The candidate explicitly says both. It accurately separates uterus, endometrium and cervix and keeps urinary urethra separate from vagina. Menstruation is correctly limited to part of the lining. Independent anatomical/transport corroboration: [OpenStax A&P 27.2, uterine tubes and uterus/cervix sections](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/27-2-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-ovarian-reproductive-system).

### Pass: fertilization versus implantation timing (lesson 05 section 3)

The source p.484 uses “zygote” too broadly for the implanting structure. The candidate preserves the historical correction: divisions occur during transport and the multicellular blastocyst implants later. It does not assert immediate implantation or a universal day. Its passage exercise explicitly traces connected structures rather than asserting survival of an intact unfertilized oocyte through menstruation. Independent corroboration: [OpenStax A&P 28.2, pre-implantation development and implantation](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/28-2-embryonic-development).

### Pass: oogenesis, ploidy and unequal cytokinesis (lesson 06 sections 1–2)

The candidate correctly separates chromosome-set number from cell size and cytoplasm. Meiosis I produces a secondary oocyte and polar body; the released secondary oocyte is haploid while retaining copied chromosomes and arresting in meiosis II. Sperm entry can trigger completion. Concentrating cytoplasm does not create an extra chromosome set. The four-spermatid/sperm comparison and one large reproductive-cell outcome are appropriately qualified.

Source slides24–26 and pp.482/485 use broad “ovum/egg” terminology. The candidate explains this without editing those images or confusing the whole follicle with the cell. The precise stage correction is independently supported by the original publisher's [OpenStax Biology 2e 43.3, Oogenesis and Figure43.13](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/43-3-human-reproductive-anatomy-and-gametogenesis), which identifies the secondary oocyte as 1n and arrested at metaphase II.

Important source caveat: OpenStax A&P 27.2's oogenesis prose currently calls a secondary oocyte “diploid.” That sentence is not reliable support for ploidy and was not adopted. The correct candidate distinction is independently supported by Biology 43.3 and the human-oocyte account in [Virant-Klun et al. 2013, section2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3590615/). The latter is a scholarly review, not a new experiment performed for this course. NCBI Bookshelf direct page opens encountered a browser check; search excerpts from those pages were not used as sole decisive evidence.

### Pass: follicular development, ovulation and stage interpretation (lesson 06 sections 1/3)

A finite starting supply, development of a group with a usual dominant follicle, and many follicles not reaching ovulation are appropriate. The candidate does not mandate alternation between ovaries. A primary ultrasound study found alternating ovulations in approximately half of successive-cycle pairs, supporting rejection of strict alternation: [Ecochard and Gougeon, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10739814/). The result does not predict an individual's next ovulation side.

In the retained Figure14.14 crop, the small overview, magnification box/arrow, upper immature/developing follicles, right mature follicle, lower-right released cell and lower yellow/regressing structures are as described. The candidate correctly interprets a sequence laid out for illustration, not clockwise physical migration or a necessary simultaneous inventory. The underlying textbook caption itself makes the non-migration qualification.

### Pass: corpus-luteum hormones and delayed withdrawal (lesson 06 section 4/worked example)

The corpus luteum remains in the ovary after release, produces mainly progesterone and some estrogen, and signals through blood to the uterine lining. The candidate correctly links later regression and falling support to menstruation instead of making oocyte death an immediate next-day switch. The corpus albicans is a regressed remnant, not an embryo. Sources: textbook pp.496–498/Figure14.14 and slide24; [OpenStax A&P 28.2 implantation account](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/28-2-embryonic-development) corroborates pregnancy signalling that maintains luteal progesterone and estrogen. The worked model does not overclaim that fertilization or implantation has occurred.

### Pass: model limits and consequential application

The ciliary worked example changes transport support while leaving the passage open; it does not infer complete immobility, travel time, hormonal failure or a clinical diagnosis. The new optional cervical-route interruption and haploid-size comparison change consequential reasoning. They remain optional, unsaved teaching prose and do not constitute independent mastery tests. B02/B05 and U04 are met within this instructional scope.
