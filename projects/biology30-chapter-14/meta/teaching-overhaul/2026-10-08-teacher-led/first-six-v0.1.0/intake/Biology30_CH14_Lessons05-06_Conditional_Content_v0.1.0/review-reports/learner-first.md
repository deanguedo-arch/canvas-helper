### L05-RW1 Required written explanation 1

Prompt: Trace the usual routes of a sperm deposited in the vagina and an oocyte released from an ovary until they may meet.

**First-attempt answer:** Sperm move from the vagina through the cervix and uterus toward an oviduct. At ovulation a secondary oocyte is released from the ovary. The fimbriae and movement near the opening guide it into the nearby oviduct, and cilia and muscular activity help move it toward the uterus. The sperm and oocyte may meet in the oviduct, the usual site of fertilization. Reaching a route does not mean fertilization must happen.

Consequential steps and visible support:
1. Sperm sequence vagina → cervix → uterus → oviduct: stated directly in “Follow both gametes” (p7); the cervical/vaginal connection is defined on p6.
2. Oocyte leaves ovary at ovulation: “An ovary produces the cell” defines ovulation as secondary-oocyte release (pp3–4).
3. Released oocyte is guided near fimbriae into the oviduct: explanation on p4 and the continuous “Follow both gametes” paragraph across pp6–7. The opening is explicitly near the ovary, not a sealed pipe attached to it (p4).
4. Movement support from cilia and muscle: p4. This does not justify a claim that the oocyte swims itself or that an open passage proves normal transport.
5. Usual meeting site and conditional outcome: p7 explicitly names the oviduct and explains that “normally” does not mean every released oocyte is fertilized.
6. Intact linked anatomy image supports relative positions and the cervix/vagina distinction. In the initial PDF, its right-side labels are absent; the prose alone still supports this written response.

### L05-RW2 Required written explanation 2

Prompt: Explain the difference between uterus, endometrium and cervix. Connect each with one function.

**First-attempt answer:** The uterus is the muscular organ that supports development after implantation. The endometrium is the uterus's inner lining; it provides the environment where an early embryo attaches and embeds during implantation. The cervix is the narrow lower region of the uterus that opens into the vagina, providing the passage sperm use from the vagina into the uterus. These names describe an organ, its lining, and its lower opening region.

Consequential steps and visible support:
1. Uterus = muscular organ and development support: vocabulary p2 and “Distinguish” p6.
2. Endometrium = inner lining and implantation site: definition crosses pp2–3; direct explanatory paragraph p6.
3. Cervix = narrow lower uterine region opening into vagina: vocabulary p3 and p6.
4. Cervical passage used by sperm: the connection on p6 plus the explicit sperm route on p7.
5. Structural contrast organ versus tissue layer is directly stated on p6. Exact endometrial boundaries are not separately labelled in the linked diagram; I use the prose rather than claiming a labelled visual identification.

### L06-RW1 Required written explanation 1

Prompt: Follow an ovarian follicle from growth through ovulation and corpus-luteum formation. Include changing hormone output.

**First-attempt answer:** A follicle contains an oocyte and its supporting cells. As the follicle develops, those supporting cells grow and estrogen output increases, helping stimulate growth of the endometrium. At ovulation the mature follicle ruptures and releases the secondary oocyte toward the oviduct. Most follicular tissue stays in the ovary and develops into the corpus luteum. This temporary endocrine structure releases mainly progesterone and some estrogen; progesterone helps maintain the prepared endometrium. The hormones travel through the blood, while the corpus luteum remains in the ovary. If it does not receive a pregnancy-supporting signal, the corpus luteum later regresses and its hormone output falls.

Consequential steps and visible support:
1. Follicle contains oocyte plus supporting cells: p2 definition and p3 explanation.
2. Growth and increasing estrogen output: p5 first paragraph. Estrogen's endometrial growth effect is also given there.
3. Mature follicle rupture and cell release: p5; precise released stage “secondary oocyte” and incomplete meiosis II are explained on p4.
4. Different fates for cell and remaining tissue: p5 and p7 final explanation.
5. Corpus-luteum formation, location, main progesterone/some estrogen output and endometrial-maintenance effect: p7 first paragraph.
6. Signal route through blood: pp5/7 and worked example p8.
7. Optional closing later fate: conditional regression and falling hormone support, p7. I do not supply a hormone name for the pregnancy-supporting signal or an exact timetable because neither is taught here.
8. The intact linked diagram labels developing follicles as producing estrogen and some progesterone, and labels the corpus luteum as producing progesterone and some estrogen. My first answer uses the prose's emphasis on rising estrogen before ovulation and chiefly progesterone afterward, rather than adding an unsupported claim that progesterone is entirely absent before ovulation.

### L06-RW2 Required written explanation 2

Prompt: Compare the products of spermatogenesis and oogenesis without giving detailed meiotic phases.

**First-attempt answer:** In spermatogenesis, one primary spermatocyte can produce four haploid spermatids that develop into sperm. Oogenesis retains most cytoplasm in one large reproductive cell and also produces small polar bodies, rather than four equal-sized functional eggs. Both processes reduce chromosome sets. The unequal cytoplasm distribution in oogenesis concentrates resources in the large cell for early development after fertilization. The released cell is a secondary oocyte and has not yet finished meiosis II.

Consequential steps and visible support:
1. One primary spermatocyte → four haploid spermatids → sperm: the comparison paragraph on p4 states the full relationship directly.
2. Oogenesis → one large cytoplasm-rich cell plus small polar bodies, not four equal functional eggs: first and comparison paragraphs on p4.
3. Shared chromosome-set reduction: explicitly stated in the comparison paragraph on p4; haploid is defined earlier on that page.
4. Resource concentration effect: p4 second paragraph.
5. Precise released cell and unfinished meiosis II: p4 third and final paragraphs. This one-sentence distinction stays within the prompt's request to avoid detailed phase recitation.
6. I do not specify an exact final number of polar bodies, claim polar-body fate, or add sperm motility/size details: those are not necessary and are not supplied by this comparison text.

### L06-A New optional application

Prompt: A small polar body and larger secondary oocyte are each labelled haploid; a student says more material makes the larger cell diploid. Explain the mistake and what unequal size preserves.

**First-attempt answer:** The larger cell can contain more cytoplasm while still having one chromosome set. Haploid refers to chromosome-set number, not cell size or amount of cytoplasm. The unequal division keeps most cytoplasm, including resources and organelles, in the large reproductive cell to support early development after fertilization. More cytoplasm is not evidence of an extra chromosome set.

Consequential steps and visible support:
1. Both cells' haploid labels and unequal size are supplied by the prompt on p8; no actual application drawing is required to extract those stated givens.
2. Define haploid as one chromosome set: p4.
3. Separate cell size/cytoplasm from chromosome number: p4 explicitly says they describe different properties and unequal division does not provide an extra chromosome set.
4. Explain preserved material and its use: p4 defines cytoplasm as material outside the nucleus including resources/organelles, then ties unequal division to early-development resources.
5. “Diploid” is used in the question but not explicitly defined in visible lesson-06 text. I can reject the student's reasoning from the supplied haploid label and explicit “no extra chromosome set” teaching, without silently importing a definition of diploid. Adding a short definition would make this more self-contained for a truly new learner.

- The anatomy explanation tells the learner how to read leader lines, where to start, why the oviduct is not a sealed attachment, and why reproductive and urinary passages should not be conflated. All route answers are explicitly supported.
- The follicle explanation distinguishes an enlargement arrow from movement and a sequence drawn around an ovary from physical clockwise migration. It also explains that the diagram combines stages through time.
- The broad figure label “ovum” is explicitly reconciled with the precise stage “secondary oocyte.” The lesson explains the unfinished division without requiring detailed phase memorization.
- Cytoplasm, cytokinesis, haploid, implantation, oocyte, follicle and corpus luteum have usable inline or visible definitions. The answers do not require optional reading/video.
- The source-number task now states its starting point, connected-passage scope and viability limitation clearly; no ambiguity about ascending number order remains.

### Gaps or possible learner friction

1. **Major static visual defect:** the initial PDF crop removes anatomy labels including fimbriae, cervix and vagina, and removes the mature/released-oocyte right side of the follicle figure. A learner cannot complete the prose's explicit visual-location instructions from those PDF images alone. Intact linked images contain them; prose still supports the assessments.
2. **Major static selection-format defect:** options concatenate without visible controls or separators. The correct strings can be recovered using the HTML, but a fresh PDF-only learner has to segment runs of text. The second lesson's second required selection crosses pp9–10 in the middle of the correct choice.
3. **Endometrium not separately labelled:** the lesson honestly states this and supplies a cavity-facing-lining instruction (L05 p6), but the figure cannot directly verify a distinct labelled endometrial boundary. This is a modest gap against the “locate” learning goal rather than a blocker for the written questions.
4. **Diploid is not defined locally:** optional L06 application still answerable via haploid and “no extra set,” but the new term adds avoidable friction.
5. **Hypothetical drawing wording:** the L06 optional application says “A drawing shows...” but has no paired polar-body drawing. Its complete verbal givens are sufficient; call it a described drawing/model or supply a simple labelled sketch if actual visual interpretation is intended.
6. **Static instructional affordances:** packet text still says select Start, Check answer, Save and Submit; the packet is intentionally inert, so it cannot verify any unlock/save/redo/progress behavior. “Not started” is not learner progress evidence.
7. **Mild navigation/numbering friction:** the saved L05 source task is labelled “Optional application 3,” while the new application above is simply “Optional application.” This does not obscure the biology, but the number's purpose is not explained to the learner.
8. **Male-system distractors and comparison terms:** epididymis, interstitial cells, primary spermatocyte and spermatid are not all fully developed in these packets. The correct answers remain supported without knowing the distractors. The L06 comparison gives the necessary primary-cell → products relation directly, so lack of a fuller local definition is not a consequential obstacle to its assigned writing task.
