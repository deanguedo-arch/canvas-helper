## Core scientific findings

### Lesson 09

1. **Normal-cycle baseline and inference limits.** Textbook 498 and 502 support the follicle/ovulation/corpus-luteum and uterine sequence. Candidate correctly separates a low measured progesterone sample from a conclusion about an entire cycle, explains a missed brief LH peak, and treats a complete changed pattern as evidence for a hypothesis. It does not infer an individual cause from one sample. The usual regression/hormone-decline/shedding chain is complete, rather than attributing menstruation to a new follicle.
2. **Ovarian ageing.** Textbook 495/498 and slides 20/34 support the contrast, but the new copy correctly qualifies a fluctuating transition and distinguishes diminished ovarian response from increased upstream stimulation. Inhibin is particularly relevant to FSH rather than being treated as the sole controller of all gonadotropins. The independent primary/research record supports reduced inhibin with increased FSH: [Burger et al., inhibin feedback](https://repository.monashhealth.org/monashhealthjspui/handle/1/34413). The human studies summarized in [Burger's menopausal-transition analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8124478/) corroborate fluctuations and explain why a single hormone result is limited. No clinical threshold, treatment or personal diagnosis is offered.
3. **hCG: supplemental evidence is essential and now independently checked.** Bounded Chapter 14 teacher/textbook pages do not develop this mechanism. It is already required by the historical lesson/config and sequence-08, so it must not be portrayed as a missing teacher handout. Primary evidence supports the supplied chain: cultured early-pregnancy trophoblast cells produce hCG ([Malek et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11464582/)); a human IVF study found hCG luteal support augmented progesterone/estradiol and reduced the midluteal decline ([Hutchinson-Williams et al. 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2307247/)); a small historical human luteal-removal study supports the early corpus-luteum/progesterone dependence and later placental shift ([Csapo et al. 1972](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5017636/)). These are mechanism corroboration, not a proposed intervention or proof that the small studies alone establish every aspect of normal pregnancy. The candidate appropriately omits clinical timing/doses and reserves detailed development for Chapter 15.
4. **Combined hormonal contraception.** The new copy correctly specifies estrogen plus progestin, diminished FSH/LH support and suppression of the LH surge, plus cervical-mucus effects. The absolute slide-35 wording is not repeated as a current general rule. Official mechanism sources independently support suppression of gonadotropins and ovulation: [FDA pharmacology review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2006/021529s000_PharmR.pdf), and [NICHD contraception mechanisms](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/factsheets/contraception). The NICHD page contains older product-specific material; this review used only the enduring mechanism passages, not availability, dosing or broad product claims.
5. **External androgen and hGH clarification.** The candidate's upstream/downstream comparison is supported by primary human testosterone-suppression evidence ([Coviello et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15713727/)). The teacher slide's hGH classification is genuinely wrong: hGH is a polypeptide, as identified by the [NLM Human Growth Hormone descriptor](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/68019382). The candidate explicitly repairs the teaching interpretation without altering the source slide. No claim is made that every listed steroid side effect is inevitable.
6. **Worked/new optional examples.** The A/B feedback contrast changes a consequential condition, not just surface wording. The hCG target-response failure explicitly excludes adequate replacement hormone output from other tissue; without that condition the inference would be underdetermined. Its conclusion is about the constructed support chain, not an individual outcome.

### Lesson 10

1. **Pathogens and route damage.** Bacterial versus viral examples agree with textbook 486–490. Candidate correctly separates pathogens from hormones, avoids stigma and recognizes asymptomatic infection. Chlamydia/gonorrhea can lead to PID and tubal scarring; a damaged pathway can coexist with ovarian function. This does not assert that every infection causes infertility or that one obstructed tube necessarily eliminates natural fertility. [CDC PID](https://www.cdc.gov/pid/about/index.html) independently supports scarring, blockage, ectopic-pregnancy risk and treatment not undoing established damage.
2. **Male tract.** The epididymis example distinguishes maturation/transport consequences from testosterone output. CDC identifies infectious epididymitis and fertility complications, supporting the qualified “can” statement rather than a universal causal claim. [CDC epididymitis](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/epididymitis.htm). No treatment regimen is imported.
3. **Different tissues and newborn routes.** HIV immune-cell effects, hepatitis-B liver effects, HPV cancer/wart distinctions and herpes without visible sores remain bounded mechanism examples. The candidate appropriately distinguishes transplacental syphilis from birth-associated chlamydia/gonorrhea/herpes exposure. Official sources support the eye/respiratory examples and differing routes: [CDC chlamydia, neonatal section](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/chlamydia.htm), [CDC newborn conjunctivitis](https://www.cdc.gov/conjunctivitis/newborns/index.html), [CDC congenital syphilis](https://www.cdc.gov/syphilis/about/about-congenital-syphilis.html), and [CDC herpes](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/herpes.htm). The narrative does not claim every pathogen crosses the placenta or every exposure has the same consequence.
4. **Prevention versus conception control.** Combined hormonal signals do not create an infection barrier. Correct and consistent appropriate-condom use can reduce transmission, with coverage limits; it is not a cure or an absolute guarantee. These qualifications are independently supported by [CDC primary prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/clinical-primary.htm). The vas-deferens comparison retains the distinction between sperm and accessory-gland fluid. No universal pregnancy-prevention guarantee is stated.
5. **Worked/new optional examples.** The bilateral blockage is explicit. Ovarian function, pathogen removal and persistence of scarring are distinct givens. Comparing an LH-surge/ovulation problem against transport obstruction establishes the intended mechanism distinction without assuming that one intervention fixes both.

## Protected-source decisions: exact issue, smallest proposal, dependencies

These proposals are NOT applied. They require teacher/source-owner disposition and version-aware changes if accepted. A separate annotation can preserve an original crop while preventing its interpretation as current authority. None authorizes rewriting assessments, source art or runtime.

### P1. Estrogen-receptor case

Affected: `ch14-p505-chapter-review-q20`, including subparts (a)–(d); mapped to lesson-01 and lesson-09; original p505 and its crop/full-page context. Exact premise: an XX individual has a genetic defect such that “cellular receptors for estrogen are lacking.” It then asks about gonads, response, male secondary characteristics from adrenal testosterone, and fertility.

Problem: the prompt does not identify receptor subtype, extent/site of loss, developmental assumptions, androgen concentrations or other intact pathways. Chromosomes and absent estrogen signalling alone do not justify a deterministic complete phenotype. Human ESR1 case reports demonstrate ovaries and disrupted development despite high estrogen, and cannot license all inferences in this broad invented case: [primary human case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23841731/), [family study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27754803/). These primary records were read through indexed abstract text; publisher/full-text retrieval was not available, so no detailed full-article claim is made.

Minimum unresolved-input requirement: specify whether this is a deliberately simplified all-estrogen-response model, assume normal ovarian determination and otherwise functioning androgen receptors, and state what evidence (if any) is supplied about androgen levels. Otherwise accept only qualified predictions and explicit uncertainty for (c)/(d).

Minimal proposed accompanying note: “Treat this as a simplified receptor-response model. State your developmental and hormone-level assumptions. Loss of estrogen responsiveness alone does not establish androgen levels or every secondary sex characteristic; identify what extra evidence each prediction needs.” Do not create a deterministic new key without those givens.

### P2. Menstruation after pregnancy

Affected: `ch14-p505-chapter-review-q23` part (b), original exact question “Explain why menstruation begins again after the pregnancy.” Its source record is mapped to lesson-09.

Problem: early hCG/corpus-luteum teaching does not complete the later pregnancy/postpartum/lactation mechanism, and the wording can be misread as immediate universal menstruation after delivery. Postpartum bleeding and resumed menstrual cycles are different; timing varies with feeding and other factors. [NHS postpartum physiology](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/your-body/) explicitly distinguishes lochia and returning periods.

Minimal proposed note: “Explain the eventual return of menstrual cycling, not the bleeding immediately after birth. Timing varies, especially during breastfeeding.” Supply the corresponding later teaching/source before expecting a full mechanism answer; otherwise defer part (b) to Chapter 15. Do not add this as a required lesson-09 outcome by stealth.

### P3. Overbroad syphilis-contact statement

Affected source: p490 first column, continuation of Syphilis: “Any contact with the infected person may spread the infection.” This is reader context for lesson-10 and supports the STI optional questions, especially `ch14-p491-section-14-2-q3/q5` and `ch14-p504-chapter-review-q6`. No linked question directly repeats that sentence.

Problem: “any contact” is materially overbroad and can support stigma. Current official explanation identifies direct contact with an infectious sore and transmission during pregnancy, while excluding casual contact with ordinary objects. [CDC syphilis transmission](https://www.cdc.gov/syphilis/about/index.html).

Minimal proposed source annotation: “Syphilis can spread through direct contact with an infectious sore, including during sexual contact, and during pregnancy. Ordinary casual contact is not the general transmission mechanism.” The new core teaching does not make the erroneous claim; an annotation decision is needed before representing the whole reader as checked/current.

### P4. Source clinical generalizations and historical information

- Source p487–488 broadly says physicians usually recommend Caesarean birth for a mother with genital herpes; p488 Q15 repeats this but **is not among these nineteen linked records**. The full supporting pages nevertheless expose it. Current CDC guidance distinguishes findings at labour and does not turn any history of herpes into one universal delivery rule. Minimal annotation: “Delivery decisions depend on the clinical circumstances at labour; this historical sentence is not a universal recommendation.” [CDC herpes, pregnancy section](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/herpes.htm)
- Source p499 lists increased colorectal-cancer risk under HRT. That is not a reliable blanket statement: the WHI combined-treatment randomized trial found lower colorectal-cancer incidence in its intervention period, with important interpretation limits. Minimal annotation: “Hormone-therapy findings depend on regimen and outcome; this historical risk list must not be used as a current universal summary.” [Primary WHI colorectal study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa032071), [NCI current synthesis](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/hormones/mht-fact-sheet). This is a source-accuracy flag, not treatment advice or a proposed treatment module.
- Source p498 FYI asserts cycles tend to synchronize when women live together. It should not be taught as an established physiological rule. Minimal annotation: “Apparent overlap is not evidence that living together synchronizes menstrual cycles.” Primary studies tested and failed to find the asserted pattern: [Yang and Schank](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26181612/), [two negative studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1745701/). The candidate neither depends on nor repeats it.
- Rates, treatment/prognosis, vaccine/product statements and broad age-group claims in 486–491/495/498–499 retain their historical date/status. Candidate correctly warns about this. No comprehensive current clinical-reference certification was performed or is implied.
