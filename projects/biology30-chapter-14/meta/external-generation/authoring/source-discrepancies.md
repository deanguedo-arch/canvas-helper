# Chapter 14 — source reconciliation and corrections

Developer/teacher record. Do not publish this file in student navigation.

The teacher deck supplies language, emphasis and examples. It is not reproduced verbatim where an identified statement conflicts with the mechanism. Original textbook-question crops remain original; adapted automatic questions are separately identified in assessment-dispositions.json. The following changes are explicit authoring decisions, not silent assertions that the original slides already said these things.

| Source issue | Treatment in this build | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Primary/secondary traits can appear to be defined only by when they develop. | Classify a reproductive organ as primary even when its function increases at puberty. Define secondary characteristics by their role, with development at puberty as context. | Lesson 1; guided example. |
| “Presence of a Y chromosome” can be read as an exhaustive account of all reproductive development. | State the typical XX/XY pattern and introduce the developmental signal without claiming one chromosome or hormone explains every variation. Anatomy is not used to infer a person's identity. | Lesson 1. |
| Spermatogonia are described as undergoing “two cycles of meiosis.” | Separate precursor-cell growth/mitosis, the primary spermatocyte, one meiosis with two divisions, spermatids and differentiation into sperm. | Lesson 3. |
| Supporting Sertoli cells and germ cells can be conflated. | FSH acts on Sertoli support functions; sperm arise from the germ-cell lineage. LH stimulates interstitial cells to release testosterone. | Lessons 3–4; feedback schematic. |
| “Steroids, such as hGH” misclassifies hGH. | Do not call hGH a steroid. Use a bounded external-androgen feedback model without drug dosing or medical treatment instructions. | Lesson 9; optional practice. |
| Slide terminology treats the ovulated cell as a completed ovum throughout. | Explain the secondary oocyte and later completion of meiosis, while retaining familiar “egg” wording after definition. | Lessons 5–6. |
| The endometrium can appear to be shed immediately when an unfertilized egg loses viability. | Trace corpus-luteum regression and the later decline in hormone support before menstruation. | Lessons 6–9. |
| Flow and follicular phases are presented as wholly separate consecutive ovarian stages. | Show menstrual flow as a uterine event overlapping the early follicular phase. Keep a 28-day graph as an illustrative model, not a universal timetable or contraception calculator. | Lessons 7–8; coordinated graph. |
| Rising estrogen inhibits FSH, but the positive-feedback transition is compressed. | Teach ordinary negative feedback before sustained high estrogen, the LH surge and ovulation. | Lesson 8. |
| Andropause is presented as a direct counterpart to menopause. | Distinguish gradual changes in some male hormone patterns from the cessation of ovarian cycles. Do not diagnose from an isolated symptom or measurement. | Lesson 9. |
| Hormonal contraception is described through broad “high dose” language. | Explain a combined-hormonal feedback model. Do not generalize its mechanism to every contraceptive method or provide personal use/timing advice. | Lesson 9. |
| STI material is listed but not developed in the Chapter 14 teaching deck. | Use the textbook's STI section and supplied Unit B outcome list, supported by Public Health Agency of Canada disease pages. Teach infection/inflammation/tubal damage and possible fertility effects without stigma. | Lesson 10. |
| Daily-plan row 27 says “495 #5”; this is not a unique matching in-text question on that page. | Record the ambiguity rather than invent a question. The chapter includes the full eligible original-question inventory and topic-based access. | daily-plan-reference.txt; textbook manifest. |
| Some old assessment items rely on a questionable health/chemical claim or an ambiguous hormone comparison. | Adapt the stem to a stated mechanism or exclude it. Preserve source identity, old stem, disposition and reason. | assessment-dispositions.json. |
| Textbook lab procedures sometimes require external observations or clinical/policy research. | Include paper-based analysis with the actual source data. Exclude physical data-collection and selected unsupported clinical/policy tasks, with reasons. No observations were invented. | textbook-question-manifest.json, excluded. |
| A new hormone graph could be mistaken for an experimental dataset. | Explicitly label it schematic and compare timing rather than absolute hormone concentrations. Rendering values are retained only as developer reconstruction data. | Lesson 8 figure and schematic-rendering-values.json. |
| Full Unit B test/review material includes later development content. | Keep all 191 physical records in the developer catalogue. Import 38 reviewed multiple-choice questions and four written tasks here. Deferred Unit B material belongs in the Chapter 15 review reconciliation, not an invented Chapter 14 test. | assessment-dispositions.json. |
