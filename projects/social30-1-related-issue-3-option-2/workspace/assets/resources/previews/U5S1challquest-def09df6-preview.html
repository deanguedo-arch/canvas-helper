Cold War Interview Questions


Some critics have said that the policy of containment during the Cold War frequently required the United States and its allies to violate many of the basic principles of liberalism, ostensibly (or supposedly) to defend liberalism. Can you identify some examples from the Cold War that might fit this description? 


Do you agree with the argument that liberal democracies sometimes have to abandon their principles to achieve a larger goal of defeating an ideological rival?


What do you think would have happened if the United States and other liberal democracies chose not to challenge communist expansionism during the Cold War?


Thinking back to some pivotal Cold War events, what’s your opinion of brinkmanship as a foreign policy tool? Can the inherent dangers be justified?


How do you think liberal democracies should conduct themselves in a world of competing ideologies? Should liberal democracies become involved in proxy wars or support liberation movements in foreign countries? Should they put pressure on non-aligned nations to “sign up” for liberalism? 


Some historians look back at the Cold War and suggest that some events led people to lose faith in government. What kind of Cold War events fit that description? Can you explain why some people lost their trust in government as a result of those events?


Do you believe that spending on nuclear weapons by the superpowers was justifiable? If so, how do you justify it? If not, how would you have prevented the nuclear arms race from occurring if you’d had the power to do so?


You worked in the White House for almost the entire length of the Cold War. What, in your opinion, were some of the positive impacts of the conflict, if any?
