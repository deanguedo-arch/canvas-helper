## What the reader is trying to explain

Lesson02 asks how a phenotype can disappear for a generation without the allele being lost. Known parentage and generation labels make the observations interpretable. The allele-copying and separation mechanism explains what passes into gametes; fertilisation explains how a pair returns; complete dominance explains the appearance of that pair. The seed cross answers the motivating question without treating a ratio as an offspring schedule.

Lesson05 asks whether following one allele contribution tells us the contribution at another locus. Different chromosome-pair orientations establish the independent model before gamete probabilities, and random fertilisation has a separate role when two parents’ contributions meet. Three retained source examples have distinct jobs; their number is not a quota:

Independent assortment at two segregating loci is explained in the pea/tomato sequence. The squash cross itself does not establish that independence: each resolved parent segregates at only one of the two loci. Keeping its inverse-inference job separate avoids overclaiming what it shows.
