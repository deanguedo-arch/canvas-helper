## Teacher source progression, with the explanation it requires

1. **Slide67: population diversity versus an individual's pair.** The title is MULTIPLE ALLELES. Its key distinction is many versions in the population although the organism inherits two. The source then introduces the three ABO symbols and antigens. Preserve this explanatory order, but qualify the locus as the ordinary diploid autosomal model and the three as the common alleles included in this model. The source's “absence of an antigen” must mean neither A nor B antigen, not a red cell with no surface antigens of any kind.
2. **Slide68 and printed605 Table17.2: translate alleles into expression.** There are six unordered genotypes and four phenotype groups. `IA` and `IB` each name one allele at one locus; their superscripts do not indicate extra genes, powers or additional copies. `i` is an allele, while O is the phenotype name. The relationship `IA = IB > i` means codominance between the first two and complete dominance of either over i; it does not mean equal allele frequencies, identical molecules, superiority or gamete bias. The table must state A antigens, B antigens, both or neither A nor B, as appropriate.
3. **Locked native square: a specific actual cross.** Before reading the figure, name the parental genotypes and derive their gametes. Top columns are `IA` and `i`; left rows are `IB` and `i`. Circled letters are locations: A identifies `IAIB`/type AB, B identifies `IBi`/type B, C identifies `IAi`/type A, D identifies `ii`/type O. The lower legend is already visible. Each gamete contribution is one allele, and each interior genotype is the resulting pair. Equal segregation and random fertilisation make each of four contribution pairs probability1/4. The four phenotypes happen to be equiprobable for this cross, not for every ABO cross or the population.
4. **Slide69: a changed parental arrangement.** The actual worked case is homozygous type A × heterozygous type B, `IAIA × IBi`. The image shows `IA` twice across the top and `IB`, then `i`, down the left. The duplicated top labels are one distinct gamete type repeated in the conventional square. Actual `P(IA from the first parent)=1`, and the second parent's two types each have probability1/2. Upper two cells are `IAIB`; lower two are `IAi`. The biological prediction is AB1/2 and A1/2, with neither B nor O. Count displayed cells only after explaining why the duplicated slots have the intended weighting.
5. **Slide70: multiplication has a stated condition.** The text says independent events and shows fair-coin two-heads1/4 and fair-coin plus fair-die three1/12. Prior probability teaching already supplies this function; reproducing both coin examples is not mandatory. It remains essential to justify the independence used in the current genetics event.
6. **Slide71: family evidence first, then blood-group prediction, then a joint event.** Source woman is type B with a type O father; source partner is AB. The O father supplies i, forcing the woman's genotype to `IBi` under the stated model. Partner `IAIB` supplies IA or IB. The square has IA/IB on top and IB/i at the side, producing `IAIB`, `IBIB`, `IAi`, `IBi` at1/4 each. Add the disjoint B genotypes to get B1/2; A and AB each1/4; O0. This is a different forward cross from the protected A-with-O-parent × O-partner writing task.
7. **Slide71's sex event requires an explicit repair in explanatory copy.** The slide says independence follows because blood type and sex are not on the same chromosome. State the simplified classroom chromosome/sex model and the assumed1/2 probability of a son, and that the blood-group event and this chromosome outcome are modelled as independent. Autosome location alone is not proof that every arbitrary phenotype/event is independent. “Son AND type B” is joint, among all children:1/2 ×1/2=1/4. “Type B among sons” conditions on sons and uses a different denominator: (1/4)/(1/2)=1/2. The second multiplication by1/2 does not belong in the conditional answer.
8. **Printed606: sample evidence has limits.** The source compares type B mother's possible genotypes with a type O father and six observed B children. Its1/64 is `P(six B children | IBi mother, ii father, stated assumptions)`, not `P(IBi mother | six B children)`. It leaves the heterozygote possible. This reasoning may be supported through a different worked event and the actual previous testcross teaching to avoid unnecessarily copying the protected forward cross after changing A to B.
