## Instructional decisions assessed against adjacent blocks

###01: information before inference

The opening gives selective breeding a purpose before introducing terminology. The dog row is used for meaningful contrasts while explicitly limiting what a photograph establishes. DNA/chromosome/gene/locus/allele meanings precede the two-copy table. The model is scoped to ordinary diploid autosomal inheritance, and a copy is distinguished from a version.

The flower-colour model is defined before the original chart. Row reading precedes interpretation of why BB and Bb can share a phenotype. Zygosity compares allele identity rather than appearance. Dominance describes the heterozygote and is separated from frequency, value and gamete transmission. Phenotype includes physiological/behavioural measurements despite the source chart’s narrower wording. The worked contribution records add parental-origin/order and information-loss reasoning; they are not a renamed old beetle question. The closure prepares Mendel’s experimental question without retelling its generations in advance.

###03: probabilities from contributions

The model and known mouse parents precede gametes and the original square. The Bb parent’s two equally weighted contributions are distinguished from the bb parent’s single distinct type. Both original squares are acknowledged as already visible. The repeated b rows are explained as a redundant display, with a compact weighted table showing why probability follows weights rather than box count alone.

The changed-parent examples earn different roles: Bb×Bb introduces two routes into the same genotype and different phenotype grouping; BB×Bb shows why a remembered3:1 is not a method. The grandchildren picture is explicitly incomplete as a mating record, so two known Bb later parents are stated before a prediction is made. The native A/a figure’s external alleles and internal location labels are distinguished, with its locked visible map retained.

Expected counts use48 rather than copying the required25-of100 numerical case. A mixed ordered event changes the mathematical demand from a single offspring; its either-order version justifies disjoint addition. Independence is explained and finite outcomes do not compensate for earlier births. Carrier and full penetrance are defined for the current applied vocabulary while condition-specific medical interpretation remains bounded. The explicit source-required Bb×bb and BB×Bb assessment overlaps are not declared resolved.

###04: evidence before another calculation

The learner sees BB and Bb as hypotheses before choosing a tester. The reason for a bb tester is explained through its known contribution and a comparison with a BB tester. Both source crosses are derived. A recessive offspring is traced backward to each parent, then combined with the unknown parent’s own phenotype. Correct parentage, reliable expression and ordinary transmission are placed next to that inference.

The absence of a recessive offspring receives a different explanation. Three- and six-offspring records quantify how the hypotheses account for an all-dominant sample. The probability of a record given Bb is not inverted into a probability that the parent is Bb. Family records then establish either a forced genotype or a remaining alternative. The full worked comparison uses those unequal information states before deriving two conditional future predictions; it does not assume two possibilities are equally probable.
