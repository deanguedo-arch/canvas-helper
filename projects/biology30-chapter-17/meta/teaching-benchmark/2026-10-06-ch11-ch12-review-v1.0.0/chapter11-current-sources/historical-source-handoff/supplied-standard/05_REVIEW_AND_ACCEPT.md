# Independent content and implementation review prompt

Review only the attached Biology 30 chapter/batch against `02_TEACHING_STANDARD.md`, its actual teaching sources, the approved manuscript, and the rendered implementation when provided. Existing reviews are leads, not proof. Distinguish a manuscript review from a rendered-course review.

Read as a first-time high-school learner. At each transition, ask: what does the student know now, what new reasoning is required next, and has the lesson actually taught that reasoning? Do not accept claims such as "explained above" without finding the explanation.

## Content acceptance
Check the opening question and its payoff; prerequisites; conceptual sequence; meaningful chunking; understandable teacher language; exact scientific reasoning; required scope; vocabulary explanations; visuals actively used in teaching; complete worked reasoning; meaningful variation; guided-to-independent transfer; feedback that explains; and continuity into the next lesson.

Follow each required outcome or essential concept through the source/outcome map to the actual teaching and practice. Identify absent content and shallow mentions separately. Flag any practice demand that exceeds the taught method or requires an unavailable resource. Judge anatomical, process, comparison, quantitative, experimental, and data-interpretation lessons according to their actual instructional demands rather than forcing a single pattern.

A complete lesson needs adequate explanatory evidence, not a quota of images/examples. Missing causal reasoning, a misleading visual, a material factual error, an untaught assessment demand, a nonfunctional essential resource without equivalent teaching, or an omitted required concept prevents content acceptance regardless of a good average score.

## Implementation acceptance
Check the rendered course against the approved copy and sequence. Verify that essential teaching is visible in the learning path; that diagrams, labels, captions, links, and answer feedback agree; and that layout remains usable on mobile and desktop. Check the preservation manifest and test evidence, including saved work and required-check semantics. Do not infer successful LMS testing from a local preview.

## Finding format
For every consequential finding, give:
- Severity: BLOCKER, MAJOR, or MINOR.
- Exact lesson ID, content block, file/anchor, and source evidence.
- The specific defect and what a student could misunderstand or fail to do.
- The concrete repair and the evidence needed to close it.

Report strengths by pointing to actual passages or examples; do not blanket-praise a chapter. Re-check repairs against the new version before marking findings closed. Do not award 100% confidence or claim no possible remaining issues.

## Final status
Report separately: CONTENT ACCEPTED / NEEDS REVISION / INCOMPLETE; IMPLEMENTATION VERIFIED / NEEDS REPAIR / NOT REVIEWED; and LMS VERIFIED / UNTESTED / FAILED. List unresolved evidence or asset gaps. Teacher acceptance remains a separate human sign-off, not a status the AI grants itself.

Before scaling from the Chapter 11 calibration batch, test the same standard on an actually identified later-chapter lesson of a contrasting instructional type. If that reveals a standard gap, update the standard and re-check the calibration batch. Then continue chapter by chapter, preserving both chapter progression and the same acceptance threshold through Chapter 20.
