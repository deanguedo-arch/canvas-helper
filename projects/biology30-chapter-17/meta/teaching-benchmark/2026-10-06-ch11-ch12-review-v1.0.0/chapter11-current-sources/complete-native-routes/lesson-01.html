Nervous communication

Your nervous system detects changes and directs a . Some responses are voluntary, such as reaching for a glass. Others, such as a change in heart rate, happen without a conscious decision.

Nervous and endocrine systems work together

Conditions inside and outside your body keep changing. Temperature may rise, water levels may fall, or your muscles may need to work harder. means maintaining relatively stable internal conditions despite these changes.

The nervous and endocrine systems work together. The nervous system uses electrochemical messages: electrical changes along neurons and chemical signals between cells. The endocrine system uses hormones, which travel in the bloodstream. This chapter focuses on the nervous system.

Follow this sequence: sensory input → integration → motor output. Sensory input carries information about a change. Integration means processing that information. Motor output carries instructions for a to an effector, such as a muscle or gland.

A first look at the two main divisions

The () is the brain and spinal cord. The () consists of nerves linking the CNS with the rest of the body. You will study their subdivisions later.

Two main types of cells

A is a nerve cell that receives and carries information to other cells. Glial cells support and nourish neurons. They also help remove wastes and protect against infection.

A neuron is one cell. A nerve contains many nerve fibres bundled together inside protective tissue. Think of a cable containing many individual fibres.
