## Findings and closures

### SRC-01 — unsupported attribution of a diagram expectation to Mendel — closed

**Initial locator:** `ch17-l02-teaching-03`, under “Compare the prediction with the counted offspring,” paragraph beginning “Mendel counted offspring rather than expecting every small group to copy the diagram.” The same sentence occurred in the complete Markdown manuscript.

**Issue:** The supplied account establishes that Mendel counted the forms and compared inheritance patterns. It does not establish the particular expectation or diagram comparison attributed to him in that sentence. The useful present-day inference is between an observed count and a model expectation; it does not need an unsupported historical mental-state claim.

**Source locator:** Textbook printed589/PDF6, Figure17.5 and adjacent “The Law of Segregation”; printed590–591/PDF7–8, explanation of Punnett's later representation.

**Minimal repair proposed:** Begin directly with the reported data or say that Mendel counted the two seed forms. Retain every count, consequential division and probability interpretation.

**Actual final repair reread in both files:** “Mendel recorded how many offspring showed each seed form.” The full 5,474/1,850 calculation and interpretation remain. Closed against the final hashes above.

### SCI-01 — copying was assigned too loosely to mitosis in an intermediate repair — closed

**Intermediate locator:** “Separate the pair, then separate the copies,” paragraph beginning “In flowering plants,” sentence “Later divisions by mitosis copy and divide that haploid chromosome set while maintaining its chromosome number.”

**Issue:** The newly introduced first-use definition risked attributing DNA copying to mitosis itself. This is particularly consequential in a lesson whose teaching job is to distinguish copying from chromosome separation. Mitosis separates already-copied sister chromatids; copying occurs before that division.

**Minimal repair proposed:** Define mitosis through separation of previously copied chromosomes, retaining the haploid chromosome number, and keep the subsequent plant gamete stages explicit.

**Actual final repair reread in both files:** “Later cell divisions use mitosis to separate sister chromatids after DNA copying, maintaining the haploid chromosome number in the resulting cells.” The paragraph continues through the haploid structures to sperm/egg production and the one-allele contribution. Closed against the final hashes above.

### READ-01 — two local first-use meanings arrived after initial mention — closed

The author independently identified that the copying paragraph began “Before meiosis” before the reduction purpose had been defined. The reviewer agreed with moving the definition to the copying paragraph and identified the analogous first use of “self-pollinate” in the earlier true-breeding paragraph. These were local sequencing refinements, not claims that the relevant definitions were absent from the complete lesson.

**Actual final repairs reread in both files:** The copying paragraph now begins “Meiosis is the cell-division process that reduces two chromosome sets to one. DNA is copied before that process begins.” The following subsection begins “Meiosis uses two divisions for that reduction,” preserving the two-stage explanation without repeating the introductory definition. The true-breeding paragraph now says “self-pollinate, using their own pollen,” before the later controlled-parentage comparison. Closed.
