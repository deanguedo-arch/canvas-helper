## Proposed connected section sequence

Chapter thread: retained05 separates transmission from expression →06 changes heterozygote expression while segregation persists →07 expands population allele diversity while an individual's copy number stays two →08 changes the chromosome-position model. Follow the biological question rather than reciting route numbers.

###06 — changing what the heterozygote expresses

1. **Observe a contrast to complete dominance.** Starting knowledge: one contribution per gamete, heterozygote versus homozygote, and an expression rule that determines phenotype grouping. State true-breeding red/white flowers yielding pink. Define R1/R2, parental genotypes and phenotype categories before slide50. Ask students to compare pink with both parents. Readiness: identify an intermediate heterozygote without claiming permanent allele blending or loss.
2. **Account for the next generation.** Name incomplete dominance after the observation. State diploid autosomal scope, equal segregation and random fertilisation. Trace both gamete lists on explicit axes, four quarter-weighted pairings, two heterozygous routes and three phenotype groups. Explain1:2:1 versus complete-dominance3:1 by regrouping the same genotypes. Readiness: justify the ratios and return of white without mutation. The caption's “one allele” must not become a claim that a heterozygote is haploid.
3. **Change a parent and predict deliberately.** Develop slides51–52's red×pink using distinct gamete weights and impossible-white reasoning. Choose a meaningfully different additional inference or representation where possible, rather than disguising the frozen pink×white guided solution. Explain expected count as probability×sample size and finite variation. Readiness: derive changed-cross probabilities and counts rather than reuse a universal ratio.
4. **Contrast intermediate with both detectable contributions.** Show the blue-roan horse, direct attention to mixed hairs, qualify photograph/model evidence, then name codominance. Keep the horse black/white model separate from red/white cattle. Use readable native tables to compare both expressed with uniformly intermediate under defined rules. Explain that codominance need not mean different cells expressing different alleles; same-cell ABO expression supplies the next illustration. Readiness: compare expression relationships while keeping transmission unchanged.
5. **Apply separate rules and close.** Reuse the contribution method across clearly defined models without confusing repeated letters. The optional mixed height/flower question requires two separately stated expression rules, supported by05. For Labrador work, explain the supplied B/E phenotype table and grouping of all ee classes, or explicitly defer the additional masking rule. Clinical textbook14 remains qualified supplementary source work if not independently taught. Closure: two versions suffice for these examples, but what if the population contains more than two?

###07 — population versions, individual pairs and ABO evidence

1. **Pose the copy-versus-version puzzle.** Activate the diploid autosomal contribution rule and06's expression distinctions. Define multiple alleles as population versions, not extra copies in one person. Introduce ABO's four groups as a reason to examine several versions. Readiness: separate the population inventory from individual pairs.
2. **Build notation and expression before the square.** Introduce antigen as a cell-surface marker in ordinary words, IA/IB/i as three versions at one locus, and plain-text/superscript equivalence. Explain both markers in IAIB and complete dominance over i. List six unordered genotypes and four phenotypes; define typeA/B uncertainty and noA/B markers in ii. Readiness: infer phenotype and retain genotype alternatives from appearance. Clarify the inherited carrier definition without calling every ABO heterozygote a disease carrier or treating typeO as disease. No new transfusion lesson is needed.
3. **Read the locked cross one contribution at a time.** Establish IAi/IBi parents before gametes; guide topIA/i and sideIB/i; distinguish interior location labels from alleles and blood groups. Work every pair and phenotype, justify quarter weights and total1. Acknowledge that the bottom key is already visible. Readiness: account for four groups and the denominator.
