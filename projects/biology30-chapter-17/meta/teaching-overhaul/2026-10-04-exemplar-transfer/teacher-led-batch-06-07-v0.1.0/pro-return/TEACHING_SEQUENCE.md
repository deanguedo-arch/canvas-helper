## Authority and connected purpose

The connected biological question is: **How do inherited allele contributions produce the range of phenotypes, and which part of a prediction changes when the expression rule or available allele versions changes?** Segregation and fertilisation remain the contribution mechanism. Lesson06 changes heterozygote expression, then distinguishes that change from interaction between two genes. Lesson07 adds population allele diversity and uses ABO to connect allele pairs, cell-surface markers, family evidence and probability denominators. Sex-linked transmission is the next question, not a third authored lesson.

### Why these examples earn their place

The P/F1/F2 flower sequence establishes observation, persistence of allele versions and probability grouping. The roan comparison changes the inference from intermediate colour to distinguishable expression. The mixed height/colour cross requires separate rules at separate loci; it is not another differently named one-locus square. The pigment/deposition cross introduces a genuinely new dependency in expression and supplies the missing prerequisite for the frozen Labrador task. No fixed example count or word target is imposed.

The original red×pink numerical example in slides51–52 directly matches protected application/flash practice. Its teaching function—derive the gamete list from each parent and check whether a required contribution exists—is retained in the generation and mixed-rule explanations. Its exact solved independent response is not added as another worked item. The full source originals remain unchanged.

The flower F2 derivation necessarily overlaps required06 MC2 and supports its conceptual checks. These are foundational instruction/recognition relationships, not certified fresh independent transfer. Original slide58's red-roan model likewise supports the comparison, with its assumptions explicit. The Labrador changed-parent model gives3:3:2 and does not reproduce the frozen9:3:4 task; no symbol-only safety claim is made.
