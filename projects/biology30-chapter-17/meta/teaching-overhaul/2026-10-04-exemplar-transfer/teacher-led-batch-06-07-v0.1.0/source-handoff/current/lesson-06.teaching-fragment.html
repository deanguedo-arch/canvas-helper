The alleles still segregate

Under , the heterozygote has an intermediate . In a stated flower model, R1R1 is red, R2R2 is white and R1R2 is pink. The alleles remain R1 and R2; they have not permanently fused into a pink .

Crossing two pink R1R2 plants gives R1R1, R1R2, R2R1 and R2R2 combinations. Grouped genotypes occur in a 1:2:1 ratio. Because all three genotypes have distinguishable phenotypes, the phenotype ratio is also 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white.

Both expressed is different from intermediate

In , both allele-associated products or characteristics are expressed in the heterozygote. In a roan cattle model, red and white hairs are both present rather than becoming uniformly pink hair. In ABO blood groups, both A and B antigens can be expressed on red blood cells.

Do not define codominance only as different cells expressing different alleles. The essential idea is detectable expression of both allele contributions. The way that expression appears depends on the trait.

Use superscripts or clear allele labels such as R1 and R2 when neither allele is treated as completely dominant. Define the notation before interpreting the cross.

Change the phenotype rule, not the whole inheritance process

Punnett-square construction works the same way: list one allele per gamete, combine the possibilities, then use the specified -to-phenotype rule. The difference from complete dominance lies in the heterozygote's phenotype.

A 1:2:1 genotype ratio can therefore produce either a 3:1 phenotype ratio under complete dominance or a 1:2:1 phenotype ratio when the three genotypes are distinguishable. The ratio alone must be read alongside the model.

Avoid calling one relationship “stronger” or “more advanced.” These are descriptions of expression, not rankings of alleles.

Worked example

Compare the same genotype cross under two models

R1R2 × R1R2 is used in one incomplete-dominance flower model and one codominant coat-colour model.

  1. The genotype probabilities are 1/4 R1R1, 1/2 R1R2 and 1/4 R2R2 in both.
  2. In incomplete dominance, the heterozygote is intermediate, such as pink.
  3. In codominance, both contributions are expressed, such as red and white hairs.
  4. The allele-segregation calculation stays the same; the interpretation of R1R2 changes.
