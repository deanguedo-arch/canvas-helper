<div class="content-section" data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01" id="ch17-l02-teaching-01"><h2>Follow the alleles across generations</h2><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01-p1" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01-p1">Mendel studied traits in pea plants and used controlled crosses between lines with contrasting forms. In a simple example, let R represent round seeds and r wrinkled seeds, with complete dominance of R. A <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-true-breeding" type="button">true-breeding</button> RR line crossed with rr produces Rr offspring in the <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-f1-generation" type="button">F1 generation</button>.</p><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01-p2" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-01-p2">Every F1 individual can have the round <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-phenotype" type="button">phenotype</button> while still carrying the r <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-allele" type="button">allele</button>. The recessive allele has not disappeared or blended into a weaker version. It remains part of the <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-genotype" type="button">genotype</button>.</p></div><div class="content-section" data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02" id="ch17-l02-teaching-02"><h2>Segregation explains reappearance</h2><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p1" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p1">An Rr individual forms gametes containing R or r. The alleles segregate as homologous chromosomes separate during meiosis. In the simple model, each allele has a one-half chance of entering a gamete.</p><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p2" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p2">When two Rr individuals produce the <button aria-haspopup="dialog" class="bio-term" data-term-id="ch17-word-f2-generation" type="button">F2 generation</button>, gametes can combine as RR, Rr, rR or rr. Rr and rR name the same heterozygous genotype. The expected genotype ratio is 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr. With complete dominance, the expected phenotype ratio is 3 round : 1 wrinkled.</p><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p3" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-02-p3">The wrinkled phenotype reappears because an offspring can receive r from both parents. No new r mutation is required to explain that result.</p></div><div class="content-section" data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03" id="ch17-l02-teaching-03"><h2>Expected ratios are probabilities</h2><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p1" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p1">A 3:1 expected ratio is not a promise that every group of four offspring contains exactly three round and one wrinkled. Each offspring is another event. Chance variation is especially noticeable in small samples.</p><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p2" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p2">Mendel's repeated patterns supported a particulate model of inheritance: inherited units remain distinguishable rather than permanently blending. Later chromosome and DNA evidence connected those units with cellular mechanisms.</p><p data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p3" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-teaching-03-p3">When interpreting a cross, identify the parental genotypes, gamete possibilities and allele combinations before applying the phenotype rule. A remembered ratio should be the result of that reasoning, not a substitute for it.</p></div><div class="worked-example" data-canvas-edit-key="ch17-l02-worked" data-canvas-helper-edit-key="ch17-l02-worked" id="ch17-l02-worked"><p class="section-label">Worked example</p><h3>Recover a hidden allele</h3><p>A true-breeding dark plant is crossed with a true-breeding pale plant. All F1 plants are dark; some F2 offspring are pale.</p><ol><li>Under complete dominance, use DD for the true-breeding dark parent and dd for pale.</li><li>The F1 genotype is Dd. The d allele remains present despite the dark phenotype.</li><li>F1 parents can each contribute d in a gamete.</li><li>An F2 dd offspring expresses pale, explaining reappearance without blending or a new mutation.</li></ol></div>
