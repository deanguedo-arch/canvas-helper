<p class="section-label">Worked example</p>
<h2>From the starting cross to the second generation</h2>
<p>Start with the true-breeding seed-shape lines: <strong>RR × rr</strong>. The multiplication-style cross sign means these are the two parents being crossed; it is not an instruction to multiply the letters.</p>
<p>The RR parent contributes R in its gametes. The rr parent contributes r. At fertilisation, one egg and one sperm join, restoring a pair of alleles in the <strong>zygote</strong>, the first cell of the offspring. Every combination available in this cross is therefore Rr. Applying the phenotype rule after combining the alleles gives round F1 seeds. The r allele is still present in each seed's genotype.</p>
<p>Grow the F1 seeds into plants. When an Rr F1 plant self-pollinates, its eggs can carry R or r and its sperm can carry R or r. The same allele-combination reasoning applies to a controlled cross between two Rr F1 plants. The F2 cross is <strong>Rr × Rr</strong>.</p>
<p>Read the small grid from its edges inward. The left edge gives an egg's allele; the top edge gives a sperm's allele. Each interior entry combines those two contributions. The edges represent gamete possibilities, and the interior entries represent offspring genotypes. They are not the same kind of cell.</p>

<p>Follow the lower-right entry first. The egg contributes r and the sperm contributes r, so the offspring is rr and expresses the wrinkled phenotype. Neither allele had to be recreated. Both were available in the F1 parents before they formed gametes.</p>
<p>Now compare the upper-right and lower-left entries. Rr and rR are the same heterozygous genotype; the written order only shows which gamete supplied which allele in this table. The gene in this model is <strong>autosomal</strong>, meaning it is on a chromosome other than a sex chromosome. Here the complete-dominance rule is the same whichever parent supplied R.</p>
<p>With equal R/r gamete probabilities and random fertilisation, the four contribution pairs are equally likely. They group into <strong>1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr</strong> for genotype. RR and Rr both express round seeds, so the phenotype grouping is <strong>3 round : 1 wrinkled</strong>. Grouping by appearance combines two genotype categories; it does not change the allele pairs inside them.</p>
<p>This distinction separates two processes. <strong>Segregation</strong> produces single-allele contributions as homologous chromosomes separate during gamete production. <strong>Random fertilisation</strong> combines one parent's gamete with the other's. Chromosomes aligning in one parent's meiosis do not themselves join that parent's gamete to a gamete from another parent.</p>
