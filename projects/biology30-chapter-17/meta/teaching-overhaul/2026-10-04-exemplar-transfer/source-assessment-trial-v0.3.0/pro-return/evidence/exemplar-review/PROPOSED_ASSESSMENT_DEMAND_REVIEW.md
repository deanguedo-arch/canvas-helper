## 1. Lesson02 writing1: copies, versions and gamete contributions

**Proposed demand:** an Mm parent has one M-bearing and one m-bearing homologue before copying, then two M-bearing sisters and two m-bearing sisters. Evaluate the claim that copying made four allele versions and that each gamete should carry Mm.

**Freshness judgment:** a modest but defensible new mechanism application. The visible lesson already teaches ordinary copying, sister chromatids, preserved R/r identity and one allele per gamete. Those are the intended prerequisites. The new decision is to reconcile the same unchanged allele identities across a copied representation and diagnose two connected errors: counting physical copies as new versions, then carrying the diploid pair into a haploid contribution. Neither the fully worked P/F1/F2 account nor either optional lesson02 model gives that complete response. The task therefore need not reconstruct the whole worked cross or reproduce the reversed process-arrow labels. Do not describe this as an unfamiliar mechanism or a far-transfer assessment: the core explanation is close and deliberately taught.

**Minimum safeguards for final wording/model:**

- State that M and m are two versions of the same autosomal gene, and that the described copying and meiosis are normal, with no mutation changing the versions. This keeps the question on the taught ordinary model.
- Require the two claims to be evaluated separately. A simple “false” or a corrected count without the separation mechanism would not demonstrate the intended reasoning.
- Distinguish two allele versions from four replicated locus copies at the stated stage. The copies are on sister chromatids of two homologues; they are not four different homologous chromosome types.
- Trace homologous separation and subsequent sister separation sufficiently to justify one allele at this locus in a normal gamete. Retaining m is not the same as every gamete receiving both M and m.
- Do not demand that every meiosis in every organism produces four viable gametes, four eggs, or an exact four-offspring ratio. The supplied plant reproductive-life-cycle account deliberately avoids that overstatement.
- Preserve segregation-versus-blending evidence explicitly: copying does not make M and m intermediate versions or remove m. Do not introduce an untaught test-cross or future phenotype-probability calculation merely to make the question look more different.

This preserves the original outcome with a new representational inference. It does not repeat the reciprocal RR/rr parent-origin task or the optional arrow-name repair.

## 2. Lesson05 MC2: combine a newly requested phenotype event

**Proposed demand:** for independent double heterozygotes with complete dominance, find the probability that one offspring expresses the recessive phenotype for at least one of the two traits.

**Freshness judgment:** suitable near transfer at the same level, provided the event is unambiguous. The parental cross and four phenotype-category probabilities are already fully taught by the pea example. The new consequential decision is to select and combine three non-overlapping categories rather than retrieve one familiar3/16 category. Addition of mutually exclusive groups is taught in the lesson, so a three-category sum does not require a new probability technique.

Independent check: the disjoint groups are `aaB_` with3/16, `A_bb` with3/16, and `aabb` with1/16. Their total is7/16. The excluded both-dominant group is9/16, and7/16 +9/16 =1.

**Minimum safeguards:**

- Make “at least one” mean **one or both of the two studied traits in a single offspring**. It must not be mistaken for at least one recessive child in a family or a selected subgroup of offspring.
- Specify ordinary equal segregation, independent assortment, random fertilisation and complete, reliably expressed dominance rules. Do not make a reading of an unlabelled phenotype necessary.
- The prompt must leave the category selection and sum to the learner. It should not supply the three target fractions or enumerate the solution as part of its initial stimulus.
- The full feedback should include the double-recessive category exactly once and distinguish this event from “exactly one recessive phenotype” and from “both recessive phenotypes.” This targets errors in event definition, not just arithmetic.
- A complement check using1−9/16 is valid, but do not require an untaught named complement rule as the only accepted method. The taught category-sum method is sufficient.
- Exactly one MC option must be defensible under the stated conditions. Distractors/options/cue have not yet been reviewed because they are not yet authored.

The existing source practice `ch17-source-OBJ_2131237` asks a single3/16 phenotype category, and the final check `ch17-final-mc-2` asks aabb1/16. The proposed grouped event is neither of those solved demands. Its cross-structure overlap should be disclosed honestly.

## 3. Lesson05 writing1: valid dimensions do not ensure valid headers

**Proposed demand:** AaBB × AaBb has correct first-parent headers AB and aB, but proposed second-parent headers AB, Ab, ab, ab. Diagnose and repair the representation, derive weights and justify each pairing's probability.

**Freshness judgment:** suitable same-level representation repair. It shares four-by-two organisation with the restored tomato, as the outcome requires. Its new inference is that the correct number of spaces does not establish that the represented possibilities are exhaustive and distinct: one allowable type is omitted while a different type is repeated. This is more than relabelling the tomato gamete lists. The repaired optional tomato task is expected to reduce four-by-two to four-by-one after a parental change; the proposed required task instead holds dimensions valid while exposing incorrect coverage within them.

Independent check:

- AaBB → AB and aB, each1/2.
- AaBb → AB, Ab, aB and ab, each1/4.
- Replace the redundant second ab entry with the missing aB entry. Reordering all four correct headers is equally valid.
- Two by four distinct gametes give eight equally likely parental pairings, each(1/2)×(1/4)=1/8, totaling1. A transposed four-by-two table is equivalent if parent ownership is clear.

**Minimum safeguards:**

- Explicitly identify the header convention as one entry per **distinct possible gamete type**, not counts of observed gametes or an arbitrary weighted sampling table. The proposed header lists are then clearly erroneous under that convention.
- State independent assortment and normal segregation/random fertilisation where weights are requested. The physical order of headers carries no probability meaning.
- Require both the missing aB and the duplicate ab diagnosis, derivation from the two allele pairs, and the probability weights. Merely supplying a prettier list should not earn all the evidence criteria.
- Do not say duplicate columns are universally biologically impossible. A deliberately redundant representation can be valid with proper weights. The supplied erroneous table is invalid because it omits an allowed type and misrepresents the possibilities under its distinct-header convention.
- Do not require all offspring genotype/phenotype classes unless the final prompt expressly needs them. That would add workload beyond this representation diagnosis without making it more distinctive.

The existing optional/Frayer items already teach valid one-allele-per-locus gametes, and the tomato teaching explains why duplicate identical chromosome copies do not create new types. Those are prerequisites, not a complete solution to this faulty-coverage case. Final criteria should reward the new coverage diagnosis rather than merely repeat “a4×4 square is unnecessary.”

## 4. Lesson05 writing2: genotype status is a different event

**Proposed demand:** for CcHh × CcHh, evaluate treating9/16 both-dominant phenotypes as the probability of homozygosity at both loci; derive the correct probability and eligible genotype classes.

**Freshness judgment:** suitable same-level application. The cross is structurally the taught pea cross. The new consequential classification is to regroup by homozygosity at each locus, which cuts across the phenotype categories. It is not the restored tomato's red/short event with changed letters. Native01/03 distinguish genotype from phenotype and native05 provides both the grid and the appropriate addition/product methods.

Independent check:

- At C, CC and cc each have probability1/4, so homozygosity at this locus has probability1/2.
- At H, HH and hh similarly total1/2.
- Independence gives(1/2)×(1/2)=1/4.
- The eligible four genotypes are CCHH, CChh, ccHH and cchh, each1/16; their total is4/16=1/4.
- Both dominant phenotypes has probability9/16 under complete dominance, but includes CCHh, CcHH and CcHh, which are heterozygous at one or both loci. Homozygous recessive genotypes can still satisfy homozygosity. The events are therefore different.

**Minimum safeguards:**

- Say homozygous **at each of the two loci**. It does not mean two different genes must have the same letter, and it does not mean homozygous dominant only.
- State the ordinary independent two-locus model. If the prompt uses the9/16 phenotype comparison, specify complete dominance for C/c and H/h, without inventing a simplistic human trait.
- Require the genotype-status classification plus its justified probability. A bare1/4 answer is inadequate because other taught events happen to share that fraction.
- The teacher model may show both the four genotype contributions and the independent per-locus sum/product. Accept either complete equivalent method unless the final prompt explicitly asks for both; avoid turning the marking guide into an unnecessary double-calculation requirement.
- Do not accidentally convert it into “homozygous at at least one locus,” whose probability would be3/4. The model and criteria must match the final exact event.

The full bank contains broad homozygous/heterozygous definitions, full F2 genotype enumeration in optional textbook practice, and several different1/4 questions. It does not contain the complete proposed both-loci homozygosity-versus9/16 classification and solution in the inspected current records. A matching numerical answer alone is not duplication.

## Overlap among the four new proposals

The two quantitative lesson05 items use a double-heterozygote cross but demand different classifications. MC2 aggregates phenotype categories for one-or-both recessive expression; writing2 distinguishes genotype status from phenotype and sums homozygous classes. Neither complete answer supplies the other. Writing1 instead audits whether a gamete representation is exhaustive and correctly weighted. Lesson02 distinguishes copying from allele identity and a diploid pair from haploid contribution. These are four distinct demands; there is no requirement to invent a fifth case, add an advanced method, or impose a target word count.

This design remains deliberately close to the taught method. It should be described as fresh **taught application**, not as assessment independent of exposure to Mendelian reasoning or to the source cross types. General conceptual overlap is necessary for fairness. The current screening found no need to expand the authorized replacement scope beyond the four named IDs.
