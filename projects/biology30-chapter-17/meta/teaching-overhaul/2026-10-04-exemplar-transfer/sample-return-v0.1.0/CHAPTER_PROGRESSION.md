## Lesson02 — from an observation to its mechanism

The opening asks how a recessive phenotype can return if its allele did not blend away. It activates genotype/phenotype and complete dominance, specifies what individual seed is being scored, then explains true-breeding history, controlled parentage and P/F1/F2 relationships. Knowing the contributors makes the next question meaningful: what happens to each allele between parent and gamete?

The chromosome account defines the physical units before one worked copy/separation trace follows them. Copying preserves R or r; the first division separates homologues; the second separates sisters. The table deliberately follows chromosome reduction rather than every stage of a plant reproductive life cycle. The guided task uses that visible mechanism to locate the first error in a described drawing and repair the movement, not just the final labels.

Fertilisation then restores a pair. The worked biological reasoning returns to the known parents, follows egg and sperm contributions, distinguishes the same heterozygote written in either order, and applies the phenotype rule after genotype grouping. Probability is defined before the historical5474/1850 count comparison explains expectation without a compulsory offspring schedule. This minimum explanation is needed by the unchanged native02 tasks; systematic grids and expected-count calculations can remain in03.

The independent task changes the inference. It compares a missing pollen-donor record with an incorrect allele-combination rule despite known gametes, then asks whether extra offspring counting fixes either. Its answer draws on already visible parentage, fertilisation and sample-variation teaching. It is not another generation-label drill or an altered version of the protected short-run question. Complete hints and diagnostic feedback are present.

## Lesson05 — from two choices to a justified joint prediction

The motivating question asks how four genotype possibilities can make only two visibly different groups. After distinguishing AaBb from a one-allele-per-locus gamete, the lesson explains separate chromosome-pair orientations. It then reads the exact native branch figure as possible gamete combinations, distinguishing allele symbols from location labels and noting that a tree is not a schedule for one meiosis.

The next block defines events and joint probabilities, separates assortment within a parent from random fertilisation between parents, and teaches addition for mutually exclusive genotype categories before asking the learner to group them. It does not presume a general addition or conditional procedure from native03.

One complete worked fictional cross, HhSS × hhSs, defines hooked/straight pods and speckled/plain petals as traits of the same scored offspring. It derives both parental gamete lists, sizes the2×2 table from those lists, accounts for each cell and its probability, groups four genotypes into two phenotype categories, checks the same event by the single-locus product method and converts the phenotype probabilities to expected counts among120 offspring. Each step changes what is being inferred; the five subheadings belong to one connected example.

The guided change replaces only HhSS with Hhss. The learner must retain the H-locus calculation and follow the changed S contribution into four phenotype groups. The later general dihybrid paragraph derives9:3:3:1 from the four product weights of two3:1 expectations and limits that ratio to its parental genotypes and assumptions. It does not introduce a new solved clone of the reserved AaBb target.

The independent task supplies exact gamete-model probabilities0.35/0.15/0.15/0.35.
