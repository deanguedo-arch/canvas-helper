## What each passage actually does

**CH11-01: A cold hand gives us a starting point.** A concrete bodily change establishes the chapter question, then the information-flow visual distinguishes jobs and explains why input precedes processing and output.

**CH11-02: How can one cell reach a distant muscle?.** A distance problem makes morphology necessary; figure reading establishes one continuous axon, receiving branches and sending terminals before structure–function inference.

**CH11-02: Use structure to explain function.** Converts a labelled shape into a causal explanation: reception surface, integration, distance and distribution each earn a distinct step.

**CH11-02: Explain a diagram error.** Tests representation repair: separate gaps in insulation from breaks in the continuous conducting cell, rather than reciting parts.

**CH11-02: Compare two different failures.** Localizes two distinct failure sites and tests whether an unrelated proposed repair restores the missing function.

**CH11-06: Follow release, diffusion, binding, and clearance.** Builds a causal sequence across two membranes, ties figure symbols to actual events, and makes new firing conditional rather than automatic.

**CH11-06: Worked model: include inhibition in the calculation.** Combines signed contributions, applies a net change to a starting voltage, compares with threshold, then changes inhibition while limiting what the simplified model claims.

**CH11-06: Worked example: an impulse arrives but no message is released.** Holds conduction success fixed, locates the blocked calcium-trigger step and follows only its consequences while limiting the inference to the affected terminal.

The distinctness is clearest in the progression within lesson02. Its shape explanation asks **what a structure makes possible**; the diagram error asks **whether a representation depicts a continuous cell and separate covering correctly**; the two-failure task asks **where a mechanism is interrupted and whether the proposed repair addresses that interruption**. These tasks share concepts but do not merely ask for the same answer three times. There is no basis for copying their count into a genetics lesson.

Lesson06 likewise separates a presynaptic release failure, a postsynaptic receiving failure, arithmetic about the combined effect and threshold, and a timing experiment. The release example keeps an explicitly successful event intact before following the blocked step. The additive worked model explains the signs and starting value, makes a threshold decision, changes one condition and limits the mathematical model. Its independent timing task requires explaining three observations, so the inference differs from the numerical example.

The diagram paragraphs are adjacent to the image or mechanism they explain. They tell the student where to look and what an arrow, gap, boundary, colour or curve means, then warn against a specific misleading reading. The neuron example explicitly preserves the continuous axon under insulation; the synapse example distinguishes messenger molecules from vesicles and a possible next action potential from a guaranteed one. A genetics visual should earn the same explanatory role without copying these metaphors.

The hints and models are intentionally revealed, while the terms, causal sequence and worked reasoning required for the attempt remain visible. A hint may focus attention, but it cannot be the first place an essential operation is taught. The quantitative passage does not delegate the reason for adding signed contributions or comparing with a threshold to hidden feedback.

None of these documents prescribes three new practice items or a fixed number of worked examples per lesson.

## Bounded review protocol for the forthcoming drafts

No new calibration learner draft has yet been supplied to this reviewer, and none is approved here. When the complete actual drafts arrive, independently read their full visible path and every hint/model/feedback at exact hashes, then check these specific questions:

- **Adjacency and causal continuity:** Does the sentence before each visual, method or task establish what the student needs next? Can the learner explain why one step permits the next, including gamete formation before combinations?
- **First-use and scope:** Are probability/expectation in02, and any subgroup denominator in05, explained before they are demanded? Are genotype, phenotype, notation and assumptions explicit without re-teaching irrelevant earlier material?
- **Example necessity:** What unique inference does each example or contrast add? If two do the same job with cosmetic changes, flag the actual duplicate passages; do not fill a count quota by inventing another item.
- **Independent demand:** Does the changed task require transfer of taught reasoning rather than an untaught procedure, an exact required answer, or the worked answer with renamed objects? Keep necessary conceptual overlap distinct from answer rehearsal.
- **Visible teaching versus optional feedback:** Could a first attempt be reasoned from visible teaching alone? Flag any essential new mechanism or calculation operation that appears only after revealing the answer. Complete explanatory feedback must still be retained.
- **Quantitative interpretation:** Are givens and symbols defined, the method justified, consequential steps explained, and genotype/phenotype groupings and denominators distinguished? Are expectations separated from actual counts and assumptions used where they matter?
- **Presentation drift:** Check actual native heading/class/vocabulary conventions, diagram references and ordering. No CH11 runtime, response-field/save convention, unearned resource claim or repeated decorative box should be transplanted.
- **Review boundary:** Report exact passage/hash, consequence and repair needed; distinguish a verified defect from a reasonable design choice. Assistant agreement, author self-review and teacher acceptance remain separate. No runtime/rendering or approval result will be inferred from this reading.
