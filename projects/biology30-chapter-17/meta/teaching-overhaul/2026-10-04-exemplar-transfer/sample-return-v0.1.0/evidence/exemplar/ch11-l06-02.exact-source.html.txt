<h3>Worked model: include inhibition in the calculation</h3><p>Use a simplified additive model at the trigger region. Resting voltage is −70 mV, threshold is −55 mV, and simultaneous contributions are +8 mV, +9 mV, and −5 mV.</p><ol class="pro-steps"><li><strong>Add all contributions.</strong> +8 + 9 − 5 = +12 mV. Leaving out the inhibitory term would overestimate the net depolarization.</li><li><strong>Apply the net change to the starting value.</strong> −70 + 12 = −58 mV.</li><li><strong>Compare with threshold.</strong> −58 is still below −55, so no action potential is initiated in this model.</li><li><strong>Test the changed condition.</strong> Removing the −5 mV input would give a predicted depolarization to −53 mV, which crosses threshold and triggers an action potential. −53 mV is the model's threshold-crossing prediction, not the voltage at which the neuron would remain afterward.</li></ol><p>Real synapses interact in more complex ways than simple addition. The calculation is a model for reasoning about combined effects, timing, and threshold; it is not a universal equation for a real neuron.</p>
