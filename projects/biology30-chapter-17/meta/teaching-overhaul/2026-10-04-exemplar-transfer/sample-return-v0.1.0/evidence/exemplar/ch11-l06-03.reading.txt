Worked example: an impulse arrives but no message is released
Suppose the action potential reaches a terminal normally, but its voltage-gated calcium channels cannot open. Predict what happens next.
Keep the successful event. Conduction along the axon occurred; there is no evidence of a failure there.
Locate the blocked event. Without the normal Ca²⁺ entry, the usual trigger for vesicle fusion is missing.
Follow the consequence across the gap. Much less, or no evoked, transmitter is released, so the postsynaptic receptors do not receive the usual chemical signal.
Limit the conclusion. The receiving cell might still respond to a different input. This result concerns communication through the affected terminal.
