Sex-linked pedigrees and testing a model

How can you test an inheritance explanation against an entire family?

Learning goal

Use chromosome transmission to evaluate X-linked models and distinguish consistency from proof in small pedigrees.

Before you begin

In the typical XX/XY model, fathers pass X to daughters and Y to sons. A small family may not show every possible outcome.

Trace the chromosome, not only the shading pattern

For inheritance, follow which parent supplies X to each offspring. Sons receive their X from their mother in the typical XX/XY model. Daughters receive one X from each parent. Fathers do not directly pass an X-linked to sons.

In a fully penetrant model, an affected son has XbY. His Xb came from his mother. An unaffected mother must therefore be XBXb under the ordinary no-new-mutation assumptions. An affected daughter needs Xb from both parents, which constrains the father's .

Compare dominant and recessive X-linked patterns

An affected father in an model passes the affected X to every daughter. With an unaffected mother, sons receive the father's Y and the mother's unaffected X, so they are unaffected under that model.

For X-linked recessive inheritance, an affected father with a unaffected mother has daughters and unaffected sons. The same chromosome route produces a different expressed pattern because the differs.

More affected males can be a clue to an X-linked recessive model, but sex counts alone are not decisive. A small autosomal family can also show an uneven distribution by chance.

Look for contradictions and retain limits

A strong analysis tests every informative relationship. A model may fit one branch but fail elsewhere. Use “consistent with” when the evidence supports a model without uniquely proving it.

State assumptions: complete , ordinary chromosome transmission, accurate parentage and no newly arising mutations unless specified. These simplify the question and make deductions possible. Real clinical pedigrees can require more evidence.

When calculating the risk to a future child, establish the relevant parents' genotypes first and read the requested subgroup. The pedigree does not remove the distinction between among sons and probability among all children.

Worked example

Test a proposed X-linked recessive model

A fully expressed recessive trait appears in a daughter whose father is unaffected. Assume ordinary XX/XY transmission and no new mutation.

  1. An affected daughter would need XbXb in this model.
  2. Her father must supply one of those Xb chromosomes.
  3. An unaffected father is XBY and cannot supply Xb under the assumptions.
  4. The proposed model is inconsistent with this relationship; another explanation or changed assumptions would be needed.
