One method, several inheritance models

Begin with the gene or genes, the allele relationships and parental genotypes. Segregation determines the allele contribution of gametes; the phenotype model determines which offspring combinations express the trait. Complete dominance, incomplete dominance, codominance and X-linkage change how outcomes are interpreted.

A two-locus product calculation requires independence. A linked model instead uses phase and recombinant frequencies. A pedigree adds constraints from relatives but may leave more than one genotype or inheritance model possible.

Show the denominator and the limit

Probability among all children differs from probability among sons or among unaffected siblings. Count the requested subgroup explicitly. Multiply independent required events, and convert a probability to an expected count only after establishing the correct cross.

Expected ratios are not guarantees for small samples. Genetic associations are not always deterministic predictions. A complete answer gives both the result and the conditions under which it follows.

Worked example

Compare probability and linkage evidence

A two-locus test cross has four phenotype classes but parental classes are much larger. A student applies 1:1:1:1 without examining the counts.

  1. The 1:1:1:1 prediction assumes independent assortment.
  2. Identify the parental chromosome arrangement and classify both recombinant groups.
  3. Compare observed counts with the independent expectation, considering sampling.
  4. Calculate recombination frequency from both recombinant groups over the whole sample.
  5. Use a justified model rather than forcing every cross into a memorized ratio.

Written explanation 2

Maximum 3200 characters. Explain the biology in your own words.

Compare after saving your own response

Under independence a heterozygote test cross can yield four equal classes. Linked loci can produce more parental than recombinant combinations. Identify phase, sum both recombinant classes, divide by all scored offspring and multiply by 100%. For short intervals this approximates map distance, with limitations from multiple crossovers and sampling.
