Genetic testing, counselling and decisions

What can genetic information tell a person—and what can it leave uncertain?

Learning goal

Distinguish a genetic test result from a guaranteed phenotype, and identify consent, uncertainty and privacy considerations.

Before you begin

Genotype does not determine every complex phenotype on its own. Pedigree conclusions rely on assumptions and available evidence.

Read the scope of the test

A genetic test investigates particular variants, regions or markers. A result must be interpreted within that scope. A negative result for one variant does not rule out every genetic cause of a trait or condition.

Some results are strongly predictive under a clear inheritance model. Others indicate an increased or remain uncertain. , other genes, environment and the quality of the evidence can influence what a result means for an individual.

Information can affect more than one person

A result can have implications for relatives because family members share some genetic information. A person may wish to know a result, decline it or discuss its meaning before deciding how to use it. helps explain inheritance, limitations and possible implications.

Consent should address what is tested, what results may be returned and how samples or data may be used. Privacy and access to information are relevant concerns. This chapter examines those issues without giving legal advice or assuming one personal choice is appropriate for everyone.

A scientific explanation should separate observed data, estimated risk and value-based decisions. An association does not itself prescribe how a person should act.

Evaluate a claim using evidence and uncertainty

Suppose a hypothetical test reports a variant associated with a higher risk of a condition. Ask which population was studied, how large the increase is, what other factors matter and whether the result has been validated for the proposed use.

A claim that one result predicts every future feature of a person's health goes beyond the evidence. Conversely, uncertainty does not mean the information has no value. The useful task is to identify what the result can support and what additional evidence or counselling is needed.

Classroom case studies should not require students to reveal their own family history or test information. Use the supplied fictional scenarios for analysis.

Worked example

Interpret a limited negative result

A fictional test detects none of three specified variants associated with one condition.

  1. The result establishes that those tested variants were not detected under the test’s conditions.
  2. It does not rule out every untested variant, every different genetic condition or every environmental cause.
  3. Interpretation requires the test’s scope and limitations.
  4. A personal decision should be informed by appropriate counselling rather than a blanket classroom rule.
