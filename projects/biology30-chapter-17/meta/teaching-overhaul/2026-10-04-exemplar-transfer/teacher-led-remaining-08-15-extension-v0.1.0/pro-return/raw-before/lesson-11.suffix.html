Read the explanation

An affected father in an X-linked dominant model passes the affected X to every daughter. With an unaffected mother, sons receive the father's Y and the mother's unaffected X, so they are unaffected under that model. For X-linked recessive inheritance, an affected father with a homozygous unaffected mother has carrier daughters and unaffected sons. The same chromosome route produces a different expressed pattern because the heterozygote phenotype differs. More affected males can be a clue to an X-linked recessive model, but sex counts alone are not decisive. A small autosomal family can also show an uneven distribution by chance.
