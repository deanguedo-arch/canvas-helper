Traits are determined by specific genes, or segments of DNA. Different variations of genes are known as alleles. In most cases, one allele is dominant over the other, influencing the way such genes are expressed…

➢The dominant allele (represented by capital letter) is always expressed when present

➢A recessive allele (represented by lowercase letter) is only expressed in the absence of the dominant allele.

The allele that encodes brown eyes (B), for example, is dominant over the recessive allele that encodes blue eyes (b).

GENES, ALLELES AND TRAITS

 

 

 

 

➢This means that although we receive genes from both parents, our characteristics are NOT determined by the “blending” of our parents traits. It is determined by the dominant vs. recessive interaction of the alleles we receive.

➢Phenotype refers to the outward expression of traits. For example, having brown eyes or having blue eyes.

➢Genotype on the other hand refers to the combination of alleles an individual has for a given trait.

➢For example, your teacher’s genotype for eye color is ‘bb’. Since she only has recessive alleles, this means that her phenotype is ‘having blue eyes’

 

PHENOTYPE VS. GENOTYPE

 

Note: A phenotype isn’t always a visible trait like color. It could be a behaviour or a physiological trait, such as ‘able to digest lactose’ or ‘not able to digest lactose.

The idea of dominant and recessive alleles was first described by Gregor Mendel…

oHe conducted test crosses with peas of different phenotypes and realized that for each trait, offspring displayed either the maternal or paternal phenotype rather than a blending of the two.

oE.g. a true-breeding (i.e. homozygous) plant with purple flowers crossed with a true-breeding (i.e. homozygous) plant with white flowers always produced offspring with purple flowers, not light purple as previously suggested by the blending theory of inheritance

oThus, he concluded that purple flowers were dominant to white

The Law of Segregation:

All individuals have two copies of each gene. These copies segregate randomly during gamete formation, and each gamete receives one copy of every gene.

The Law of Segregation forms the basis of modern theories of inheritance.

 

 

 

 

Furthermore, Mendel observed that …

 

…when true-breeding parents with contrasting traits are crossed together, only one form of the trait appears in the next generation.

 

This is called the Law of Dominance.

SEX-LINKED TRAITS

oGenes that are located on the X or Y chromosome display different inheritance patterns than genes located on autosomes (chromosomes 1-22)

oThis is because males do not receive an X-chromosome from their father, and females must inherit their father’s single X-chromosome

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

Colour blindness is a recessive trait carried on the x-chromosome

 

 

 

X-linked traits were discovered by Thomas Hunt Morgan through experiments with fruit flies (Drosophila melanogaster).

●He observed that white-eyed mutations appeared only in male flies when a red-eyed female was crossed with a white-eyed male.

Morgan proposed that the gene for eye color was on the X chromosome. Males (XY) inherit this trait directly from their mother, while females (XX) can carry the mutation without expressing it if they have one normal copy. This was the first evidence linking genes to specific chromosomes, shaping our understanding of sex-linked inheritance.

 

 

 

Example: Colour Blindness

(Mode of inheritance: X-linked recessive)

oTo have regular vision, a dominant allele must be present. So,

○in females, two recessive alleles are required to be colour blind

○In males, there is no second X-chromosome to carry a possible dominant allele; thus, only one recessive allele is required be colour blind. This is why colour blindness is more common in males.

EXTRA BUT FUN AND RELEVANT!

 

BARR BODIES

Although females carry two X-chromosomes in each of their cells, only one of these chromosomes is active; the other one condensed into what is called a Barr Body.

 

 

 

 

 

 

The particular X chromosome that is inactivated in each cell is completely random. This mode of inheritance accounts for the colour patterns seen in tortoiseshell and calico cats. These are female cats that are heterozygous for the X-linked color traits. Each cell randomly inactivates an X chromosome and will express a random available color.  Therefore only female cats can have this random coloring pattern!

To read more: https://www.bio.miami.edu/dana/dox/calico.html

 

 

 

 

 

 

Note: We focused on examples on the X-chromosome (i.e. X-linked traits) mainly. There are Y-linked traits as well, but because only males have/need the Y-chromosome, there are a lot less genes on this and these are typically only relevant to male development and functioning. This is also why the Y-chromosome is much smaller than the X-chromosome.

So we often use sex-linked and X-linked interchangeable, because most sex-linked traits are X-linked.

 

 

 

 

MULTIPLE ALLELES

Often, more than two alleles may exist for a particular trait (i.e. although an organism only inherits two alleles for each gene, many different alleles can exist within the population)

 

In humans, for example, a single gene determines blood type. However, three different alleles exist:

 

IA, IB, and i

 

Note: the gene is designated “I” or “i” depending on the presence of an antigen. IA thus indicates the presence of an A antigen on red blood cells, and IB indicates the presence of a B antigen. The absence of an antigen is marked by “i”

 

 

 

 

 

Different combinations of these three alleles produce four different phenotypes, which are commonly referred to as A, B, AB, or O.

Alleles for blood type:

IA, IB, and i

A = IAIA or IAi

B = IBIB or IBi

AB = IAIB

O = ii

Alleles IA and IB are codominant, but both are dominant over the recessive allele i.

 

Order of Dominance: IA = IB > i

TOPIC 5: GENE LINKAGE & MAPPING

 

 

 

GENE LINKAGE

Typically, the inheritance of a particular trait is thought to follow Mendel’s law of independent assortment, allowing us to accurately predict the phenotypic and genotypic ratios of offspring. Occasionally, however, genes may be observed as “breaking” this rule…

➢Genes that are located close together on the same chromosome are called linked genes.

➢Alleles for these genes tend to segregate together during meiosis unless they are separated by crossing over.

○Crossing over occurs when two homologous chromosomes exchange genetic material during meiosis I. The closer together two genes are on a chromosome, the less likely their alleles will be separated by crossing over

 

 

 

Unlinked genes found on different chromosomes will assort independently and yield all possible allele combination in a 1:1:1:1 ratio.

 

Ab:AB:ab:aB

1 : 1 : 1 : 1

Linked genes found on the same chromosomes will not assort independently and are only expected to yield the original/parental combinations:

 

Ab:AB:ab:aB

0 : 1 : 1 : 0

Linked genes found on the same chromosomes will not assort independently, however crossing over may occur between then, yielding some recombinants

 

Ab:AB:ab:aB

some : most : some : most

 

The farther apart the genes are, the greater the likelihood of crossing over and greater the number of recombinants.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

Linkage Map for the Human X Chromosome. This linkage map shows the locations of several genes on the X chromosome. Some of the genes code for normal proteins. Others code for proteins that are associated with genetic disorders.

➢Which pair of genes would you expect to have a lower frequency of crossing over: the genes that code for hemophilia A and G6PD deficiency, or the genes that code for Protan and Xm?

 

Hemophilia A and G6PD would have a lower crossing over frequency because they are closer together. There’s a lower chance that the chiasma (break in the chromosome where crossing over occurs) will occur between these two genes.

 

 

 

 

However, because the genes for hair and eye colour are on the same chromosome, they will undergo crossing over less often, causing them to produce fewer recombinant gametes…  

 

 

Non-linked genes = 50% parental gametes,

  50% recombinants

 

Linked genes = less than 50% recombinants

The genes for hair and eye colour are therefore said to be linked.  

 

 

 

oFor example, genes H and d will cross over less often than genes

   B and H, thus producing fewer  

   recombinant gametes

oBased on the number of recombinant gametes for each set of genes, then, we can determine their distance relative to one another on a chromosome (gene mapping)

 

 

Genes that are closer together on the same chromosome will cross over less frequently, thus producing fewer recombinant gametes. These genes are more closely linked than genes that are further apart on a chromosome…  

 

 

 

In Summary...

oGenes are more likely to be linked if they are found on the same chromosome

oWe know that two genes are linked if we see gametes with more parental genotypes than recombinant genotypes (because crossing over occurs less frequently when genes are on the same chromosome)

oWe also know that two genes are linked if we see a different ratio of parental & recombinant offspring than predicted

 

 

 

 

Gene Mapping

●The relative distance between two genes on the same chromosome may be determined using a method known as gene mapping.

 

●Recall that in non-linked (“normal”) genes, 50% of gametes will be parental, and 50% will be recombinant.

 

●In linked genes, less than 50% of gametes will be recombinant due to reduced frequency of crossing over.

 

●Genes that are closer together cross over less frequently. So, the lower the recombination frequency, the closer together they are.

●For example, genes H and d will cross over less than genes B and H, producing fewer recombinant gametes.

 

 

 

Gene Mapping

Example:

In smurfs, white hair (H) is dominant to blonde hair (h), and large noses (N) are dominant to small noses (n).

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

A smurf heterozygous for both traits (HhNn) is crossed with a blonde haired, small nosed smurf (hhnn). If these two genes are NOT linked, what would we expect the phenotypic ratio of offspring to be?  

 

 

 

Although this cross should yield a 1:1:1:1 phenotypic ratio, you observe the following…

 

 

o6 offspring with white hair and large noses

o2 offspring with white hair and small noses

o2 offspring with blonde hair and large noses

o6 offspring with blonde hair and small noses

 

 

 

 

  6 offspring with white hair and large noses (HhNn)

  2 offspring with white hair and small noses (Hhnn)

  2 offspring with blonde hair and large noses (hhNn)

o6 offspring with blonde hair and small noses (hhnn)

Recall that the original cross was

HhNn x hhnn…

 

Because there are more parental genotypes (HhNn and hhnn) than recombinants (Hhnn and hhNn) compared to the predicted ratio, we can conclude that the genes for hair colour and nose size are likely linked (i.e. they are on the same chromosome)

 

But how far apart are they?

 

 

 

 

With a recombination frequency of 25%, we may conclude that the genes for hair colour and nose size in smurfs is 25 map units apart:

 

 

 

➢A map unit (mu), also known as a centiMorgan (cM), is a unit of measure for gene linkage. It describes how closely together are two gene loci (plural of locus) are located.

○A cM is about 1 million base pairs long, but we don’t want to think of it in terms of an actual physical distance, but rather as a measure of the recombination frequency and therefore how close together those two genes are, relatively speaking.

○For example, if hair colour and nose size are 25 map units apart, it means that 25% of the time they will cross over and create recombinants, as we have seen in this example

 

‘Map What’??

Named in honour of Thomas Morgan

o1 Eenn (brown eyes, short nose)

What is the position of the gene that encodes eye colour relative to the genes encoding nose size and hair colour?

 

 

 

 

The genes for eye colour and nose size are 12.5 map units apart, and we already know that the genes for nose size and hair colour are 25 map units apart. So…
