## 2 What a student should experience

A lesson feels like an experienced Alberta Biology 30 teacher helping a student understand a real question. It does not feel like a collection of definitions, an audit report, or instructions about how the course was assembled.

Start with a concrete phenomenon or problem. Activate the particular prerequisite needed to reason about it. Build the explanation in an order the student can follow. Introduce vocabulary when its meaning becomes necessary. Show what happens, why it happens and how one stage produces the next. End by answering the opening question and connecting it to the next lesson.

The story is this explanatory thread. It does not require a fictional character, a repeated anecdote, a forced hook in every paragraph, or one scenario stretched across the entire chapter.

An essential concept must be taught in the lesson itself. Vocabulary popups, textbook reading and video support the explanation; they must not contain the only explanation needed to attempt the work. Preserve current approved reading directions while reconciling older instructions with this requirement.

## 3 Language and depth

Use clear, respectful high-school classroom language while retaining verified Biology 30 scope and scientific vocabulary. Explain a new term at its first meaningful use before demanding it in practice. Prefer concrete subjects and explicit causal connections to phrases such as "plays an important role" or "is involved in."

Chunk by instructional purpose, not by a total word quota. A change of mechanism, representation or reasoning step can justify a new heading. Later lessons must not become thinner because a response is running out of space. Continue in another complete file or batch instead.

Do not equate a low reading score with good teaching. Read the actual lesson as a first-time learner: can the student tell what changed, why it changed, what the figure means and what they should do next? Preserve existing pinned course-standard requirements during integration; this project note does not silently change their configuration.

## 4 The teaching sequence

Use these functions where the concept needs them. They are not compulsory repeated headings or decorative boxes.

1. Establish the question and the needed starting knowledge.
2. Explain the mechanism or relationship in manageable connected chunks.
3. Use a purposeful visual and explicitly teach the student how to read it.
4. Model a complete solution, including the reason for each important step.
5. Contrast changed conditions or a plausible mistake when that clarifies the principle.
6. Give a guided attempt with a useful hint and an explained solution.
7. Give a fresh independent application with explanatory feedback or clear marking criteria.
8. Return to the question, check the conclusion and connect forward.

Not every minor fact requires its own diagram and exercise. Every substantive learning demand does need enough teaching and practice to make the independent task fair.

## 5 Concrete Chapter 11 anchors

These are observed examples of the desired teaching moves, not blanket scientific or teacher acceptance of the imported chapter.

- **Lesson 01:** the cold-hand example motivates detection, processing and response. The lesson explains the jobs in order, uses the diagram to follow the message and returns to homeostasis. Keep the reasoning, not just the memorable situation.
- **Lesson 02:** neuron shape is taught through the problem of sending information to a distant muscle. Students are shown how to read the parts, continuous axon and direction of travel before reasoning about structure and function. A terminal-release problem and a myelin problem are meaningfully different applications.
- **Lesson 03:** withdrawal before deliberate awareness motivates the pathway. The spinal route and information sent toward the brain are distinguished. Preserve the difference between a withdrawal reflex and deliberately avoiding a ball; do not give away the exact required response as the worked example.
- **Lesson 04:** the change from −70 to −60 is interpreted using a stated reference and meaningful arithmetic, not merely named. Explanations distinguish gradients, permeability and pump roles, and immediate effects from later effects.
- **Lesson 06:** the electrical to chemical to electrical transition is explained step by step. Blocking release and blocking a receiving receptor illustrate different failure points. Summation examples interpret the numbers biologically rather than stopping at a calculation.
- **Lesson 11:** measurement, interpretation and uncertainty are separated. Students use supplied data to make a limited claim and consider alternatives, rather than treating a bright image as proof of a broad causal claim.

For future chapters, copy these instructional functions, not the nervous-system scenario or a fixed number of paragraphs.

## 6 Worked examples and feedback

A worked example states the problem, identifies relevant information, selects and explains the method, carries out each meaningful step, checks the result and interprets the biological conclusion. Merely putting an answer below a scenario is insufficient.

Guided practice removes some support. Independent practice changes a meaningful condition, representation or inference; it is not the model answer with a different name or number. Teach every concept or procedure needed for that task first.

Feedback explains why a conclusion follows, where a plausible mistake breaks the reasoning and how the student can recover. Open writing uses a model answer and criteria; do not pretend it has reliable automatic grading. Keep solutions behind the existing intentional reveal pattern where appropriate.

The candidate's 27 new formative writing fields are marked unsaved and ungraded. That is an observed limitation, not a decision to add a new save system. Decide explicitly whether these remain unsaved practice or need separately scoped native saving. They must not silently become required checks or completion gates.

## 7 Visuals and media

Use an image to answer a teaching question. Tell the learner where to look, what an arrow or boundary represents, what changes and what conclusion is justified. A labelled image without that guidance is not sufficient teaching.

Reuse approved artwork and locked labeling mappings. For an essential new or revised image, specify its ID, placement, structures, labels, arrows, legend, scientific constraints, caption, accessible description and source locator in the developer appendix. Mark it approved existing, needs revision, or new asset needed. A specification or generation prompt is not a finished image.

Video needs a specific purpose, a viewing question and a follow-up connection. Verify footage and captions before claiming instructional suitability; successful loading is not playback proof. Supply an equivalent explanation in the lesson when a clip is blocked. Do not invent timestamps, URLs or an existing recording.

## 8 Different biology needs different modelling

Structure and function require position, relationships, features and consequences. Physiological mechanisms require starting conditions, signals, changes and outcomes. Genetics and population calculations require defined symbols, assumptions, a justified method, steps, checks and biological interpretation. Evidence tasks require the actual data or figure, observation versus inference, controls where relevant and limitations.

The calibration is Chapter 19 `lesson-04`, Calculating expected genotype frequencies, with lessons 01–03 as prerequisite context. Its pass must demonstrate those quantitative functions. It must not simply transplant Chapter 11's narrative headings into a calculation lesson.
