Reading autosomal pedigrees

How do family relationships constrain possible genotypes?

Learning goal

Read pedigree symbols and generations, infer autosomal genotypes under a stated model, and preserve unresolved alternatives.

Before you begin

An affected phenotype means different genotypes in dominant and recessive models. Define the model before assigning letters.

Key terms

Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

A diagram tracing a specified trait through family relationships across generations.
A model in which a trait is expressed when two recessive alleles occur at an autosomal locus.
A model in which one dominant allele at an autosomal locus is sufficient for expression under the stated assumptions.
A level of descent in a family diagram, commonly labelled with a Roman numeral.

Read the diagram before solving it

A uses symbols and connecting lines to represent family relationships and a specified trait. Conventional squares and circles often represent male and female individuals in the model; shading indicates expression of the trait. A key should explain any additional symbols.

Roman numerals identify generations and ordinary numbers identify individuals within a . A label such as II-3 refers to the third numbered individual in generation II. Follow the parent–offspring line carefully; neighbouring symbols are not automatically siblings or partners.

Use required allele contributions

For an model, affected individuals are aa. Two unaffected parents with an affected child must each contribute a. If both are unaffected, each is therefore Aa under the simple fully penetrant model.

For an model, unaffected individuals are dd. An affected parent with an unaffected child must be able to contribute d, so that affected parent is Dd rather than DD. The child's constrains the parent's possible alleles.

These deductions use the stated model, accurate relationships and complete expression. New mutations, reduced penetrance or other complexities can change the interpretation; do not add them silently to a simple problem.

Leave uncertainty where it belongs

An unaffected sibling of an aa child from Aa × Aa can be AA or Aa. Excluding aa leaves three equally likely genotype combinations: AA, Aa and aA. Therefore the that an unaffected sibling is a is 2/3, not 1/2, under this cross.

Do not assign AA simply because a person is unshaded. Write the allowed alternatives unless other evidence resolves them. The distinction between “must be” and “could be” is important.

A pedigree can make some genotypes certain within a model while leaving others unresolved. A good explanation identifies the relative whose contribution provides the evidence.

Worked example

Infer both parents from a recessive child

Two unaffected parents have an affected child in a fully penetrant autosomal recessive model.

  1. The affected child is aa and needs a from each parent.
  2. Each unaffected parent must also carry A.
  3. Therefore both parents are Aa.
  4. An unaffected sibling may be AA or Aa; do not automatically mark that sibling as a carrier.
  5. Conditional on being unaffected, the carrier probability is 2/3 for this parental cross.
