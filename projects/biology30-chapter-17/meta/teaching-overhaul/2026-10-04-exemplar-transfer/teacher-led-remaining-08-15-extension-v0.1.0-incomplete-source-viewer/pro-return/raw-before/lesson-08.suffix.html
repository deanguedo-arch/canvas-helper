Read the explanation

Consider XBXb × XBY. The four equally likely combinations are XBXB, XBXb, XBY and XbY. One of four children is expected to be affected. Among sons only, one of two is expected to be affected. The probability of “a son who is affected” is 1/4 among all children. The probability that “the next son is affected” is 1/2 conditional on being a son. The wording changes the denominator, not the biological cross. An affected father passes his X chromosome to daughters and his Y to sons in this model. Father-to-son transmission therefore is not expected for an X-linked allele through that direct chromosome path.

For XAXa × XAY, one of four total offspring combinations is an affected son, so the joint probability is 1/4. Among the two son combinations, one is affected, giving a conditional probability of 1/2. The denominator differs.
