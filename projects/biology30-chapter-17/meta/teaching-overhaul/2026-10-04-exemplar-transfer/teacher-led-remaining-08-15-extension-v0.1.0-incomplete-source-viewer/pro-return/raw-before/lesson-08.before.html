Keep alleles attached to their chromosomes

Use X superscripts or clear plain-text forms such as XB and Xb. In a typical XX/XY model, an XX parent contributes an X to every offspring; an XY parent contributes X or Y. Many loci have no corresponding on Y.

For a simple condition, XbY expresses the condition because there is no second X-linked allele to mask it. XBXb can be an unaffected under the stated complete-expression assumptions, while XbXb is affected. These are inheritance models, not a complete account of every variation in sex development or expression.

Use the cross to find the correct denominator

Consider XBXb × XBY. The four equally likely combinations are XBXB, XBXb, XBY and XbY. One of four children is expected to be affected. Among sons only, one of two is expected to be affected.

The of “a son who is affected” is 1/4 among all children. The probability that “the next son is affected” is 1/2 conditional on being a son. The wording changes the denominator, not the biological cross.

An affected father passes his X chromosome to daughters and his Y to sons in this model. Father-to-son transmission therefore is not expected for an X-linked allele through that direct chromosome path.

Separate recessive and dominant X-linked models

For a simple fully penetrant X-linked dominant trait, an affected father with an unaffected partner passes his affected X to every daughter and to no son. An affected mother can pass the allele to either sons or daughters.

These patterns are useful evidence, but a small family may not contain every possible outcome. Later pedigree lessons will require testing candidate explanations against the whole family rather than treating one clue as proof.

Always define allele meaning, parental genotypes and the subgroup asked about before calculating.

Worked example

Answer two similar-looking questions

A carrier XAXa mother and unaffected XAY father have children in a simple X-linked recessive model.

  1. List the four equally likely combinations: XAXA, XAXa, XAY and XaY.
  2. Affected child: XaY is one of four, so probability = 1/4.
  3. Affected among sons: XaY is one of two son outcomes, so probability = 1/2.
  4. A son who is affected among all children: probability remains 1/4.
  5. Write the denominator in words to show which question you answered.
