Read the explanation

Cross a heterozygote with aabb. The tester contributes only ab, so the offspring's phenotype or genotype can reveal the heterozygote's gamete contribution. Under independent assortment, the four combinations are expected in equal proportions. If the heterozygote has AB/ab and the genes are linked, AB and ab gametes often occur more frequently than Ab and aB. The corresponding parental offspring classes are larger, and the recombinant classes smaller. Read the actual counts, not just the presence of four categories. Linked genes can produce all four combinations if crossing over occurs, but the proportions can differ from 1:1:1:1.

A homozygous recessive tester contributes a fixed allele combination. Offspring classes reveal the heterozygote’s gamete combinations. An excess of parental combinations over recombinants can support linkage, while equal combinations are expected under independent assortment, subject to sampling and model assumptions.
