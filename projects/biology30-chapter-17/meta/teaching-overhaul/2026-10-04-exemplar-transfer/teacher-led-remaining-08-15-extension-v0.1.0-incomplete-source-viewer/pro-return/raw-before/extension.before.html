Shared data can support research

A biobank stores biological samples and associated information for research. Comparing many samples can help investigate relationships between variants, environments and phenotypes.

Consider a hypothetical study that finds a variant more often in one group than another. The groups also differ in age and sampling location. The association is a starting point, not proof that the variant alone causes the phenotype.

Evaluate evidence and participation

Researchers need to consider sampling, confounding variables, validation and how results generalize. Participants also need clear information about consent, data use, access and possible family implications.

The case asks you to distinguish scientific interpretation from decisions about participation. No personal sample, family history or disclosure is required.
