Calculate the fraction carefully

equals recombinant observations divided by total scored observations, multiplied by 100%. In a , add both reciprocal recombinant classes. Do not divide only by the parental classes or by one quarter of the offspring.

For 84 parental class 1, 76 parental class 2, 22 recombinant class 1 and 18 recombinant class 2, the total is 200. Recombinants total 40. Frequency = 40/200 × 100% = 20%.

In a simple short-interval model, this suggests approximately 20 map units between the loci. The result is a relative genetic distance estimate, not an exact physical measurement.

Use three distances to infer order

Suppose A–B is 6 map units, B–C is 14 and A–C is 20 in a simple additive map. Since 6 + 14 = 20, B lies between A and C. Either A—6—B—14—C or its reversed orientation expresses the same relative order.

Two distances alone may leave more than one order possible. Place the genes tentatively and ask what additional comparison would distinguish the alternatives. Do not invent a third distance merely to finish the drawing.

Understand the limits of two-locus data

Observed recombination frequency approaches 50% for independently assorting loci or sufficiently separated loci. It does not provide an unbounded direct ruler. Multiple crossovers can restore parental outside-marker combinations, so some exchange events are not detected by scoring only two loci.

A chromosome map can span more than 50 map units when shorter intervals are combined, even though a two-locus recombination fraction does not rise above the independent limit in the standard model. These are different quantities.

The connection between centimorgans and base pairs varies across regions and organisms. Report the calculated genetic distance and assumptions, rather than converting every into one fixed DNA length.

Worked example

Calculate frequency and check the denominator

A test cross gives parental counts 90 and 86 and recombinant counts 12 and 12.

  1. Total offspring = 90 + 86 + 12 + 12 = 200.
  2. Recombinants = 12 + 12 = 24.
  3. Frequency = 24/200 = 0.12 = 12%.
  4. Short-interval map estimate: about 12 cM.
  5. Reasonableness check: recombinant classes are less common than parental classes, and 12% is below 50%.
