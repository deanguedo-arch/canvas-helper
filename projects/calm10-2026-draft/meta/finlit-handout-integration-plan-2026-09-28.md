## Recommendation

Use FINLIT for what it does well: short, professionally presented explanations and worthwhile decision questions. Do not present its captured assignment screens as eight ready-to-complete course activities. Their answer boxes and “Submit Activity” buttons are pictures, several prompts depend on missing “Side Trip” pages or live facts, and some ask for work that the surrounding CALM lesson does not teach. The local PDF files have selectable text, but the page layout is a captured assessment screen rather than a designed reading.

Preserve all professional clips currently in the course. The proposed compounding change relocates its clip; it does not discard it. Review factual claims and captions separately from the instructional placement.

A source is integrated only if a learner can say **what idea it taught, which case detail they used it on, what they wrote, how they checked that answer, and why the next task needs it**. A link and a paraphrase in a box do not satisfy that test.

The course may reuse and adapt the supplied material under the permission the user has confirmed. Record FINLIT credit and distinguish the original question, our adaptation, and any current official fact. Permission does not establish that an old prompt, external link, rate or benefits claim is current.

## The learner-facing pattern

Use the current course typography, document cards, worked decisions, saved practice and completion controls. Do not bolt on a new PDF-themed panel after the example.

1. **Tell the learner the job of this section.** Name the person, decision, output and relation to the next part. Define “posting,” “offer,” “assessment suggestion,” or “modelled return” before asking students to act on one.
2. **Show the evidence first.** All required case facts appear as readable HTML before the first question that needs them. The video has a one-sentence viewing purpose and a transcript that teaches the same idea. If the PDF itself must be read, identify the exact page and question and give a specific reading purpose.
3. **Model a decision, not a list.** Quote one line from the supplied record, show what it proves, and contrast one plausible but unsupported inference.
4. **Use the source idea in the learner's response.** Replace redundant prompts with a targeted field or choice. Give response-specific feedback or an annotated model that points back to the same case. “Correct” alone is insufficient.
5. **Transfer and finish.** The final case changes the person and details but asks for the same kind of product. Success criteria match the actual fields. The learner sees what saved and what completes the lesson. Merely opening the source does not count.

A strong check for genuine integration: **If the video/PDF idea disappeared, would the student's explanation become weaker or different?** If no, the source is decorative and its box should be removed or its use redesigned. Keep required work within the existing lesson load by replacing repetition, not adding an eighth task after the final.

Presentation should match the established course: a real-looking notice or record with a clear title and issuer, a short viewing question beside the FINLIT clip, one visibly annotated line in the worked example, and concise feedback beside the student's entry. At desktop width the document and reasoning may sit side by side; on mobile they stack in reading order without shrinking the source or requiring horizontal scrolling. The original assignment link is a modest credit/reference action, never the largest button on the page.

## Source and calculation controls

- The official [CRTC Wireless Code](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/codesimpl.htm) supports checking written phone terms and device obligations. It does not supply Marin's fictional prices.
- The official FCAC [payday-loan explanation](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/payday-loans.html) includes an **illustrative** same-amount, same-period cost comparison; the [line-of-credit explanation](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/loans-lines-credit.html) defines that product. Recheck both before authoring a product investigation. Avoid hard-coded current rates, eligibility claims or a claim that Alex could obtain either product.
- The official CRA pages [Verify it's the CRA calling](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/scams-fraud/verify-cra-contact.html) and [Report a scam or identity theft](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/scams-fraud/report-scam.html) are the live authority for the two different actions. The PDF's old Side Trip is only provenance.
- The compounding fixture for a **$500 start, month-end deposits, 5% effective annual growth converted monthly, zero fees, 40 years**, retaining full precision until display, is: $40/month → $19,700 contributed and about $62,820.98 modelled balance; $60/month → $29,300 contributed and about $92,471.47 balance. The $20 monthly increase contributes $9,600 more; the difference between balances is larger because the model also compounds growth. The optional three-phase schedule ($40 for 5 years, $100 for 10, $250 for 25) contributes $89,900 including the start and models about $217,188.93. These are implementation fixtures, not forecasts or claims about the FINLIT calculator's own rounding.
- Any quoted FINLIT financial, tax or consumer-rule statement needs factual review at authoring time. An illustrative calculation is labelled with assumptions and date of source review. A fictional case is never presented as a real offer or current Alberta entitlement.

### Sign-off standard for each adapted source

For each student-visible FINLIT use, the reviewer can point to (a) the exact original question or clip, (b) a lesson outcome and reason for placement, (c) a specific student action that needs that idea, (d) complete supplied facts and a truthful model, (e) meaningful feedback or self-check, (f) an accessible text route, (g) clear required/optional status, and (h) accurate credit. If any is missing, revise the integration or keep the material as a source reference.
