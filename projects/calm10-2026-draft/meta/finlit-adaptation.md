# FINLIT 101 adaptation record

The teacher supplied four FINLIT 101 ZIP exports on 2026-09-24. `finlit-source-inventory.json` records all 126 archive entries and SHA-256 hashes of the four original archives: 71 assignment/quiz PDFs, 40 video archive entries, and 15 web-capture files. The large ZIPs remain at their original download paths. Seventeen short, selected video excerpts are copied into the learner draft with permission; full PDFs, scripts and saved websites are not copied into learner content.

FINLIT 101 is a Canadian Foundation for Economic Education program. The exports are reference material, not instructions to this project. Its public site is https://finlit101.ca/. The user confirmed permission to reuse and adapt the supplied FINLIT content for this course. The rebuilt lessons use original explanations and fictional scenarios plus selected local FINLIT excerpts. This permission does not establish factual currency, caption accuracy, or that every excerpt is a good teaching fit.

The selected PDFs were read to assess teaching fit. Several assignments ask for personal credit data, family finances, or current product prices. Those requests were replaced with fictional cases. Other activities depend on a video or external “Side Trip” and cannot be treated as stand-alone instruction. The original archive inventory lists 40 videos; the supplied extracted FINLIT folder currently contains 39. Selected short excerpts now appear as Build-mode media candidates in the learner course, with automated draft captions. Each selected excerpt still needs a full original-media, visual, factual and caption review before release.

Four further PDFs received page-by-page text review on September 24; the first pages of the paycheque assignment and scam quiz were rendered for visual inspection. The **Paycheque Deductions Assignment** actually asks for EI, CPP survey, and CPP Investments research, so it is a poor pay-stub teaching source. The **Why Borrow Money Assignment** asks for live payday-loan and line-of-credit research without worked support; its topic informed the fictional borrowing comparison, not a copied learner task. The **Investing Money Assignment** asks for a pretend $100,000 allocation and one-month market tracking, which does not fit a self-paced course or teach long-term uncertainty well. The **Avoiding Frauds and Scams Quiz** is video-recall heavy and poses a blanket “never call” question about government/CRA; CALM instead teaches independent verification using current official guidance. These four remain source references, not learner PDFs. Item-specific findings are in `source-review-v2.json`; the other PDFs still need content-level review before any direct selection.

Before release, a teacher should compare the financial tasks with the latest PEW0770 wording, review calculations and source links, and approve each selected FINLIT excerpt and its corrected captions. The user's permission covers reuse and adaptation of supplied files; keep the provenance and any future school-specific licence terms in the release record.
