# FL3-03 — Separate an account from what it holds

Complete authored script. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### Two different choices are hiding in one question

“Should I use a TFSA or buy a GIC?” mixes an account arrangement with a product. A suitable account may hold different investments, each with different access, fees and risks. You will separate those choices and use product records to match money to a purpose.

Your final product is a two-goal comparison for a fictional adult. Choose an appropriate place for near-term money, explain a possible long-term holding, and check a supplied account limit. You will not open an account or disclose a real tax record.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Noor's two decisions** and **Account and product reference**. Learn how the two layers work, follow the near-term example and practise a changed goal. Answer six questions. Complete **Milan's two goals**, compare, revise and sign off. All required product information is supplied; official links are optional.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Noor's two decisions

Noor is a fictional 19-year-old with **$500 needed for a confirmed course fee in six months**. Losing part of that money or being unable to withdraw it at the deadline would prevent payment. Noor is comparing the following invented product records, all available in a non-registered account or the provider's TFSA for this exercise.

| Product record | Return and charges | Access and risk |
|---|---|---|
| S: savings deposit | 2% simple annual interest for this six-month illustration; no fee | Withdraw when needed; balance does not move with market prices under the stated terms |
| G: one-year non-redeemable GIC | 5% for the full year; no fee | No early withdrawal under this fictional contract; principal and stated interest payable at year-end |
| F: diversified equity fund | Return uncertain; annual management cost 0.5% of assets; other charges zero in this illustration | Can request a sale, but proceeds depend on market value and settlement; loss possible |

**Account note:** Noor's eligibility to open the case TFSA and **$1,000 remaining contribution room** are explicitly confirmed for the exercise. That is Noor's fictional remaining room, not a current annual limit for everyone. No contributions or withdrawals occur except those described. A non-registered account has no TFSA contribution-room restriction but does not receive TFSA tax treatment.

### Account and product reference

The following short record summarizes selected CRA plan distinctions, read September 28, 2026. It is enough for this lesson; actual eligibility, room and withdrawal conditions must be checked before a real transaction.

| Arrangement | Purpose and tax distinction |
|---|---|
| TFSA | Contributions are not deductible; eligible growth and withdrawals are generally tax-free, subject to the rules and available room. |
| RRSP | Retirement-oriented saving; deductible contributions within limits may reduce taxable income; ordinary withdrawals are generally taxable. |
| FHSA | Eligible first-home saving; contributions may be deductible and qualifying home-purchase withdrawals tax-free. Conditions apply. |
| RESP | Education saving for a beneficiary; contributions are not deductible. Eligible grants and investment earnings paid as educational assistance are reported by the beneficiary. |
| Non-registered | No registered-plan tax shelter; income or gains may be taxable under the applicable investment rules. |

**Read the records:** Which item is the account arrangement rather than the holding inside it?

A. The equity fund. B. The one-year GIC. C. The TFSA. D. The savings deposit.

C is the arrangement with account rules. A, B and D are products or holdings. A TFSA does not by itself tell you which of those is inside it.

## 3. Learn the method

### Read the two layers

Think of the **account** as a set of rules around eligible holdings. The **holding** is what the money is actually placed in. “A GIC held in a TFSA” names both layers. “A TFSA earns 5%” is incomplete unless a specific holding or offer provides that rate. The account label does not guarantee a return or prevent a market loss.

Choose using the goal's deadline and the consequences of loss first. Then examine the account rules. A tax advantage cannot make locked money accessible before its contractual date. Nor does a familiar product name establish that all versions have identical terms.

### Compare access, risk, return, fees and knowledge

**Liquidity** describes how readily a holding can become available money, considering time and cost. Noor's GIC cannot be redeemed at six months under its supplied contract. The fund can be sold, but that does not guarantee its value at sale or instant access. A saleable asset and a stable balance are different features.

**Risk** includes the possibility of loss or of failing to meet the goal. **Return** includes gains or losses and income, after relevant costs. Fees reduce what the investor keeps. The time horizon matters, but a long horizon is not a guarantee of profit. **Risk tolerance** concerns willingness to accept uncertainty; the ability to bear a loss also matters. Noor cannot make the fee payment if this $500 is lost, even if Noor says market swings feel exciting.

Before using a product, a person should be able to explain what it holds, how money is accessed, what can cause loss and what fees apply. If that knowledge is missing, pausing to investigate is an informed action.

### Recognise common holdings

A deposit records money held with an institution under its account terms. A GIC commits money under specified repayment and interest conditions. A **bond** represents lending to an issuer; issuer failure and changes in resale price can create risk. A **share** represents an ownership interest in a company. A **fund** pools investments; its actual holdings determine how diversified and risky it is. Diversification spreads exposure, but does not eliminate losses.

**Crypto assets** are digital assets with potentially substantial price, platform and access risks. A token's popularity or a platform's registration does not guarantee its value. The Alberta Securities Commission's CheckFirst material identifies potential loss and liquidity risks. No crypto purchase is required here, and no advertised return is treated as evidence of suitability.

**Second representation:** **Goal and date → holding's access/risk/fees → account eligibility and room → decision with one limit.** Keep both layers named in your answer.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Keep the course-fee money available

Noor needs the money after six months. S permits access then. G's higher full-year rate is irrelevant to that access problem: its contract does not allow early withdrawal. F could be worth less at the deadline, which Noor cannot absorb for this goal.

For S, the illustrative six-month interest is $500 × 0.02 × 6/12 = **$5**, giving $505 before any tax applicable to the chosen arrangement. Its terms specify this simple calculation. Do not substitute G's full-year $25 interest into a six-month comparison.

**Complete comparison:** “I would use S for the course-fee money because its supplied terms preserve access and avoid market-price loss before the six-month deadline. If held in the case TFSA, the $500 contribution fits Noor's confirmed $1,000 room; S is the holding and TFSA is the arrangement. G locks the money too long, while F exposes the required fee money to uncertain value. The 2% rate and account room are supplied case facts, not promises for another person.”

This conclusion uses both product and account evidence. Simply writing “TFSA is safest” would fail to identify what is held or what could happen to it.

## 5. Practise with support

### A different $500 has an eight-year purpose

Consider a separate version of Noor's case: **$500 is for a flexible goal eight years away**, emergency money is already separate, and Noor can tolerate some loss without missing an essential payment. The same $1,000 TFSA room is available in this version; do not add the previous model's $500 contribution. Product F's record is unchanged.

Select **What must still be checked?**: **F's holdings, fee effects and risk fit**, **only its account name**, **whether a friend earned money last month**, or **a guarantee that eight years prevents losses**. Enter **Account arrangement** and **Holding** in two labelled fields. Write **Why F could be considered and what it cannot promise**, two or three sentences.

The first choice investigates the actual product. The second omits the holding; the third is not evidence of Noor's result; the fourth seeks a guarantee that the time horizon does not provide. The two labels should distinguish TFSA from diversified equity fund. If both fields say TFSA, revisit the layers.

**Model:** “The arrangement could be Noor's case TFSA and the holding F, a diversified equity fund. The longer, flexible goal and separate emergency money make market exposure possible to consider, but Noor still needs to understand F's holdings, fees and possible losses. Eight years does not guarantee a gain, and another suitable choice could be made after comparing those features.”

Revise your two labels and explanation. The model is a conditional assessment, not a recommendation to purchase a real fund.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Which phrase identifies both layers?

A. “Tax-free, so guaranteed.” B. “A GIC held inside a TFSA.” C. “Any account earns 5%.” D. “A popular app.”

Answer B. A confuses tax and loss protection; B names holding and arrangement; C invents a return; D names neither relevant set of terms.

### Q2 — Why does G fail Noor's six-month need?

A. Every GIC is a scam. B. Its one-year rate is too small to count. C. TFSA rules erase its interest. D. Its supplied contract prevents access before one year.

Answer D. A is false; B does not address the deadline; C invents a rule; D uses the actual product condition.

### Q3 — What does diversification do?

A. Spreads exposure across holdings without guaranteeing no loss. B. Guarantees every asset rises. C. Removes every fee. D. Makes all funds identical.

Answer A. A states its role and limit; B–D are unsupported consequences.

### Q4 — Which account claim agrees with the reference record?

A. Every registered contribution is deductible. B. An RESP and TFSA have identical withdrawal rules. C. Different registered plans have different eligibility, contribution and tax rules. D. A non-registered holding has no possible tax consequences.

Answer C. A ignores TFSA/RESP contributions; B erases distinct purposes and rules; C reflects the record; D contradicts the tax distinction.

### Q5 — What is still true of a crypto asset on a registered platform?

A. Its market price cannot fall. B. Registration does not guarantee its return or remove asset risk. C. It is suitable for every deadline. D. All withdrawals must be immediate.

Answer B. A, C and D invent protections; B separates platform oversight from investment performance.

### Q6 — What is a responsible response to a product you cannot explain?

A. Buy because its name is familiar. B. Assume the account label supplies the missing facts. C. Copy someone else's risk tolerance. D. Investigate holdings, access, costs and risk before deciding.

Answer D. A–C substitute assumptions for knowledge; D identifies the information needed.

## 7. Apply independently

### Milan's two goals

Milan is a fictional 22-year-old. **$600 must remain available for a course payment in three months**. A separate **$900 is for a flexible goal at least ten years away**; a loss in that long-term amount would not affect the course payment or essential costs. Emergency money is separate. The case confirms TFSA eligibility and **$1,200 remaining contribution room**, with no other contributions or withdrawals during this decision.

**New product records:** S2 is an accessible savings deposit with no withdrawal fee, a stable balance under its terms and 1.5% simple annual interest in this illustration. G2 is a two-year non-redeemable GIC at 3% annually, no early access under its contract. F2 is a diversified equity fund with uncertain value, 0.5% annual management cost and no other charges in this exercise; sale proceeds depend on market value and settlement. All three can be held in the case TFSA or non-registered arrangement. Milan understands F2's supplied features and can consider its risk for the long-term goal.

Choose **Holding for the three-month money: S2 / G2 / F2**. Enter **Total amount I propose contributing to the TFSA ($)**. Write **My two-goal account and holding plan**, 4–5 sentences naming both layers, access, risk and the contribution limit. You may allocate the TFSA room in more than one defensible way; do not exceed the supplied limit or claim the account prevents losses.

**Complete model:** “I would keep the $600 course money in S2 in a non-registered arrangement, because access and a stable balance matter at three months. For the separate long-term goal, I could consider $900 in F2 inside the TFSA, using $900 of the $1,200 room. That fund can lose value and charges a fee even though its eligible growth has the account's tax treatment. I would not put the course money in G2, which is locked for two years. This is a conditional plan using Milan's supplied room and risk circumstances, not a claim that F2 is guaranteed or best for everyone.”

Another valid arrangement could use TFSA room for all $600 in S2 and $600 of the long-term holding, with the remaining $300 long-term outside it. Explain both layers and tax-rule limits; this lesson does not supply enough tax circumstances to declare one allocation uniquely optimal.
