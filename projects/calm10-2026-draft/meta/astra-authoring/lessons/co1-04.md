# CO1-04 — Work through a changing shift

Complete script for lead review. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### A changed plan needs a new order of actions

During a busy shift, an equipment problem can affect a deadline while visitors still need help. Working faster on the original list may not solve the new problem. You will practise deciding what must stop, what can continue and what the shift lead needs to know.

Your final product is a timed decision log for a room change. It will name a first action, protect private information, use an approved alternative and explain one improvement from earlier feedback. All events are supplied simulations; no physical setup, supervised placement or contact with a real workplace is required.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **The printer incident and the shift rules**, then check who can approve an alternative. Learn **Replan without guessing**, follow the revised printer response, and practise **The outdoor change**. After six questions, complete **A room becomes unavailable**. Compare the model and times, revise and sign off. Pause after the outdoor practice if needed; your work saves.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### The printer incident and the shift rules

Rowan helps at fictional Prairie Library Hub. Morgan leads the shift. A **blocker** is a condition that prevents a task from progressing as planned. An **alternative** is a different way to reach the purpose; it still needs to meet the rules.

**Desk rules for these cases**

- Report an equipment blocker when it is first noticed. State what it affects and the deadline.
- Do not dismantle, force or repair equipment. A normal restart is allowed only if it is safe and does not delay reporting.
- Morgan approves changes to activity method or location. A trained staff member must check a new activity area before setup. Rowan can observe and report a concern but cannot sign off that check.
- Keep participant names and contact details in the secure tray. Public notices may include activity, time and approved location, not participant details.
- Blank cards and public brochures may be prepared while waiting, if no immediate hazard needs attention.
- Morgan can be contacted using the desk extension. If unavailable, leave a clear message and wait for required approval before the restricted action.

**Completed incident record — Monday, September 14**

At 10:05 the printer stopped. Cards were needed for an 11:00 activity. Rowan spent fifteen minutes trying to get printing working and notified Morgan at 10:20. At 10:25 Morgan approved blank cards with handwritten activity headings; preparing them took twenty minutes, and the cards were ready at 10:45. No private information was needed on the cards. The activity began on time.

**Morgan's noon feedback:** “The cards were accurate and ready, but I needed to know about the printer at 10:05. An early message would have given us more time to choose a backup. Please report a blocker when you first notice it.”

**Check the authority:** Who can approve replacing the printed cards with handwritten headings?

A. Any visitor waiting nearby. B. Rowan automatically, because the deadline is close. C. Nobody can ever change a plan. D. Morgan, under the supplied desk rules.

Feedback: D matches the case. A invents authority, B treats urgency as permission, and C ignores the stated approval route.

## 3. Learn the method

### Replan without guessing

A **priority** is something that needs attention before another task. Priority depends on consequence and sequence, not just on which request is loudest. Immediate safety and privacy boundaries continue to apply when a deadline changes. After protecting those boundaries, identify the task whose delay would block other work.

A **dependency** is a step that must happen before another can begin. Location approval and an area check are dependencies for setting up in a new room. Writing a draft public notice can happen while waiting, but posting an unapproved location would skip a dependency. This difference lets a person stay useful without pretending approval has arrived.

### Communicate the problem before it becomes a surprise

A concise update gives a time, blocker, consequence and request. “Printer stopped at 10:05; cards are needed by 11. May I use blank cards with the activity headings?” gives Morgan information for a decision. An early message does not require Rowan to know the cause of the failure. Reporting a problem and offering an option are compatible with taking initiative.

The original incident ended on time, but the feedback still identifies a process worth changing. A successful result does not prove every step was effective. Conversely, one late report does not prove Rowan is generally unreliable. Keep the improvement specific: report blockers when noticed and name the affected deadline.

### Use time as evidence

Work backwards from the deadline. If a task takes ten minutes and must finish at 10:45, it must start by 10:35. If approval cannot arrive until 10:25, check whether the remaining sequence still fits. Activities that can occur at the same time must be assigned to different available people; one person cannot independently perform two ten-minute tasks simultaneously.

**Replanning map:** protect people and information → report the change → identify permissions and dependencies → assign feasible tasks and times → check the deadline → record the next improvement.

**Optional training reference, read September 28, 2026:** CCOHS explains that workers need information and instruction about hazards before the work. In this lesson, that supports waiting for an authorized area check; the fictional desk rules determine who is authorized. [“Right to Know”](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/legisl/legislation/three_rights.html).

**Hint:** Draw arrows between steps that cannot change order. Put approval before posting a new location, and the area check before moving in equipment.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Improve the printer response

We are looking back at September 14, not rewriting what actually happened. The incident record says Rowan reported at 10:20. The model below proposes what Rowan could do at 10:05 in a similar future situation.

First, stop repeated repair attempts. The rules allow a normal safe restart, not dismantling or a fifteen-minute delay before reporting. The deadline is 11:00, and the purpose is readable activity headings, not printing for its own sake. Blank cards might meet that purpose, but Morgan must approve the change.

**Improved decision log**

- **10:05, first action:** leave the stopped printer safe and notify Morgan by extension.
- **Update:** “The printer stopped at 10:05 and the activity needs cards by 11:00. I have stopped repeated attempts. May I prepare blank cards with handwritten headings as the backup?”
- **While waiting:** gather blank cards and keep participant information in the secure tray; do not copy names onto public cards.
- **After approval:** prepare the approved headings and check them against the activity list. The recorded backup took twenty minutes; use that duration to plan the work.
- **Improvement:** report the blocker when noticed instead of waiting until a backup becomes urgent.

This is a complete response even though Rowan does not diagnose the printer. “Fix the mechanism quickly” requires skills and permission not supplied. “The activity started on time, so no change is needed” ignores the feedback about avoidable pressure.

## 5. Practise with support

### The outdoor change

A separate shift takes place Monday, September 21. At 10:20, Morgan sends a message proposing that the 11:00 activity move outdoors. Cards are ready. A public notice must be posted by 10:45. Morgan is available by extension at 10:25 to confirm the final location and assign work. Trained staff member Lee can check the outdoor area in ten minutes beginning 10:25 if Morgan assigns it. Rowan needs ten minutes to finish and post the public notice after location approval. Equipment setup takes ten minutes and may begin only after the area is checked. Rowan and Lee are both available for setup after their first tasks. Participant records remain at the desk, secured before Rowan leaves.

Choose the workable sequence: **set up outside immediately / draft a notice now, seek confirmation at 10:25, then split notice and area-check tasks / wait until 10:45 to report the change**. Use **Check the sequence**.

Feedback: The second choice uses waiting time without skipping approval. Immediate setup skips the trained check. Waiting until 10:45 consumes the notice deadline before work begins.

Fill **First action at 10:20**, **My 10:25 update and task split**, and **Times for notice, check and setup**. Then open **Compare the outdoor plan**.

**Hint:** Rowan can prepare wording now but should not publish an unapproved destination. At 10:25 Rowan and Lee can do different ten-minute tasks concurrently.

**Model:** “At 10:20 I secure the participant records and draft a notice with a space for the approved location. At 10:25 I ask Morgan: ‘Cards are ready; the notice is due at 10:45. Please confirm the outdoor location and assign Lee's trained area check while I finish the public notice.’ With approval, Rowan completes and posts the notice 10:25–10:35 while Lee checks the area 10:25–10:35. If Lee confirms it is ready, equipment setup takes 10:35–10:45. The notice meets its deadline and setup finishes before 11:00. If the check finds a problem, keep setup paused and ask Morgan for an approved alternative.”

Revise a plan that assigns Rowan both the area check and notice, begins setup before the check or omits what happens if the check fails. The model is a conditional plan, not a claim that an unperformed check succeeded.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What is a dependency in the outdoor case?

A. The colour of the cards. B. The area check must happen before setup. C. Rowan must repair the printer. D. The notice must list participant phone numbers.

Answer B. A is unrelated to task order; B names a required preceding step; C and D contradict the case rules.

### Q2 — Why report a blocker before knowing its cause?

A. A report replaces all action forever. B. It proves someone is at fault. C. Morgan can plan with the affected deadline while the cause remains uncertain. D. It automatically authorizes any solution.

Answer C. A confuses reporting with abandoning work; B invents blame; C explains the planning value; D invents permission.

### Q3 — What can be learned from a successful activity with a late report?

A. The result was achieved, and the communication process can still improve. B. Every step was perfect. C. Rowan cannot ever work with people. D. Feedback is irrelevant if a deadline is met.

Answer A. A separates result and process; B and D ignore the feedback; C overgeneralizes from one event.

### Q4 — Two ten-minute tasks can run concurrently when…

A. one person simply promises to be faster. B. the deadline is important. C. their names are similar. D. different available, authorized people can do them without an unmet dependency.

Answer D. A–C do not supply capacity or permission; D identifies what makes the schedule possible.

### Q5 — What should a public location notice contain here?

A. Every participant's contact details. B. Activity, time and approved location. C. Another person's login. D. An unconfirmed destination presented as final.

Answer B. A and C expose protected information; B contains the authorized public content; D could misdirect visitors.

### Q6 — An area check discovers a hazard. What changes?

A. Nothing, because the plan has already been written. B. The notice deadline cancels the hazard. C. Setup stays paused and Morgan is asked for an approved alternative. D. Rowan signs off the check instead.

Answer C. A and B ignore new evidence; C updates the plan within the rules; D invents Rowan's qualification.

## 7. Apply independently

### A room becomes unavailable

New shift: Friday, September 25. At **12:10**, the door to Activity Room A jams. The 12:45 activity cannot use that entrance. Rowan has not been trained or authorized to force or repair the door. People can wait safely in the clear welcome area; Rowan must direct them away from the jammed entrance.

Morgan can respond by extension at **12:15**. Room B is available and Morgan has already completed its area check that morning. Moving the activity there still needs Morgan's approval. Updating and posting the public notice takes **ten minutes** after approval. Moving the prepared card box by the clear route takes **five minutes** after approval. Rowan is the only helper available for those two tasks, so perform them sequentially. The notice must be posted by **12:35**. The participant contact list is face up at the desk and must be secured before Rowan leaves. No other task or equipment move is required in this case.

Write **My changed-room decision log**, using times and short paragraphs. Include the immediate action at 12:10, privacy step, exact message for Morgan, a feasible post-approval sequence and one improvement linked to the earlier printer feedback. Also enter **Notice posted by** and **Card move finished by** as times.

**Complete model:** “At 12:10 Rowan directs visitors to wait in the clear welcome area and away from the jammed door, avoids repair attempts, secures the contact list face down in the tray and leaves Morgan a clear extension message. At 12:15: ‘Room A's entrance is jammed and I am keeping visitors clear. The activity starts at 12:45; may we use the already checked Room B? I need ten minutes for the notice and five to move the cards.’ If Morgan approves at 12:15, Rowan posts the notice from 12:15–12:25, then moves the card box from 12:25–12:30. The notice meets 12:35 and the cards arrive before 12:45. Reporting immediately applies the earlier feedback instead of spending time trying an unauthorized repair.”

The reverse task order also fits if the box moves 12:15–12:20 and the notice is posted 12:20–12:30. Both need approval first, secured records and visitors protected. If approval does not arrive, the plan must remain conditional; do not report a completed relocation as fact.

- My first action protects visitors without an unauthorized door repair.
- I secured the contact list and kept private details off the notice.
- My message requests the needed approval, and my task times fit both deadlines.
- I identified an earlier reporting habit drawn from Morgan's printer feedback.
