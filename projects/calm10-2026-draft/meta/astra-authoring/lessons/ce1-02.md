## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Jordan enjoyed rearranging a poster so readers could find the important information. Jordan wants work involving visual problem solving and prefers predictable finishing times. The question is: **Could graphic design be worth exploring while keeping that schedule preference visible?**

An **occupation profile** summarizes a kind of work across workplaces. It is not an advertisement for one vacancy. ### A different document: one employer's notice

A **job posting** advertises one opening. The following posting is invented for this lesson, not extracted from the ALIS page.

“Help check event flyers and prepare corrected drafts. Initial work is supervised. Usual hours are Monday to Friday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m. During the two weeks before our annual festival, one Saturday shift may be scheduled. Applicants provide three examples of layouts they have created. **Read the two documents.** Which statement describes work a person would do? Select one and use **Check my reading**.

A. Retrieved September 28, 2026. B. Alberta occupation profile. C. Usual hours are 9 a.m.–4 p.m. D. Help check event flyers and prepare corrected drafts.

Feedback: **D** names a duty. **A** is the access date. **B** describes the source's scope. **C** describes a working condition, not the task performed.

## 3. Learn the method

### Begin with the work, not a title

A **job** is a particular position with an employer. An **occupation** groups work with similar duties. A **career** is a longer path that may include different jobs, learning and other life roles. Jordan could hold a layout job while exploring the broader graphic-design occupation; neither would determine every later career choice.

A **career cluster** is a useful way to group related work for exploration, such as work involving visual communication. A classification system has a different purpose: it organizes occupations so sources can describe or compare them consistently. Canada's **National Occupational Classification**, or **NOC**, assigns codes to occupational groups. A code helps you check that two sources concern the same group. It is not a personal ranking or a guarantee that all jobs in the group have identical duties.

### Sort what each sentence can establish

A duty answers, “What would I do?” A working condition answers questions such as, “When, where or under what demands?” Preparation concerns learning, experience or credentials used to enter the work. These categories answer different parts of Jordan's question. An appealing duty does not answer a concern about evening work.

Be attentive to words such as **common**, **some**, **preferred** and **required**. If some employers prefer experience, that is narrower than a rule that every applicant must have it. A broad profile's preparation summary is not a particular program's admission decision. A credential is a formal record of qualification; a portfolio is a selected collection of work showing what someone can do. They are different kinds of evidence.

### Keep the source attached to the claim

Read a source in three passes: locate the relevant field; state the fact in your own words; explain what it supports and what it leaves open. Preserve the date and scope. An update date indicates when a section was revised. A retrieval date says when you accessed it. Accessing an older section today does not turn it into a newly updated section.

Use this comparison strip:

| Question | Best starting evidence | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| What tasks are typical? | Occupation-profile duties | The exact duties of one opening |
| Would the schedule fit Jordan? | Working conditions plus a posting | The employer's actual scheduling expectations |
| What preparation should be investigated? | Profile preparation section | Exact program admission and employer requirements |

### Disagreement can reveal a better question

A broad profile may mention deadline overtime while one posting mostly lists weekday hours. These statements can both be true. They concern different levels of detail. Do not average them or ignore the inconvenient one. Ask what the particular employer expects during busy periods. In this course, the supplied posting answers part of that question without requiring a real conversation.

**Optional first-step hint:** Highlight mentally the action words in a sentence. “Prepare drafts” is a duty. “On Saturday” changes the condition under which the duty occurs.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Test Jordan's first idea

I start with Jordan's interest in visual problem solving, then choose the duties field in Record G. Preparing visual solutions gives a reason to explore the occupation. I have a match between an interest and a task category; I do not yet have evidence that Jordan can do every professional task.

Next I check the schedule concern. Record G warns that deadlines can affect hours. The fictional posting makes that issue concrete: it includes a possible Saturday shift before the festival. A regular weekday pattern therefore does not establish that weekends are always free.

**Completed research note:** “Graphic design is worth investigating because its visual-solution work relates to Jordan's interest in arranging information. Record G is an Alberta profile, with relevant sections updated March 22, 2023 and retrieved September 28, 2026. It also indicates that deadlines can affect hours. Prairie Print Studio's fictional posting includes a possible Saturday shift, so Jordan should not assume every job has a predictable weekday-only schedule. The next useful question is how often a particular opening changes its hours. Jordan also needs to investigate portfolio expectations before applying.”

The alternative “Jordan likes posters, so this job definitely fits” skips both the schedule and preparation. “No designer ever has regular hours” is also unsupported: the record describes possibilities, not every workplace. A careful conclusion keeps the promising connection and the remaining question together.

## 5. Practise with support

### Read the preparation evidence

Continue with Jordan and the same two documents. This time, focus on the posting's request for three layout examples and its statement about previous paid work.

Choose how to classify “Previous paid studio work is helpful but is not required”: **universal legal requirement / preference in this opening / guaranteed admission to a program**. Use **Check the category**. The correct category is **preference in this opening**. The notice does not establish a law or a program's admission policy.

Write in **Evidence and source**, **What Jordan can conclude** and **One unresolved preparation question**. Use two or three sentences in total. Your response should explain why Jordan can investigate this opening without pretending that three examples establish professional expertise.

**Optional hint:** Keep “three layout examples” separate from “previous paid work.” One is requested evidence; the other is a stated preference.

**Compare with the preparation model**

“The fictional Prairie Print Studio notice requests three layout examples and says paid studio experience is helpful rather than required. Jordan can prepare a small portfolio using truthful school work; the notice does not require claiming paid experience. Jordan should still check what kinds of layouts and file formats the employer expects. The broader profile does not answer that opening-specific question.”

If your answer says a paid job is mandatory, compare that claim with the notice's wording. If it says Jordan is certain to be selected, replace that guarantee with what the evidence actually supports. Use **Return to my practice** to revise your entries.

## 6. Check your understanding

Each question has its own **Check answer** control. Feedback explains the selected option.

### Q1 — What is a job posting most directly evidence about?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q1`.

A. Every workplace in Canada. B. One employer's opening. C. A guaranteed career outcome. D. Every course a school offers.

Answer: B. A overextends its scope. B matches the purpose of a posting. C is not established by an advertisement. D requires school information.

### Q2 — A source says “some employers prefer experience.” Which paraphrase is accurate?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q2`.

A. All employers legally require experience. B. Experience guarantees selection. C. Experience is irrelevant. D. The preference applies to some employers and needs checking for a specific opening.

Answer: D. A changes both scope and legal meaning. B invents certainty. C dismisses a potentially important preference. D preserves the source's limit.

### Q3 — Why record a NOC code with labour information?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q3`.

A. To check which occupational group the information describes. B. To rank the learner's talent. C. To guarantee matching schedules. D. To replace reading the duties.

Answer: A. A supports comparison of like groups. B mistakes a classification for a score. C ignores differences within a group. D skips the evidence needed to understand the work.

### Q4 — A profile section was updated in 2023 and retrieved in 2026. What is true?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q4`.

A. It was necessarily rewritten in 2026. B. It cannot contain any useful information. C. The two dates describe different events and should be kept separate. D. Neither date matters.

Answer: C. A confuses access with revision. B dismisses rather than evaluates older evidence. C preserves the research trail. D prevents a reader from judging currency.

### Q5 — A profile mentions overtime; a posting lists mostly weekdays. What is the best next step?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q5`.

A. Compare their scope and the posting's busy-period conditions. B. Declare both false. C. Average the number of words. D. Assume weekends are impossible.

Answer: A. B is unsupported. A investigates a plausible difference. C has no evidentiary value. D ignores the overtime information.

### Q6 — Which statement separates a portfolio from a credential?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-02:q6`.

A. Both always mean a university degree. B. A portfolio presents selected work; a credential records a formal qualification. C. A credential is a personal opinion. D. A portfolio guarantees employment.

Answer: B. A wrongly collapses distinct evidence. D promises an outcome. C misdefines a credential. B explains the distinction taught above.

## 7. Apply independently

### Compare two directions for Nia

Nia likes testing whether a website is easy to use and improving confusing screens. Nia also enjoys arranging images and text. Nia values knowing when work will finish and has not yet completed occupational training. Your task is to compare two directions, not assign Nia a permanent career.

For this new case, use **Record G** above and this additional dated record. Both remain beside the final fields in the learner view.

**Record W — Web Designer, ALIS; Alberta**

| Field | Dated source record |
|---|---|
| Duties | Web designers plan website goals, create mock-ups and test display/function across devices or browsers. |
| Working conditions | Work may be in an office or at home; evening, weekend or overtime work can occur. |
| Preparation | Backgrounds vary. Some employers prefer at least two years of related post-secondary study. |
| Classification | 2021 NOC 21233, Web designers. |
| Dates | These sections show March 31, 2022. Retrieved September 28, 2026. |

[Optional original Web Designer profile](https://alis.alberta.ca/occinfo/occupations-in-alberta/occupation-profiles/web-designer/).

Prepare a comparison using **Graphic-design evidence**, **Web-design evidence** and **A provisional direction for Nia**. For each occupation, identify one duty, a condition, a preparation detail and the relevant record/date. Then explain which direction to investigate first, what remains uncertain and one next question for each direction. About 180–260 words across the three fields is a useful guide.

**Compare my work with a supported comparison**

“Record G describes visual-solution work, which connects with Nia's interest in arranging images and text. Deadline-related extra hours may conflict with the preference for a predictable finish. Its preparation note points toward design study and a portfolio, rather than proving Nia already meets a particular program's requirements. Record W more directly matches Nia's interest in testing websites across devices. Its schedule also may vary, and the two-year study statement is a preference of some employers, not a universal rule. G's relevant sections are dated March 22, 2023; W's are March 31, 2022; both were retrieved September 28, 2026. The NOC groups differ, so their data should not be treated as one occupation. I would investigate web design first because testing is a strong interest, while retaining graphic design as an alternative. For web design I would ask which openings involve testing and what hours they require. For graphic design I would ask what a beginner's portfolio should demonstrate and how often deadlines change the schedule.”

Graphic design first is also defensible if the explanation gives more weight to Nia's visual-layout interest and still handles both schedule limitations. Do not claim a job, admission or fixed hours are guaranteed. Compare and revise the three fields.
