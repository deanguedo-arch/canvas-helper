# CO2-04 — Recognize discrimination and seek help

Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Assessment reference A2. All case records are fictional. Alberta Human Rights Commission records checked September 28, 2026. Authoring packet, not a legal determination.

## 1. Start here

Being treated badly at work can leave someone uncertain about what to call it or whom to ask. A useful first step is to separate what happened, why a protected characteristic may be connected, and what still needs to be understood. You do not need to decide a legal case before asking for information or support.

You will examine a documented disability-related concern, practise updating a record when an employer provides new information, and then prepare a support plan for a different scheduling and religious-accommodation concern. Use the supplied fiction; no personal experience or identity disclosure is requested.

Your route is **Meet the situation and read the documents → Learn the method → Follow a complete example → Practise with support → Check your understanding → Apply independently → Review and complete**. Pause whenever needed, including between the case and the example. All learning can be completed in this course. No interview, confrontation, complaint submission or external account is required. Guided writing and six questions are practice; the independent concern record and support plan form your final evidence.

**Vocabulary.** A protected ground is a characteristic covered by human rights law. An adverse impact is a negative effect, such as loss of a work opportunity. A connection is evidence that a protected characteristic was a factor in that effect. Accommodation is a change that addresses a need linked to a protected ground so a person can participate. An allegation is a concern someone reports; it is not itself a legal finding. An inquiry asks for information; a complaint begins a formal process with its own requirements.

Optional disclosure: **Official pages for a current concern**. Dated in-course records below support every required task. Direct links are optional for checking current information. These practice fields do not contact the Commission or your employer.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Jules is 18 and works at fictional Clearview Tickets, an Alberta provincially regulated employer. Jules has a hearing disability and normally uses written confirmations of work instructions. Jules is seeking scheduled shifts at the customer counter, where work includes reading booking information, speaking or writing with customers, and processing tickets. The concern concerns access to those shifts.

### Document A — Role and training record

**Counter-shift criteria, issued September 1, 2026:** complete booking training, demonstrate accurate booking entry in the assessment, and be available for the offered shift. Employees can use the approved written instruction checklist.

**Training record, September 4:** Jules completed the training and met the booking-entry assessment standard. The record shows availability for the two counter shifts advertised for September 14 and 16. No missed qualification is identified in this record. This does not reveal every operational decision the employer may have made.

### Document B — Dated communication record

| Date | Exact supplied record |
|---|---|
| September 5 | Jules emailed supervisor Pat: “I would like the September 14 and 16 counter shifts. Because of my hearing disability, please provide changes to the instructions in writing as well as spoken instructions. I can use the approved checklist.” |
| September 6 | Pat replied by email: “I am not assigning counter shifts to someone with your hearing disability. You can stay on filing.” No individual assessment or accommodation discussion is included in that reply. |
| September 8 | The published schedule assigned Jules to filing and assigned the two counter shifts to other staff. The reasons for the other staff's assignments are not supplied. |

Jules saved the role criteria, training record, email thread and schedule. No threat of violence or immediate physical danger is reported. Jules wants advice and a fair review; Jules has not made a formal complaint.

### Document C — Supplied workplace support route

> An employee may raise a concern with HR coordinator Ash, including a concern involving the employee's immediate supervisor. Ash can receive a written summary and arrange a private discussion. The employee may ask to bring a support person. Requests about work-related access needs can be made in writing. This route is an available option, not a requirement to confront Pat alone.

### Document D — In-course human rights records

**Alberta Human Rights Commission, “Discrimination,” “Protected grounds,” and “Rights & Responsibilities”; checked September 28, 2026.** Employment is a protected area. A discrimination inquiry examines a protected characteristic, a negative impact and whether the characteristic contributed to that impact. Examples of protected grounds include disability, religious beliefs, race, colour, ancestry, place of origin, gender, gender identity, gender expression, sexual orientation, marital status and family status. The Act's age ground concerns people 18 or older. A concern outside a protected ground may still need another workplace response.

**Commission, “Duty to accommodate at work”; checked September 28, 2026.** Employers must consider needs connected to protected grounds and take reasonable accommodation steps up to undue hardship. Workers explain relevant needs and cooperate in the process. Assessment is individual; a preferred solution is not automatically the only reasonable one. Disability-related documentation may be needed, but a specific diagnosis does not have to be disclosed to the employer. Undue hardship requires more than ordinary inconvenience.

**Commission, “Making a complaint,” “Accessibility,” and “Contact us”; checked September 28, 2026.** The Commission provides human-rights information and can explain complaint requirements; it does not give legal advice. Its stated complaint limit is one year after the discriminatory act or treatment, so timely information matters. Support may include accessible formats, interpretation, a support person or adjusted communication arrangements. Availability is discussed according to the person's needs. A formal complaint has its own process; an inquiry does not automatically file one.

### Read the documents

**Which statement gives evidence of a possible connection between Jules's disability and the lost counter shifts?** Choose, then select **Check document reading**.

- A. Pat's September 6 email explicitly connects the counter-shift decision to Jules's hearing disability. **Correct.** The supplied wording directly supports investigating that connection.
- B. Every other staff member has identical qualifications and circumstances. Feedback: Their full records are not supplied.
- C. Jules dislikes filing more than counter work. Feedback: The documents do not establish that preference, and preference alone would not explain a protected-ground connection.
- D. All managers always reject accommodations. Feedback: That generalization is unsupported and is not needed to investigate this case.

## 3. Learn the method

### Use three connected questions

Start with the employment event rather than a label. What opportunity, condition or treatment changed? Then ask which protected ground may be involved and what evidence connects it to the negative effect. The source's three-part test helps focus the record, but this lesson does not replace the full assessment of the facts, explanations and applicable law.

| Question | Jules's evidence | What the evidence does not decide |
|---|---|---|
| Is a protected characteristic involved? | Hearing disability is stated in Jules's request. | Every medical detail or diagnosis. |
| What is the negative effect? | Jules requested two counter shifts and was assigned filing instead. | The complete reasons for every other worker's assignment. |
| What suggests a connection? | Pat's email expressly links the decision to disability. | The final legal outcome after a complete review. |

Not every disappointment establishes discrimination. If two qualified applicants seek one shift, one will not get it; that fact alone does not show a protected-ground connection. At the same time, a rule that sounds identical for everyone can create a barrier for someone with a protected need. “Everyone must receive instructions only by hearing them once” could disadvantage a worker who needs accessible communication. Ask what the rule does in practice.

### Do not require proof of a person's private motive

A worker often cannot know what a manager privately intended. Document the words, decisions and effects instead. A stated positive intention does not erase a barrier, and feeling upset does not by itself establish the legal connection. Jules has a specific email worth preserving; there is no need to add guesses about Pat's character.

Use three labels while writing: **fact**, **possible interpretation**, **information needed**. “Pat sent the September 6 email” is a fact within the supplied record. “Disability appears to have been a factor” is a supported concern. “I want the employer's individual assessment and explanation of the decision” identifies needed information. Writing “possible” does not mean dismissing the concern; it shows where a proper review is still required.

### Understand accommodation as a process

Accommodation aims to address a barrier connected to a protected need. The worker should explain the relevant work need and participate in exploring reasonable options. The employer should consider it individually and take reasonable steps up to undue hardship. In Jules's case, the request is written confirmation of changed instructions. It is not a request to waive accurate booking work.

The job's actual tasks and available controls matter. An unsupported assumption that a disability makes someone unsuitable is different from examining whether the person can do the work with reasonable accommodation. A request also does not automatically entitle someone to any chosen shift or their preferred solution. The question is whether the opportunity and accommodation need were considered fairly under the applicable duties.

Protect privacy while explaining the need. A support record can state “written confirmations needed for work instructions” without including unnecessary medical history. Other employees' private diagnoses or religious practices are not yours to demand or circulate. Relevant scheduling criteria and accommodation steps can be discussed without collecting a coworker's medical file.

### Choose a safe, suitable support route

An internal route such as Ash may help arrange review. If the supervisor is involved in the concern, an alternative recipient can be useful. A worker may ask about an accessible communication method or support person before a meeting. The Commission can provide information about human rights and complaint requirements. The source describes approaching the other party as something to consider; this lesson does not require a worker to confront them first or alone.

Different needs may need different supports. The Commission addresses protected-ground concerns; Employment Standards addresses minimum employment conditions such as wages and hours; OHS addresses workplace health and safety. An immediate threat requires moving to safety and an appropriate emergency response rather than waiting for an administrative complaint process. Our fictional records do not describe such a threat.

Keep dates because formal processes have time limits. The Commission's one-year limit is a reason to seek timely information, not a reason to wait until the last day. An internal discussion should not be assumed to pause that limit. For a real situation, verify the current process and get help calculating deadlines if uncertain; this lesson does not ask you to calculate or submit a real claim.

**Optional first-step hint:** “Begin your guided response with one exact event from the timeline, its effect and the words connecting it to the protected ground. The question you write can ask for information a fair review would need.”

## 4. Follow a complete example

I begin with September 5 because it establishes the request and the work-related need before the decision. The September 6 email supplies a direct connection, so I do not need to invent a remark about disability that was never in the record. The September 8 schedule establishes the effect.

I include Jules's completed assessment because it is relevant to the published criteria. I still leave room for the employer to explain its assessment and the other relevant circumstances. That is different from ignoring the explicit email: the concern deserves a specific response.

**Complete concern record and support plan:**

> Jules requested the September 14 and 16 counter shifts on September 5 and asked for written confirmation of changed instructions because of a hearing disability. The supplied training record says Jules completed the booking training and met the entry standard. Pat's September 6 email states that Jules will not receive counter shifts because of the hearing disability. The September 8 schedule assigns Jules to filing. This is a possible disability-discrimination and accommodation concern: there is a protected characteristic, an adverse work-opportunity effect and explicit wording connecting them. The complete individual assessment and any other relevant explanation are still needed; this record does not decide the legal outcome.
>
> Jules can keep the original criteria, training record, emails and schedule together and make a dated summary. An available safe next step is a written request to HR coordinator Ash for a private review of the counter-shift decision and the written-instruction need, with a support person or accessible communication if helpful. Jules can also seek information from the Alberta Human Rights Commission about the protected-ground concern and current requirements without first proving the case. A question is not a filed complaint. The dated source says the formal complaint limit is one year after the discriminatory treatment, so the internal review should not become indefinite waiting.

“Jules is automatically entitled to both shifts and a guaranteed legal victory” goes beyond the evidence. “There is no issue unless someone admits bad intentions” also misreads the method: the recorded words and effects already support seeking a review.

## 5. Practise with support

Keep Documents A–D. Add this new entry:

**Document E — September 10 response from Ash.** “I have received Jules's concern. I will review the training record, the September 6 email and the allocation criteria. I would like to discuss the written-instruction need with Jules. No decision about the two shifts has yet been made.” Jules can reply in writing or ask for an accessible meeting with a support person under the supplied workplace route.

Write an update in three fields:

1. **What is confirmed, and what remains unresolved?** Distinguish receipt/review from a changed decision or completed accommodation.
2. **Draft Jules's response to Ash.** Identify the relevant work need and two records/questions to include. Use a short respectful message; do not supply a real medical diagnosis.
3. **Record the follow-up and support choice.** Choose written communication or an accessible meeting, state why it fits this case and keep the option of Commission information visible.

Controls: **Show guided hint**, **Compare with the model**, **Revise my update**. Hint: “Ash has promised a review, not yet its result. Ask what will be reviewed and when Jules can expect an update; do not invent a promised date.”

Comparison feedback: “A useful update retains the original concern and accurately records Ash's response. If you wrote 'problem solved,' revise it to 'review pending.' If you demanded coworkers' private records, replace that with a question about the relevant allocation criteria. If you included a diagnosis, remove information not supplied or needed.”

**Complete matching model:**

1. “Ash received the concern and intends to review the records and discuss the instruction need. The shift decision and accommodation response are still unresolved.”
2. “Ash, I can discuss this in writing. My work need is written confirmation when instructions change; I can use the approved checklist. Please include the September 4 training result and September 6 email in the review. How were the counter-shift criteria applied, and what options for accessible instructions will be considered? When should I expect an update?”
3. “Written communication provides a clear record and fits Jules's stated instruction need. Jules can keep the dated reply with the original documents and consider a support person if a meeting becomes useful. Commission information remains an option, and Jules should not assume an internal review changes the formal time limit.”

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q1`.

Which combination is relevant to the source's discrimination test?

- A. A worker dislikes a decision and a coworker agrees. Feedback: Feelings and agreement do not by themselves establish the protected-ground connection.
- B. A manager intended to be helpful and a policy exists. Feedback: Those facts do not settle whether a protected person experienced a negative impact.
- C. A worker has any disagreement, regardless of the reason. Feedback: A workplace disagreement can need action without meeting the human rights test.
- D. A protected characteristic, a negative impact and evidence the characteristic was a factor. **Correct.** These are the connected elements to investigate in a protected area.

### Q2

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q2`.

Which statement stays within Jules's records?

- A. “Pat has always discriminated against every disabled worker.” Feedback: The supplied record does not establish a history involving other workers.
- B. “Pat's email explicitly links the shift decision to Jules's disability, supporting a concern that needs review.” **Correct.** It uses the documented connection without declaring a final outcome.
- C. “No concern exists because Jules still has a filing assignment.” Feedback: A negative effect on a work opportunity can still matter.
- D. “The other workers' medical histories must be published.” Feedback: They are not supplied or needed for this initial record, and privacy matters.

### Q3

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q3`.

Which statement best explains workplace accommodation?

- A. A process to address a protected need through reasonable changes, with worker cooperation and employer duties up to undue hardship. **Correct.** It concerns participation and an individual process.
- B. Automatic approval of every preferred arrangement. Feedback: A preferred option is not automatically the only reasonable one.
- C. Removal of every essential task without examining options. Feedback: Actual work requirements and possible arrangements need individual consideration.
- D. A worker's obligation to disclose their entire medical history publicly. Feedback: Relevant need information is different from unnecessary public medical disclosure.

### Q4

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q4`.

Ash says a review will occur. What should the record say now?

- A. The formal complaint has been filed. Feedback: No formal complaint was submitted by receiving an internal concern.
- B. Jules has won both shifts. Feedback: Ash says no decision has yet been made.
- C. The concern was received and review is pending; the decision and accommodation outcome remain unresolved. **Correct.** This preserves the difference between process and outcome.
- D. The September 6 email should be deleted. Feedback: A new review does not remove the relevance of the original record.

### Q5

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q5`.

Which support plan is available without making Jules confront Pat alone?

- A. Publicly post every document with personal information. Feedback: That can compromise privacy and is not the supplied review route.
- B. Wait indefinitely because an internal review cancels all deadlines. Feedback: Do not assume a review suspends the Commission's time limit.
- C. Ask OHS to decide every discrimination concern. Feedback: OHS is the health-and-safety route; the Commission provides human-rights information.
- D. Use Ash's written review route or seek Commission information and ask about accessible communication/support. **Correct.** These are suitable options; the lesson requires no live action.

### Q6

Implementation key: `practice:co2-04:q6`.

Why can an identical scheduling rule still need an accommodation review?

- A. Every employee preference is a protected ground. Feedback: Preferences and protected-ground needs are different.
- B. A rule applied to everyone may negatively affect someone because of a protected need. **Correct.** Its practical effect and possible accommodation matter.
- C. Employers cannot set any schedules. Feedback: Employers can manage work subject to applicable duties.
- D. A stated staffing concern automatically proves undue hardship. Feedback: The situation and possible arrangements need evidence; ordinary inconvenience is not enough by itself.

## 7. Apply independently

### A different concern: a religious observance and a schedule

Noor is 19 and an ordinary employee at fictional Riverbend Market, a provincially regulated Alberta business. Prepare a **concern record and support plan**. Do not decide a legal complaint. Use these records only; no personal religious information is requested.

**Document F — Request, October 5, 2026.** Noor emails scheduler Vic: “I need to attend a religious observance on Saturday, October 17, from 10:00 a.m. to noon. I am requesting accommodation for this religious need. I can work that day from 1:00–5:00 p.m. or discuss another reasonable arrangement. Please let me know what information you need.” Noor's usual Saturday shift is 10:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m.

**Document G — Reply and schedule.** Vic replies October 6: “We are short-staffed, so the 10:00–2:00 shift stays.” No accommodation options or supporting staffing details are discussed in the reply. The schedule posted October 9 retains Noor's 10:00–2:00 shift. Two coworkers' October 17 shifts have changed, but Noor does not know why, when they requested changes or what duties they cover. No comment attacking Noor's religion is reported. Noor remains employed and has not been disciplined.

**Document H — Available route and privacy.** Riverbend has a people-services coordinator, Sam, who accepts written accommodation-review requests and can arrange a discussion with a support person. Noor may keep copies of Noor's own request, reply and schedule; private coworker records are not supplied. Noor wants the religious need considered and prefers a written first contact. No immediate physical threat is reported. No review beyond Vic's reply has occurred.

Write one coherent product using four fields:

- **Dated facts and effect** — summarize the request, response and resulting conflict, without inventing discipline or remarks.
- **Possible protected-ground connection and missing information** — identify the ground, explain the concern and name at least two relevant questions. Distinguish what is known from what the coworker comparison cannot establish.
- **Draft the review request** — write a short message to Sam describing the need, the request already made and a reasonable question about options. Do not demand private coworker details or claim only one accommodation can work.
- **Support, records and timing plan** — choose a safe accessible route, list relevant records and explain why timely information about the Commission's process matters. You are planning, not submitting anything.

Success means recognizing a possible protected-need barrier, using accurate dates and effects, asking for individual consideration and retaining uncertainty about the complete staffing evidence and outcome.

**Compare with a complete model**

> On October 5, Noor requested accommodation for a religious observance from 10:00 a.m. to noon on October 17 and offered 1:00–5:00 p.m. or another reasonable arrangement. Vic's October 6 reply cited short staffing, and the October 9 schedule retained 10:00 a.m.–2:00 p.m., which overlaps the stated observance. Religious beliefs are a protected ground; the remaining schedule creates a possible barrier linked to that need. There is no supplied religious insult or disciplinary action. We need to understand the actual staffing/essential-duty needs and what options were considered. The coworkers' changes do not prove unequal treatment because their reasons, timing and duties are unknown; their private information should not be demanded.
>
> Sam, please review my accommodation request for October 17. My October 5 message explained a religious need from 10:00 a.m. to noon and offered 1:00–5:00 p.m. or another reasonable arrangement. Vic's reply cited short staffing, and the posted shift still overlaps the observance. Could we discuss which duties need coverage and whether a later shift, swap or another arrangement could address both needs? Please explain what relevant information you need from me and when I can expect a response. I prefer written communication initially and may ask for a support person if a meeting is useful.
>
> Noor can retain the original request, reply and schedule with a dated summary and any response. Sam is an available internal review route; Noor can also seek Alberta Human Rights Commission information about religious accommodation and complaint requirements. Accessible communication or a support person may be requested according to need. The source's one-year complaint limit makes timely information important; Noor should not assume internal discussion pauses it. The final outcome cannot be determined from these limited records. No live request or complaint is part of the lesson.

Other reasonable options can be proposed, but no answer should guarantee a particular shift or treat “short-staffed” as a complete proven undue-hardship finding.
