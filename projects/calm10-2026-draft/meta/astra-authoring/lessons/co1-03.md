# CO1-03 — Practise a truthful interview

Complete script for lead review. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### Let the interviewer hear what you actually did

An interview is a conversation used to explore whether a person's experience and a role's needs fit. A general claim such as “I work well under pressure” gives the listener little to examine. A short, accurate example gives them actions, results and something useful to ask about.

You will prepare answers for Rowan using supplied fictional experience notes. Your final product is a 4–6 sentence answer about a disagreement, followed by a role-research question and a sensible way to investigate it. You may rehearse aloud or read silently; no recording, live partner or real interview is required.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **The role and Rowan's event notes**. Learn **Build an answer a listener can follow**. Follow the cancellation example, then practise with the activity-card mix-up. Check six ideas and complete **Answer a question about disagreement**. Compare your answer with the annotated model, revise and sign off. Pause after rehearsal if useful; the written fields save.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### The role and Rowan's event notes

Rowan is preparing for a fictional welcome-helper interview. The role involves explaining sign-in steps, keeping attendance information accurate, sharing tasks and asking the shift lead when an exception needs approval. Training is provided for the local system. The notice does not state which career qualification could lead to a later program-coordinator role.

An **experience record** is a description of an event already completed. It is evidence for an answer, not a script Rowan must pretend happened in a paid job. These events took place at school or in a volunteer setting. Use that context truthfully.

**Record A — cancelled helper, school welcome evening**

Ten minutes before doors opened, one of three helpers could not attend. Rowan was responsible for organizing the sign-in table. Rowan asked the remaining helper to check the attendance list, took the greeting position and explained the revised two-person arrangement to the event lead. The lead approved it. Arrivals moved through without a long line, and every arrival was recorded. After the event Rowan wrote, “Next time I would prepare a backup task plan before opening.”

**Record B — mixed activity cards, community open house**

Two stacks of activity cards had been placed under the wrong headings. Rowan noticed the mismatch before visitors used them, checked the approved activity list with the lead and relabelled the stacks with the lead's confirmation. A second helper distributed them. Visitors received the intended card for their activity. Rowan has used this local list once; the record does not establish expertise with every registration system.

**Check the record:** Which wording correctly identifies Rowan's role in Record A?

A. Rowan completed all three jobs alone. B. Rowan has managed a paid staff for years. C. Rowan took greeting, arranged a two-person split and obtained the lead's approval. D. Rowan prevented every future cancellation.

Feedback: C describes supplied personal actions. A erases the other helper. B invents employment and experience length. D turns one successful response into a guarantee.

## 3. Learn the method

### Build an answer a listener can follow

A **behavioural question** asks for an example of how someone acted, such as “Tell me about a time a plan changed.” Start by identifying the skill the question is exploring. A question about change needs an event involving change; an unrelated achievement may be impressive but fail to answer it.

Give enough **situation** and **task** for the listener to understand the problem and your responsibility. Then spend most of the answer on **action**: what you personally did and why. Finish with the **result**, limited to what the record actually reports. You can add learning or a next step when it helps. This structure is a guide for meaning, not a requirement to say the words “situation, task, action, result” aloud.

**Answer path:** what happened → what I was responsible for → what I did → what changed → what I learned or would improve. In a short answer, one sentence can contain more than one part. A precise action often communicates more than several adjectives.

### Own your action and share the result

Teamwork creates a common wording trap. “We kept the line moving” credits the group but does not identify Rowan's contribution. “I did all the work” supplies a contribution by inventing one. Combine them: “I took greeting and arranged the task split; together we kept arrivals moving and recorded everyone.” The result can be shared while the action remains specific.

A **limit** marks what an example cannot prove. One local event does not establish years of professional management, and following an approved procedure does not establish qualification to design a new system. Naming a sensible limit strengthens the evidence instead of weakening it.

### Practise the delivery and prepare a useful question

Rehearsal helps you notice long openings, missing verbs and unexplained results. Read your answer once as if a listener knows nothing about the event. If speaking is useful to you, say it aloud without recording. If you prefer silent rehearsal, underline the action and identify the result in the text. Both routes check the same content; accent, eye contact and performance style are not scored here.

**Networking** means building professional connections to exchange information and learn about opportunities. It need not begin with asking for a job. A career event, a workplace information session or a short informational conversation can help a person understand a role. A focused question respects the other person's time: “What training lets a welcome helper take on program coordination?” is more useful than “Tell me everything about your career.” In this course, you draft the question and choose an appropriate route; you do not contact anyone.

A **mentor** is someone who shares experience and feedback to support another person's development. Mentoring is a relationship, not a credential or a guarantee of an opportunity. An **internship** is an organized period of work-related learning; its actual duties, supervision, training, pay and entry conditions must be checked in its own agreement. A **mock interview** rehearses interview questions before a real selection process. This lesson provides that rehearsal in writing. Preparing a question for a mentor or employer is different from claiming that a conversation or internship has already happened.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### “Tell me about a time a plan changed.”

Record A directly answers this question: a helper cancelled shortly before opening. I need only one sentence of background. Rowan's response is the important part, so I name the task split and approval. The recorded result is a moving line and complete attendance, not a numerical increase in efficiency that nobody measured.

**Complete answer:** “At a school welcome evening, a helper cancelled ten minutes before opening while I was organizing sign-in. I asked the remaining helper to check the attendance list and took the greeting position myself. I explained the revised arrangement to the event lead and received approval. Together we kept arrivals moving without a long line and recorded everyone. Next time I would prepare a backup task plan before opening.”

**How the answer works:** sentence 1 provides situation and responsibility; sentences 2–3 identify Rowan's actions and authority; sentence 4 credits the team result; sentence 5 uses the recorded improvement. It does not imply that Rowan supervised paid staff or handled an unknown computer system.

“I'm always calm and can solve any problem” sounds confident but supplies neither an event nor a limit. The complete answer lets a listener judge the evidence. The next example gives you a different event to organize, rather than asking you to copy this one.

## 5. Practise with support

### Answer from the card mix-up

Question: **“Describe a time you noticed a detail that needed correcting.”** Use Record B only. First choose the most relevant action: **relabelled after checking the approved list with the lead / ran every registration system / cancelled the open house**. Use **Check my evidence**.

Feedback: The first action is recorded and relevant. The second invents expertise; the third did not happen. The cancellation from Record A does not belong in this answer.

Fill **Situation and responsibility**, **My action and the shared result**, and **A truthful limit or next improvement**. Turn those notes into **My practice answer**, 4–6 sentences. Read it aloud or silently, then open **Compare the card example**.

**Hint:** The card problem was discovered before visitors used the stacks. Keep that sequence; do not invent visitors receiving incorrect cards.

**Complete model:** “At a community open house, I noticed two stacks of activity cards were under the wrong headings before visitors used them. I checked the approved list with the lead and relabelled the stacks after confirmation. Another helper distributed the cards, and visitors received the intended card for their activity. This shows attention to detail in that local process, not expertise with every registration system. Next time I would check the labels against the approved list before distribution begins.”

The last sentence is a proposed improvement, so it says “would,” not “I already introduced a new policy.” Compare your own action with sentence 2 and your result with sentence 3. Use **Return to my answer** to revise any unsupported claim. Writing receives a model and criteria, not an automatic quality score.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What should come first when selecting an interview example?

A. Identify what the question is asking about. B. Choose the longest story. C. Invent an impressive result. D. Remove every reference to school.

Answer A. A guides relevance; B confuses length with evidence; C is untruthful; D discards valid experience without reason.

### Q2 — Which result is supported by Record A?

A. Revenue doubled. B. Rowan trained every helper. C. The team eliminated all future queues. D. Arrivals moved without a long line and everyone was recorded.

Answer D. A–C add facts not observed; D uses the actual record.

### Q3 — How can an answer credit teamwork and show personal contribution?

A. Use only “we” without explaining a role. B. Name Rowan's action, then describe the team's result. C. Claim every task personally. D. Omit the result.

Answer B. A hides contribution; B distinguishes action and outcome; C invents ownership; D loses useful evidence.

### Q4 — Why say “next time I would check labels early”?

A. To claim a policy was already implemented. B. To imply the entire event failed. C. To identify a proposed improvement without inventing a past action. D. To avoid answering the question.

Answer C. A changes a future proposal into false history; B ignores the successful correction; C correctly marks the proposed step; D misreads a relevant learning point.

### Q5 — Which is an appropriate informational question about the role brief?

A. “Will you guarantee me a career?” B. “Can you send private participant records?” C. “Why do all jobs work exactly alike?” D. “What training would help a welcome helper progress toward program coordination?”

Answer D. A seeks an unsupported promise; B requests private data; C assumes uniform roles; D investigates a specific missing pathway detail.

### Q6 — What does this written rehearsal establish?

A. Practice in selecting and explaining evidence. B. A completed real job interview. C. A guarantee of employment. D. Supervised workplace experience.

Answer A. A names the actual learning activity; B–D claim events or outcomes not supplied by the online task.

## 7. Apply independently

### Answer a question about disagreement

New question: **“Tell me about a disagreement in a team and how you responded.”** Use this new record, not the cancellation or card mix-up.

**Record C — late arrivals at a community workshop**

Two helpers wanted late arrivals to skip sign-in to shorten the line. Rowan was responsible for keeping attendance accurate under the lead's procedure. Rowan restated the shared aim of welcoming visitors while recording everyone, proposed one helper greeting and one helper staying with the list, and asked the lead to confirm the arrangement. The lead approved it. The team kept the line moving, every visitor signed in and no participant details were shared. The record does not say Rowan acted without support or that the disagreement never recurred.

The welcome-helper role still values accurate information and approved processes. A later program-coordinator role interests Rowan, but its training pathway is not supplied.

Fields: **My 4–6 sentence interview answer**, **My question about the role or pathway**, and **How I could investigate that question** (choose an appropriate route and explain it in one sentence: employer information session, informational conversation, or current official role/program information). Do not send a message; describe the route.

**Complete model:** “At a community workshop, two helpers wanted late arrivals to skip sign-in while I was responsible for keeping attendance accurate. I restated our shared aim of welcoming people while recording everyone. I proposed splitting greeting from list-checking and asked the lead to confirm the arrangement. With approval, the team kept the line moving, recorded every visitor and protected participant details. I would use the same approach of naming the shared goal and checking the procedure in a future disagreement; this one supported event does not prove I can resolve every conflict alone.”

**Question and route model:** “What training is needed to move from welcome-helper tasks to program coordination? I could ask that focused question at an employer information session, then verify any credential requirement on the current program or regulator's official page.”

An answer need not use these exact words. It must explain Rowan's own actions, preserve the lead's role and credit the team outcome. Compare and revise your fields.
