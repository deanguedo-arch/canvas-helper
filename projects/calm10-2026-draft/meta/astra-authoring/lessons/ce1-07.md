## 3. Learn the method

### Separate goal, route and timing

A **goal** is what you are trying to accomplish. A **route** is the way you will work toward it. **Timing** places the route into an actual day or week. Maya's goal is to explore design through two samples. The club and written package are possible routes. Tuesday or Saturday are possible times if they fit the records.

A changed constraint does not automatically cancel the goal. It does require checking the route. Conversely, new experience might show that the goal itself no longer fits. The useful question is which part the new evidence changes, rather than treating every revision as failure.

### Count the whole block

An hour-long session can require two hours when travel is included. Do not overlap travel with the task that requires being somewhere else. A plan must also allow transitions: putting materials away, getting ready for the next commitment or dealing with a small delay. A **buffer** is time left unassigned so every step does not depend on a perfect sequence.

Use the time line:

**Travel or preparation → focused activity → return or transition → next commitment.**

If any part overlaps a fixed commitment, the route needs a change. Having a total number of “free hours” is not enough if those hours occur at the wrong time or in fragments too short for the task.

### Make digital habits serve the plan

Digital tools can support planning through calendars, reminders, saved notes and accessible materials. They can also consume the time reserved for the task. In a one-hour block, repeated message checking can leave less time for the example and draft even though the calendar still says “one hour.” A useful plan names one manageable habit: silence nonessential notifications during the example, keep the task document visible or put a reminder at the transition time.

The habit is a support, not a claim that everyone can solve every barrier through willpower. If the required task still cannot fit, revise the route or amount of work. Keep privacy in mind when using a digital planner: fictional labels are enough for this course, and a public calendar need not reveal another person's personal circumstances.

### Treat overload as information

Time pressure can make a plan stressful. A reasonable planning response is to reduce conflicting demands, break a task into workable pieces or use an available support. Removing sleep, meals or all recovery to make the chart balance would hide a new problem. In this case the protected rest period stays protected.

Support does not have to mean someone making the decision for you. Here the alternative written package and clear scheduling notice provide support. A precise future question can identify an uncertainty; no actual message needs to be sent to finish the lesson.

### State when the plan will be reviewed

A revision note should record **old assumption → new evidence → changed route/timing → review trigger**. A review trigger is the event that makes another look useful: a changed timetable, unavailable materials or evidence that the task takes longer than planned. Include a date too, so “review later” becomes an action you can locate.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Replace Maya's Tuesday route

I begin with the old assumption: Maya could leave home at 15:30 on Tuesday and return at 17:30. The new family commitment uses that entire interval. Moving only the session label would not solve the travel conflict.

M3 supplies a written alternative taking 40 + 20 = 60 minutes. M1 supplies a full hour at home on Saturday from 10:00 to 11:00, with the device/materials available. This is a supported replacement; I am not inventing an online class or permission. Thursday's existing session remains feasible under M2.

**Revised board:** Tuesday 15:30–17:30 stays with the family commitment. Thursday keeps the 15:30 departure, 16:00–17:00 practice and 17:30 return. Saturday 10:00–10:40 becomes the written example and 10:40–11:00 the layout draft. Saturday 11:00–11:30 remains a buffer, and 14:00–16:00 remains rest/recreation.

**Completed revision note:** “The old plan assumed Tuesday travel was available. The new four-week family commitment removes that route, but the design-learning goal still matters to Maya. The supplied one-hour written package fits Saturday 10:00–11:00 without changing Thursday or the protected rest block. During the example, Maya can silence nonessential notifications and use an 11:00 reminder to stop and review. On Sunday October 11, review whether the written work actually fitted the hour. If it took longer, revise the next week's task size or use another supplied option rather than consuming the protected rest time.”

The unsupported alternative “do both the family commitment and travel” makes two simultaneous demands. “An online session must be available Wednesday” invents an option. The revised board works because its timing and alternative both appear in the documents.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Which part of Maya's plan changed first?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q1`.

A. The value of all education. B. The need for meals. C. The availability of Tuesday travel time. D. The existence of every design occupation.

Answer: C. A and D are far broader claims than the notice. B is not changed. C identifies the actual new constraint.

### Q2 — A session runs 16:00–17:00 and needs 30 minutes of travel each way. What full block does it require?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q2`.

A. 16:00–17:00. B. 15:30–17:30. C. 15:30–16:00 only. D. Any hour on that day.

Answer: B. A omits both journeys. B includes outward travel, session and return. C includes only the outward journey. D ignores when the session occurs.

### Q3 — Which statement describes a buffer?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q3`.

A. A guarantee no delay can occur. B. Another name for a prerequisite. C. A reason to remove rest. D. Unassigned time that allows transition or a small delay.

Answer: D. A overpromises. B confuses time planning with course dependency. C creates rather than manages pressure. D explains the function.

### Q4 — What makes the written package a supported alternative?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q4`.

A. The notice gives its availability, duration and necessary access. B. Maya hopes any website will do. C. The word “online” means it takes no time. D. Someone might create a course later.

Answer: A. A supplies facts needed to plan. B is a guess. C ignores the work. D relies on an unconfirmed future option.

### Q5 — Repeated message checking makes a task exceed its planned hour. What is a useful response?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q5`.

A. Pretend the task still took an hour. B. Try a defined focus habit and use actual task time to revise the plan. C. Conclude Maya cannot learn design. D. Delete all sleep from next week.

Answer: B. A hides evidence. C turns one difficulty into a fixed judgement. B connects a support to a review. D introduces an unreasonable trade-off.

### Q6 — Which is a concrete review trigger?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-07:q6`.

A. “Think about it sometime.” B. “Assume transport will always return.” C. “Never change a written goal.” D. “Recheck if the package consistently takes longer than the allocated hour.”

Answer: D. A has no event or date. D identifies new evidence that calls for revision. C treats planning as fixed. B invents certainty.
