## 1. Start here

### Start with the need and the deadline

Credit can provide something before enough cash has been saved. It also commits future money. A useful decision begins with what is needed, when it is needed and what alternatives actually exist. You will compare a loan with a confirmed temporary solution and a saving plan.

Your final product is a short recommendation about getting required work boots before a supplied deadline. You will calculate the borrowing cost and test the timing of a cash alternative. You will not apply for credit or use personal information.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Nico's device decision**, learn **Test purpose, timing and cost**, and follow a comparison that uses a confirmed loaner. Try a changed deadline with support. Answer six checks, then solve **Taylor's boots deadline**. Compare, revise, sign off and complete. The worked comparison is a useful pause point; entries save automatically.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Nico's device decision

Nico is a fictional adult beginning a ten-month online certificate course. The course needs a device that meets its supplied technical requirements. This case does not claim those requirements belong to a real provider.

**Device quotation:** a suitable device costs **$420**, including all charges. A financing offer requires **18 monthly payments of $32**, with the first due one month after purchase, plus a **$20 setup fee paid today**. No other fee applies if paid on time. The purchased device meets the requirements; its useful life is not guaranteed by the repayment term.

**Nico's budget note:** $100 is available today. After essential costs, $80 can be set aside at the end of each of the next four months. This amount is confirmed for the case. No later income is promised. Nico has no other device.

**Equipment-library confirmation:** “A suitable device is reserved for Nico from the course start until immediately after the fourth monthly saving deposit. There is no borrowing fee or deposit. It must be returned on that date. The same $420 purchase quotation is valid through that date.”

The equipment loaner and the money loan are different arrangements. The library lends an object to be returned; the financing offer lends purchasing power to be repaid in money.

**Read the documents:** Which fact makes saving during the first four months a possible way to meet the course's immediate device need?

A. The loan has 18 payments. B. The device might last forever. C. A suitable free loaner is confirmed through the fourth deposit. D. Nico has $420 today.

C supplies access while saving. A describes repayment, not immediate access without borrowing; B invents durability; D contradicts the $100 available.

## 3. Learn the method

### Test purpose, timing and cost

First name the **need**, the job the purchase must do. Nico needs reliable access to a suitable device for the course. Owning it on day one is one possible way to meet that need, not the definition of the need itself. Separating access from ownership makes alternatives visible without dismissing the importance of the course.

Next mark the **deadline**. Money expected after a deadline cannot pay a bill due before it. Nico's fourth deposit arrives just before the loaner must be returned, and the quotation remains valid. Those details make the proposed sequence possible. If either were unknown, the plan would need verification rather than a confident promise.

Then calculate the **whole obligation**. Add every payment and separate fee, subtract the cash price to find the extra financing cost, and compare the duration with the intended use. Eighteen payments continue beyond a ten-month course. A useful purchase can still have costly or inconvenient terms; a label such as “education” does not settle affordability.

### Separate a confirmed alternative from a hopeful one

“Use a loaner” is helpful only when its suitability, dates and cost are known. “Work more hours” is not confirmed income unless the case supplies approved hours and take-home money. A sound recommendation names what is available and what remains uncertain. It does not solve every gap by assuming a gift, refund or new job.

A loan application may request identity, income, existing debts and credit information. Approval is a lender's decision; the lesson's ability to calculate a payment is not approval. A co-borrower shares contractual obligations, rather than merely acting as a reference. Never add another person to a plan as if their consent and repayment ability were supplied.

### Compare consequences across time

A payment that looks small can limit later choices. Conversely, delaying a genuinely necessary purchase without a workable alternative can interrupt learning or work. Compare the actual consequences of each feasible option. Use an explicit decision line: **need → deadline → available alternatives → total and timing → recommendation**.

**Optional FCAC reading record:** *Buy now, pay later plans* identifies instalment arrangements as credit with terms to inspect, including fees and payment timing. Read September 28, 2026. The supplied quotation controls this exercise; no real product application or outside reading is required.

**Hint:** On paper or in the supplied note field, put “now,” “month four,” “month ten” and “month eighteen” on a line. Place access, saving and repayment beside the correct point before deciding.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Meet the device need without inventing money

Financing costs 18 × $32 + $20 = **$596**. Compared with the $420 cash price, the extra cost is **$176**. Nico could pay the setup fee from the $100, but a first payment that fits today says little about all 18 future payments when only four months of available money are confirmed.

With the loaner, Nico retains the $100 and saves 4 × $80 = $320. The total reaches **$420** at the fourth deposit. The loaner covers the time until then. This plan uses the specific confirmation, not a general assumption that equipment libraries always have stock.

**Complete recommendation:** “I recommend using the reserved loaner while setting aside $80 at each of the next four month-ends. With $100 already available, Nico reaches $420 before returning it and can use the valid cash quotation. Financing would total $596, $176 more, and continue for eight months beyond the course. I would confirm any changed loaner date or price before relying on this sequence.”

“Wait four months without a device” ignores the immediate learning need. “The $32 payment is cheap, so borrow” ignores total cost and the unconfirmed later budget. The model explains how access, not just money, is maintained.

## 5. Practise with support

### The loaner is available for only two months

Change only the confirmation: the suitable free loaner must now be returned immediately after the **second** $80 deposit. No replacement, extension or gift is confirmed. The $420 price and $100 opening money remain unchanged.

Enter **Cash available at return ($)** and **Gap to the cash price ($)**. Write **What to verify before choosing**, two sentences naming a specific missing arrangement and why it matters. Use **Check the deadline**.

The correct cash amount is $100 + 2 × $80 = **$260**; the gap is $420 − $260 = **$160**. An answer of $420 includes deposits arriving after the new deadline. An answer of $160 as available cash confuses the gap with the balance. A zero gap assumes a resource that has not been supplied.

**Supported model:** “Only $260 is available when the loaner is due, leaving $160 short. I would check whether a suitable extension, another confirmed device or a different payment arrangement can cover the remaining period and at what cost. Until that is known, the original cash plan does not meet the access deadline. Financing remains an option to assess, but I cannot declare its later payments affordable from the four-month budget alone.”

Revise your note so it asks a question rather than claiming approval. There is no requirement to contact a real provider.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What is Nico's underlying need?

A. Own the most expensive model. B. Have suitable device access for the course. C. Make 18 payments. D. Improve a credit score through any purchase.

Answer B. A adds a preference; B names the task the device must perform; C is a financing method; D invents a goal and result.

### Q2 — What is the total financing cost in the original offer?

A. $32. B. $420. C. $576. D. $596.

Answer D. A is one payment; B is the cash price; C omits the $20 setup charge; D adds every payment and that fee once.

### Q3 — Which statement about a temporary alternative is justified?

A. Check suitability, dates and charges before relying on it. B. Every library has the same loaner. C. A suggested alternative is already reserved. D. Return dates never matter.

Answer A. A tests whether the alternative solves the case; B–D assume facts that could make the plan fail.

### Q4 — Why compare loan duration with course duration?

A. A course automatically cancels debt at its end. B. Devices always break on the last course day. C. Payments may continue after the original use or income situation changes. D. A longer loan must have no fees.

Answer C. A and B invent events; C identifies an ongoing obligation; D confuses length and charges.

### Q5 — Which is confirmed money for Nico's two-month loaner case?

A. Four future deposits before return. B. $100 plus two $80 deposits by return. C. A hoped-for gift. D. The full credit limit as savings.

Answer B. A includes late money; B matches the dates; C is unconfirmed; D confuses borrowed capacity with owned funds.

### Q6 — A lender approves an application. What still needs review?

A. Nothing; approval guarantees comfortable repayment. B. Only the advertisement colour. C. Whether a friend likes the device. D. Actual costs, payment dates and fit with the borrower's budget and needs.

Answer D. A overstates approval; B and C do not assess the obligation; D keeps the decision connected to circumstances.

## 7. Apply independently

### Taylor's boots deadline

Taylor, a fictional adult, needs approved work boots **14 days from today**. A written workplace specification is supplied, and the quoted boots meet it. Their all-in cash price is **$144**, valid through day 14. Taylor has **$60 available now** and a confirmed **$90 net payment on day 7** that can be used for this purchase after other costs.

A financing quotation requires **six monthly payments of $26**, first due one month from today, plus a **$12 setup fee today**. There are no other charges for on-time payment. A notice mentions a free borrowed pair for five days, but their fit has not been checked; no longer loan is offered. Do not assume they meet the specification or bridge day 14.

Enter **Financing total ($)**, **Extra cost above cash ($)** and **Cash available on day 7 ($)**. Write **My recommendation for Taylor**, 3–4 sentences linking the requirement, dates, cost and any uncertainty. The final product is a recommendation, not a credit application.

**Complete model:** “Financing totals 6 × $26 + $12 = $168, $24 above the $144 cash price. Taylor will have $60 + $90 = $150 on day 7, before the day-14 deadline, leaving $6 after buying the suitable quoted boots. I recommend that cash purchase under the supplied confirmation and price validity. I would not rely on the unverified five-day borrowed pair to satisfy the requirement.”

A response can discuss financing's earlier access, but the supplied case does not require the boots before day 14. Explain any different choice without inventing an earlier deadline. Compare and revise.
