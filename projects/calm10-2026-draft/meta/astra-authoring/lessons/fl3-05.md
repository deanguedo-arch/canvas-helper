# FL3-05 — Build a plan for more than one goal

Complete authored script. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### Coordinate the goals before choosing products

A financial plan connects goals, available money, dates and review points. It should show which goal receives each contribution and what changes when a goal is finished. You will build a phased plan and identify the kind of information or service that could help review it.

Your final product is a coordinated plan for two fictional goals that cannot both meet their original dates. You will keep the supplied contribution limit and target amounts while proposing realistic revised dates. All work can be completed here.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Fei's planning notes** and the support directory. Learn **Build one plan from several targets**, follow the phases, then practise a smaller monthly amount. Answer six checks. Complete **Mina's revised dates**, compare your plan, revise and sign off. Pause after the phase table if useful; fields save.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Fei's planning notes

Fei is fictional and has two goals. The amounts include all charges in this exercise, prices stay constant, and no interest or investment return is assumed.

| Goal | Target | Already allocated | Desired deadline |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Suitable laptop | $480 | $120 | After month 8 deposit |
| Training fund | $1,200 | $100 | After month 30 deposit |

Fei can allocate **$70 after other costs at the end of each month**. Emergency money is separate and not available for these goals. The laptop is the nearer priority. Any money left after finishing it may be redirected to the training fund. The training fund is a saving target, not a claim about a real course's admission, tuition or eligibility.

### What different planning supports can do

This directory is supplied so you can evaluate support without contacting anyone.

| Support | What it can help with | What still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| Government budget or goal calculator | Organise figures and test stated assumptions | It does not know whether income, costs or product terms are accurate |
| Financial institution's product staff | Explain their accounts, access rules and charges | Product range, compensation and fit; a sales conversation is not a guarantee |
| Appropriately qualified financial planner or advisor | Help examine goals and circumstances within their expertise | Credentials, relevant registration, services, fees and potential conflicts |
| Official tax or program information | Explain the relevant rules or requirements | Whether the particular person's circumstances meet those rules |

FCAC's *Choosing a financial advisor*, read September 28, 2026, recommends examining qualifications, services and compensation. A title alone is not enough to establish expertise. This lesson asks you to choose a useful question, not hire a professional or provide private records.

**Read the notes:** What is the maximum combined monthly contribution for the two goals?

A. $70 for each, or $140. B. Any amount needed to make the dates work. C. $70 total. D. The $220 already saved every month.

C is the shared amount after costs. A doubles it; B invents resources; D confuses opening balances with recurring money.

## 3. Learn the method

### Build one plan from several targets

Start with each gap: target minus money already assigned to that goal. Then calculate what an even contribution would require before its deadline. Finally add the contributions to check the shared limit. If that fails, a plan needs a change, not more confident wording.

For Fei, the laptop gap is $360 over eight months, requiring $45 monthly. That leaves $25 of the $70 for training during those months. The training goal does not need its own unchanging contribution forever; after the laptop is funded, the full $70 can move to training.

A **phase** is a period during which the allocation rule stays the same. Name the start and end, each goal's monthly amount and the resulting balance. At a boundary, carry forward the balance rather than starting over or counting the opening money again.

### Include an action and a review point

“Save $70” does not say where it goes or when to review. A stronger instruction names the month-end transfer, the destination and the checkpoint. A separate record for each goal helps prevent double-counting. The money can be tracked without requiring a particular bank feature; a labelled ledger works for this exercise.

A review point asks whether the assumptions still hold. Has the target price changed? Is the monthly amount still available? Has a deadline moved? Has a new work, education, family or health situation changed the priorities? A plan is a working decision, not a promise that life will remain unchanged.

### Select support for the unresolved question

Choose a resource because it can answer the question. A calculator can check a projection but cannot confirm admission requirements. An official program page can state requirements but cannot promise personal investment returns. Product staff can explain an account's terms, while broader planning advice requires appropriate competence and an understanding of how the person is paid.

For a real advisory relationship, verify relevant credentials and registration independently and ask about costs and scope. In this course, all cases use supplied records and simulated questions. Your progress never depends on reaching a teacher, advisor or financial institution.

**Hint:** Write a two-row phase table first. Use the first deadline as the boundary. Check each row's contributions add to no more than the available amount.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Follow Fei's money through the phases

In months 1–8, allocate $45 to the laptop and $25 to training. The laptop reaches $120 + 8 × $45 = **$480**. Training reaches $100 + 8 × $25 = **$300**.

In months 9–20, put $70 monthly toward training. Twelve deposits add $840, bringing it to **$1,140**. A final **$60 in month 21** reaches $1,200, before the month-30 target. The last $10 of that month's available $70 remains unallocated until a new decision.

**Complete plan:** “At each month-end for eight months, assign $45 to the laptop and $25 to training. Review the laptop price and available amount at month 4. Once the laptop reaches $480 at month 8, redirect up to $70 monthly to training. Twelve full deposits and $60 in month 21 finish the training target. I would use a goal calculator to check the arithmetic, and verify actual program costs separately before treating $1,200 as sufficient for a real enrolment.”

Putting $45 toward the laptop and $70 toward training during the first eight months would require $115. A final total cannot rescue a monthly instruction that spends unavailable money.

## 5. Practise with support

### The monthly amount becomes $60 from the start

Use a new version of Fei's plan with **$60 monthly throughout**, unchanged opening balances, targets and deadlines. Keep the laptop contribution at $45 for months 1–8. Allocate what remains to training, then redirect all $60 after the laptop is funded.

Enter **Monthly training contribution in months 1–8 ($)**, **Training balance after month 8 ($)** and **Month when training first reaches $1,200**. Write **The revised phase instruction**, two sentences. Use **Check the phases**.

Targets are $15, $220 and month 25. Training begins phase two with $100 + 8 × $15 = $220, leaving $980. Sixteen $60 deposits in months 9–24 add $960, bringing it to $1,180; the final $20 arrives in month 25. If you answer month 17, you counted the remaining deposits from month zero instead of after month eight. If you answer $300 at month eight, you kept the old $25 training contribution.

**Model:** “For months 1–8, contribute $45 to the laptop and $15 to training, reaching $480 and $220. Then put $60 into training in months 9–24 and the final $20 in month 25. The training deadline still works, though the target is reached four months later than under the $70 plan.”

Select a useful next question from the directory: **Has the actual program cost changed?**, **Can a calculator guarantee admission?**, **Will every asset rise?**, or **Does a title prove an advisor's expertise?** The first can be checked through current official program information; the other questions contain promises or assumptions the resource cannot supply. Revise your plan after comparing.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Why use phases in Fei's plan?

A. To hide missed payments. B. To redirect an existing contribution when an earlier goal is funded. C. To count opening savings again each month. D. To guarantee future income.

Answer B. A is not the purpose; B coordinates timing; C double-counts money; D overstates planning.

### Q2 — What must be checked in every phase?

A. That all goals have the same amount. B. That the headings look impressive. C. That the period is exactly a year. D. That combined contributions fit available money and balances carry forward correctly.

Answer D. A and C are unnecessary restrictions; B does not validate a plan; D checks feasibility and continuity.

### Q3 — What can a calculator confirm?

A. The output produced by its inputs and assumptions. B. Personal admission to a program. C. A future market return. D. That all entered costs are accurate.

Answer A. A describes calculation; B–D require evidence outside the tool.

### Q4 — Why ask how an advisor is paid?

A. All paid advice is wrong. B. Free advice is always complete. C. Costs and incentives are relevant to assessing the service. D. Compensation guarantees a result.

Answer C. A and B are blanket claims; C identifies relevant information; D invents certainty.

### Q5 — Which is a useful review trigger?

A. A changed goal price, income amount or deadline. B. Only the colour of the ledger. C. Never review once written. D. Wait until every goal fails.

Answer A. A affects feasibility; B is cosmetic; C and D delay needed adjustment.

### Q6 — A plan exceeds the shared monthly amount. What should happen?

A. Count the same income twice. B. Assume unconfirmed gifts. C. Hide one goal's contribution. D. Revise an amount, date, priority or genuinely confirmed resource.

Answer D. A–C conceal the gap; D changes the plan's actual constraints transparently.

## 7. Apply independently

### Mina's revised dates

Mina is fictional and has **$35 monthly after other costs** for two goals. The nearer goal costs **$300**, with **$80 saved**, and was desired after month 5. A longer-term goal costs **$900**, with **$150 saved**, and was desired after month 24. Both dates can be moved; target amounts stay fixed in this task. No interest, gifts or additional income are supplied. The nearer goal has priority until funded. All deposits arrive at month-end.

Enter **Earliest month the nearer goal can be fully funded** and **Earliest month both goals can be fully funded under that priority**. Write **Mina's revised plan**, 5–6 sentences showing the phases, original-date problem, final partial deposits, a review point and one useful support question. Keep all contributions within $35 monthly.

**Complete model:** “The nearer gap is $220, which would need $44 monthly over five months, more than the available $35. I would put $35 there in months 1–6, bringing it to $290, then the final $10 in month 7. The other $25 from month 7 goes to the longer goal, taking that balance from $150 to $175. Months 8–27 add twenty $35 deposits, reaching $875, and $25 in month 28 finishes the $900 target. I would revise the dates to months 7 and 28 and review the available amount and prices at month 3. My support question is: ‘Can the goal calculator confirm that both revised dates fit the shared $35 monthly limit?’”

The original month-24 deadline cannot fit both unchanged amounts: $230 already saved plus 24 × $35 totals $1,070, below the $1,200 combined targets. Explain the revised dates rather than presenting them as failure. Compare and revise.
