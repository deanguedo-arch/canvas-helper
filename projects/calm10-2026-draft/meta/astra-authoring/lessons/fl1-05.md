## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Marin needs at least 4 GB of data each month and calling/texting. The existing phone works, is compatible with both fictional networks and meets Marin's needs. Both offers have confirmed coverage for Marin's current location and include 8 GB, calling and texting. No extra usage occurs in the comparison. Marin can spend at most $45 each month after other commitments.

**Offer A — new phone and service, twelve-month written agreement**

Headline: “Service from $20/month.” Service is $20 monthly for months 1–3, then $38 monthly for months 4–12. A new device adds $15 each month for twelve months: $180 total, with no interest. No setup fee. All stated prices include taxes. Service prices are fixed to this schedule for the twelve-month agreement. If service ends early, the unpaid device balance becomes due immediately; no other cancellation charge is included in this fictional agreement. At the end of twelve months the device is paid off. Service continues at $38 monthly unless cancelled or changed under the stated process. No resale value is assumed.

**Offer B — use the existing phone, month-to-month service**

Service is $43 monthly; no device payment or setup fee. All stated prices include taxes. Cancel before the next monthly billing period without a cancellation fee; there is no partial-month refund in this fictional comparison. For the twelve-month calculation, assume this price remains unchanged. The notice does not promise that every future year will have the same price.

## 3. Learn the method

### Read the service, device and exit terms

A **subscription** is a recurring arrangement for access to a service. Its payment repeats until the agreement ends or changes. A **fixed-term agreement** has a specified commitment period; **month-to-month** describes a shorter recurring period. Neither phrase tells you every cancellation rule. Read the actual notice, payment dates and any separate device obligation.

A promotional price is a temporary price. Count the months it applies, then count the remaining months separately. Adding a device payment can change the bill even while the service price stays the same. Three labels are therefore useful: service, device and combined bill. The combined bill is the one that must fit the monthly allowance.

**Payment timeline:** A months 1–3 = service 20 + device 15; months 4–12 = service 38 + device 15; after month 12 = stated ongoing service, device paid off. B has one service payment and no new device in this case. This is a comparison of ways to meet a need, not a claim that buying a new phone and keeping an old one provide identical ownership benefits.

### Count the whole period, then test the hardest month

A twelve-month total answers “What will the supplied plan cost over a year?” It does not answer “Can I meet every bill?” An average can hide higher later months. Check the largest required bill and the amount available then. Paying a low first bill is not evidence that the later schedule is affordable.

An **exit cost** is money due when the agreement ends. Some balances remain payable even when service stops. Here, ending A does not erase the unpaid device amount. Separate payments already made from the remaining balance; do not count the same device payment twice.

### Use the right consumer reference

**CRTC reference record — read September 28, 2026:** the Wireless Code requires clear wireless contract information, including prices, service limits, commitment period, relevant cancellation terms and one-time costs. It applies to wireless service; it is not a rulebook for every streaming or gym subscription. [Optional official page: sections A and B](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/codesimpl.htm).

For an unfamiliar real agreement, use its permanent written copy to verify these terms. In this lesson all needed terms are supplied. No external page can fill a deliberately missing fictional price; flag the gap instead.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Compare Marin's first year

A's service costs 3 × $20 + 9 × $38 = $60 + $342 = $402. Its device costs 12 × $15 = $180. The first-year total is **$582**. B costs 12 × $43 = **$516**. B is **$66 lower**, while using a phone Marin already has.

A's first three bills are $35, within the $45 allowance. Its next nine bills are $53, **$8 above** that allowance. B's $43 bill fits, leaving $2 monthly. The first-year average of A is $48.50, but the $53 later bill is the more direct timing check.

**Completed recommendation:** “I recommend B for Marin's stated needs. Both plans supply the required data and coverage, and the existing phone is adequate. B costs $516 over twelve months under the given price assumption, $66 less than A. Its $43 bill fits the $45 monthly allowance. A includes a new phone, but its $53 bills after month three exceed the allowance; the $20 headline omits that later price and the device payment.”

“A is only $20, so it saves $23 monthly” compares one part of one offer with the whole of another. Always state what is included in the figure.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What does A's $20 headline describe?

A. The full bill forever. B. The device price. C. Service only during the first three months. D. The exit balance after month nine.

Answer C. A ignores both conditions; B confuses device and service; C reads the promotion's scope; D refers to a different calculation.

### Q2 — Which amount tests A against Marin's allowance in month four?

A. $53. B. $20. C. $15. D. $38.

Answer A. A combines the $38 service and $15 device; B is the expired promotion; C and D include only part of the bill.

### Q3 — Why is “month-to-month means every term can be ignored” wrong?

A. It means the service is free. B. It automatically purchases a phone. C. It applies only to music. D. Billing, notice and cancellation conditions still need to be read.

Answer D. A–C invent meanings; D identifies the written conditions that remain relevant.

### Q4 — A customer ends A after nine months. What device amount remains?

A. $180. B. $45. C. $135. D. $0.

Answer B. A ignores nine paid instalments; B is three remaining $15 payments; C is already paid; D assumes cancellation erases the debt.

### Q5 — Can the Wireless Code alone establish cancellation rules for a music subscription?

A. Yes, all digital services use it. B. Yes, if paid by phone. C. No; check rules and written terms applicable to that service. D. No subscription has written terms.

Answer C. A and B confuse payment/device with service scope; C identifies the need for an applicable reference; D is an unsupported generalization.

### Q6 — Which comparison is fair for these needs?

A. Headline against headline only. B. Newest device always wins. C. Upfront price alone. D. Required functions, full payment timeline, exit terms and ownership differences.

Answer D. A–C omit material evidence; D compares the arrangements against the user's actual purpose.

## 7. Apply independently

### Sacha's two service offers

Sacha has a suitable existing phone and needs 6 GB monthly for study. Both fictional offers provide 8 GB and calling/texting, with confirmed coverage in the current location. No device purchase or extra usage is involved. Sacha can spend **$50 at signup** and **$42 in any later month**. All charges include taxes.

**Offer C:** twelve-month written service agreement: $28 monthly for six months, then $40 monthly for six months; $18 setup paid with the first bill. These prices are guaranteed for the stated twelve-month schedule. The agreement permits cancellation before a new billing month without a cancellation fee; the setup fee is not refunded. No partial-month refunds.

**Offer D:** month-to-month service at $36, no setup or cancellation fee; no partial-month refunds. Use $36 throughout the comparison, while noting that this scenario does not establish future prices indefinitely.

Sacha might need only six full months because of a possible move. Coverage in the new location has not been checked. Compare both a full year and departure after six full months, before the seventh bill. Do not assume a move automatically makes either current plan usable in the new location.

Fields: **C: twelve-month total ($)**, **D: twelve-month total ($)**, **C: six-month total ($)**, **D: six-month total ($)** and **My recommendation for Sacha** (4–6 sentences, including first-bill affordability, the difference between the two periods, and one fact to verify before the move).

**Model:** “C costs 6×$28 + 6×$40 + $18 = $426 for twelve months. D costs 12×$36 = $432, so C is $6 lower over a year. For six months C costs $186 and D $216, making C $30 lower. C's first bill is $46 and later maximum $40, within Sacha's supplied limits. I recommend C under these terms, while checking coverage at the possible destination before deciding whether to keep service there. The cancellation terms, not the phrase ‘twelve-month,’ establish the cost of leaving in this fictional offer.”

A preference for D can be explained, but it should acknowledge the actual price differences and cannot invent an exit fee for C. Revise after comparing.
