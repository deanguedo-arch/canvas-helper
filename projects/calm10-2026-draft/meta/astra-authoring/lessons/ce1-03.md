# CE1-03 — Compare routes into a career

## 1. Start here

### Two ways forward can have different costs and conditions

Leah likes organizing equipment and explaining how to use it. She wants to explore technical support at community events. An employer-training route and a college route appear possible. Before choosing, Leah needs to know what each route actually leads to, what she must do first and which costs fit the available money.

You will compare fully supplied fictional routes. These invented programs are designed to practise the method; they are not Alberta admission rules or trade qualifications. All research for the required work happens in the records on this page. Your final product is a primary-and-backup route comparison for Owen, a different learner.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### The destination comes first

A pathway is a sequence of learning and work steps. Two programs can concern similar equipment yet lead to different jobs or credentials. Leah therefore begins with a description of the work she wants to investigate.

**Fictional Harbour Events role record — Event support assistant**

“Assist a supervisor with equipment inventories, room diagrams and visitor instructions. Follow the venue's approved setup procedures. This role does not include electrical installation or independent repair work. For this practice recruitment, the employer will consider either completion of its four-month trainee route or the ten-month Event Support certificate described below. Selection is not guaranteed by either route. Applicants need clear communication, accurate checking and willingness to ask about unfamiliar work.”

**Leah's preparation and constraints**

Leah has completed the fictional school courses Communication 1 and Practical Mathematics 1. She has organized equipment lists for a club and wants more practice explaining unfamiliar instructions. She can live at home throughout either route. She has $1,400 available for the listed training/travel costs and wants to enter the field within one year. Everyday living costs are unchanged between these examples and are outside the comparison.

**Reading check:** Why can these two routes be compared for the same first role? Select one and use **Check my reading**.

Feedback: **A** traces the connection to the role record. **B** invents universal equivalence. **C** is a topic similarity, which is not sufficient. **D** contradicts both route records.

## 3. Learn the method

### Compare destinations before comparing prices

A credential is a record of a qualification, such as a certificate, diploma or degree. An employer training record describes learning recognized by that employer. Similar subject matter does not make them interchangeable. A short course about equipment does not automatically authorize work requiring a different qualification. The link between a route and a destination must be stated or verified.

In this case, the employer has supplied that link for one assistant role. It has not said the routes are identical or that either leads to every technical occupation. Leah should compare the routes for the stated purpose and keep their different learning and completion records visible.

### Recognize different kinds of learning routes

The names below help you interpret a provider's offer. They describe different learning arrangements; a longer list of credentials does not automatically make one route the best fit.

| Term | What it means for a route comparison |
|---|---|
| **Certificate** | A post-secondary credential commonly used for focused preparation for an occupation. Check the particular program: some certificates are for beginning learners, while others require an earlier diploma or degree. |
| **Diploma** | A post-secondary credential preparing learners for a field or group of occupations. Its admission requirements, learning and completion requirements belong to the specific program. |
| **Degree** | A credential from a degree program. A bachelor's degree includes theoretical or academic learning; advanced degrees build on earlier study. The word alone does not identify a particular occupation's entry requirements. |
| **Apprenticeship** | A formal education route combining on-the-job learning with technical classroom training and required examinations. Check the specific trade's requirements and the qualification earned. Ordinary employer training is not automatically an apprenticeship. |
| **Microcredential** | Recognition of a specific skill or competency after a brief learning program and assessment. There is no single fixed duration or assessment format. Check what is assessed and whether the intended employer or next program recognizes it. |
| **Dual credit** | High-school programming connected with post-secondary or industry pathways. Offerings vary by school. Alberta distinguishes single-enrolled courses, which provide high-school credits, from dual-enrolled courses, whose credits appear on both high-school and post-secondary transcripts. Check the actual pathway and credit recognition; the name does not mean a whole diploma or degree has been completed. |

**Dated source record — checked September 28, 2026:** Alberta's credential guide distinguishes program outcomes and admission requirements. ALIS explains that microcredentials assess focused skills and usually add to other learning. Alberta's dual-credit guidance distinguishes the two course arrangements above. These are category descriptions, not admission offers. [Optional credential guide](https://www.alberta.ca/post-secondary-credential-types), [optional microcredential explanation](https://alis.alberta.ca/plan-your-career/workplace-trends/micro-credentials-can-boost-your-career/) and [optional dual-credit guidance](https://www.alberta.ca/dual-credit-planning-and-programming).

For Leah, Route A is the employer training record described in the fictional document, not a registered apprenticeship. Route B is the fictional certificate described there. Neither record promises transfer credit, a microcredential or dual credit. Their stated connection to the assistant role is what makes this particular comparison possible.

### Trace entry conditions back to today

A prerequisite must be completed before the next course or step. Route B's Mathematics 2 is not a label to copy onto a plan; Leah needs time to complete it after Mathematics 1. Her current strengths help explain why the direction interests her. Her growth area—explaining unfamiliar instructions—helps her inspect what each route teaches.

Think in a chain:

**Current experience and courses → missing preparation → entry decision → route → possible work.**

A career cluster can suggest related directions, but a cluster does not choose the school course for Leah. The actual route record supplies the requirement. This is why high-school planning belongs in a career comparison.

### Compare complete cost categories

A one-time fee is paid once. A monthly travel cost repeats for each month in the stated route. To compare listed direct costs, multiply each recurring amount by its number of months, then add the one-time costs. Do not compare $120 with $80 and stop: four months and ten months produce different totals.

The resulting total has a boundary. Here it includes the listed fees, supplies and travel only. We cannot subtract trainee earnings because none are supplied. We also cannot claim the total includes every living expense. An estimate becomes useful when you state what it includes and avoid pretending unknown amounts are zero.

### Make a primary route and a real backup

A **primary route** is the option you would investigate first under the current evidence. A **backup route** is a researched alternative with its own conditions. A backup is not simply “do something else.” It might become possible after another course, more saving or a changed start date.

Show the trade-off: the benefit you gain and what you give up. A shorter route may fit a deadline but depend on employer selection. A longer route may provide broader practice but exceed the available budget. A justified choice explains both.

**Dated reference record:** ALIS's *Explore School and Program Options*, read September 28, 2026, recommends beginning with the intended direction and investigating entry, timing, costs and alternative learning routes. Specific program information belongs with its provider. Our fictional records supply those facts here. [Optional original](https://alis.alberta.ca/explore-education-and-training/explore-school-and-program-options/).

**Optional hint:** Start a cost line with “fee + supplies + (monthly travel × months).” Label the total “listed direct costs.”

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Investigate Leah's trainee route first

I first check the destination. The employer explicitly considers its trainee route for the assistant role. I then check Leah's current record: she has both listed school prerequisites. That makes investigation reasonable, but it does not mean she has been selected.

For costs, I keep one-time and recurring charges separate. Travel is $120 × 4 = $480. Adding $300 fees and $200 supplies gives **$980 in listed direct costs**. Against the $1,400 available, that leaves $420 for the stated comparison. I do not call this spare spending money for every purpose because other future needs are outside these records.

**Completed Route A note:** “I would investigate Route A first. Leah has its two stated school prerequisites, it takes four months and its listed direct costs are $300 + $200 + ($120 × 4) = $980. That is within the $1,400 available for these categories. It also includes supervised practice with visitor instructions, which relates to her growth area. The next decision is the March 15 selection round: a training place is not confirmed. I would keep Route B as a researched alternative while checking its extra course and cost.”

“Route A guarantees a paid technical career for $980” would add three unsupported ideas: selection, pay and access to broader technical work. The correct calculation does not make those claims true.

## 5. Practise with support

### Complete the other side of Leah's comparison

Use Route B and Leah's same $1,400 limit. Calculate travel and total listed direct costs. Then identify the course she still needs and explain what must change before B becomes a workable backup.

Enter **Route B travel cost ($)** and **Route B listed total ($)**, then use **Check my costs**. Correct calculations receive “You multiplied all ten travel months and included both one-time costs.” A travel answer of $80 receives “That is one month; this route lasts ten months.” A total of $1,930 receives “You added only one month of travel.” Other errors receive “Rebuild the line: $1,600 + $250 + ($80 × 10).”

Write in **Preparation still needed**, **Trade-off and funding gap** and **Backup condition and checkpoint**. Short notes are enough.

**Optional hint:** Subtract available funds from listed cost to describe the gap. Do not invent a grant or assume someone will pay it.

**Compare with the Route B model**

“Travel is $800 and the listed total is $2,650. Leah would need another $1,250 for those categories. She also needs Mathematics 2 after Mathematics 1, completed before the September start. Route B offers a longer course with group-project practice, but takes ten months and requires more money. It could be a backup if the course sequence and funding are resolved before the April 30 application decision; admission itself remains unconfirmed.”

The extra learning can be a benefit without automatically making B better. Use **Return to my comparison** to revise costs or reasoning after checking.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Two courses concern the same equipment. What must be checked before calling them equivalent routes?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q1`.

A. Whether their brochures use the same colour. B. Whether they have the same number of words. C. Which work or qualification each actually leads toward. D. Whether one name sounds more advanced.

Answer: C. A and B do not establish a destination. C checks the relevant relationship. D substitutes a title impression for evidence.

### Q2 — A travel estimate is $50 monthly for six months. What belongs in the six-month cost comparison?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q2`.

A. $300. B. $50. C. $56. D. $600.

Answer: A. A multiplies the monthly amount by six. B includes only one month. C adds unlike units. D doubles the required total without a supplied reason.

### Q3 — Leah has Route A's school prerequisites. What follows?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q3`.

A. Pay is guaranteed. B. Every related occupation is open to her. C. The employer must select her. D. She meets those stated preparation conditions; selection remains separate.

Answer: D. A invents pay. B expands beyond the role. C ignores selection. D distinguishes eligibility for consideration from an outcome.

### Q4 — What makes Route B a useful backup?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q4`.

A. Calling it Plan B without reading it. B. Ignoring the extra course. C. Assuming its fee will disappear. D. Knowing its preparation, costs, timing and conditions for use.

Answer: D. A gives no actionable alternative. D makes the backup reviewable. C invents funding. B leaves a required step unresolved.

### Q5 — Why not subtract possible trainee pay from the listed costs?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q5`.

A. Earnings never matter. B. No pay amount or arrangement is supplied in this case. C. Travel is always free. D. All trainees receive identical pay.

Answer: B. A incorrectly dismisses income. C contradicts the records. B explains the evidence boundary. D invents a universal rule.

### Q6 — Which high-school step follows from Leah's Route B record?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-03:q6`.

A. Investigate Mathematics 2 after her completed Mathematics 1. B. Replace all courses with event work. C. Assume a certificate cancels prerequisites. D. Put Mathematics 1 after Mathematics 2.

Answer: A. A follows the supplied dependency. B is unsupported. C invents an exemption. D reverses the chain.

“C costs $900 + $150 + ($60 × 6) = $1,410. D costs $1,800 + $300 + ($50 × 12) = $2,700.
