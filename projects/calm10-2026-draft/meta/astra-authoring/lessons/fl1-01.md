# FL1-01 — Read a pay stub

Authoring status: complete script for lead review. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### Did the payment match the work?

Mika has received a pay stub: the employer's written breakdown of a payment for work. The amount deposited is smaller than hours multiplied by the hourly wage. That difference might be an explained deduction, an error, or a detail that needs checking. Looking only at the deposit cannot tell you which.

You will learn to follow the payment from hours to earnings to deductions to the amount received. Your final product is a short pay-check note for a different worker, including calculations and a question about a mismatch. All records are fictional. You will not enter your own employment or tax information.

**How to complete this lesson**

1. In **Read Mika's records**, locate the period, hours and current amounts. Check your reading.
2. In **Follow the money**, learn how the pay-stub parts fit together. Follow the complete calculation for Mika.
3. In **Check a missing shift**, calculate from a second record, check the numbers and revise your explanation.
4. Answer the six understanding questions, then complete **Check Ellis's two records**. Review the model and sign off on your final note before choosing **Complete lesson**.

You can pause after the guided calculation. All entries save; practice is separate from the final note. Optional CRA reference and vocabulary disclosures sit beside this roadmap.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Read Mika's records

Mika is an adult working at the fictional Pine Street Print Shop. A **time record** lists hours worked. A **pay stub** explains how an employer calculated a payment. A bank deposit is evidence of money received, but does not show the whole calculation. Read the two documents together.

**Pine Street Print Shop — statement of earnings**

Employee: Mika. Pay period: September 1–14, 2026. Payment date: September 18, 2026. Currency: Canadian dollars. This exercise includes one regular-pay line and three supplied deductions; no overtime, vacation-pay line, reimbursements or other adjustments.

| Earnings | Hours | Rate per hour | Current pay | Year to date, including this pay |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Regular work | 12.50 | $18.00 | $225.00 | $1,125.00 |

| Deductions | Current pay | Year to date, including this pay |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income tax withheld | $16.00 | $80.00 |
| Pension contribution | $10.80 | $54.00 |
| Employment Insurance premium | $3.60 | $18.00 |
| Total | $30.40 | $152.00 |

**Payment summary:** current gross $225.00; current deductions $30.40; current net deposit $194.60. Year-to-date net: $973.00.

**Mika's time record:** September 3, 4.00 hours; September 7, 4.50 hours; September 12, 4.00 hours. Mika confirms these are the hours actually worked. All deduction amounts are fictional supplied figures for reading practice, not rates or eligibility rules to use for a real worker.

**Check your reading:** Which two items should you compare first to check the hours on this payment?

A. September 1–14 time entries and the current 12.50 hours. B. The payment date and $973.00. C. The year-to-date gross and one shift. D. The pension contribution and hourly wage.

**Feedback:** A compares the same period. B mixes an issue date with a cumulative amount. C compares several pay periods with one shift. D cannot establish how many hours were recorded.

## 3. Learn the method

### Follow the money

**Gross pay** is earnings before deductions. For the single regular-pay line in this case, multiply hours by the hourly rate. The unit check helps: hours × dollars per hour produces dollars. The decimal 12.50 means twelve and a half hours; it does not mean twelve hours and fifty minutes.

A **deduction** is an amount removed from gross pay. Some payroll deductions contribute to public programs or tax withholding; others can relate to benefits or an agreed arrangement. The label tells you what is being withheld. It does not by itself prove the amount is correct or that it applies to every worker. In this lesson the amounts are supplied, so add them. Do not try to calculate current tax or contribution rates from memory.

**Net pay** is what remains after the listed deductions. It is the amount this statement says should be paid. Subtract deductions once: they are already removed from the displayed net amount. If you begin a spending plan with net pay and subtract them again, you make the available payment too small.

**One-payment map:** time entries → hours × rate → gross earnings → subtract current deductions → net payment. A second check compares that net figure with the actual deposit. A matching deposit can still come from incorrect hours, which is why checking only the last box is insufficient.

### Choose the right column and date

A different stub may include several earnings lines. **Overtime** is pay recorded for hours treated as overtime under the applicable arrangement; an **additional earning** might be a separately listed bonus. Read each line's supplied hours, rate and amount before adding gross pay. Do not assume that every worker's overtime threshold or rate is the same.

**A second format to read:** a fictional adult worker's statement lists regular earnings, 10 hours at $20 = $200; approved overtime earnings, 2 hours at the expressly supplied $30 rate = $60; and a $15 bonus. Gross is $200 + $60 + $15 = $275. These are the statement's supplied terms, not a lesson about legal overtime eligibility. A separately labelled expense reimbursement is money repaid for an expense; inspect its treatment on the actual statement instead of automatically calling every deposit amount wages.

**Current** means this pay period. **Year to date** combines payments within the current calendar year. The figures serve different questions. Use current amounts to check this deposit; use the cumulative column to examine totals over the year. Never subtract cumulative deductions from one period's gross.

The pay period and payment date also answer different questions. The period tells you which work is included. The payment date tells you when the money is paid. A shift after September 14 would not automatically belong on this stub simply because payment arrived September 18.

### Describe a mismatch without inventing its cause

If the time record and stub disagree, identify the two records, period and difference. An omitted shift is a possible explanation, but the documents may not tell you whether the cause was entry, approval or processing. A useful question requests a check of a specific line. “You stole my pay” is an unsupported conclusion from a mismatch alone.

**CRA reading note — retrieved September 28, 2026:** the CRA's pay-stub lesson distinguishes the pay period, current amounts and year-to-date totals. Use those labels to keep your calculation within one payment. [Optional CRA explanation](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/educational-programs/starting-work.html).

**Hint — First calculation:** Add the three time entries before multiplying. Keep cents to two decimal places when recording money.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Check Mika's payment

I start with the dates: all three time entries fall inside September 1–14. Their hours total 4.00 + 4.50 + 4.00 = **12.50 hours**. That matches the regular-work line. I multiply 12.50 × $18.00 = **$225.00 gross**.

Next I add only the current deductions: $16.00 + $10.80 + $3.60 = **$30.40**. Subtracting gives $225.00 − $30.40 = **$194.60 net**. The payment summary reports that amount. I can therefore say the supplied arithmetic and time entries agree; I have not independently verified real payroll rules.

**Completed pay-check note:** “For September 1–14, Mika recorded 12.50 hours. At $18.00 per hour, gross pay is $225.00. Current deductions total $30.40, leaving $194.60. These figures match the supplied statement. The record supports this calculation; it does not establish that the fictional deductions would apply to another worker.”

An answer of $73.00 comes from $225.00 − $152.00. It uses the year-to-date deduction total. The subtraction is arithmetically possible, but its two numbers describe different spans of time. Choosing the appropriate figures is part of doing the calculation correctly.

## 5. Practise with support

### Check a missing shift

This is a new record for Jules, an adult at fictional Valley Repairs. Period: October 1–14, 2026; payment date October 18. Rate $16.00 per hour. Supplied time entries are 5, 5, 4 and 6 hours, all within the period. The stub lists **18 hours**, **$288.00 gross**, supplied current deductions **$31.00**, and **$257.00 net**. There are no other earnings or adjustments in this record.

Enter:

- **Hours in the time record** — a number of hours.
- **Gross from those hours** — dollars.
- **Net calculated from the stub's listed gross and deductions** — dollars.
- **What needs checking?** — two sentences naming the mismatch and a precise question.

Use **Check my calculations**. Then compare your explanation with the model. The third field deliberately checks the stub's internal arithmetic; it does not ask you to invent revised deductions.

**Hint:** The stub can subtract deductions correctly and still list too few hours. Calculate its net separately from the gross supported by the time record.

**Numeric feedback:** Hours 20.00: the four entries total 20. An answer of 18 repeats the disputed stub. Gross $320.00: 20 × $16; $288 repeats the stub, and $32 is the difference rather than total gross. Net $257.00: $288 − $31. An answer of $289 uses corrected gross with the old deductions, which cannot establish a corrected real payment. Other answers: show the relevant operation and ask the student to compare their operands.

**Complete model:** “The time record totals 20 hours, which gives $320.00 gross. The stub uses 18 hours, or $288.00, a difference of 2 hours and $32.00 gross. Its listed net is internally consistent: $288.00 − $31.00 = $257.00. Could payroll check which two hours were omitted or assigned to another period and issue a revised breakdown if needed? The revised net is not established because revised deductions are not supplied.”

Return to your four fields and revise any part that treated $32.00 as a guaranteed net increase.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — A stub shows $400 gross and $60 current deductions. What net does it report?

A. $460. B. $340. C. $60. D. $400.

Answer B. A adds the withheld amount; B subtracts it once; C names deductions only; D ignores them.

### Q2 — The second-format stub lists $200 regular pay, $60 approved overtime pay and a $15 bonus. What gross do those lines support?

A. $200. B. $260. C. $275. D. $215.

Answer C. A omits both additional lines; B omits the bonus; C adds all three earnings amounts; D omits the overtime line. Use the supplied overtime rate rather than inventing a legal threshold.

### Q3 — The deposit matches the stub's net. What remains worth checking?

A. The time period, hours, rate and deduction entries. B. Nothing can be wrong. C. Only the employer's logo. D. Whether gross and net are identical.

Answer A. A checks inputs as well as the transfer; B assumes correct output proves correct inputs; C provides no pay calculation; D would ignore legitimate listed deductions.

### Q4 — Which question is most useful for a two-hour mismatch?

A. “Why is work unfair?” B. “Can I use someone else's stub?” C. “Are all deductions optional?” D. “Can you compare my October 1–14 time entries with the 18 hours on this statement?”

Answer D. A lacks a checkable detail; B substitutes a different worker's record; C does not address hours; D names the period and line to investigate.

### Q5 — Why should a budget normally start with this payment's net rather than gross?

A. Net includes all future wages. B. Net identifies the payment after listed deductions. C. Gross is always an error. D. Net already pays rent and groceries.

Answer B. A confuses a payment with a forecast; B identifies the received amount; C confuses a useful earnings figure with a mistake; D invents personal spending deductions.

### Q6 — A corrected gross is known but corrected deductions are not. What can be stated?

A. Net must rise by exactly the gross difference. B. Deductions become zero. C. The corrected gross and the need for a revised breakdown. D. The year-to-date figure is the new net.

Answer C. A and B invent the revised withholding; C separates established and missing information; D changes the meaning of the cumulative column.

## 7. Apply independently

### Check Ellis's two records

Ellis is an adult at fictional Northside Books. You are checking the November 1–14 payment, issued November 18. The agreed rate in this case is $17.00 per hour. There are no other earnings or adjustments. The time record lists November 2: 4 hours; November 5: 6 hours; November 9: 5 hours; November 12: 5 hours. The stub lists **19 hours**, **$323.00 gross**, supplied current deductions **$35.50**, and **$287.50 net**. The bank deposit is **$287.50**. Year-to-date gross is $2,040.00 and deductions $223.00; those totals are supplied only so you can select the proper column.

Create a pay-check note: calculate the hours and gross supported by the time record, check the listed net, explain the mismatch and write one specific payroll question. You are not asked to calculate revised deductions.

Fields: **Time-record hours**, **Gross supported by the time record ($)**, **Net supported by the current stub ($)**, and **My pay-check note** (about 4–6 sentences; include the gross difference and the limit of what you can conclude).

**Compare with a complete note:** “Ellis's four entries total 20 hours. At $17.00, the time record supports $340.00 gross. The stub records 19 hours and $323.00 gross, one hour and $17.00 less. Its net arithmetic is correct: $323.00 − $35.50 = $287.50, matching the deposit. Could payroll check the November 1–14 entries against the 19-hour line and explain or correct the difference? I cannot establish a revised net until the deductions are reviewed.”

Different wording is welcome. The essential distinction is between a deposit that matches the stub and a stub that matches the work record. Revise your own note after comparing.
