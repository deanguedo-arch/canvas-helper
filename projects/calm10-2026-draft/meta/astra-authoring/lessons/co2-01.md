# CO2-01 — Check the rules before a first shift

Authoring version: `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Implementation packet; learner source remains the canonical course HTML. Assessment reference: A2. All people, businesses, dates and schedules below are fictional planning documents. Official rule record checked September 28, 2026.

## 1. Start here

A shift offer can sound simple: “Can you close on Thursday?” To check it, a young worker needs more than the finish time. Age, workplace, school hours, tasks, permission and supervision can change which rules apply.

You will help Ellis check a proposed retail shift, practise checking a corrected offer, and then write a shift-review note for a different restaurant worker. Your final note will connect particular facts to particular rules and propose a workable next step. You are practising source-based investigation, not issuing a legal decision about a real employer.

Work through **Meet the situation and read the documents → Learn the method → Follow a complete example → Practise with support → Check your understanding → Apply independently → Review and complete**. Pause after the example or guided practice if useful. Your answers save automatically. Guided work and six questions are practice; the independent review note is your saved lesson evidence.

**Useful terms.** An employee works for an employer; the youth rules below concern employees. A permit is official permission for specified employment, not the same as a parent's consent. Retail sells goods, including food, directly to customers. Supervision means an adult is actually supervising the work; being able to telephone someone elsewhere is not the supervision supplied in our examples. A school day is a day on which this learner is scheduled to attend school.

You do not need to open a website, contact anyone or enter personal employment details to finish this lesson.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Ellis is starting an ordinary employee job at fictional North Path Books, an Alberta retail bookstore. Ellis wants to answer a shift offer before it becomes the final schedule. Read the worker record, offer and rule record together: none is enough by itself.

### Document A — Worker and school record

| Item | Supplied fact |
|---|---|
| Age on the proposed shift | 15 |
| Job | Retail employee: cashier and shelving books |
| Arrangement | Ordinary provincial employment; not an approved off-campus education program, work-integrated learning placement, farm or ranch job |
| School on Thursday, October 8, 2026 | 8:45 a.m.–3:15 p.m. |
| School on Friday, October 9 | 8:45 a.m.–3:15 p.m. |
| Parent's consent | Parent has agreed to evening work ending by 11:00 p.m.; no consent to a later finish |
| Transport | Parent can collect Ellis at 11:00 p.m. |

### Document B — Draft shift offer, received October 5

> Thursday, October 8: 9:30 p.m.–12:30 a.m. the next morning. Duties: cashier and shelving. Adult supervisor Mara, age 32, is on site and supervising until 10:00 p.m. Jordan, age 17, then supervises the closing team. Mara can be called after leaving. Please confirm whether this draft works before we publish it.

Jordan is experienced, but is not an adult. Ellis has not accepted or worked this proposed shift.

### Document C — In-course Alberta youth employment record

**Government of Alberta, “Employment standards rules – Youth employment laws”; relevant sections “Basic rules,” “Employees 13 to 14 years of age,” and “Employees 15 to 17 years of age.” Checked September 28, 2026. Limited paraphrase for this lesson:**

| Group | Rules relevant to the supplied cases |
|---|---|
| Age 15–17 | Permits are not required. Parent/guardian consent is required for work during restricted hours. A 15-year-old cannot work during regular school hours except in an off-campus education program. |
| Age 15–17, retail/hospitality | Work from 9:00 p.m. to midnight requires adult supervision. Work from 12:01 a.m. to 6:00 a.m. is prohibited. |
| Age 13–14 | Parent/guardian consent is required. Specified jobs may be done without a permit; other jobs require one. No work during regular school hours except in an off-campus education program. Maximum two hours outside school on a school day, eight on a non-school day. No work between 9:00 p.m. and 6:00 a.m. |
| Age 13–14, restaurants | Listed permit-free tasks include hosting, cashier work and bussing/cleaning tables. Food-service work requires continuous adult supervision. |

This selected record does not list every permitted occupation or every employment rule. Permission does not erase an hours restriction. Meeting these selected rules does not establish that all employment and safety requirements have been met.

### Read the documents

**Which fact is missing from the original offer's claim of adult supervision after 10:00 p.m.?** Choose one, then select **Check document reading**.

- A. A promise that Jordan knows the store well. Feedback: Experience is useful, but it does not make a 17-year-old an adult.
- B. An adult who is actually supervising that portion of the shift. **Correct.** Mara leaves at 10:00 p.m.; a reachable phone number is not the on-site supervision supplied in this case.
- C. A higher hourly wage. Feedback: A wage amount does not answer who supervises the late shift.
- D. A later school start on Friday. Feedback: School information matters, but does not replace the retail evening supervision condition.

## 3. Learn the method

### Identify the rule before judging the offer

Start with the details that select a rule: **age → employment arrangement → workplace/task → time**. “Teen worker” is too broad. Ellis is 15, so the 13–14 school-day two-hour limit is not the rule to apply to Ellis. North Path Books is retail, so the retail evening rule is relevant. An exception for another kind of workplace cannot simply be moved to this one.

An official page is evidence about the rules; a shift offer is evidence about the proposed facts. The task is to connect them. Write “Ellis is scheduled after midnight in retail, which conflicts with the recorded overnight restriction,” rather than “the website says it is bad.” The first sentence lets another reader check your reasoning.

### Divide the shift where a condition changes

Midnight crosses into the next calendar day. Adult coverage can change before the shift ends. A whole-shift label such as “night work” hides these differences. Break the offered shift into pieces:

| Portion of Ellis's offer | What changes? | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| 9:30–10:00 p.m. Thursday | Mara is supervising | Are the evening consent and supervision conditions supplied? |
| 10:00 p.m.–midnight | Mara leaves; Jordan is 17 | Is an adult supervising? Does parental consent cover the planned finish? |
| 12:01–12:30 a.m. Friday | Overnight restricted period | Can adult supervision or consent remove this retail prohibition? |

The answer to the last question is no under the supplied record. Do not spend time negotiating an adult supervisor for a portion that cannot be scheduled under that rule. Revise the hours themselves.

### Separate a mismatch from an unanswered question

A **mismatch** exists when supplied facts conflict with the selected rule: a retail shift continuing to 12:30 a.m. is one here. An **unknown** exists when the documents do not tell you a relevant fact. Write a question for an unknown instead of assuming its answer. In Ellis's documents, the ages and coverage are supplied, so “maybe Jordan is an adult” is not an unknown.

A check can have several results. The shift is outside the recorded school hours, but that does not cancel the supervision and overnight problems. Conversely, finding one problem does not mean every detail is wrong. State which conditions your evidence answers.

### Ask for a specific correction

A useful review ends with an action the recipient can understand: a revised start and finish, named adult coverage and confirmation that permission covers those hours. “Fix it” leaves the next decision unclear. You can be direct without accusing someone of deliberate wrongdoing: this is a draft offer and its reason is unknown.

A personal arrangement, such as a ride home, also affects whether the worker can accept. Label it accurately. The parent's 11:00 p.m. pickup is a case constraint, not a province-wide closing rule.

## 4. Follow a complete example

I first select the age 15–17 retail rules. Ellis is 15 and the arrangement is ordinary employment. The shift begins after Thursday's school hours. That answers the school-hours question; it does not approve the rest of the shift.

Next I follow the time line. Mara supplies adult supervision only until 10:00 p.m. Jordan is 17, so the offer does not supply the required adult coverage from 10:00 p.m. to midnight. The parent has only consented to an 11:00 p.m. finish, so the offer also exceeds that permission. Finally, the offered 12:30 a.m. finish includes the prohibited 12:01–6:00 a.m. retail period. Neither permission nor adult coverage can make that portion fit the record.

Here is a complete draft review:

> Ellis is a 15-year-old employee in an Alberta retail bookstore. The proposed October 8 shift is outside the supplied school hours, but it needs revision. Alberta's youth employment record, checked September 28, requires adult supervision for retail work from 9:00 p.m. to midnight. Mara leaves at 10:00 p.m., and Jordan is 17. The 12:30 a.m. finish also includes a prohibited overnight period. Parental consent currently ends at 11:00 p.m. Please offer a shift ending by 11:00 p.m. with an adult supervising the entire evening portion and confirm the corrected schedule before Ellis accepts. The parent's pickup is available at 11:00 p.m. This checks the listed youth conditions; it is not a complete workplace safety review.

“Ellis can work until 12:30 because the parent could give permission” is an unsupported alternative. It treats consent as an override of a prohibition. “Ellis cannot work more than two hours because school is tomorrow” selects the wrong age group's rule and does not use Ellis's actual school-day facts.

## 5. Practise with support

Ellis, the job, age and school record stay the same. Replace Document B with this corrected offer, received October 6:

> Thursday, October 8: 9:00–11:00 p.m. Mara, age 32, will remain on site and supervise for the whole shift. Cashier and shelving duties are unchanged. The parent confirms consent to these exact hours and the 11:00 p.m. pickup. This is the only proposed shift that day.

Create a short verification note in three saved fields.

1. **Which recorded facts select the rules?** Name the age, work arrangement and sector. Use one sentence.
2. **Check the corrected time and supervision.** Explain school hours, overnight boundary, adult coverage and consent in two or three sentences.
3. **What can Ellis conclude, and what is the next action?** State the limited conclusion and the schedule-confirmation action. Do not claim all employment or safety law has been checked.

Controls: **Show a first-step hint**, **Compare with the model**, **Revise my note**. Hint: “Read the corrected offer as a new document. Do not carry forward Jordan's coverage or the 12:30 finish.”

The comparison response says: “Check whether your note uses the new 11:00 p.m. finish, identifies Mara's full adult coverage, and retains the parent and school facts. If it still says Jordan closes alone, revise the old fact. If it says everything is legal, narrow the conclusion to the selected youth conditions.” No text is automatically marked right or wrong.

**Matching model:**

1. “Ellis is 15 and works as an ordinary employee in an Alberta retail bookstore.”
2. “The 9:00–11:00 p.m. shift is outside the 8:45 a.m.–3:15 p.m. school hours and ends before the overnight prohibited period. Mara, 32, supervises throughout, and the parent has consented to the exact hours.”
3. “The corrected offer fits the selected youth conditions in the September 28 record. Ellis can ask the scheduler to confirm this version, then decide whether to accept it; separate job training and other employment requirements still apply.”

Select **Revise my note** to return to your fields. Keep a correction or add a sentence explaining why your original note already matches the evidence.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q1`.

Which information best selects the applicable youth employment rules?

- A. The worker's age, employment arrangement, workplace/task and hours. **Correct.** These determine which recorded conditions apply.
- B. Whether a friend has worked a similar shift. Feedback: A friend's different age or job could change the rules.
- C. Whether the worker wants extra income. Feedback: Income goals do not replace employment conditions.
- D. Whether the employer calls the shift “short.” Feedback: A description does not supply age, clock times or supervision.

### Q2

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q2`.

In Ellis's original retail offer, what resolves the work scheduled from 12:01 to 12:30 a.m.?

- A. Replace Jordan with an adult for those minutes. Feedback: Supervision does not remove the recorded overnight prohibition.
- B. Ask the parent to approve a later pickup. Feedback: Transport and consent do not override this restriction.
- C. Call those minutes volunteer closing time. Feedback: Renaming required employee work does not make the offered hours fit the record.
- D. Change the work hours so that portion is not scheduled. **Correct.** The conflict is with the time itself.

### Q3

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q3`.

A case does not state a proposed supervisor's age. What is the strongest review note?

- A. “They probably count as an adult because they are a supervisor.” Feedback: A title is not evidence of age.
- B. “Confirm that an adult will supervise the restricted evening hours.” **Correct.** It identifies the needed fact without inventing it.
- C. “There is definitely no adult.” Feedback: Missing evidence is not proof that no adult is available.
- D. “Ignore supervision until a problem occurs.” Feedback: The schedule needs that condition checked before acceptance.

### Q4

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q4`.

Which detail in Ellis's case is a personal planning constraint rather than a province-wide rule?

- A. Age 15–17 retail evening supervision. Feedback: That is in the official record.
- B. The prohibited retail overnight period. Feedback: That is also a rule in the source record.
- C. The parent's available pickup at 11:00 p.m. **Correct.** It is supplied by Ellis's family, not stated as a rule for every worker.
- D. The school-hours restriction for a 15-year-old. Feedback: The source supplies this restriction; the school's exact timetable is the case fact.

### Q5

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q5`.

For a 14-year-old restaurant employee, which conclusion follows from the record?

- A. Parent consent removes the school-day hours limit. Feedback: Consent and the hours limit are separate conditions.
- B. All restaurant tasks are automatically permit-free. Feedback: The record lists selected permitted tasks; do not expand the list to every task.
- C. A 17-year-old coworker supplies the continuous adult supervision condition. Feedback: The coworker is not an adult.
- D. The reviewer must check task, consent, school-day hours and continuous adult supervision together. **Correct.** Meeting one condition does not replace the others.

### Q6

Implementation key: `practice:co2-01:q6`.

Which conclusion stays within the evidence after a revised shift meets the selected record?

- A. “Every workplace obligation has been satisfied.” Feedback: This lesson has not examined all safety, pay and other requirements.
- B. “The revised shift fits these selected youth conditions; confirm the final schedule and any other relevant requirements.” **Correct.** The conclusion has a clear scope.
- C. “The earlier draft proves the employer intended to break the law.” Feedback: A conflicting draft does not establish intent.
- D. “The official page never needs checking again.” Feedback: Real rules and individual circumstances can change.

## 7. Apply independently

### A new worker and a new age group

Rina is 14 and has been offered a school-day shift at fictional Willow Bowl Café in Alberta. Write a **shift-review note to the scheduler**, using the in-course source record. This is a saved simulation; do not send a message.

**Document D — Rina's record.** Rina is an ordinary employee, not in an off-campus education program or other special arrangement. On Tuesday, October 13, 2026, school runs from 8:40 a.m. to 3:20 p.m. Rina has no other work that day. A parent has consented to hosting and bussing/cleaning tables after school until 8:00 p.m. An adult supervisor, Anika, age 29, will provide continuous supervision during any shift between 4:00 and 8:00 p.m. Rina is available within that window. Rina has not accepted the draft.

**Document E — Draft offer.** October 13, 4:30–7:30 p.m., with no break scheduled: greet and seat guests, bus and clean tables. No cooking or other duties. Employer proposes no permit because the tasks are hosting and bussing/cleaning tables. The café asks Rina to confirm whether the hours work.

Make one coherent note using these fields:

- **Case facts and applicable conditions** — identify age, sector/tasks, school-day status, consent and adult coverage. Two or three sentences.
- **Hours check and proposed revision** — calculate the offered work duration, compare it to the correct school-day limit, then propose exact revised start/finish times inside the supplied availability. Explain the calculation. Two or three sentences; state hours, not just numbers.
- **Review note and next step** — assemble a respectful note naming the dated rule record, the condition that needs changing and what the scheduler should confirm. About one short paragraph; use fictional facts only.

Success means selecting Rina's age group, checking the actual tasks and conditions, correcting the school-day duration, and requesting a specific schedule. There are several defensible revised schedules; show how yours fits all supplied constraints. You do not need to make the original draft acceptable.

**Compare with a complete model — available whenever useful**

> Rina is a 14-year-old restaurant employee working on a school day. Hosting and bussing/cleaning tables are listed permit-free tasks; parental consent and continuous adult supervision are supplied. The proposed 4:30–7:30 p.m. shift lasts three hours with no break, exceeding the recorded two-hour school-day limit. A revised 4:30–6:30 p.m. shift lasts two hours, is after school, ends before 9:00 p.m., and lies within Rina's availability, parental consent and Anika's adult coverage. Please confirm those corrected hours and the listed duties before Rina accepts. This review uses Alberta's youth employment record checked September 28, 2026, and addresses the selected conditions rather than every employment requirement.

A 5:00–7:00 p.m. shift is also defensible with the same checks. Keeping 4:30–7:30 and saying “the parent agrees” does not solve the duration conflict. Removing all supervision discussion would leave a separate restaurant condition unchecked.
