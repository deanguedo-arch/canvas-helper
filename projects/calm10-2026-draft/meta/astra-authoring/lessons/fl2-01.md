## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Alex's two offer summaries

Alex is a fictional adult considering borrowing $600 for equipment needed next month. After all other commitments, Alex has $60 each month available for repayment and $20 separately available now for a setup fee. Both offers provide exactly $600; no optional insurance is included. All required payments are stated, and neither offer permits additional borrowing through the same agreement.

**Offer A — fixed instalment loan**

Receive $600. Repay $55 monthly for twelve months, starting one month after receiving the money. No setup fee or other charge if paid as scheduled. The payment total includes the loan principal and borrowing charges. Missed-payment consequences and early-payment terms are not supplied; they need checking before a real agreement.

**Offer B — longer instalment loan**

Receive $600. Pay a separate $15 setup fee from existing cash when the loan begins. Repay $31 monthly for twenty-four months, starting one month later. The payments include principal and borrowing charges; the separate setup fee is not included in the $31. No other charge if paid as scheduled. Missed-payment and early-payment terms remain unspecified.

**Application-information card**

A lender may examine identity, income, existing obligations and credit history before approving an application. A quote is not approval. Some products require collateral or another responsible borrower. You are reading a fictional offer, not deciding legal eligibility or completing an application.

**Check the offer:** What does B's $31 describe?

A. Everything Alex will ever repay. B. One monthly payment, repeated twenty-four times, excluding the separate setup fee. C. The original amount borrowed. D. A guaranteed reduction in all borrowing costs.

Feedback: B includes the amount's unit and count. A mistakes one payment for the total. C contradicts the $600 advance. D assumes a small payment means a low total.

## 3. Learn the method

### Separate the money borrowed from the promise to repay

**Credit** is an arrangement that permits borrowing. **Debt** is the amount owed after borrowing occurs. The **principal** is the borrowed amount before interest and other charges. **Interest** is a charge for using borrowed money, while a **fee** is another specified charge. **Total repayment** includes returning the principal; **cost of borrowing** is the amount paid above the principal, including relevant fees in the comparison.

An **instalment loan** provides an amount repaid through a stated series of payments. A **line of credit** permits borrowing up to a limit under its terms; repaid amounts may become available again, and the outstanding amount can change with new borrowing. A credit limit is not a gift or income. Paying only interest on an outstanding line-of-credit balance does not reduce the principal.

**Offer-reading map:** amount received → payment amount and frequency → number of payments → fees → total paid → amount above principal → fit with the repayment budget. Keep separately paid fees separate until adding the total, so they are not omitted or counted twice.

### Recognize purposes and responsibilities

Different products serve different purposes. A vehicle loan finances a vehicle and may use that vehicle as **collateral**, an asset securing repayment. A **mortgage** is a loan used for a property purchase, with property security and further terms. A student loan supports education under its program's rules; it is different from a bank's student line of credit. A credit-card **cash advance** takes borrowed cash against credit, with its own fees and interest terms. **Buy now, pay later** spreads or defers a purchase payment and is still credit, even when presented as a checkout option.

A **secured** loan has specified collateral; an **unsecured** loan does not use a pledged asset in that way. Unsecured does not mean optional repayment. A **co-borrower** shares responsibility for the debt under the agreement. Asking another person to join an application is not simply asking for a reference; the responsibility must be understood.

An app can make a product convenient and can emphasize an attractive first payment. Ease of tapping “accept” is not evidence of affordability. Read the repayment schedule before treating the approval screen as a recommendation.

### Test the commitment, not just approval

A lender's willingness to offer credit does not prove that repayment will leave enough for a particular person's goals and obligations. Compare the payment with available money and consider a lower-income period. If key charges or consequences are absent, name exactly what must be clarified. Do not invent a missing late fee, and do not silently treat it as zero.

**Dated FCAC reference notes, read September 28, 2026:** its line-of-credit explanation distinguishes a borrowing limit from money actually borrowed. Its buy-now-pay-later page identifies these arrangements as credit and directs attention to payment schedules and fees. Use these definitions to recognize the product before comparing it. [Lines of credit](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/loans-lines-credit.html) · [Buy now, pay later plans](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/buy-now-pay-later.html).

**Hint:** Multiply the regular payment by its count first. Then add fees paid outside those payments and subtract the amount actually borrowed to find the extra cost.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Read offer A all the way through

Alex receives $600. Twelve payments of $55 total **$660**. No separate fee is listed for scheduled repayment, so the borrowing cost in this comparison is **$60**, calculated as $660 − $600. Calling $660 “interest” would include money merely being returned.

The $55 payment fits the supplied $60 monthly allowance, leaving $5. That is a narrow margin, and the allowance is a planning assumption. It does not show how a reduction in income would be handled.

**Complete offer note:** “A advances $600 and requires twelve $55 payments, or $660 total. The extra borrowing cost is $60 under on-time repayment. The payment fits Alex's current $60 allowance with $5 remaining each month. Before a real agreement, Alex would need the missing early-payment and missed-payment terms and a plan for a lower-income month. The quote itself does not establish approval.”

“$55 is cheap, so borrow” gives no duration or purpose. The complete note supplies a total and a limit before a recommendation.

**Model:** “B totals $744 in instalments plus $15 setup, or $759. Its borrowing cost is $159, which is $99 above A's $60. B's $31 payment leaves more room in a month, but lasts twelve additional months. Alex's $20 setup cash covers the fee, leaving $5 of that separate amount.”

**Change:** Alex's repayment allowance falls to $40 monthly for the next six months; the $20 setup cash is unchanged. Choose **A still fits because its total is lower / B fits this payment limit but costs more overall / both become free**. Use **Check the changed condition**. The second statement fits $31≤$40; A's $55 exceeds $40, and neither debt disappears. A defensible next step may still be to delay the purchase or seek a supplied non-borrowing option rather than choose the only quoted payment that fits.

Add one sentence to your comparison explaining this change. Do not claim B is automatically a good decision simply because the monthly number fits.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What is A's principal?

A. $600. B. $660. C. $60. D. $55.

Answer A. A is money borrowed; B is total repayment; C is the extra cost; D is one instalment.

### Q2 — Why is B's borrowing cost not $744?

A. Monthly payments never count. B. Fees cannot exist. C. Repayment is optional. D. $744 includes returned principal and omits the separate fee; cost above principal is $159.

Answer D. A–C contradict the agreement; D distinguishes the components correctly.

### Q3 — What can happen if only interest is paid on an unchanged line-of-credit balance?

A. The principal automatically doubles every month. B. The principal remains owing. C. The limit becomes income. D. All future charges disappear.

Answer B. A invents growth beyond the terms; B identifies the absent principal payment; C and D misstate credit.

### Q4 — What does collateral do?

A. It cancels repayment. B. It is always an optional gift. C. It identifies an asset securing the debt. D. It proves a purchase has no risk.

Answer C. A and B misunderstand security; C states its role; D ignores the possibility of losing the pledged asset under the agreement.

### Q5 — A checkout calls instalments “only a payment plan.” What should the reader ask?

A. Whether the button is attractive. B. Whether the debt can be ignored. C. Whether every plan has zero fees. D. What credit obligation, schedule and charges the agreement creates.

Answer D. A focuses on presentation; B and C assume unsupported terms; D investigates the commitment.

### Q6 — Why discuss co-borrowing separately from a reference?

A. Co-borrowing can create repayment responsibility. B. Both are only compliments. C. A reference always pays the loan. D. Neither needs to understand the agreement.

Answer A. A identifies the material distinction; B–D misrepresent responsibility.

## 7. Apply independently

### Bea's coat decision

Bea, a fictional adult, needs a suitable winter coat within two weeks. Two financed offers provide the same new coat, cash price $240. Bea has no purchase cash now, but can reliably allocate **$45 monthly** after other commitments; first payments would be due one month from now. There is also a confirmed alternative described below.

**Offer C:** six monthly payments of $44, no setup fee, no other on-time charges. **Offer D:** twelve monthly payments of $24 plus a $12 setup fee due today. Bea has no separate money for that fee. Missing-payment terms for both offers are not supplied.

**Community clothing desk:** an inspected suitable coat in Bea's size is available for collection this week at no cost and meets the stated warmth and fit needs. It is a different colour from the new coat. No eligibility application or payment is required in this fictional case.

Fields: **C total and cost above $240**, **D total and cost above $240**, **My recommendation and terms to check**. Write the amounts with units in the first two short fields; use 4–5 sentences in the recommendation. Explain the monthly and immediate-cash constraints, the benefit sought and a non-borrowing choice. Do not apply for anything.

**Complete model:** “C totals 6×$44=$264, a $24 borrowing cost. D totals 12×$24+$12=$300, a $60 borrowing cost. C's $44 payment fits the $45 limit narrowly, while D needs a $12 setup payment Bea cannot make today. I recommend the confirmed no-cost coat because it meets the required fit, warmth and timing without creating debt. Bea gives up the preferred colour. If considering credit instead, the missing-payment consequences would need checking before a real agreement.”

A response can discuss C as an option, but must acknowledge the $1 monthly margin and the available suitable non-borrowing alternative. It cannot treat D's lower instalment as a solution to the unpaid setup fee.
