# CE1-05 — Build a high-school route with checkpoints

Authoring status: complete script for lead review. Version: `2026-09-28.astra.1`.

## 1. Start here

### A course list becomes a plan when the order works

Tariq wants to keep two post-school directions possible: visual communication and information services. Listing interesting courses is a start, but a useful plan must put prerequisites before later courses, meet application dates and fit the time available.

You will build and revise a targeted Grade 10–12 pathway plan using complete fictional school and provider records. These invented course names and requirements are not actual Alberta admission or graduation rules. No counsellor appointment or teacher feedback is needed: the notices and feedback required for the activity are already here. Your final plan belongs to a new fictional learner, Nessa.

Work through **Meet the situation**, **Learn the method**, **Follow a complete example**, **Practise with support**, **Check your understanding**, **Apply independently** and **Review and complete**. ## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Tariq's planning file

**Tariq's current record:** Grade 10 Communication Foundations and Digital Media 1 are completed. Tariq enjoys arranging visual information and wants to improve the accuracy of written records. The career goal is to explore design while keeping an information-services route open. The life goal is to retain evenings for existing commitments. The financial goal is to keep the listed elective-material costs within $100 across this plan.

**Reading check:** Which item is required for Program A, rather than merely recommended? Select and use **Check my reading**.

Feedback: **D** is required entry evidence. **A** and **B** are recommended ways to prepare it; this fictional provider does not require those exact courses. **C** is not an admission condition.

## 3. Learn the method

### Work backwards, then check forwards

Working backwards starts with the destination. Program A needs portfolio pieces by April 15, so Tariq must have time to make them before then. Working forwards starts with what is already completed. Digital Media 1 allows Digital Media 2, which allows Portfolio Lab. A workable plan makes the backward deadline and forward prerequisite chain meet.

Draw the chain before assigning dates:

**Digital Media 1 → Digital Media 2 → Portfolio Lab → two pieces before April 15.**

The communication chain is separate: **Foundations → Applications → Capstone**. A course can support both routes. Recognizing shared preparation avoids treating the two directions as entirely separate sets of work.

### Separate a requirement from a useful way to prepare

Required means the provider's record makes it a condition. Recommended means it may help but is not stated as compulsory. Tariq could theoretically prepare two portfolio pieces another way, but this case supplies a complete school route with known time and materials. Using that route is sensible without rewriting it as a universal requirement.

A course appearing on a program page does not prove the school offers it in the needed term. The provider controls its entry conditions; the school record controls the local sequence in this exercise. In a real plan, both sets of information would need current confirmation. Here their fictional dates and offerings are fully supplied.

### Fit the route into a life, not just a table

The plan must respect Tariq's one-elective slot and three-hour weekly limit. Putting Digital Records and Portfolio Lab together would use two slots and five additional hours. The courses could be valuable but the arrangement would contradict the supplied capacity.

Financial and career goals also connect. The three elective material charges total $95. That fits the $100 limit for those categories, but it says nothing about future tuition. Record what a calculation covers. A plan that assumes unnamed funding or unlimited study time is not made workable by choosing an attractive program.

### Use checkpoints and supplied feedback

A **checkpoint** is a planned moment to review evidence and make a decision. A **trigger** is something that makes a revision necessary, such as a cancelled course or changed application requirement. Record the old assumption, the new fact and what will change. Revision is how a plan stays useful.

Support can take the form of clear information, a different course format, an accessible planning tool or a knowledgeable service. For this lesson the support is in the school/provider notices and the model feedback. You can practise drafting a precise question without sending it. “Will Portfolio Lab finish before April 15?” is more answerable than “What should I do with my life?”

**Dated reference record:** ALIS's *Explore School and Program Options*, read September 28, 2026, identifies entry conditions, deadlines, costs and provider information as useful research. These fictional records let you apply that method completely inside the course. [Optional original](https://alis.alberta.ca/explore-education-and-training/explore-school-and-program-options/).

**Optional hint:** Put the required later step at the right end of the timeline, then follow each prerequisite arrow backwards until you reach a completed course.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Keep both Tariq routes possible

I begin with the core communication chain. Applications belongs in Grade 11 after the completed Foundations course; Capstone follows in Grade 12. These are reserved core courses, so they do not use the elective slot.

For the design preparation, I put Digital Media 2 in Grade 11 fall and Portfolio Lab in Grade 11 winter. The prerequisite order works and the pieces will be ready well before the Grade 12 application date. Digital Records can then occupy Grade 12 fall, leaving winter as a buffer rather than filling every available slot.

| Period | Planned pathway step | Why it belongs there |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 10, completed | Foundations; Digital Media 1 | Starting evidence |
| Grade 11 core | Communication Applications | Next communication prerequisite |
| Grade 11 fall elective | Digital Media 2 | Builds from Media 1 |
| Grade 11 winter elective | Portfolio Lab | Follows Media 2 and produces the pieces |
| Grade 12 core | Communication Capstone | Meets A's communication requirement if completed |
| Grade 12 fall elective | Digital Records | Completes B's additional requirement |
| Grade 12 winter elective | No extra course assigned | Leaves room to revise pieces and manage existing work |

**Completed planning note:** “This keeps design as Tariq's primary direction and information services as a researched backup. Each elective fits the three-hour limit and uses one slot. Materials total $95. At the end of Grade 11, Tariq can review the completed pieces and interest in design; before April 15 in Grade 12, the portfolio deadline must be checked against the final pieces. A precise preparation question would be whether the provider accepts the two chosen types of piece. The plan does not guarantee admission.”

An unsupported alternative puts Portfolio Lab before Digital Media 2. Having the right course names would not fix the missing prerequisite. Another alternative puts two electives together and exceeds the capacity. Order and feasibility matter as well as selection.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — What does a prerequisite determine?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q1`.

A. Which earlier step must be completed before a later one. B. A guaranteed admission offer. C. The learner's personal worth. D. Whether a course is always free.

Answer: A. A defines the dependency. B is a different decision. C is unrelated to course sequencing. D requires cost evidence.

### Q2 — Program A recommends Portfolio Lab but requires two pieces. What follows?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q2`.

A. The exact Lab course is legally mandatory. B. No portfolio is needed. C. Lab is one supplied way to develop the required evidence. D. Taking Lab guarantees admission.

Answer: C. A changes recommendation into a mandate. B ignores the required pieces. C preserves the distinction. D promises selection.

### Q3 — Which record answers whether a school course runs in Grade 12 fall?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q3`.

A. A friend's memory. B. The fictional school offering table. C. An occupation's wage figure. D. A provider's general mission statement.

Answer: B. A is unverified. B directly supplies availability. C concerns pay. D does not describe the school timetable.

### Q4 — Why leave a buffer in a plan?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q4`.

A. To avoid having goals. B. To guarantee nothing changes. C. To remove all deadlines. D. To leave room for revision and existing commitments.

Answer: D. A misinterprets flexibility. B is impossible to guarantee. C ignores actual dates. D explains a practical benefit.

### Q5 — A course is cancelled but available later. What should happen first?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q5`.

A. Declare the career impossible. B. Trace how the later offering affects prerequisites, deadlines and capacity. C. Pretend the cancellation did not occur. D. Place the course in any convenient term.

Answer: B. B tests the changed constraint. A jumps beyond the evidence. C makes an obsolete plan. D invents availability.

### Q6 — Which question is most useful to a program provider?

Implementation key: `practice:ce1-05:q6`.

A. “Which file formats are accepted for the two required portfolio pieces?” B. “Is everything fine?” C. “Can you promise I will like my career?” D. “Can you decide all my life goals?”

Answer: A. C asks for an impossible assurance. B hides the actual uncertainty. A identifies a specific requirement the provider controls. D gives away decisions without useful evidence.

## 7. Apply independently

### Nessa's plan: records work with a visitor-services backup

Nessa has completed Communication Foundations and Digital Basics in Grade 10. Nessa likes keeping information accurate, wants to retain a visitor-services option, and has no more than three additional study hours weekly. There is one elective slot per term. The elective-material budget is $120. The rest of the fictional core program is reserved.

**New fictional provider records:** Program C, Community Information certificate, requires Communication Capstone and Records 2 completed by June of Grade 12; application deadline May 1. Program D, Visitor Services certificate, requires Communication Applications plus two original visitor-information samples by April 1 of Grade 12; Media Lab is recommended, not mandated. Admission remains separate from meeting these conditions.

**New fictional school record**

| Course | Prerequisite and offering | Weekly additional work | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communication Applications | Foundations; Grade 11 core | Already planned | Included |
| Communication Capstone | Applications; Grade 12 core | Already planned | Included |
| Records 1 | Digital Basics; Grade 11 fall | 2 hours | $20 |
| Records 2 | Records 1; Grade 11 winter or Grade 12 winter | 3 hours | $30 |
| Media Lab | No extra prerequisite; Grade 11 winter or Grade 12 fall; samples ready by the end of its term | 3 hours | $50 |

**Supplied planning feedback:** “Placing Records 2 and Media Lab together in Grade 11 winter exceeds one slot and six hours exceed Nessa's three-hour limit. Move a course to one of its other stated offerings. Keep the April 1 sample deadline visible.”

Create **Nessa's Grade 10–12 route** listing the communication core and all four elective slots. Then complete **Goals, costs and revision reason** and **Two checkpoints and support questions**. Explain how the plan addresses the supplied feedback, supports the primary and backup, stays within the weekly/material limits and handles both deadlines. About 180–260 words across the fields is a guide, not a gate. Draft questions only; do not send a message.

**Compare with a supported plan**

“Grade 10 Foundations and Digital Basics are completed. Applications is planned in Grade 11 core, followed by Capstone in Grade 12 core. I would place Records 1 in Grade 11 fall, Records 2 in Grade 11 winter, Media Lab in Grade 12 fall and leave Grade 12 winter without an additional elective. This separates the two courses that conflicted in the feedback, keeps every elective at three hours or less and totals $20 + $30 + $50 = $100, within $120. The primary information route has its course chain; the visitor-services backup gets samples by the end of Grade 12 fall, before April 1. A first checkpoint is the end of Grade 11, to review the Records sequence and interest in that work. A second is before April 1 in Grade 12, to check the samples. Questions to retain are whether the provider accepts the planned sample formats and whether any entry dates or requirements have changed. For this exercise, the supplied records are enough to complete the plan; these are future verification questions, not missing assignment steps.”

Another valid sequence places Media Lab in Grade 11 winter and Records 2 in Grade 12 winter. Explain why that still meets the respective deadlines and what later completion changes. Revise your own plan after comparing.
