# FL1-04 — Compare a major purchase

Complete script for lead review. Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Canonical course unchanged.

## 1. Start here

### Compare the whole arrangement

A small monthly price can make two offers look similar. The total, commitment, ownership and costs after purchase can still differ. You will help Dani compare two laptop purchase agreements, then make an independent recommendation about a calculator rental or purchase.

Your final product is a comparison note that distinguishes **total cost** from **cash needed now**, explains what the user receives and identifies one fact still worth checking. All offers are invented and all prices include the charges specified in the case. No purchase or live shopping is required.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Dani's laptop offers** and locate the payment terms. Learn **Put offers on the same basis**, then follow the complete comparison. In **Add a repair possibility**, test how one event changes the totals. Answer six questions and complete **Riley's sixteen-week calculator choice**. Review the model, revise and sign off. Pause after the laptop comparison if needed; every field saves.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Dani's laptop offers

Dani needs a laptop for a two-year program starting next month. Both offers supply the same model, which the fictional program has confirmed meets its software requirements. Dani has **$120 available now** and can commit **up to $40 each month for 24 months** after other obligations. Dani has no other purchase fund or borrowing option in this case.

**North Computer Shop — purchase offer A**

Pay $120 at pickup and $34 each month for 24 months. All scheduled charges and taxes are included. Ownership transfers after the final payment. The price includes a one-year warranty for manufacturing defects, not accidental damage. Accidental repair costs are not supplied. Cancelling does not erase the unpaid purchase balance. There is no extra fee for paying the balance early.

**Central Computer Shop — purchase offer B**

Pay $0 at pickup and $41 each month for 24 months. All scheduled charges and taxes are included. Same ownership and early-payment terms as A. Same one-year defect warranty, plus 24 months of accidental-damage cover: one eligible repair, with a $50 claim fee. Loss and theft are excluded. No repair occurs in the base comparison.

**Check the offers:** Which calculation gives A's scheduled total?

A. $34 × 24. B. $120 + $34. C. $120 + ($34 × 24). D. $120 × 24.

Feedback: C includes the one-time amount and every monthly payment. A omits the amount at pickup. B includes only one month. D repeats a one-time cost every month.

## 3. Learn the method

### Put offers on the same basis

A **purchase agreement** sets out how a buyer pays for an item and what conditions apply. An **instalment** is one payment in a series. The **term** is the period covered by the agreement. A **lease or rental** pays for use under stated conditions and does not automatically give ownership. Read the ownership rule rather than assuming that the smallest monthly price buys the item.

Choose the same comparison period. Here both offers last 24 months. Add the upfront amount, all scheduled payments and any other mandatory charges. Do not add a fee twice if the offer already includes it. This gives a scheduled total under stated assumptions; a possible repair should be shown separately until an event is specified.

**Cost map:** amount at pickup + number of payments × payment amount + additional mandatory charges = scheduled total. Alongside it, keep a second line: money needed on the first date and on each later date. A lower total can still be inaccessible if the upfront payment is too large.

### Compare benefits and remaining exposure

A **warranty** promises a remedy for conditions described in its terms. It is not a promise that every future problem will be free. A cover plan can have exclusions, limits and a claim fee. B includes one eligible accidental repair, but theft remains excluded and an accepted claim still costs $50. Writing “B includes free repairs” would erase important terms.

A decision should connect the feature to a plausible need. Both computers already meet the program's software requirements, so a claim that the more expensive plan runs the software better is unsupported. The useful difference is the cover, together with its price and limits.

### Keep ownership costs visible

Large purchases can create later costs: maintenance, insurance where needed, replacement parts, interest, storage or operating costs. A vehicle, for example, can require insurance and ongoing maintenance in addition to its purchase price. Amounts depend on the actual vehicle, use and agreement, so do not invent a universal estimate. Mark missing costs and find an appropriate quote before a real commitment.

In this case the task supplies enough to compare scheduled payments. It does not supply a probability of accidental damage or A's repair prices. You can identify exposure without pretending to calculate an expected repair bill.

**Optional reference note, September 28, 2026:** FCAC's personal-loan comparison method checks payment count and total repayment, alongside fees. Here that arithmetic helps compare the supplied agreements; it does not establish a real loan's legal terms. [Read “Comparing loan options”](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/personal-loans.html).

**Hint:** Circle each offer's “at pickup,” “each month” and “24 months” phrases. Put those three figures into the same formula.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Compare Dani's base plan

Offer A totals $120 + 24 × $34 = **$936**. Offer B totals 24 × $41 = **$984**. B costs **$48 more** in scheduled payments. That difference buys a defined cover feature, not a different computer.

Now I check Dani's resources. A needs the full $120 available at pickup and then $34 monthly, leaving $6 of the $40 monthly allowance. B needs no upfront payment but requires $41 each month, $1 above the stated limit. Having no upfront payment does not solve that repeated shortfall.

**Completed comparison:** “Offer A fits Dani's supplied upfront and monthly limits and has a $936 scheduled total. B costs $984 and includes one eligible accidental repair with a $50 claim fee, but its $41 monthly payment exceeds Dani's $40 limit. I recommend A within this case, while checking repair prices before a real purchase and considering how to set aside the remaining $6 monthly. A's warranty does not cover accidental damage; the cheaper scheduled total does not eliminate repair risk.”

The alternative “B is best because it costs nothing today” considers only the first date. The alternative “A will definitely cost less under every possible event” confuses a base comparison with a guarantee about unknown repairs.

## 5. Practise with support

### Add a repair possibility

Keep both offer terms, but examine a specified event: one accidental screen break in month 10. A's shop provides a confirmed **$160 repair quote**. B confirms that this event is eligible and its only charge is the **$50 claim fee**. Assume no other repair and no change to scheduled payments. This is a comparison scenario, not a prediction that the break will happen.

Enter **A total including this repair ($)**, **B total including this claim ($)** and **What changes, and what does not?** (2–3 sentences). Use **Check these totals**, then compare the model.

Hint: Start with the scheduled total, not just the monthly price. Add the new event cost once.

Feedback: A $1,096; B $1,034. An answer of $160 or $50 names only the event cost. $984 for B omits its claim fee. $1,144 adds A's repair price to B instead of using B's supplied claim terms. Other answers show the matching formula and invite revision.

**Model:** “With this particular repair, A costs $936 + $160 = $1,096 and B costs $984 + $50 = $1,034, so B is $62 lower for this scenario. B still requires $41 each month and does not fit Dani's $40 monthly limit. The event comparison changes cost, not the supplied monthly resources. A real decision would need a feasible payment plan and a way to handle any repair charge when due.”

Revise your explanation if it calls this event certain or ignores the original monthly limit. A conditional comparison can be accurate without making the original option affordable.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Why compare both offers over 24 months?

A. It gives both scheduled payment streams the same time basis. B. All contracts last 24 months. C. It guarantees no repair. D. It makes ownership irrelevant.

Answer A. A prevents an unequal comparison; B invents a universal term; C assumes away events; D ignores a material condition.

### Q2 — What is missing from “B costs $41”?

A. The colour of the shop sign. B. Proof Dani likes computers. C. The employee's opinion. D. The payment frequency and count, and therefore the total commitment.

Answer D. A–C do not describe the payment obligation; D identifies the units needed to interpret $41.

### Q3 — Which statement accurately describes B's cover?

A. It covers theft with no fee. B. It covers one eligible accidental repair with a $50 claim fee. C. It makes all repairs free forever. D. It changes the computer's software capability.

Answer B. A contradicts the exclusion; B follows the terms; C erases limits; D invents a technical benefit.

### Q4 — A rental's monthly total is lower. What must still be checked?

A. Nothing else. B. Whether its advertisement looks expensive. C. Ownership, return conditions, fees and whether its use period meets the need. D. Whether every renter eventually buys it.

Answer C. A ignores contract differences; B substitutes presentation for terms; C identifies useful conditions; D assumes a universal outcome.

### Q5 — Why does a lower event-scenario total not settle Dani's decision?

A. Arithmetic can never inform a decision. B. Repairs are always avoidable. C. A shop cannot provide a quote. D. The chosen arrangement must also fit money available on each required date.

Answer D. A rejects useful evidence; B and C are unsupported; D joins total cost with affordability.

### Q6 — Which should be labelled as unknown in the original base case?

A. A's accidental repair price. B. A's upfront payment. C. B's payment count. D. B's claim fee.

Answer A. Only A is absent from the original terms; B, C and D are explicitly supplied.

## 7. Apply independently

### Riley's sixteen-week calculator choice

Riley needs an approved calculator for exactly sixteen weeks. Both devices below meet the course's requirements. Riley has **$140 now**, no additional equipment money during the sixteen weeks, and can care for and return a rental on time. All prices include stated charges. No loss, damage or late return occurs in the base comparison.

**Rental desk notice:** $7 per week for sixteen weeks, all sixteen weeks paid at pickup. A separate $25 deposit is also paid at pickup and returned in full after an on-time, undamaged return. The calculator must be returned; it does not become Riley's property. Damage or late-return charges are not specified in this notice.

**Used-equipment notice:** $95 purchase price. The seller has demonstrated all required functions. A fresh $12 battery is needed immediately. Riley owns the calculator after payment. There is no warranty; future repair costs and any resale value are unknown.

Write **My calculator comparison and choice**, about 5–6 sentences. Calculate the rental's money needed at pickup and net base cost after the deposit returns; calculate the used purchase's immediate cost. Recommend one option using Riley's need, ownership preference or risk, and identify one specific term to verify. Both options may be defensible if the explanation fits the facts.

Fields: **Rental cash at pickup ($)**, **Rental cost after deposit return ($)**, **Used purchase plus battery ($)** and **My calculator comparison and choice**.

**Model:** “Rental requires 16 × $7 + $25 = $137 at pickup, leaving $3 immediately. After the $25 deposit returns, the sixteen-week rental costs $112. Buying used with the battery costs $95 + $12 = $107 and leaves $33. I choose the used calculator because it meets the stated functions, costs $5 less in the base comparison and remains Riley's property. There is no warranty, so I would ask about repair options before a real purchase; no resale income is assumed.”

A rental recommendation can instead prioritize returning the device after the course, while acknowledging its $137 initial cash requirement, $5 higher base cost and unknown damage/late terms. Do not describe the refundable deposit as permanently spent or as money available during the rental.
