# CO1-05 — Use feedback to revise a career route

## 1. Start here

### Turn a comment into a next step

Feedback is useful when it helps you identify what worked, what needs practice and what evidence to gather next. “You were great” is encouraging, but it does not tell you which skill to keep developing. One difficult moment also does not decide an entire career.

You will help Rowan revise an employability claim and choose a realistic next test. Your final product is a short reflection and plan based on a new feedback note. Everything happens in the supplied simulation; you do not need a real supervisor, placement or personal work history.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Rowan's feedback and current plan**. Learn **Move from observation to a testable plan**, then follow the printer-feedback example. Practise a communication claim and compare working arrangements. Answer six questions, then complete **A second lead's observation**. Compare your revised plan with the model, check the criteria and complete the lesson. Pause after practice if useful; all entries save.

## 3. Learn the method

### Recognize what kind of experience the record describes

Work-related learning can take different forms. **Volunteering** contributes time to an activity; **community service** supports a community need. **Job shadowing** observes someone doing a role, while a **career fair** offers information and conversations about several directions. A workplace visit can build familiarity with a setting; an entrepreneurship project can practise organizing a service or product. The specific arrangement determines what the learner actually observes or does. Being present at an event does not prove performing its skilled tasks.

Rowan's supplied record describes actions in a fictional community setting. Your response can analyze those actions and the feedback. It cannot turn the simulation into a real placement. When reflecting on your own growth in this course, use something you actually did here—such as checking a timetable or revising an unsupported claim—and name that learning context.

### Move from observation to a testable plan

An **observation** describes something noticed: eight accurate entries or a fifteen-minute delay before reporting. An **interpretation** explains what it may suggest: attention to detail or a communication habit worth improving. A **career hypothesis** is a possibility to investigate, such as “A role combining explanation and record work may suit these emerging skills.” It is not a final verdict about a person's future.

Build the interpretation from the evidence, not from the emotional force of the comment. “I am terrible under pressure” is broader than a late report. “I need to report a blocker earlier” names a behaviour that can be practised and checked. Likewise, “I am a professional records expert” exceeds eight entries completed with training.

### Make growth observable

A **growth action** states what the person will do differently. A **next test** creates another opportunity to observe it. “Improve communication” is too vague to check. “Draft a two-sentence blocker update at the first sign of a delay, including the deadline” is visible. The test also needs a criterion: does the update identify the problem, consequence and request before repeated troubleshooting?

Inside this course, a next test can be another supplied scenario or a written rehearsal. A real placement might provide other evidence later, but it is not required to finish this simulation. Label the scope honestly when saving the reflection in a portfolio.

### Fit the work arrangement to the wider plan

Employment arrangements affect time, income predictability and responsibilities. **Full-time** generally describes a substantial regular weekly work schedule, while **part-time** involves fewer hours; exact thresholds and benefits depend on the context and agreement. **Temporary** describes work with an expected endpoint. **Seasonal** work occurs during a particular part of the year. **Self-employment** involves organizing work or a business and finding customers, with costs and responsibilities beyond the service itself. Having **multiple jobs** combines commitments; each small schedule does not create a separate week of available time.

Do not infer benefits or guaranteed hours from a label. Read the supplied offer. Rowan's part-time card totals five weekly hours and fits the eight-hour school-term limit. The temporary role totals nine and does not. The fourteen-hour seasonal role fits the sixteen-hour summer limit, but does not provide current school-term work. The self-employed estimate totals nine, with uncertain bookings; flexible scheduling does not eliminate those hours.

**Reflection path:** observation → limited skill claim → growth action → next test and criterion → revised career/education direction that fits time and resources.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Use the late-report feedback

I separate the outcome from the process. The cards were ready for the activity; that is a successful outcome. Morgan still needed the information earlier. The record gives exact times: 10:05 to 10:20 is fifteen minutes. I should not rewrite that as either “never reported” or “reported immediately.”

**Completed reflection:** “The approved card backup was accurate and ready before 11:00, but Rowan waited fifteen minutes to report the printer problem. This suggests Rowan can complete the task with support while needing a more immediate reporting habit. In the next written shift simulation, Rowan will draft a two-sentence update as soon as a blocker appears: what stopped, what deadline is affected and what approval is needed. The test is whether the update appears before repeated troubleshooting and contains all three details. Program-support work remains worth investigating, with communication under changing conditions as a growth focus; this single incident does not rule the pathway in or out.”

The next test is a specific learning action. It does not require claiming an actual workplace experience. A plan that says “be confident” might describe a hoped-for feeling but gives no behaviour to practise or evidence to review.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Which is an observation rather than a broad interpretation?

A. Rowan is suited to every office. B. Rowan lacks all communication skill. C. The printer stopped at 10:05 and was reported at 10:20. D. Rowan's career is settled.

Answer C. A, B and D are sweeping conclusions; C reports the supplied timing.

### Q2 — What makes a next test useful?

A. A specific action and evidence that would show whether it improved. B. A promise never to make a mistake. C. A career label without a task. D. Praise without a criterion.

Answer A. A can be reviewed; B is unrealistic; C and D do not specify observable improvement.

### Q3 — Which offer fits Rowan's school-term time limit as written?

A. Temporary project: nine total hours. B. Self-employment estimate: nine hours. C. Seasonal role: fourteen hours in summer, treated as current. D. Ongoing part-time role: five hours including travel.

Answer D. A and B exceed eight; C changes the season and limit; D uses the actual school-term constraint.

### Q4 — What can be assumed about benefits from “part-time” alone?

A. Full benefits are guaranteed. B. Neither presence nor absence of benefits is established; read the agreement. C. Benefits are forbidden. D. The job has no responsibilities.

Answer B. A and C invent terms; B preserves the unknown; D misreads the label.

### Q5 — Rowan considers two jobs, each four hours plus one hour travel weekly. How should time be checked?

A. Each is below eight, so both always fit. B. Ignore travel. C. Add both: ten hours exceeds the eight-hour limit. D. Double the available study time.

Answer C. A tests separate pieces instead of the combined plan; B omits a supplied commitment; C totals the real demand; D invents time.

### Q6 — Which revised pathway statement is defensible?

A. “Program-support work remains a possibility; I will test clarification and investigate training requirements.” B. “Eight accurate entries qualify me for every records role.” C. “One delayed message ends this pathway.” D. “A simulation proves supervised experience.”

Answer A. A links evidence, growth and inquiry; B overstates qualification; C overgeneralizes a difficulty; D misrepresents the activity.

## 7. Apply independently

### A second lead's observation

New record: October 2, a different fictional program lead, Casey, reviews Rowan's intake work after the local process was demonstrated. Four unambiguous forms were entered accurately. Two forms had unclear activity choices. Rowan correctly left those two entries pending rather than guessing, but did not ask the visitors a clarifying question while they were available. Casey later obtained the choices and completed the entries. Casey says, “Keep checking the form. Next, practise asking a clear, neutral question when a choice is ambiguous.”

**Current planning context:** Rowan remains interested in program-support work and has the same eight-hour school-term work-plus-travel limit. A school communication elective is available next term inside Rowan's existing course timetable; it includes written clarification practice. It is optional preparation, not a stated admission requirement. The ongoing part-time desk option still totals five hours weekly. No formal qualification requirement for program coordination is supplied.

Write **My revised reflection and career step**, about 6–8 sentences or equivalent labelled notes. Include one evidenced strength, a limit, one concrete growth action, a new in-course practice test with a success criterion, and one change to the career or school plan. Then complete **What I still need to investigate** with a focused training/role question.

**Complete model:** “Rowan entered four clear forms accurately after instruction and left ambiguous forms pending instead of inventing choices. This supports careful record handling in this local task, not expertise with every intake system. The growth need is to clarify uncertainty while the visitor is available. Rowan could practise writing a neutral question for a supplied form showing two possible activities: ‘Which activity would you like me to record?’ The test is whether the question offers no assumed answer and the entry stays pending until a clear response is supplied. Program-support work remains a reasonable hypothesis, with clarification as a growth focus. Rowan could add the optional communication elective within the existing timetable and investigate the five-hour part-time role without exceeding the eight-hour limit. The elective supports practice but is not presented as a required professional credential.”

**Investigation question:** “Which training or qualifications does the actual program-coordinator role require, and which could be gained through entry-level work?”

A different practice test or plan change is acceptable if it uses the supplied opportunities and states how improvement would be recognized. Do not invent a new mandatory course or claim that this online reflection is proof of a real placement. Revise after comparing.
