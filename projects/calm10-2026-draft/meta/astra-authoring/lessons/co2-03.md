# CO2-03 — Act on responsibilities and concerns

Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Assessment reference A2. Fictional documents throughout; Alberta source records checked September 28, 2026. Authoring packet only.

## 1. Start here

A concern becomes easier to act on when another person can understand what happened, locate the evidence and see what response is needed. “Work was unfair” may describe a feeling accurately, but a report also needs the event, date, records and requested action.

You will follow a report about a missing machine guard, practise recording a response without declaring the issue resolved too soon, then investigate a different worker's pay-record discrepancy. Your final product will be a factual review request with a calculation and a suitable follow-up route.

Follow **Meet the situation and read the documents → Learn the method → Follow a complete example → Practise with support → Check your understanding → Apply independently → Review and complete**. You can pause after the example or before the new case. Everything needed is supplied. Writing saves automatically; practice answers and model comparisons do not grade you. The independent report and review sign-offs become your lesson evidence.

**Vocabulary.** An observation records what someone saw, heard or did. An inference is an interpretation drawn from information. A discrepancy is a difference between records that should agree. A pay period is the date range covered by a pay statement. Gross regular wages are earnings for regular hours before deductions; net pay is what remains after deductions. A follow-up records what happened after the first report. Advocacy means expressing a need or right and requesting action supported by evidence.

Optional disclosure: **Official sources for real questions**. The dated source records are available within the lesson. Current links are optional; this activity does not submit an actual complaint or require contact with a workplace.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Owen is an employee at fictional Birch Sign Workshop in Alberta, a provincially regulated workplace. Owen has been trained to use Cutter 2 only with its fitted safety guard in place. Owen has not been trained to repair or alter the equipment.

### Document A — Owen's observation note, October 7, 2026

> 4:05 p.m., cutting bench, Cutter 2. Before starting the assigned cutting task, I saw that the fitted guard was not attached. The machine was switched off; I did not turn it on or touch its controls. I do not know why the guard was absent. No injury occurred. I stayed clear of the machine. Supervisor Tessa was at the packing desk and could be reached safely.

### Document B — Training card and report log

**Training card:** “Before use, verify the fitted guard is present. If it is absent or damaged, do not operate Cutter 2. Report to Tessa. Only an authorized competent person may inspect, repair and clear the machine for use. Do not perform a test cut to check an unguarded machine.”

**Report log, October 7:**

| Time | Recorded event |
|---|---|
| 4:08 p.m. | Owen told Tessa that Cutter 2's guard was missing and the task had not started. |
| 4:10 p.m. | Tessa said, “Keep it out of use. I will arrange a competent inspection. Work at the packing table, which you are trained to use.” |
| 4:15 p.m. | Owen began the safe packing task. No inspection result had yet been provided. |

These facts establish an observation, a report and an interim response. They do not establish who removed the guard or that the machine has been cleared for use.

### Document C — In-course responsibilities and source map

**Government of Alberta records, checked September 28, 2026:**

| Topic and source | Limited paraphrase relevant here |
|---|---|
| **Young worker rights and responsibilities** — “Workplace safety,” “Job training” | Workers report unsafe conditions, follow safe procedures and ask for needed training. Employers ensure workers have the training and skills to do their work safely. |
| **Employer responsibilities** — “Overview” | Employers arrange competent supervision, hazard information, safe practices and required safety training/equipment. A worker's report does not transfer the employer's responsibility to provide controls to that worker. |
| **Refuse dangerous work** — “Worker obligations,” “Employer obligations” | Dangerous-work refusals require prompt notice and employer investigation. Unless resolved immediately, the worker receives the investigation report. Safe alternative work may be assigned. Unresolved danger belongs with OHS. |
| **Payment of earnings** — “Pay statements” | Statements identify the pay period, work hours and rates, separate earnings items and deductions with their reasons. This makes comparison with an employee's records possible. |
| **Make an employment standards complaint** — “What a complaint can be about,” “Other workplace issues not covered” | Provincial Employment Standards handles matters such as wages and hours. Safety concerns go to OHS; protected-ground discrimination concerns go to the Alberta Human Rights Commission. Eligibility and procedures differ. |

The selected source depends on the question. You may need more than one route if a situation contains different issues. None of these services is an all-purpose “workplace complaint department.”

### Read the documents

**At 4:15 p.m., which status is supported?** Choose, then select **Check document reading**.

- A. The guard was removed by Owen. Feedback: The record says Owen does not know why it was absent.
- B. Cutter 2 has passed an inspection. Feedback: No inspection result has been provided.
- C. Tessa has refused to respond. Feedback: The log records an instruction to keep it out of use and arrange inspection.
- D. The concern has been reported, safe alternative work has begun, and the inspection result is still outstanding. **Correct.** It states the actual stage of the response.

## 3. Learn the method

### Make the concern traceable

A report needs enough detail for someone who was not present. Include **who, when, where, what was observed, what was done, and what response is requested**. Use a date as well as a clock time; “yesterday” becomes unclear when the note is read next week. Name the machine or pay period so the recipient does not check the wrong record.

Record the source of information. “I saw the guard was absent” differs from “a coworker said the guard had been repaired.” The first is Owen's observation; the second would be another person's statement. Both could be relevant, but they are different forms of evidence. Quote only words supplied as exact words. If you remember the meaning rather than the wording in a real situation, label it as a summary.

### Explain the responsibility without guessing intent

Owen's responsibilities are to avoid the prohibited use, report the condition and follow safe arrangements. The employer's responsibilities include ensuring safe practices, competent assessment and training. Owen can name those responsibilities without saying “Tessa deliberately endangered me.” A report about the condition is useful even when the reason for it is unknown.

Use a clear request: “Please confirm the inspection outcome and the conditions for resuming use.” This is more actionable than “make the workplace better.” An initial response can be useful without being the final resolution: moving Owen to packing reduces immediate exposure but does not repair or clear Cutter 2.

### Read records on the same basis

For a wages question, first compare the same dates and kind of amount. A four-hour personal work record cannot be compared directly with a whole month's net deposit. Find the matching pay period and the regular-hours line. Then compare hours × rate with gross regular wages. Deductions affect net pay; they do not explain why a statement's regular-hours line differs from the work record.

For example, a clearly labelled fictional illustration of two regular hours at $20 per hour produces $40 of regular wages before deductions. A $37 net deposit could reflect deductions and does not by itself prove three dollars of unpaid regular wages. You would need the earnings and deduction lines. In the independent case, the necessary lines and timing are supplied; do not invent tax rates.

### Select the route that can address the issue

| Evidence you are investigating | Suitable first organizational recipient | Relevant outside information route |
|---|---|---|
| Missing guard or unresolved danger | Supervisor/employer or designated safety contact | Alberta OHS |
| Hours or regular-wage discrepancy | Payroll/employer with the dated records | Alberta Employment Standards |
| Negative treatment possibly connected to a protected ground | Appropriate support/HR contact when safe and useful | Alberta Human Rights Commission |

An internal request and a formal complaint are different actions. The Employment Standards source explains starting with the employer and, for ordinary pay issues, waiting one pay period before a formal complaint to see whether the next statement resolves it. Urgent matters can differ. Its question service can help explain the applicable process; asking a question is not filing a complaint. A record should not promise an agency will accept a complaint or decide in the worker's favour.

### Recognize other employment-standards questions

Pay concerns can involve more than the regular hours shown on a statement. **Dated source record — checked September 28, 2026:** Alberta's Employment Standards rules cover the categories below. Recognizing the category helps identify the right records and official explanation before deciding what is owed.

| Category | What it concerns and what to verify in a real case |
|---|---|
| **Vacation time and vacation pay** | Time away from work and the pay connected with vacation are related but distinct. Check length of employment, applicable eligibility, time taken and vacation pay already recorded or paid. A vacation-pay entry alone does not describe when time off was taken. |
| **General holidays** | Rules for recognized holidays and associated pay or time off. Check the holiday, eligibility, whether it was a regular workday and whether the employee worked. Do not assume every holiday or worker has the same arrangement. |
| **Overtime** | Work hours that meet applicable overtime rules, and the resulting compensation or a valid arrangement for paid time off. Check daily and weekly time records, applicable occupation or industry exceptions and any written agreement. A long shift by itself does not settle the calculation. |
| **Ending employment** | Questions about notice, possible termination pay and final earnings when an employment relationship ends. Check who ended it, length of employment, the circumstances and applicable exceptions. A temporary lay-off and a permanent end of employment are different situations. |

The [optional official rules directory](https://www.alberta.ca/alberta-employment-standards-rules) links to each category. This overview gives no new legal rates or entitlement calculation. The later regular-wage case supplies the information needed for its stated comparison; do not add hypothetical holiday, overtime, vacation or termination amounts to it. Safety hazards still belong with the OHS route, and possible discrimination with the human-rights route.

### Preserve the evidence and the outcome

Keep a copy of the relevant original record and the dated response. Do not alter an original time entry just to make it match payroll. If you find your own error, write a dated correction that preserves what changed. Record resolution with specifics: what was corrected, by whom and how you know. “Closed” with no evidence prevents later checking.

**Optional first-step hint:** “Turn 'the machine is dangerous' into an observation with a date, machine name and missing part. Then add the action already taken and the response still needed.”

## 4. Follow a complete example

I start the report at 4:05, before the later conversation, so the sequence is clear. The missing guard is the observed condition. The training card explains why it matters: Owen must not use Cutter 2 without it. I can state that connection without claiming an injury or knowing who removed the guard.

Next I record both sides of the response. Owen reported at 4:08 and Tessa acted at 4:10. Omitting Tessa's actual response would make the record misleading. Finally I say what remains open: a competent inspection and clearance. Safe packing work does not establish that Cutter 2 is safe.

**Complete report, prepared at 4:20 p.m. on October 7:**

> At 4:05 p.m. on October 7, 2026, I observed that Cutter 2 at the cutting bench had no fitted guard. The machine was off and I did not operate it. My training card says not to use it without the guard and to report the condition. I do not know why it was absent, and no injury occurred. I told Tessa at 4:08. At 4:10 she told me to keep it out of use and said she would arrange a competent inspection. I began my trained packing task at 4:15. Please confirm who is responsible for the inspection and provide the result and conditions for resuming use. Until then I will follow the instruction to keep it out of use. I will keep this log and the response together; unresolved safety concerns belong with the employer's safety process and Alberta OHS.

“Tessa did nothing and the machine was fine by 4:15” contradicts the documents twice. It ignores the interim instruction and invents a final outcome. Reliable advocacy depends on describing both the concern and any action already taken.

## 5. Practise with support

Keep Owen's original report. Add this new information; it does not erase the earlier log.

**Document D — October 8 follow-up.** At 3:50 p.m., coworker Lee tells Owen, “I heard someone looked at Cutter 2.” Owen has not seen a report or received instructions clearing use. At 3:55, Owen asks Tessa for an update. Tessa replies, “The inspection is booked for 4:30 today. Continue the packing task; Cutter 2 remains out of use.” No injury has occurred.

Complete a dated follow-up in three fields:

1. **What did Owen hear, and what did Tessa confirm?** Separate Lee's statement from the confirmed inspection status.
2. **Append the follow-up entry.** Include date, times, interim work and unresolved action. Write a short paragraph; do not rewrite history as if inspection had happened.
3. **Write the next check and source route.** Explain what outcome Owen needs before resuming and why OHS is the suitable external safety route if the danger remains unresolved.

Controls: **Show guided hint**, **Compare with the model**, **Revise my follow-up**. Hint: “An inspection booked for 4:30 is not a completed inspection at 3:55.”

Comparison feedback: “If your report says the machine is cleared, compare the event time with the booking time. If it calls Lee a liar, revise: the evidence establishes an unconfirmed statement, not Lee's intention. Keep Tessa's instruction and the next check visible.”

**Complete matching model:**

1. “Lee reported hearing that someone had looked at the machine; this is unverified. Tessa confirmed the inspection was still booked for 4:30 p.m. on October 8.”
2. “October 8, 2026: At 3:50 p.m., Lee said they had heard someone looked at Cutter 2. At 3:55 I asked Tessa. She said the inspection was scheduled for 4:30 and that Cutter 2 must remain out of use. I will continue the packing task. No inspection outcome is yet supplied.”
3. “I need the competent inspection result and clear instructions about any restored guard, controls and authorization before using Cutter 2. I will keep the result with the original report and raise remaining safety concerns with the designated contact; Alberta OHS is the external safety route, rather than Employment Standards for a pay question.”

Select **Revise my follow-up** to return to your entries.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q1`.

Which sentence is an observation supported by Owen's original note?

- A. “Someone removed the guard to save time.” Feedback: Neither the person nor the motive is established.
- B. “The workshop always ignores safety.” Feedback: One event cannot establish that broad claim.
- C. “At 4:05 p.m. on October 7, Cutter 2's fitted guard was absent.” **Correct.** It gives the observed condition and identifies the event.
- D. “Cutter 2 injured Owen.” Feedback: The note states no injury occurred.

### Q2

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q2`.

Which responsibility belongs to Owen in the supplied case?

- A. Inspect and repair the guard without training. Feedback: Only an authorized competent person may do that work.
- B. Follow the no-use instruction, report the concern and use the assigned safe packing task. **Correct.** These actions match Owen's training and responsibilities.
- C. Certify the machine based on Lee's comment. Feedback: A coworker's unconfirmed comment is not clearance.
- D. Delete the report once a repair is booked. Feedback: A booking does not resolve the recorded concern.

### Q3

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q3`.

Why record Tessa's response as well as the original concern?

- A. Any response proves the hazard is gone. Feedback: An interim response is different from resolution.
- B. It removes the need to identify the machine. Feedback: The report still needs enough detail to locate the issue.
- C. It proves the employer intended the original condition. Feedback: The response does not establish prior intent.
- D. It gives an accurate sequence and shows what is done and what remains open. **Correct.** A useful record tracks the response, not only the initial concern.

### Q4

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q4`.

Which comparison best investigates whether regular work hours were recorded correctly?

- A. The employee's hours and payroll's regular-hours line for the same pay period. **Correct.** Matching dates and units is the starting point.
- B. One day's hours and the next month's net bank deposit. Feedback: Both date range and amount type differ.
- C. A coworker's wage and the worker's age. Feedback: These do not establish the hours worked in the relevant period.
- D. Gross wages and the worker's planned spending. Feedback: Spending plans are not time or pay evidence.

### Q5

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q5`.

Which route is the best match for an unresolved regular-wage recording question at an ordinary provincially regulated, non-union retail employer?

- A. OHS solely because it happened at work. Feedback: OHS is the safety route, not the ordinary wage-record route.
- B. The Human Rights Commission without any protected-ground concern. Feedback: A wage discrepancy alone does not establish a human rights issue.
- C. Payroll/employer, with Employment Standards information if unresolved. **Correct.** The route fits the subject and supplied employment arrangement.
- D. A public accusation before checking the dates. Feedback: It skips evidence review and may spread an unsupported claim.

### Q6

Implementation key: `practice:co2-03:q6`.

Which statement correctly distinguishes a question from a complaint?

- A. Saving this lesson automatically files a complaint. Feedback: Course responses stay within the course's evidence system.
- B. Asking Employment Standards for process information does not itself submit a formal complaint. **Correct.** A complaint has its own eligibility, records and procedure.
- C. A question guarantees a favourable decision. Feedback: Information is not adjudication of the case.
- D. Every workplace concern uses the same agency and deadline. Feedback: Different topics have different authorities and procedures.

## 7. Apply independently

### A different record: Nadia's regular wages

Nadia is 18 and an ordinary, non-union employee at fictional Cedar Art Supplies in Alberta, a provincially regulated retail business. Write a **pay-record review request to payroll**. All figures are fictional; the stated hourly rate is an agreed case rate, not a claim about the legal minimum wage.

**Document E — Agreed rate and work record.** The agreement states regular wages of $18.00 per hour. For the October 5–11, 2026 pay period, Nadia worked only Thursday, October 8, from 4:00–8:00 p.m. The signed time record confirms the whole interval was work, with no unpaid break. No overtime, holiday pay or time off in lieu applies in this supplied case. Vacation pay is accrued separately and is outside this regular-wage comparison. Nadia remains employed.

**Document F — Relevant pay-statement extract, issued October 16.**

| Line | Statement value |
|---|---:|
| Pay period | October 5–11, 2026 |
| Regular hours | 3.50 hours |
| Regular rate | $18.00/hour |
| Gross regular wages | $63.00 |
| Overtime hours / earnings | 0 / $0.00 |

This is a relevant extract, not a complete model pay statement. Net pay, deductions and accrued vacation are not shown because this task compares the regular-hours and gross regular-wage lines. Do not calculate net pay from this extract.

**Document G — Supplied communication log.** Nadia checks the statement on October 16 and has not yet asked payroll about the difference. Payroll contact Morgan is available through the employer's private payroll message channel. The next pay period ends October 25; its statement is scheduled for October 30. There is no supplied explanation for the difference. The workplace has no relevant variance or special agreement in this case.

Create one report using four fields:

- **Compare the matching records** — identify the pay period, time worked and statement hours. Calculate the hours difference with units.
- **Calculate the regular-wage difference** — show expected hours × agreed rate, the statement's amount, and the difference in dollars. State what the calculation does not establish.
- **Draft the review request** — give the date, relevant records and specific request in a short message to Morgan. Ask for a review/explanation or correction; do not assume deliberate underpayment.
- **Plan the follow-up and source route** — explain which records Nadia should retain, what to check next and which authority fits an unresolved pay issue. No actual contact or submission is required.

Success means comparing the same dates and amount type, showing the arithmetic, preserving uncertainty about the cause and choosing a proportionate evidence-based follow-up.

**Compare with a complete model**

> The October 5–11 work record shows 4:00–8:00 p.m. on October 8: four hours, with no unpaid break. The statement shows 3.50 hours, a difference of 0.50 hour. At the agreed $18.00 per hour, the work record gives $72.00 of regular wages. The statement's $63.00 is consistent with its own 3.50-hour entry, but is $9.00 below the work-record calculation. This is a discrepancy to review, not proof of the reason for it; it is not a net-pay calculation.
>
> October 16, 2026 — Morgan, please review my October 5–11 regular-hours entry. My signed record shows work from 4:00–8:00 p.m. on October 8 with no unpaid break, while the statement lists 3.50 hours and $63.00 at $18.00/hour. My calculation is four hours × $18.00 = $72.00, a $9.00 difference. Please compare the original time entry and payroll entry, explain any difference, and confirm whether a correction is needed and when it will appear. I can provide copies of the agreement, signed time record and statement.
>
> Nadia should retain the originals and a dated copy of the request and reply. Check any correction and the next statement, scheduled for October 30, rather than assuming the matter is resolved when someone says it is being reviewed. Employment Standards is the external information route for this ordinary wage issue. Its current complaint instructions distinguish an employer review, waiting one pay period for ordinary pay corrections, and a formal complaint; Nadia can ask about the process if the issue remains unresolved. OHS is not the wage-record authority. Nothing is submitted by this classroom task.
