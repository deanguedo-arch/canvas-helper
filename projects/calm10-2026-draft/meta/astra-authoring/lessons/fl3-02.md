## 1. Start here

### One amount cannot do every job at once

Spending, saving, repaying debt and investing can each serve a purpose. The useful question is which purpose takes priority with the money actually available. You will explain a choice and its **opportunity cost**: the next-best alternative given up.

Your final product is a decision note for a fictional learner choosing between a necessary repair, an outing and a speculative investment idea. You will identify the amount needed, the priority and what is forgone. You do not need personal financial information.

**How to complete this lesson:** Read **Avery's $80 decision**, learn **Compare purposes before returns**, and follow a recommendation using Avery's stated priorities. Practise with one changed condition. Answer six checks, then complete **Jules's repair decision**. Compare, revise and sign off. Your entries save, and you can pause after the guided comparison.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

### Avery's $80 decision

Avery is a fictional adult with **$80 unallocated today**, after essentials and required debt payments. This is the only extra money available for this decision.

**Situation note:** Avery has $20 in an accessible emergency reserve and wants that reserve to reach $100. A separate debt balance is $300 at a fixed 18% annual rate. Its required payment is already covered in the budget. Extra principal payments are allowed without a fee. Reducing its balance reduces later interest under unchanged terms.

**Available choices:**

- Add the $80 to the accessible reserve; there is no withdrawal fee or lock-in in this fictional account.
- Make an extra $80 principal payment, reducing debt from $300 to $220. Paid principal cannot be withdrawn from this loan again.
- Buy an investment for $80. Two invented one-year scenarios value it at $100 or $60, with no fees in those scenarios. Neither outcome is promised; selling earlier could have another value.

**Avery's priorities, supplied for this case:** First bring the emergency reserve to $100; then reduce costly debt; then consider long-term investing. The investment is not needed to meet a particular deadline.

**Read the note:** Which $80 is being allocated?

A. Money already used for required debt payments. B. A promised investment gain. C. The $300 debt balance. D. The extra amount left after essentials and required payments.

D follows the budget note. A spends committed money again; B assumes an uncertain gain; C is an obligation rather than available cash.

## 3. Learn the method

### Compare purposes before returns

**Saving** sets money aside for a purpose, often with access and stability important for near-term needs. **Investing** puts money into assets with possible income or growth, alongside risks and possible losses. **Debt repayment** reduces an obligation. **Spending** buys a good, service or experience. None of these words alone proves a choice is suitable.

Ask when the money may be needed and what happens if it is unavailable or worth less. Avery's reserve needs ready access. The investment scenarios do not guarantee $80 will be available when an emergency occurs. Even a possible higher return does not answer the access question.

An extra debt payment can avoid future interest under the loan's terms, but it also moves cash into a use that may not be reversible. This case expressly says Avery cannot borrow that paid amount back. A decision about debt and saving therefore compares both cost and access.

### Name the next-best alternative

**Opportunity cost** is the value of the best alternative you give up when you choose. It is not automatically the sticker price and not the sum of every rejected option. Use the person's stated priorities to identify the next-best feasible option.

For Avery, if the reserve receives $80, the next-best use is extra debt repayment. The opportunity cost includes the debt reduction and associated interest saving delayed by choosing the reserve. The uncertain investment is a further alternative, not an additional opportunity cost to add to the debt repayment as though both could have been chosen with the same $80.

You can recognise a tradeoff without declaring a choice wrong. “This builds access, but postpones debt reduction” is a more useful explanation than “saving is always good.” Different priorities or circumstances can justify a different allocation.

### Test claims about return

A **return** is the gain or loss from an investment relative to money invested, considering relevant costs. Avery's $100 scenario would be a $20 gain before any costs outside the stated model; the $60 scenario would be a $20 loss. They demonstrate uncertainty, not the probability of either outcome. A sales headline showing only the gain is incomplete evidence.

**Optional reading record:** FCAC's *Basics of investing* identifies goals, time, financial circumstances, risk and costs as relevant to an investment decision. Read September 28, 2026. The case supplies all required choices and priorities; no live product research is required here.

**Hint:** Complete two sentences: “I choose ___ because the case prioritises ___.” Then: “The next-best use I give up is ___.” Keep the same $80 in mind in both.

## 4. Follow a complete example

### Apply Avery's stated priority

The reserve gap is $100 − $20 = **$80**, exactly the unallocated amount. Putting all $80 there reaches the first priority without touching required payments. Debt stays at $300 for now; the extra repayment is postponed, not somehow achieved as well.

**Complete decision note:** “I would put the $80 in Avery's accessible reserve, bringing it from $20 to $100. This meets the first stated priority and keeps money available for an unexpected cost. The next-best alternative forgone is an extra $80 debt payment, which would have reduced the balance to $220 and reduced later interest. I would review extra debt repayment with the next genuinely available amount rather than assume the investment will fund both goals.”

“Choose the investment because it becomes $100” leaves out the $60 scenario and other possible values. “Save $80 and repay $80” spends $160 when only $80 is available. The model's strength is that it states what remains unfinished.

## 5. Practise with support

### The reserve is already funded

Use a changed version of Avery's situation: the accessible reserve is **already $100**, and there is still **$80 unallocated today**. The debt remains $300, minimums remain covered, extra principal payments have no fee and cannot be redrawn. The priority order is unchanged. This is a fresh scenario, not another $80 added to the previous answer.

Choose **extra debt payment**, **duplicate the completed reserve target without explanation**, **assume the investment gain is certain**, or **spend $160 across two uses**. Enter **Debt balance after my extra payment ($)**. Write **The benefit and opportunity cost of this choice**, two sentences. Use **Check the priority**.

The first choice follows the next unmet priority. The balance is $300 − $80 = **$220**. An answer of $380 adds repayment to debt; $80 states the payment rather than the balance. The other choices either require an explained new priority or rely on unsupported money/returns.

**Model:** “With the reserve already at its target, I would pay $80 extra toward debt, leaving $220. This reduces future interest under the unchanged loan terms, but the money can no longer be used as accessible cash or put toward the next investment opportunity. Under Avery's stated ranking, investing is now the next-best alternative being postponed; its return remains uncertain.”

If you prefer increasing the reserve above its target, state that you are changing Avery's priority and explain the reason. Do not call it the only conclusion required by the supplied ranking. Revise your note to name the actual alternative.

## 6. Check your understanding

### Q1 — Opportunity cost is:

A. The value of the next-best alternative given up. B. Every rejected alternative added together. C. Always a tax. D. Always the purchase price.

Answer A. A defines the comparison; B double-counts incompatible uses; C identifies a different cost; D may miss time, access or benefits forgone.

### Q2 — Why might accessible saving suit Avery's first priority?

A. It guarantees the highest return. B. It cancels the debt. C. It keeps the reserve available rather than exposed to the supplied investment's uncertain value. D. It creates a second $80.

Answer C. A is not established; B and D invent effects; C connects access to purpose.

### Q3 — What does the $60 investment scenario show?

A. Exactly a 50% chance of loss. B. All investments will end at $60. C. The loan balance disappears. D. The investment could be worth less; the scenario does not state its probability.

Answer D. A invents odds; B generalizes one scenario; C is unrelated; D preserves the evidence's limit.

### Q4 — Extra debt repayment can have what tradeoff in this case?

A. It increases the debt by the payment. B. It lowers principal but uses cash that cannot be redrawn. C. It guarantees investment profits. D. It replaces every future required payment automatically.

Answer B. A reverses repayment; B states the supplied tradeoff; C is unrelated; D ignores the remaining debt and terms.

### Q5 — What changes when Avery's reserve is already $100?

A. The next unmet priority can guide the new $80. B. The old $80 can be spent twice. C. Every investment is now safe. D. All future income is guaranteed.

Answer A. A uses the changed condition; B–D invent resources or certainty.

### Q6 — Which explanation demonstrates a reasoned choice?

A. “Everyone should make the same choice.” B. “The biggest possible return settles it.” C. “This meets the stated deadline, while postponing the next-best use.” D. “There is no tradeoff if I dislike the alternative.”

Answer C. A ignores circumstances; B ignores risk/timing; C identifies purpose and sacrifice; D does not remove an alternative's value.

## 7. Apply independently

### Jules's repair decision

Jules is fictional and needs a bicycle for a confirmed activity starting **two weeks from now**. A suitable repair costs **$100**, including all charges, and must be booked and paid by that date. **$60 is already reserved for the repair**. Jules has **$40 extra today** after other obligations, with no further money confirmed before the deadline.

An optional outing today costs **$40**. A fictional investment advertisement suggests putting $40 into an asset that “could grow quickly,” but supplies no guaranteed value or withdrawal date. Jules's stated priority is the repair first, outing second, investment third. The repair price is valid until the deadline.

Enter **Additional repair money needed ($)** and **Repair funds if the extra $40 is saved ($)**. Write **Jules's decision and opportunity cost**, 3–4 sentences using the dates and ranking. Explain why the investment headline does not close a funding gap.

**Complete model:** “The repair needs another $100 − $60 = $40. I would reserve the extra $40, bringing repair funds to $100 before the two-week deadline. The next-best alternative forgone is today's $40 outing, following Jules's stated ranking. The investment headline promises neither a value nor access date, so it cannot supply dependable repair money in this plan.”

The opportunity cost is not $40 plus every possible future investment gain. Explain the single next-best alternative, then revise your own note.
