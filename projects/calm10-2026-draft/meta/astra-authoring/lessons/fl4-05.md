# FL4-05 — Coordinate a recovery plan

Version `2026-09-28.astra.1`. Assessment A7. All people, incidents, messages, provider replies and reference labels are fictional. Official Canadian response guidance checked September 28, 2026. No real report, account change or disclosure is required.

## 1. Start here

After fraud, a person may need several kinds of help. A bank can address a payment route; an email provider can address account access; police and fraud-reporting services have reporting roles. Sending one report does not automatically complete the other jobs. A calm plan assigns each action to the organization that can carry it out and records what has actually happened.

You will coordinate a response to a ticket-payment and password incident, update the plan when providers reply, and then build a distinct plan for identity information and an independently observed tax-account change. The final product is a prioritized response plan with specific recipients, safe routes, evidence and follow-up conditions.

Follow **Meet the situation and read the documents → Learn the method → Follow a complete example → Practise with support → Check your understanding → Apply independently → Review and complete**. Work in small parts and pause after the response table if useful. Everything happens in the fictional record. Do not open a real financial account, disclose a SIN, contact an agency or ask another person to supervise. Guided work and six questions are practice; the new independent plan and sign-offs complete this lesson. Your writing saves automatically.

**Vocabulary.** Recovery is the process of responding to an incident and restoring security or addressing losses; it does not promise that money will be returned. An affected provider controls a relevant account or service. Identity fraud involves misuse of identity information; exposure and confirmed misuse should be distinguished. A credit report records credit information; a fraud alert asks potential creditors to take additional identity-verification steps. A recovery scam offers to recover losses as a way to demand more money, access or secrets. A status log separates planned actions, actions taken and confirmed results.

Optional disclosure: **Official recovery records and routes**. The in-course records below support all required decisions. Live pages are optional for current real use.

## 2. Meet the situation and read the documents

Ari is a fictional adult who tried to purchase a concert ticket. The incident has two confirmed exposures: a personally approved payment induced by deception, and a password entered on a false delivery page. Read the complete starting record before choosing agencies.

### Document A — Incident log, October 18, 2026

| Time / category | Supplied facts |
|---|---|
| 2:00 p.m., payment | Ari personally approved a $160 electronic transfer from Ari's bank to a supposed ticket seller. The transfer shows as deposited in the genuine bank app. No ticket was supplied. The actual recipient/account details are omitted from this course. |
| 2:10 p.m., information entry | A false “ticket delivery registration” page asked for email login details. Ari entered the email login/password, name and mailing address. That same password is used for a music account. The email is the music account's recovery address. |
| Not entered / not observed | No SIN, date of birth, identity-document image, banking password, payment-card details or one-time code was entered. No app was installed and no remote access granted. No new credit account, unfamiliar bank transaction or CRA-account change is reported. |
| 2:20 p.m., current status | Ari can still access the bank, email and music services through previously known routes. A separate updated personal device is available. No provider has yet been contacted and no security change has been recorded. |
| Evidence already available | Seller conversation, original transfer record, message requesting the delivery login and a screenshot without the entered password. No need to reopen the false page. |

### Document B — Available independent routes and controls

- Ari's genuine bank app, opened directly on the separate device, contains the bank's fraud-contact route. The bank can review the transfer and advise on the payment/account response. The record does not promise the deposited transfer can be recovered.
- Ari has the known email app and a saved official music-service bookmark. These fictional providers allow a unique new password, recovery-detail review, review/sign-out of other sessions and MFA. Their guide says a password change does not automatically end all other sessions. Official recovery support is available if access is lost. These interface details are case assumptions, not universal service instructions.
- The official local police website and CAFC's official “What to do if you're a victim of fraud” page can be reached independently. The latter links to **Report Cybercrime and Fraud**, the official online reporting route. A course response is not sent to either service.

### Document C — New message from an unverified stranger, 2:25 p.m.

> I recover stolen ticket money. I know your loss was $160. Pay my $40 administration fee and send your email verification code, and I guarantee the money will be returned today. Do not speak to the bank because that will delay us.

The sender is not independently verified. No actual address, link, telephone number or code is supplied. Knowing the amount does not explain how the sender obtained it or establish authority.

### Document D — In-course agency and response records

**CAFC, “What to do if you're a victim of fraud,” and “Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre Impersonation”; checked September 28, 2026.** Keep relevant records, promptly contact the institution that transferred money, and report fraud to local police and the official fraud/cybercrime reporting route. Recovery offers can demand fees or more information. Independently reach official services; a convincing name, document or detail from the incident is not sufficient verification. Reporting does not guarantee recovery.

**Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, “Protecting yourself from identity theft online”; checked September 28, 2026.** Identify the information and accounts affected, secure compromised/reused credentials and connected accounts, contact affected providers and monitor relevant activity. Choose identity-protection measures from the actual exposure or misuse, not an invented list of every possible stolen document.

**CRA, “Report a scam or identity theft”; checked September 28, 2026.** Suspected CRA-account compromise or unauthorized changes to CRA information, including bank or address details, warrant contacting CRA through its official reporting/support route. An unfamiliar call is not itself proof of an account change. A person can independently open the official CRA page to identify the relevant secure or telephone option. CRA account support is distinct from a CAFC report.

**Service Canada, “SIN fraud and data breaches”; checked September 28, 2026; page modified May 13, 2026.** Suspected SIN misuse or exposure calls for monitoring credit information and relevant accounts. The page points to both Canadian credit bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, and explains fraud alerts and responses when fraud is identified. It says a person does not need to notify the SIN Program about suspected misuse or a breach. Issuing a new SIN is not a routine cure: the old number and its records do not simply disappear. Contact affected institutions or government programs for the misuse involving them.

## 3. Learn the method

### Start with an exposure map

List what actually left the person's control: money, a password, card details, a SIN, an identity document or device access. Then connect each item to its provider. This is more useful than a universal instruction to “call everyone.” Ari's payment belongs in a bank response; the reused credential belongs in email and music-account responses.

Separate facts from possible consequences. Ari's name and address could assist future impersonation, so continued caution matters. Those facts do not establish a new credit account or tax-account intrusion. Conversely, if a case supplies a SIN exposure and an independently observed unauthorized tax-account change, identity and CRA responses have concrete reasons. Match the measure to the evidence.

### Prioritize time-sensitive controls, then coordinate

Do not postpone urgent provider contact while perfecting an evidence file. Ari already has the essential payment record and message. The bank needs prompt notice of the deceptive transfer, and exposed accounts need prompt protection through independent trusted routes. Write an order and a reason; some actions could occur close together rather than waiting days for one organization to finish.

For the connected credentials, securing the recovery email before the music account is defensible because access to that email can help reset the music account. Follow the supplied session and recovery controls as well as changing passwords. A person who has lost access may need the provider's official recovery process instead. The course cannot certify that a real account is secure.

### Give each organization one clear job

| Confirmed issue | Suitable recipient and route | Evidence / requested help |
|---|---|---|
| Deceptive transfer or exposed financial account | Financial institution through an existing statement/card or genuine app | Payment details and truthful account of approval/exposure; ask about protection, investigation and possible recovery. |
| Entered/reused credentials | Affected account providers through known apps or independently reached official support | Affected account names and access status; secure credentials, sessions and recovery methods. |
| Fraud incident or identity misuse | Local police via independently reached official local route; CAFC-linked Report Cybercrime and Fraud | Dated incident, amounts/categories, evidence and provider references; reporting is separate from account controls. |
| Unauthorized CRA-account information change | CRA through the official Canada.ca report-scam/identity-theft page | Identify the observed change and request the relevant account protection/identity assistance. |
| SIN exposure or credit identity concerns | Both credit bureaus through links on Service Canada's official SIN page, plus affected creditors/programs when relevant | Review reports, ask about appropriate alerts or dispute unfamiliar credit activity; an alert is not a universal account lock. |

The table supplies routes rather than memorized phone numbers. A route is independent when it comes from a trusted source already held or an official page reached without the suspicious message's link. A caller who says “use this number to verify me” is still controlling the proposed route. An official-looking logo or knowledge of an incident does not change that.

### Keep a living status log

Use three columns: **action planned → action taken and reference → result or next check**. “Reported to bank” does not mean “money refunded.” “Changed password” does not mean every active session ended. “Subscription closed” does not mean a disputed charge was reversed. Specific status language prevents false reassurance and shows which task remains.

Record times, amounts, contact labels, categories disclosed, responses and case references. Do not put a password, SIN, full account number or one-time code into the course. Existing evidence can be kept safely; do not revisit an unsafe page or continue a scam conversation to gather more. Real institutions may have secure evidence processes; reach them independently before sharing sensitive originals.

### Reject a second loss without blame

An offer of guaranteed recovery can be attractive after a loss. That is why a recovery scam may repeat the amount, impersonate an agency and insist on a fee, code or secrecy. Ari's stranger asks for both money and an email code while discouraging the bank. Stop that interaction and preserve the existing message. The person's first mistake does not create an obligation to keep paying or to prove they deserve help.

**Optional first-step hint:** “Draw two branches from Ari's record: $160 → bank; reused email/music password → connected providers. Add reporting and follow-up after those controls.”

## 4. Follow a complete example

I begin with the financial fact: Ari approved the transfer under deceptive circumstances. I do not call it an unapproved transaction, and I do not assume a deposited transfer can simply be cancelled. I direct prompt notice to the bank through its genuine app, using the separate device.

Next I connect the email and music accounts. Both use the entered password, and the email is a recovery path for music. The provider guide tells me to handle sessions separately. The stranger's knowledge of $160 does not verify the offer; the proposed $40 payment and email code would create further exposure.

**Complete coordinated plan:**

> At 2:00 p.m. on October 18, 2026, Ari approved a $160 transfer for a ticket that was not supplied; the genuine bank app shows it deposited. Promptly use that app's independently known fraud-contact route from the separate updated device. Explain the deception, approval and deposited status; ask about payment/account protection, investigation and any available recovery. Preserve the existing transfer record and record the bank's reference and instructions. No refund or cancellation is established.
>
> Stop using the false delivery page and do not answer the recovery stranger, pay $40 or send a code. Promptly secure the email account, then music, through the known app/bookmark. Use different strong passwords, review recovery details and other sessions, sign out other sessions using the supplied controls, and enable supported MFA. If access or recovery details cannot be secured, use official provider recovery support. Record completed steps and remaining issues.
>
> Keep the seller conversation, transfer record, login request and recovery message without putting passwords or full financial details in this plan. Report the fraud to local police through the independently reached official local route and to the CAFC-linked Report Cybercrime and Fraud service; retain any references. Continue checking the affected bank and accounts for changes and follow provider advice. The supplied exposure is money, an email/music credential, name and mailing address. Do not invent SIN theft, a new credit account or a CRA change, or assume a credit alert is automatically necessary solely because the name/address were disclosed. Reassess if new evidence appears. Reporting supports the response but does not itself secure the accounts or guarantee the $160 returns.

The unsupported shortcut is “CAFC will recover everything; wait for its answer.” It leaves the bank and exposed credentials unattended and invents a result. Coordination means several specific jobs, each with a relevant recipient.
