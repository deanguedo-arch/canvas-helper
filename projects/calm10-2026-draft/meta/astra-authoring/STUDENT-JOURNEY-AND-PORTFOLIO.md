# Student journey, orientation and transfer portfolio

Complete copy for the existing Overview, My Work and Final portfolio routes. Version: 2026-09-28.astra.1. This is an implementation specification, not a new learner route or a forty-first lesson. The seven-hour portfolio allocation already exists in the 75-hour design budget.

## Overview: learner copy

### Welcome to CALM 10

In this course you will practise decisions about careers, work, money and personal information. You will read realistic documents, follow an explained example, try a decision with help and apply the method to a new situation.

All the information needed for the course activities is supplied here. People, employers, budgets and incidents in the cases are fictional. Official reading records are labelled with their source and the date they were checked. You can open the original page for current information, but you do not need another account or a paid service to do the lesson.

### Start with one lesson

1. Open **Find evidence about your strengths**.
2. Read **Start here** to see what you will make. Open **How to complete this lesson** for the steps.
3. Meet the person and read the documents. Their facts are the evidence for your answers.
4. Follow the explanation and example. Try the supported activity, check its feedback and revise.
5. Complete the new independent case. Compare the model, review the four criteria and select **Complete lesson**.

You can pause at a section break. A lesson's fields save automatically when saving is available; check the status beside your work before closing. **My Work** lets you return to a draft, see completed lessons and download a backup.

### What the symbols mean

- **Draft**: you have started a final response.
- **Green check / Complete**: you have filled the required final fields, reviewed the criteria and selected Complete lesson.
- **Practice feedback**: explains a choice or calculation. You can try again.
- **Model response**: one supported answer with reasoning. Other answers may work when the documents support them.
- **Optional reference**: opens further reading. The in-course record supplies what this lesson needs.

A completion check records your review. It does not mean a computer has judged the quality of your writing. Editing the independent answer reopens that lesson for review and keeps your text.

### If you get stuck

**I do not understand a word.** Open Core vocabulary beside the lesson guide. Return to the sentence where it appears and connect it to the case.

**I do not know which document to use.** Read the heading directly above the task. It names the person and record. A “new situation” has its own documents; do not carry numbers from the previous example.

**I do not know how to begin.** Open the first-step hint. Find one fact before writing the whole answer. The complete model remains available whenever you need it.

**My calculation differs.** Check the units, time period and rounding note. Compare one step at a time. A blank field is different from zero.

**My answer is different from the model.** Check whether every claim is supported, whether you respected the case constraints and whether you explained your choice. Use the self-checks to revise. The writing tasks can have more than one sound response.

**A website or video will not open.** Continue with the in-course reading record and written explanation. No required answer is hidden in a video or behind an external login.

**Saving is unavailable.** Keep this page open and download your work. The status will tell you when a browser save failed. A download is a backup file; it is not a submission.

### Plan your pace

The course has 40 lessons in seven sections. The suggested schedule spreads approximately 75 hours across 15 weeks. Some lessons need more time for calculations or revision. Use a section break as a stopping point and return to your last work.

Field label: **My study rhythm**. Help: “Name two possible study times and one place you will pause to review. You can change this plan later.” Example: “Monday and Thursday after lunch; review my saved work after every two lessons.” Retain the existing study-rhythm key, if present, with a versioned prompt. This field does not add a lesson to the progress count.

## Replace the old baseline self-check

Use the course's light practice section, normal body text and labelled controls. Keep it optional and outside completion. No dark solid-green panel. Four questions with reveal/check feedback:

1. **A fictional $50 purchase has 10% added tax. Total ($):** correct $55. Feedback for $5: “That is the tax only: add it to $50.” Feedback for $45: “The tax is added in this case.” Other: “10% of $50 is $5; the total is $55.” This is a supplied exercise rate, not a claim about Alberta tax.
2. **Gross pay is $400; current deductions total $60. Available after deductions ($):** correct $340. For $460 explain deductions subtract; for $60 explain the deduction is not take-home pay; otherwise show $400 − $60.
3. **One program page has no date. Another is current and lists a prerequisite the learner has not completed. Which note is sound?** A “The undated page must be easier.” B “Admission is guaranteed.” C “Record the missing prerequisite and verify the undated claim before planning.” D “Ignore both dates.” Correct C. A invents an easier rule; B overlooks the missing course; D removes context.
4. **An award message asks for a bank password today. Next action?** A Reply with it. B Open the linked login. C Pay a verification fee. D Reach the actual award organization using an independently known route. Correct D. A exposes a secret; B trusts the suspicious route; C adds an unsupported payment.

Keys: baseline:tax, baseline:net, baseline:program, baseline:scam. Preserve any historical keys by alias/archive; do not create a second progress counter. No mark, pass threshold or prerequisite lock.

Numeracy help disclosure: **Find the base, change and result**. Copy: “For a 10% increase on $50, the base is $50 and the change is 0.10 × $50 = $5. The result is $55. For a decrease, subtract $5 to get $45. Interest also needs a time period and a stated method; use the method supplied in that lesson.”

## Final portfolio: learner copy

### Bring your decisions together

A portfolio is a small, explained selection of work. Choose evidence that shows a decision, its reasons and an improvement. You do not need a paid job or personal financial records. Use your own learning actions in this course; money and workplace examples can remain fictional.

Work on the four parts below across the final weeks. For each part, choose the relevant lesson from a dropdown, review your saved answer and write the requested explanation. The course shows the selected answer read-only beside the portfolio fields. Missing lesson work has a link to the lesson; it is never filled by a model without your action.

### Part 1 — A strength, a goal and the next step

Choose one actual action you took while learning here: checked a calculation, compared two dated claims, revised a sentence to remove an exaggeration, or corrected a plan after feedback. Explain what that action suggests about a skill and what it does not yet prove.

Fields:
- **Lesson and action I am using as evidence** — select lesson; write 2–3 sentences describing your own action.
- **A possible direction and a life priority** — write 2–3 sentences. A direction may be tentative, such as work involving careful checking; a life priority may be predictable study time. You do not need to disclose family circumstances.
- **My next learning step and review point** — one action you can take in the course and when you will review it. For example, compare your next budget with its model after the first attempt.
- **School-route question still to verify** — name one course, preparation or admission question from Build a high-school route with checkpoints. Open that lesson’s supplied route records beside this field. You may use its supplied fictional route; identify it as fictional. “Not yet known” is acceptable when paired with a precise question.

Complete example: “In Read a pay stub, I checked the current deductions instead of using the year-to-date column, then revised my calculation. This suggests careful checking after feedback; one corrected example does not establish payroll expertise. I would like to explore work that uses organized records, while keeping time for activities outside school. My next step is to check both dates and totals in Build a flexible budget, then review the first attempt against the model. The route records in Build a high-school route with checkpoints show how prerequisites link together; I still need to verify which actual courses prepare for a real records-related program before using that as my own school plan.”

Specific checks: own observable course action; cautious skill claim; direction and life priority; feasible next step and clearly labelled unverified question.

### Part 2 — A money decision with a revision

Select one of the five final tasks below. Explain its original constraint and one calculation, then use the listed change. Every other fact in that independent case remains the same. These are separate hypothetical versions; do not move facts from the lesson's guided case into its independent case.

| Final task | Exact changed condition |
|---|---|
| Compare a major purchase: Riley | Total cash available for the tool choice is $130 instead of $140. |
| What a major purchase really costs: Lee | Total seasonal budget is $540 instead of $550. |
| Give a savings goal a job: Lena | A one-time $10 required cost is deducted from the eighth weekly contribution before the fee is paid. The first seven contributions remain $18 each; only $8 of the eighth is available for this fee. |
| Choose what the next dollar does: Jules | The extra amount available before the repair deadline is $30 instead of $40. The saved $60 remains. |
| Check what your money will buy: Riley | Current savings are $238 instead of $248. The confirmed later $10 and the case's prices/dates remain. |

Do not invent an interest rate, approval, price reduction or extra income. A sound revision can identify an unresolved gap and the exact option that would need confirmation.

Fields: **Selected money lesson**; **Original decision and calculation** (3–4 sentences); **Changed condition and revised decision** (3–4 sentences).

Example using Give a savings goal a job: “Lena has $18 weekly for an eight-week $144 fee, so 8 × $18 meets it exactly. I would pause the $6 hobby contribution because no additional money is supplied. With the new $10 cost taken from the eighth contribution, the fee fund would reach 7 × $18 + $8 = $134 and be $10 short. I could identify that gap and investigate a confirmed extension or lower-cost alternative; I could not claim either exists. The original plan depends on all eight contributions being available.”

Checks: correct base facts and units; visible calculation; change affects the conclusion; no invented resource.

### Part 3 — A safe response and its limit

Select a workplace or personal-information lesson. Explain the action you chose, the evidence for it, the appropriate person/organization and one limit to what the record establishes.

Fields: **Selected workplace or information lesson**; **My response and reason** (4–6 sentences).

Example: “In the library shift, the spill blocks the shorter route and the cleaning spray requires training. Rowan should keep clear, place the cone only if it can be reached safely and contact Morgan using the supplied extension. The activity-room route is clear on the floor plan. Rowan should not use the spray or change a record without authority. This is a decision within the supplied exercise; it does not prove workplace training or supervised experience.”

Checks: hazard/exposure identified; action fits supplied authority; correct contact/route; uncertainty or limit stated.

### Part 4 — Show the improvement

Select one answer you revised. Display the earlier saved version if available, or let the learner quote a short earlier sentence from their own notes. A missing earlier version is not fabricated; they may describe the specific weakness and quote the current sentence instead.

Fields: **Work I revised**; **Earlier idea or specific weakness**; **My improved response**; **Why this is better and what I will check next** (3–4 sentences).

Example: “My earlier interview sentence called Rowan an expert at records. The supplied open-house record showed that Rowan noticed activity cards under the wrong headings, checked the approved list with the lead and relabelled the stacks with confirmation. I changed it to describe those actions and the result: visitors received the intended card. The revised claim is narrower and supported. In my next application example, I will check that every result comes from the supplied record.”

## Portfolio implementation and curriculum limit

Use a separately versioned portfolio group with keys portfolio:transfer:{lesson,action,direction,next,schoolQuestion}; portfolio:money:{lesson,original,change}; portfolio:safety:{lesson,response}; portfolio:revision:{lesson,before,after,reason}; four portfolio signoffs and a distinct reviewedAt. Preserve every existing portfolio key/history; do not replace historical A/T evidence.

All named written fields are required for portfolio review; four lesson selectors must name existing saved work; the money selector contains only the five eligible final tasks above. “Unknown + precise next question” counts as a response; no arbitrary minimum length, keyword scoring or fabricated mastery. Disclosure of sensitive personal data is not required.

This adds genuine reflection on the learner's course performance and tentative goals. It still does not supply a verified personal school graduation/admission plan, real professional networking, supervised work participation or an actual workplace reflection. Those limitations remain in OUTCOME-EVIDENCE-MATRIX.md.
