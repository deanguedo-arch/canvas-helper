## 1. Summary and boundary

The five lessons use supplied fictional documents to teach a connected sequence: profile information and account layers → suspicious and possibly genuine contacts → account/card exposure response → consumer marketing and purchase disputes → coordinated recovery. All required facts, questions, constructed activities, models and official-source paraphrases are in the lesson scripts. No outside reading, login, real account action, phone call, personal financial data, teacher feedback or supervised experience is needed to complete them.

Every lesson contains the contract's ten exact headings, a document-reading check, three guided construction fields, six four-option MC questions with option-specific explanations, four new-case independent fields and four criterion signoffs. Prior task text/responses must be preserved during implementation. Completion is learner review of a saved product, not automated writing correctness or evidence of an actual protected account.

The existing five routes, A7 assessment reference and approximate course design budget remain. No arbitrary duration was assigned to individual lessons. Original course source and archived resources were preserved.

## 3. Deliberate source distinctions and resolutions

- **CRA calls:** retain the possibility of genuine contact. Teach an independent channel and the phone-checker's coverage limit, rather than the false rule “CRA never calls” or “not listed means fraud.”
- **SIN instructions differ:** the CRA page modified March 20 contains generic advice to contact Service Canada in suspected SIN misuse. The specific Service Canada SIN page modified May 13 expressly says SIN Program notification is not required. FL4-05 uses the current topic-specific instruction, explains monitoring/affected-program responses and rejects routine replacement as a cure. Future verification should recheck both pages together rather than silently restoring a generic contact list.
- **Financial events differ:** FL4-03 René did not make/approve the $84 card transaction; FL4-04 Omar recognizes the subscription but disputes the $54 terms/charge; FL4-05 Ari approved a $160 transfer under deception. All three need truthful descriptions and appropriate provider guidance; they are not assigned a single automatic refund rule.
- **Authority roles differ:** Alberta CIU consumer enforcement, Competition Bureau marketing reports, bank/provider controls, CRA account protection, police reports and CAFC-linked reporting each have a defined purpose. No source record says one report completes every task.
- **Credit response is proportional:** no blanket fraud alert for Ari's name/address exposure alone; Nico's SIN exposure plus actual CRA misuse supports credit checks/appropriate alert consideration. No guarantee that alerts lock all accounts.
- **No external prerequisite:** official facts are supplied as limited attributed paraphrases inside editable lesson HTML at implementation. Optional links support later real-life updates. Existing mandatory source-opening gates must be replaced with these complete reading records and actual constructed decisions.
