Learn the basics

Different ways of working carry different responsibilities

An interest can lead toward or a small venture. Before comparing the offers, learn who arranges the work, which costs each person carries and what the stated money amounts represent.

Separate employment from running a venture

An employee works under an employer’s arrangements and direction. A self-employed person obtains work from clients and takes responsibility for providing the service. involves developing an opportunity to offer a product or service and accepting uncertainty about demand and costs.

The same task can appear in either arrangement. The important difference may be who finds customers, sets the schedule, buys materials, provides training and handles problems. A small venture still has responsibilities.

Read the money and evidence honestly

is sales money before expenses. An expense is a cost of providing the service. For an invented example, five sales at $10 bring $50 revenue. Subtracting $15 of listed materials leaves $35 before any unlisted costs or time. Calling that complete take-home pay would hide missing information.

An employee’s gross wage is earnings before payroll deductions. A venture’s remainder after some costs is a different measure. Compare the categories and limits before comparing the amounts.

Use this path: identify the arrangement → assign responsibilities → label earnings and costs → check training, time and uncertain demand → propose a suitable learning step. School or community experience can support an application without being renamed as paid employment.

When you read the case: Read the shop offer for what the employer supplies, then the venture record for what the operator must arrange. The first question asks who carries one stated responsibility. Use the actual offer rather than assumptions about all businesses.

Meet the situation and read the documents

Kai's starting evidence

Kai is a fictional 19-year-old who has completed Grade 12. During eight community workshop sessions, Kai checked part labels against an inventory list, explained completed steps to visitors and asked the workshop leader before unfamiliar work. Kai has not had a paid repair-shop job. Kai likes practical work and explaining it, wants to develop technical knowledge and values a predictable short weekly shift.

A work offer record describes what a particular employer is offering. A venture record organizes a person's proposed sales and expenses. The documents below are original fiction, with invented prices and wages; they are not current offers or legal guidance.

Cedar Cycle shop helper offer

One four-hour Saturday helper shift at $18 per hour, before payroll deductions. The shop schedules customers, supplies tools and materials, handles payments and directs the work. Kai would check inventory, explain the service-booking process and ask the supervisor about unfamiliar requests. Training for those duties is provided. Kai is not being hired as an independent repair technician. This record confirms one offered shift only; later hours are unknown.

Kai's first-week service trial

For the scenario, five service jobs are completed under appropriate workshop supervision and all five customers pay $30. Replacement parts cost $75 in total; transport and other supplies cost $25 in total. Kai spends six hours on the work and administration. Use the method with the case

Compare the way of working as well as the task

An employee works within an employer's direction and arrangements. A self-employed person is responsible for obtaining work from clients rather than being on an employer's payroll. involves developing an opportunity and taking on the uncertainty of offering a product or service. A venture can be small; size alone does not remove its responsibilities.

The same interest can appear in either route. Kai may enjoy bicycles while preferring the shop's scheduling and supervision. Or Kai may want to learn about pricing and customer needs as well as technical work. Neither preference makes a person more capable or more ambitious. The useful question is which responsibilities and supports fit the current goal.

Follow the money through the record

is money from sales before expenses. An expense is a cost of providing the product or service. Subtracting the listed expenses gives an amount remaining under the supplied assumptions. If some costs are not recorded, do not label that remainder complete profit or take-home pay.

Use the flow number of paid sales × price → revenue → subtract listed costs → remainder before unlisted costs/time. It keeps a sales headline from hiding the resources used to earn it. A fixed cost stays the same in the particular scenario; a per-item cost repeats for every item. Fewer sales do not necessarily reduce every expense.

Wages before payroll deductions are also different from take-home pay. Four hours at $18 produces a gross wage calculation, not proof of the amount deposited. Comparing that wage directly with a venture's incomplete remainder as if both were final income would mislead. Explain the categories and missing information before making a recommendation.

Test an opportunity without pretending demand is certain

A person liking an idea is not the same as paying the proposed price. One week's sales do not establish reliable future demand. Consider who needs the service, what alternatives they have and what conditions they will accept. Customer feedback can justify changing the size, timing or scope of an offer. It does not justify removing a necessary safety step or pretending a material cost has disappeared.

For this course, testing means using supplied scenario cards and calculations. A next learning step can be making a more complete cost record or revising an explanation. No real purchase, customer research or unsupervised work is required.

Prepare truthful evidence for an employment route

A résumé summarizes relevant skills, education and experience in an organized record. A basic structure is a name/contact heading, a short relevant summary, skills supported by examples, dated experience entries and education/training. Real applications may need contact information; a public practice page should not display private addresses, identification numbers or other people's information.

Using only the Cedar Cycle offer and Kai's starting evidence, this compact fictional résumé section would be truthful:

Kai — practice candidate; private contact details omitted

Summary: Interested in supervised bicycle-shop helper work and developing technical knowledge.

Relevant experience: Community workshop participant, eight sessions. Checked part labels against an inventory list; explained completed steps to visitors; asked the leader before unfamiliar work.

Education: Grade 12 completed in this fictional case.

This describes experience without changing it into a paid job. A cover letter is a message to a particular employer explaining why selected evidence fits the opening. For the Cedar Cycle offer, a truthful sentence would be: “My workshop experience checking labels against a list relates to your inventory duty.” A portfolio contains selected examples with explanations, rather than every file a person owns. An online employment profile presents relevant background on a digital service; it is not a reason to post private documents publicly. No résumé, message or profile is sent in this lesson.

For example, a portfolio could contain a blank sample inventory checklist that Kai created to demonstrate the checking method, accompanied by a note explaining the task and Kai's contribution. A visitor's identifiable repair form would be a poor public sample: it could reveal someone else's details and would not by itself explain Kai's work. Select the minimum relevant evidence and remove private information. A cover letter then points the employer to the useful example; it does not need to retell every item in the résumé.

The claim starts with a valid multiplication: five paid jobs × $30 = $150 revenue. The error is naming that entire amount profit. The record lists $75 parts and $25 other costs. Subtracting both leaves $50. Time and other potential costs have not been valued, so I keep that boundary in the sentence.

It does not prove $150 profit or recurring weekly sales. The Cedar Cycle offer provides $72 gross wages for one supervised four-hour shift, with the shop handling customers, tools and payments. I would investigate the shop helper route first because Kai wants technical learning and a predictable short shift.
