p  :: Learn the basics

h3 ce1-05-foundations-title :: How courses, prerequisites and deadlines form a route

p ce1-05-foundations-purpose :: A high-school route connects what a learner has completed with what a future program requires. A useful plan shows the order of preparation, the dates it must fit and the time and money available.

h3 ce1-05-foundation-heading-1 :: Read the condition before scheduling the course

p ce1-05-foundation-copy-2 :: Required means a stated condition must be met. Recommended means it may help but is not stated as compulsory. A prerequisite chain is an ordered sequence: if Course A is needed for Course B, and B for C, the plan must put A before B before C.

p ce1-05-foundation-copy-3 :: A portfolio is a selected collection of work showing what someone can do. A provider may require portfolio pieces without requiring one particular school course to produce them. Keep the provider’s requirement separate from the school’s available preparation route.

h3 ce1-05-foundation-heading-4 :: Check backwards and forwards

p ce1-05-foundation-copy-5 :: Work backwards from the deadline to identify what must be ready. Work forwards from completed preparation to find which next steps are possible. Then check when the school offers them. In a simple example, a sample due in May cannot depend on a course that finishes in June.

p ce1-05-foundation-copy-6 :: Capacity is the available time, course space or other resource for the plan. Count travel, additional work and materials when supplied. Shared preparation can support two directions, but one slot cannot hold two courses at once.

p ce1-05-foundation-copy-7 :: A checkpoint is a planned review moment; a trigger is a change that requires another review. Build the plan as destination → requirements → prerequisite order → dates and resource limits → checkpoints.

p ce1-05-foundations-reading :: When you read the case: When you read the program notices, separate required items from recommendations. Use the school timetable to decide when the preparation is actually possible. The first reading check concerns that distinction, before you build the full plan.

p ce1-05-foundations-next :: Next: Meet the first situation. The explanation above is for learning; your first answer comes after you have read the supplied records.

h3  :: Work backwards, then check forwards

p  :: Working backwards starts with the destination. Program A needs portfolio pieces by April 15, so Tariq must have time to make them before then. Working forwards starts with what is already completed. Digital Media 1 allows Digital Media 2, which allows Portfolio Lab. A workable plan makes the backward deadline and forward prerequisite chain meet.

p  :: Draw the chain before assigning dates:

p  :: Digital Media 1 → Digital Media 2 → Portfolio Lab → two pieces before April 15.

p  :: The communication chain is separate: Foundations → Applications → Capstone. A course can support both routes. Recognizing shared preparation avoids treating the two directions as entirely separate sets of work.

h3  :: Separate a requirement from a useful way to prepare

p  :: Required means the provider's record makes it a condition. Recommended means it may help but is not stated as compulsory. Tariq could theoretically prepare two portfolio pieces another way, but this case supplies a complete school route with known time and materials. Using that route is sensible without rewriting it as a universal requirement.

p  :: A course appearing on a program page does not prove the school offers it in the needed term. The provider controls its entry conditions; the school record controls the local sequence in this exercise. In a real plan, both sets of information would need current confirmation. Here their fictional dates and offerings are fully supplied.

h3  :: Fit the route into a life, not just a table

p  :: The plan must respect Tariq's one-elective slot and three-hour weekly limit. Putting Digital Records and Portfolio Lab together would use two slots and five additional hours. The courses could be valuable but the arrangement would contradict the supplied capacity.

p  :: Financial and career goals also connect. The three elective material charges total $95. That fits the $100 limit for those categories, but it says nothing about future tuition. Record what a calculation covers. A plan that assumes unnamed funding or unlimited study time is not made workable by choosing an attractive program.

h3  :: Use checkpoints and supplied feedback

p  :: A checkpoint is a planned moment to review evidence and make a decision. A trigger is something that makes a revision necessary, such as a cancelled course or changed application requirement. Record the old assumption, the new fact and what will change. Revision is how a plan stays useful.

p  :: Support can take the form of clear information, a different course format, an accessible planning tool or a knowledgeable service. For this lesson the support is in the school/provider notices and the model feedback. You can practise drafting a precise question without sending it. “Will Portfolio Lab finish before April 15?” is more answerable than “What should I do with my life?”
