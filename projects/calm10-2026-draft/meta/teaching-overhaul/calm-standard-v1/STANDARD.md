# CALM lesson teaching and review standard

The accepted CE1-03 Version B is the exemplar for CALM’s connected, independent-online teaching. Dean adopted this authoring and review contract on October 5, 2026. Each subsequent lesson must be written and read as a complete journey. Existing activities, media and learner-state contracts remain protected. Adoption of the standard does not establish acceptance of an unwritten or unreviewed lesson.

## Authoring rules

1. Begin visible teaching with the lesson purpose and why it matters, the first person and decision, the teaching-to-practice sequence and the exact final product. Establish this orientation before a numerical hook, technical exposition or dense case record; a collapsed roadmap does not substitute for it.

2. Introduce the fictional person, goal and constraints before referring to their evidence. Define document types, technical terms and shorthand when first needed.

3. Explain a useful basic idea before presenting dense case records or asking a decision. Integrate case-specific explanation beside the lines it interprets.

4. Write one connected manuscript before implementation. Replace repetitive or superseded passages; connect each section to the unresolved question from the previous one.

5. Model the action and why it matters: locate relevant evidence, interpret it, calculate when appropriate, state what follows and what remains uncertain.

6. Keep calculation units, repeated and once-only amounts, exclusions, rounding conventions and dates visible. Explain the meaning of the result for this person.

7. Introduce supported practice as a controlled change: identify what stays the same, what changes, the fields to use and how feedback helps revision. Do not give the selection answer in the task lead-in.

8. Introduce independent work as a new application: name the new person and records, identify which method transfers, and align prompt, labels, model and signoffs.

9. Give existing videos, transcripts, handouts and tools a specific viewing/reading/experiment purpose. Connect their point to the next learner action; retain professional sources.

10. Keep essential teaching and task requirements visible. Retain approved numbered instructions plus initially collapsed additional guidance, hints and deliberate model reveals.

11. Use CALM native typography, colours, sidebar, document presentation and stacked core-vocabulary disclosure. Dotted-underlined words use the existing keyboard-accessible popup.

12. Close by answering the opening question and explaining the next lesson connection. Respect alternative defensible responses while explaining the evidence limits.

13. Preserve outcomes, original task facts, controls, IDs, answer keys, task versions, media, history, completion and namespaces. Flag task changes separately before any migration.

14. Every required course activity remains possible with supplied information. Do not add teacher contact, outside account, personal disclosure, real transaction or supervised experience dependencies.

15. For Alberta course planning, distinguish diploma requirements from program entry preparation. Use verified real course names/prerequisites and actual credit values; label invented people, offerings, fees and program conditions. A partial pathway exercise must not claim to be a complete graduation audit.

## Review rules

1. Read the complete lesson in order without assuming earlier knowledge beyond what it explicitly recalls. Record concrete gaps with the passage and repair.

2. Before any example amount or technical explanation, can a first-time student explain what this lesson teaches, why it matters, who the first case concerns, how learning and practice unfold, and what they will produce? Read with the roadmap closed. A hook alone does not meet this requirement.

3. Does each person, document, label and term have a meaning before the first instruction that uses it?

4. Does the worked example model decisions and why, instead of presenting only a polished answer or a list of amounts?

5. Do calculations match the unchanged records and explain units, duration, exclusions and the decision they inform?

6. Are section transitions purposeful, with no duplicate instructions, unexplained reset or hidden essential teaching?

7. Does supported practice invite an attempt before intentional feedback/model support? Does a new person get their own facts and conditions?

8. Do independent prompts, field labels, model content and signoff requirements ask for the same product? Are multiple evidence-supported answers handled fairly?

9. Do retained media and sources have an explicit purpose and usable text equivalents, with their original limits preserved?

10. Does the rendered lesson exactly match the manuscript, retain the approved course style and support native vocabulary keyboard use?

11. Compare all task controls, IDs, versions, media, history and completion requirements to the frozen original. Structural counts are compatibility evidence, not proof of teaching quality.

12. Keep teacher acceptance, source review, local technical checks, canonical integration and release proof as separate statuses.

## Scope and enforcement

The project-local owner is meta/calm-teaching-standard.json, linked as required reading in prompt-pack.md. authoring-rules.json and review-rules.json are paired, hash-bound rules using the same standard identity. The existing generic standards renderer requires a different one-lesson scaffold; it cannot prove CALM’s native 40-lesson teaching quality. No shared renderer, global default, promotion queue or other course changes are included. The source preservation checker protects mechanical contracts; a full reading and teacher review govern instructional acceptance.

## First batch

Prepare CE1-01, CE1-02, CE1-04, CE1-05, CE1-06 and CE1-07 as complete manuscripts and native-shell review copies. CE1-03 remains the accepted exemplar. Keep current canonical content and all Before snapshots intact until review of the new batch. Subsequent batches cover workplace responsibilities and experience, then financial decisions, borrowing, saving and personal information. The 39-lesson audit determines each lesson’s particular rewrite needs.
