Career websites contain several kinds of information. Before reading the profile and employer notice, learn which question each kind can answer. This will help you compare work with a person’s interests and preferred conditions.

B

In this lesson, you will learn to read an occupation profile and use its information to make a careful next-step recommendation.

A

When you explore a career, you may read that an occupation is growing, pays well or has few openings nearby. Each statement is a labour-market claim: something a person or publication says about work and workers. You need to understand what the statement describes before using it to make a plan.

B

“There are lots of jobs in that field” can sound like a good reason to choose training. Before using that advice, we need to know what the speaker counted, where the information applies and how old it is. In this lesson, you will learn to examine a labour-market claim and explain what its evidence actually supports.

A

A high-school route connects what a learner has completed with what a future program requires. A useful plan shows the order of preparation, the dates it must fit and the time and money available.

B

Course planning connects what you do in high school with choices you may want after graduation. A plan needs to do two jobs: meet Alberta’s graduation requirements and prepare you for the particular next step you are considering. In this lesson, you will learn how to check those jobs separately and arrange preparation in a realistic order.

A

An interest can lead toward employment or a small venture. Before comparing the offers, learn who arranges the work, which costs each person carries and what the stated money amounts represent.

B

Enjoying a practical activity can lead to different ways of earning money. You might do it for an employer, offer a service to clients or investigate a small venture. In this lesson, you will learn how to compare those arrangements by looking at responsibilities, costs and evidence of demand, as well as the money coming in.

A

A change in time or responsibilities can make an old plan stop fitting. Revising means checking what changed and finding a workable next step toward the goal.

B

A plan for learning or work has to fit alongside the rest of life. When transport, family commitments or available time changes, the original route may stop working. In this lesson, you will learn to revise a plan while checking whether its underlying goal can still be kept.
