**Disclosure: Core vocabulary**

Select a dotted-underlined word in this lesson to see its meaning and an example. You can also select any of the four words below.

**Promotional price**

A temporary price that applies only for its stated period and conditions.

**Fixed term**

An agreement lasting for a specified period under its terms.

**Month-to-month**

An agreement continuing by monthly periods under its stated cancellation terms.

**Cancellation charge**

An amount that the agreement says may be payable when ending it under specified conditions.

Learn the basics

### Learning to read a contract across time, not just its first bill

An introductory price can be accurate and still hide what later months require. In this lesson, you will learn to trace changing charges, device balances and cancellation terms. The aim is to compare the service needed with the commitment being accepted.

Marin’s current phone works and both plans meet the stated data and coverage needs. One offer adds a device payment and a short promotion; the other supplies service without that device obligation. Marin has $45 each month.

First, learn to separate service charges from the device balance. Read the written terms and follow twelve months of costs. Practice asks what remains payable if Marin leaves after nine months, so cancellation is examined rather than assumed free.

The final task introduces Sacha’s new case. Calculate each plan over twelve and six months, then make a recommendation using the signup and later-month limits and possible move. Explain what still needs checking about future coverage.

### Distinguish service, device and commitment

A subscription is a recurring arrangement for access to a service. A fixed term is a specified commitment period; month-to-month describes a shorter repeating period. Neither label supplies every cancellation condition. Read what ends, what continues and what remains payable.

A promotional price lasts for the period stated. A separate device payment can be due at the same time. For a simple example, $10 service plus a $5 device payment makes a $15 bill. If service later becomes $20 while the device payment continues, the bill becomes $25.

### Check the whole period and the hardest month

Draw a timeline for the promotional months, later months and device-payment ending date. Multiply each combined monthly bill by the number of months it applies, then add separately due charges once.

A year’s total and an affordable first bill do not establish that later bills fit. Compare the largest required bill with the money available in that month. When examining an early exit, separate amounts already paid from the unpaid balance; ending service does not automatically erase a device obligation.

Use service price → device price → combined bill by period → total → later affordability → exit conditions. The course supplies the written terms for every required calculation.

Use the method with the case

### Read the service, device and exit terms

Break a changing price into its periods: three promotional months and nine later months. Count the separate device charge for every contracted month. An average can describe total cost but cannot tell Marin whether month four fits the budget.

Cancellation is another calculation. Payments already made and unpaid device principal are different amounts. Read what becomes due on exit; do not assume stopping service erases a purchase balance or produces a refund.

Payment timeline: A months 1–3 = service 20 + device 15; months 4–12 = service 38 + device 15; after month 12 = stated ongoing service, device paid off. B has one service payment and no new device in this case. This is a comparison of ways to meet a need, not a claim that buying a new phone and keeping an old one provide identical ownership benefits.

### Count the whole period, then test the hardest month

A twelve-month total answers “What will the supplied plan cost over a year?” It does not answer “Can I meet every bill?” An average can hide higher later months. Check the largest required bill and the amount available then. Paying a low first bill is not evidence that the later schedule is affordable.

An exit cost is money due when the agreement ends. Some balances remain payable even when service stops. Here, ending A does not erase the unpaid device amount. Separate payments already made from the remaining balance; do not count the same device payment twice.

### Use the right consumer reference

CRTC reference record — read September 28, 2026: the Wireless Code requires clear wireless contract information, including prices, service limits, commitment period, relevant cancellation terms and one-time costs. It applies to wireless service; it is not a rulebook for every streaming or gym subscription. [Optional official page: sections A and B](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/phone/mobile/codesimpl.htm).

For an unfamiliar real agreement, use its permanent written copy to verify these terms. In this lesson all needed terms are supplied. No external page can fill a deliberately missing fictional price; flag the gap instead.
