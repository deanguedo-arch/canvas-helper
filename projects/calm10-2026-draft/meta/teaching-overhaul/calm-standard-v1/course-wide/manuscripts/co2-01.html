Learn the basics

Learning to check a proposed shift against the applicable rules

A shift offer is a proposal you can check before accepting it. In this lesson, you will learn to select the rule group that fits a worker’s age, job and arrangement, then compare the offered hours and with that record. One condition being satisfied does not settle every other condition.

Know what the documents establish

An employee works for an employer. An is a minimum employment rule in the applicable system. identifies which rules or authority apply. For these cases, read the supplied Alberta youth-employment record rather than applying a general idea about all teen work.

A permit is official permission for specified employment. Parent or guardian is a different condition. Adult means an adult supervises the work as described in the record; another worker’s experience or an adult’s reachable telephone number does not establish that condition.

Match the facts before deciding

Use age → employment arrangement → workplace and task → day and time to locate the relevant rule. Separate a shift where supervision or the calendar day changes. One condition being met does not cancel another condition: for example, permission cannot be assumed to remove a stated hours restriction.

A mismatch is a supplied fact conflicting with a rule. An unknown is a relevant fact not supplied. State a mismatch with the matching rule; ask a specific question for an unknown. Keep personal constraints, such as transport, distinct from rules applying to a group of workers.

For a simple reading example, a rule requiring adult coverage until closing needs evidence about who covers the final portion of the shift. “An adult supervises the opening” leaves a different question unanswered.

Use the method with the case

Identify the rule before judging the offer

Select the worker’s age group and job category before applying a number. A duration limit from the younger group cannot be transferred to Ellis just because it is familiar. Then trace the shift through each period: school hours, coverage, and prohibited time each answer a separate question.

A revised offer must resolve the identified conflicts together. Adult coverage does not override a prohibited overnight interval; a parental pickup does not itself provide on-site . We will use Ellis’s timeline to see how a specific scheduler request can preserve these distinctions.

Start with the details that select a rule: age → employment arrangement → workplace/task → time. “Teen worker” is too broad. Ellis is 15, so the 13–14 school-day two-hour limit is not the rule to apply to Ellis. North Path Books is retail, so the retail evening rule is relevant. An exception for another kind of workplace cannot simply be moved to this one.

An official page is evidence about the rules; a shift offer is evidence about the proposed facts. The task is to connect them. Write “Ellis is scheduled after midnight in retail, which conflicts with the recorded overnight restriction,” rather than “the website says it is bad.” The first sentence lets another reader check your reasoning.

Divide the shift where a condition changes

Midnight crosses into the next calendar day. Adult coverage can change before the shift ends. A whole-shift label such as “night work” hides these differences. Break the offered shift into pieces:

Portion of Ellis's offer What changes? Question to ask
9:30–10:00 p.m. Thursday Mara is supervising Are the evening consent and supervision conditions supplied?
10:00 p.m.–midnight Mara leaves; Jordan is 17 Is an adult supervising? Does parental consent cover the planned finish?
12:01–12:30 a.m. Friday Overnight restricted period Can adult supervision or consent remove this retail prohibition?

The answer to the last question is no under the supplied record. Do not spend time negotiating an adult supervisor for a portion that cannot be scheduled under that rule. Revise the hours themselves.

Separate a mismatch from an unanswered question

A mismatch exists when supplied facts conflict with the selected rule: a retail shift continuing to 12:30 a.m. is one here. An unknown exists when the documents do not tell you a relevant fact. Write a question for an unknown instead of assuming its answer. In Ellis's documents, the ages and coverage are supplied, so “maybe Jordan is an adult” is not an unknown.

A check can have several results. The shift is outside the recorded school hours, but that does not cancel the supervision and overnight problems. Conversely, finding one problem does not mean every detail is wrong. State which conditions your evidence answers.

Ask for a specific correction

A useful review ends with an action the recipient can understand: a revised start and finish, named adult coverage and confirmation that permission covers those hours. “Fix it” leaves the next decision unclear. You can be direct without accusing someone of deliberate wrongdoing: this is a draft offer and its reason is unknown.

A personal arrangement, such as a ride home, also affects whether the worker can accept. Label it accurately. The parent's 11:00 p.m. pickup is a case constraint, not a province-wide closing rule.

I check Ellis’s age and retail duties first. The offer is outside school hours, but Mara leaves at 10:00 p.m. and the seventeen-year-old replacement does not supply adult coverage. The 12:30 a.m. finish also crosses the record’s prohibited period and exceeds the supplied.

I first select the age 15–17 retail rules. Ellis is 15 and the arrangement is ordinary employment. The shift begins after Thursday's school hours. That answers the school-hours question; it does not approve the rest of the shift.

Next I follow the time line. Mara supplies adult supervision only until 10:00 p.m. Jordan is 17, so the offer does not supply the required adult coverage from 10:00 p.m. to midnight. The parent has only consented to an 11:00 p.m. finish, so the offer also exceeds that permission. Finally, the offered 12:30 a.m. finish includes the prohibited 12:01–6:00 a.m. retail period. Neither permission nor adult coverage can make that portion fit the record.

Here is a complete draft review:

Ellis is a 15-year-old employee in an Alberta retail bookstore. The proposed October 8 shift is outside the supplied school hours, but it needs revision. Alberta's youth employment record, checked September 28, requires adult supervision for retail work from 9:00 p.m. to midnight. Mara leaves at 10:00 p.m., and Jordan is 17. The 12:30 a.m. finish also includes a prohibited overnight period. Parental consent currently ends at 11:00 p.m. Please offer a shift ending by 11:00 p.m. with an adult supervising the entire evening portion and confirm the corrected schedule before Ellis accepts. The parent's pickup is available at 11:00 p.m. This checks the listed youth conditions; it is not a complete workplace safety review.

“Ellis can work until 12:30 because the parent could give permission” is an unsupported alternative. It treats consent as an override of a prohibition. “Ellis cannot work more than two hours because school is tomorrow” selects the wrong age group's rule and does not use Ellis's actual school-day facts.
