Learn the basics

Read a contract as a payment timeline

A phone or subscription offer may combine service, a device and a temporary . Learn to separate those parts before reading the offers.

Distinguish service, device and commitment

A subscription is a recurring arrangement for access to a service. A is a specified commitment period; describes a shorter repeating period. Neither label supplies every cancellation condition. Read what ends, what continues and what remains payable.

A promotional price lasts for the period stated. A separate device payment can be due at the same time. For a simple example, $10 service plus a $5 device payment makes a $15 bill. If service later becomes $20 while the device payment continues, the bill becomes $25.

Check the whole period and the hardest month

Draw a timeline for the promotional months, later months and device-payment ending date. Multiply each combined monthly bill by the number of months it applies, then add separately due charges once.

A year’s total and an affordable first bill do not establish that later bills fit. Compare the largest required bill with the money available in that month. When examining an early exit, separate amounts already paid from the unpaid balance; ending service does not automatically erase a device obligation.

Use service price → device price → combined bill by period → total → later affordability → exit conditions. The course supplies the written terms for every required calculation.

When you read the case: Read the price periods and device lines on both offers before the first check. That check asks for a combined bill, not the advertised service price alone. Later you will compare the full timeline and stated exit balance.

Offer A — new phone and service, twelve-month written agreement

Service is $43 monthly; no device payment or setup fee. All stated prices include taxes. Use the method with the case

Read the service, device and exit terms

A subscription is a recurring arrangement for access to a service. Its payment repeats until the agreement ends or changes. A fixed-term agreement has a specified commitment period; month-to-month describes a shorter recurring period. Neither phrase tells you every cancellation rule. Read the actual notice, payment dates and any separate device obligation.

A is a temporary price. Count the months it applies, then count the remaining months separately. Adding a device payment can change the bill even while the service price stays the same. Three labels are therefore useful: service, device and combined bill. The combined bill is the one that must fit the monthly allowance.

Payment timeline: A months 1–3 = service 20 + device 15; months 4–12 = service 38 + device 15; after month 12 = stated ongoing service, device paid off. B has one service payment and no new device in this case. This is a comparison of ways to meet a need, not a claim that buying a new phone and keeping an old one provide identical ownership benefits.

Count the whole period, then test the hardest month

A twelve-month total answers “What will the supplied plan cost over a year?” It does not answer “Can I meet every bill?” An average can hide higher later months. Check the largest required bill and the amount available then. Paying a low first bill is not evidence that the later schedule is affordable.

An exit cost is money due when the agreement ends. Some balances remain payable even when service stops. Here, ending A does not erase the unpaid device amount. Separate payments already made from the remaining balance; do not count the same device payment twice.

Use the right consumer reference

CRTC reference record — read September 28, 2026: the Wireless Code requires clear wireless contract information, including prices, service limits, commitment period, relevant cancellation terms and one-time costs. It applies to wireless service; it is not a rulebook for every streaming or gym subscription. Optional official page: sections A and B.

For an unfamiliar real agreement, use its permanent written copy to verify these terms. In this lesson all needed terms are supplied. No external page can fill a deliberately missing fictional price; flag the gap instead.
