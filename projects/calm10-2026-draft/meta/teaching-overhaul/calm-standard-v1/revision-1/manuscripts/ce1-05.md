**Prerequisite chain** — An ordered sequence in which each required earlier course permits the next step.

**Required** — Stated as a condition that must be met for the particular route.

**Recommended** — Suggested as helpful, without being stated as compulsory.

**Checkpoint** — A planned time to review evidence and decide whether a plan needs changing.

Learn the basics

### The courses look right. Will the plan actually work?

A student can list all the courses that seem useful for a career and still have a plan that cannot be followed. A later course may require an earlier one, a school may offer it only in a particular term, or a portfolio may be due before the course that helps produce it finishes. How do you turn a list of useful preparation into a route that fits the actual time available?

### Start with the conditions at the destination

A prerequisite is a step that must be completed before another course or entry point. Required preparation is a stated condition; recommended preparation may help without being compulsory. If a program requires two pieces of work, a particular school course may be one way to create them. We need to distinguish the evidence the program asks for from the route used to produce it.

A portfolio is a selected collection of work with explanations of what it demonstrates. Some programs set a submission deadline for that evidence as well as a later date for completing a school course. Those are different points on the timeline. Applying before final school results are available can fit the supplied conditions if the record explicitly allows completion later. It still does not guarantee admission.

### Join the deadline to the starting point

Working backwards identifies what must be ready by the deadline and which earlier steps it needs. Working forwards checks what the learner has completed and when the next course is actually offered. Imagine a course requires an introductory course first: putting both names in the same term would not make the prerequisite complete. The two directions of reading need to meet in one workable sequence.

Capacity means the space and resources available for the plan. A course can be offered and useful but still conflict with another elective or exceed the additional study time available. Money matters as well. We will count the listed material charges and state what they cover, rather than assume a school exercise supplies every future tuition or living cost.

A checkpoint is a planned moment to review progress. A trigger is a change that makes revision necessary, such as a cancelled course. Leaving some room in the plan can make a later revision easier. A plan is therefore more than a timetable: it explains why the steps fit and when we will check whether those assumptions still hold.

We will use Tariq’s school and provider records to build a design route while keeping information services possible. I will explain the first sequence. You will revise it after a course cancellation using the same facts and support. The final task introduces Nessa, whose records contain a different elective conflict. Your response will show the route, explain its limits and include checkpoints and questions.

Use the method with the case

### Find the latest point at which evidence must be ready

Program A’s portfolio is due April 15 of Grade 12. That means two original pieces need to exist by then. Portfolio Lab is a supplied way to develop them, finishing by March 30 of its year. I compare those dates first because a course finishing after the submission date would not support this route, however relevant its content might be.

Now follow Portfolio Lab’s prerequisite backwards. It needs Digital Media 2, which needs the Digital Media 1 already completed. The school offers Media 2 in fall and the Lab in winter, so the sequence can move forward from Tariq’s existing preparation. We have connected a destination deadline to an available chain rather than simply selecting appealing course names.

### Keep the shared preparation visible

The communication chain runs from the completed Foundations to Applications in Grade 11 core and Capstone in Grade 12 core. These are already reserved if the prerequisites are completed. They do not compete for the elective slot. Applications contributes to Program B’s preparation, while Capstone meets the later condition named by Program A.

Program B also requires Digital Records, which has an available place in the school table. It is a real condition within this fictional case, not a vague backup label. To keep B possible, the plan needs to show where that course will be completed and retain the May 1 application deadline.

### Check whether each term has enough capacity

Every additional elective needs its own available slot. Portfolio Lab takes three extra weekly hours; Digital Records takes two. Putting them together would use two slots and five hours, beyond both limits. A workable sequence separates those demands even when both courses are useful.

The listed materials are $40 for Media 2, $35 for the Lab and $20 for Records. Adding them once each gives $95. Tariq has a $100 material limit, leaving $5 in that category. This is an elective-material calculation. It does not establish the cost of post-secondary study or provide money for an expense omitted from the record.

### Decide what a checkpoint should check

A good checkpoint has an object: the completed pieces, a prerequisite result, continued interest or a deadline. “Review at the end of Grade 11” becomes useful when the plan says what will be reviewed then and why the result could change the next step. A trigger, such as cancellation, may require a review before the scheduled date.

Support questions should identify an uncertainty that matters. Asking which formats are accepted for the two portfolio pieces is more useful than asking whether everything is fine.
