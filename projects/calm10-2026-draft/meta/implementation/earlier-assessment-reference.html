Applied-task briefs and success criteria

Open the task when its lessons are complete. Drafts saved in lessons are preparation; use the submission route supplied by your course. Use fictional information unless a task asks for a dated public source.

A1 · Career and high-school plan

Supplied case

Jordan enjoys visual problem solving, has coordinated a school event, and values predictable hours. Compare two related Alberta occupations using current occupation profiles and current training or program pages.

What to submit

Submit a two-route comparison covering duties, working conditions, entry routes, training costs or unknowns, dated Alberta labour-market evidence, and a Grade 10–12 sequence with prerequisites to verify. Revise the route after Jordan loses Tuesday transport.

Success criteria

Occupation evidence; source and date accuracy; prerequisite logic; realistic trade-offs; justified revision.

A2 · Workplace rights and safety case

Supplied case

A fictional 15-year-old retail worker receives a late schedule, finds a 30-minute difference between posted and entered time, and is asked to use an unlabelled cleaner without task-specific training.

What to submit

Submit a case file separating known and missing facts, current Alberta employment and safety sources, an immediate safe response, a factual time-record question, and an appropriate help route.

Success criteria

Correct source scope; hazard recognition; safe action; factual records; careful reasoning without declaring a violation before the required facts are known.

A3 · Online workday simulation

Supplied case

Use the Prairie Library Hub posting, first-shift note, timed shift board, disagreement record, and fictional lead feedback in CO1-01 through CO1-05.

What to submit

Submit two truthful skill statements, one record-access question, the timed priority sequence, a two-sentence lead update, a feedback reflection, and a revised career hypothesis with a next test.

Success criteria

Application evidence; privacy and safety judgement; prioritization; communication; accurate use of feedback; justified revision. This online simulation does not verify supervised participation under CO1-SP04.

A4 · Paycheque, budget and purchase

Supplied case

Mika’s fictional statement lists 12.5 hours at $18, gross pay of $225, and deductions of $16, $10.80, and $3.60. A fictional monthly plan has $1,200 income, $630 essentials, $180 flexible spending, $120 saving, and $90 transport; transport then rises by $75. Phone A costs $35 for six months, then $55 for six months, plus $40 setup. Phone B costs $48 monthly with no setup. Ignore tax and state that assumption.

What to submit

Submit an annotated pay statement, revised balanced budget, both 12-month phone totals, and a choice that uses at least one verified feature besides price.

Success criteria

Gross/net reading; arithmetic and units; balanced revision; complete total-cost comparison; justified trade-off.

A5 · Borrowing comparison

Supplied case

Two fictional loans each advance $2,400. Offer A: 12% nominal annual rate divided monthly, 24 payments, no fee. Offer B: 8% nominal annual rate divided monthly, 36 payments, plus a $90 fee paid up front. No missed or extra payments.

What to submit

Submit transparent calculator inputs or formula, monthly payment, total payments and fee, cost above principal, a recommendation for a fictional $90 monthly limit, and one sensitivity test.

Success criteria

Comparable assumptions; correct calculation; affordability versus total cost; treatment of fee and uncertainty; justified recommendation.

A6 · Financial plan and revision

Supplied case

A fictional learner wants a $1,200 emergency buffer. They can transfer $60 in ordinary months but only $25 in a low-income month. Compare accessible savings with a market investment for money that may be needed within one year.

What to submit

Submit a dated contribution schedule, explanation of access needs, account-versus-product comparison, transparent scenario assumptions, and a revised plan after two low-income months.

Success criteria

Cash-flow feasibility; liquidity and product distinction; transparent assumptions; risk, fee and inflation interpretation; reasoned revision without assuming a higher return.

A7 · Fraud and information protection

Supplied case

A bank message threatens an account lock in 20 minutes and gives a link. A school program directs the learner to its known portal without a link. A marketplace seller requests identity before discussing a purchase. Then the fictional learner reports entering a reused password.

What to submit

Classify each contact using quoted evidence, verify independently without a message link, choose a safe response, and build a recovery plan using the relevant institution or current official reporting source.

Success criteria

Indicator analysis; uncertainty; independent verification; safe response; recovery order; correct source and reporting route.
