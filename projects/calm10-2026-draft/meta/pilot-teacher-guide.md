# Three CALM 10 benchmark lessons · teacher review guide

These are the depth and presentation samples for the course rebuild. Review the learner pages in the local preview at `#ce1-03`, `#co2-02`, and `#fl2-03`. Each has an opening case, goal and success criteria, explicit explanation, worked and contrasting examples, a decision model, guided correction, an interactive task, independent portfolio application and a source link. The career pilot also has an original narrated sample with editable written transcript, captions and a low-bandwidth written route. The two other pilots use accessible HTML decision steps and tables as their visual models. No pilot has a Brightspace grade or submission connection yet.

## CE1-03 · Compare career pathways · about 95 minutes

**Teach:** A career title is a destination; a route is a sequence of verified entry steps. Use the fictional Riley comparison to distinguish admission facts from assumptions. The example costs are invented for reasoning practice, not current program prices.

**Pause prompts:** At the route table, ask which condition must be checked with a school and which with an employer. At the video, pause before the fourth slide and ask whether either route is guaranteed. The correct response is no: admission, sponsorship, recognition and employment remain uncertain.

**Practice key:** A comparison holds the destination constant and names each route's entry step, credential, time/cost/access constraint and source date. An undated blog's employment guarantee should be checked against a current program source and separate labour-market or employer evidence. An honest unresolved question is stronger than an invented certainty.

**Independent evidence:** Two sourced routes for one occupation, a high-school preparation plan and a revision trigger. Give feedback on source/date quality and feasibility before asking for a preferred route.

**Access:** Video is optional; the transcript and HTML tables carry the essential teaching. Offer a text pathway map for students unable to use the interactive comparison.

**Teacher check:** Verify the Alberta occupation/program links and any actual admission rules used by students before marking them as current. Assess the reasoning, not the prestige or ambition of the chosen career.

## CO2-02 · Respond to a hazard · about 80 minutes

**Teach:** Separate hazard recognition, immediate safe action, reporting, and the current Alberta process. The unlabelled bottle plus exposed wire changes the risk. The formal refusal threshold depends on the facts; the student should not guess a legal conclusion from a thin scenario.

**Practice key:** Do not touch the wire or use the unknown cleaner. Warn others about the immediate area without improvising a repair. Tell the supervisor what and where the hazard is, what task is being paused, and why. Ask for training and controls. If the condition persists, check current Alberta OHS refusal guidance and a trusted help route. “Just be careful” is not a control for an exposed wire.

**Independent evidence:** A case file naming hazard, affected people, first safe action, relevant worker right, employer/worker responsibilities, current source and escalation. Accept a written or teacher-conference explanation if the same reasoning can be assessed.

**Teacher check:** Verify the current Alberta legal wording and school placement policy before release. Do not treat this instructional case as legal advice or a substitute for site-specific training.

## FL2-03 · Compare repayment periods · about 90 minutes

**Teach:** With fictional $2,400 principal, nominal annual 12% compounded monthly and equal monthly payments, 24 months yields about $112.98 monthly and $311.43 total interest; 36 months yields about $79.71 and $469.72. The 36-month choice costs about $158.29 more in the model, while its payment fits the fictional $85 monthly limit. Discuss waiting, fees and changing circumstances.

**Practice key:** For the 24-month choice, first-month interest is $24.00; about $88.98 of the payment reduces principal; the next opening balance is about $2,311.02. A $75 fee paid upfront changes total cost but not the modeled monthly payment. If financed into principal, both payment and interest may change. To isolate repayment period, hold principal, rate, fee treatment and payment timing constant.

**Calculator:** Ordinary-loan mode uses nominal annual rate ÷ 12. Canadian mortgage mode converts a nominal annual rate compounded semi-annually to a monthly effective rate with `(1 + j/2)^(1/6) - 1`. Display inputs, repayment total, fees and cost above principal. These illustrations do not model rate changes, prepayment penalties, renewals or insurance.

**Independent evidence:** A record of two fictional offers with assumptions, a transparent calculation, a budget check, and a reasoned recommendation that identifies one condition that would change it.

**Access:** The HTML first-payment table and worked numbers provide a non-interactive route. Provide a teacher-marked calculation sheet if a student's device cannot run the calculator.

## Review decision

Mark each pilot **ready to scale / revise / stop** on (1) clarity for independent learners, (2) quality of worked reasoning, (3) Alberta accuracy and currency, (4) interaction and feedback, (5) phone/keyboard/reading access, (6) assessment workload. Review the narration voice and caption accuracy separately. Record a concrete change for every “revise.” Full-course production should use the accepted sample as the standard.
