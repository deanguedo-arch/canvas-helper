Decision Making Patterns

There are many ways of making a decision. We all use some of them. Have you seen any of these patterns in the past? Have they worked for you? It is important to be aware of your patterns and, if necessary, take steps to change them.

    1. Wish Pattern - You make a choice hoping it will get you what you want. But, as with all wishes, these may not come true.

    1. Fatalistic Pattern - You let the environment decide, leaving it up to fate.

    1. Submissive Pattern - You let someone else decide or give in to group pressure.

    1. Delaying Pattern - You put off making a decision. You procrastinate.

    1. Agonizing Pattern - You get so overwhelmed by possible choices that you donâ€™t know what to do.

The Five-Step Decision-Making Model

Many of us fall into faulty decision-making patterns. Examine the following flowchart to discover one of the most effective decision-making models - The 5 Step Decision Making Model.

Step 1: Determine the problem and assess the situation.

When something happens to change your life, stop, step back a bit, and look at your situation.

Step 2: Consider the options available and list possible solutions.

Find out about options open to you. Think about your needs, wants, and abilities.

Step 3: Decide on the best solution.

Go over each possibility and think about it. Then, choose the best one for you.

Step 4: Plan a course of action and implement the solution.

Make a plan to carry out your decision. Follow through on it and make it happen.

Step 5: Reassess

After a few days or weeks, look at the decision again and make adjustments if needed.

The Pomodoro Technique

If you find yourself having difficulty focusing or concentrating, a powerful productivity technique to use is called the Pomodoro Technique. The Pomodoro Technique is very simple:

  1. Select the task that you want to focus on. Remove all distractions.
  2. Set a timer for 25 minutes.
  3. Work on the task until the timer goes off, then put a checkmark on a sheet of paper.
  4. Take a break for 5 minutes.
  5. Every 4 checkmarks, take a longer break (20-30 minutes).

Why is this method effective? Firstly, by working for 25 minutes without interruptions, you are able to focus your energy, while knowing that a break is coming soon. Secondly, by building in breaks, you are allowing time for your mind to relax and also to reward yourself for focusing. Lastly, it forces you to get started, which is usually the hardest part of a big project. Once you get started, you usually find that the task you are trying to do isnâ€™t so bad after all.
