How This Course Works

A repeatable learning process

  1. Read the brief and vocabulary. Identify the result you are working toward.
  2. Study the lessons and worked example. Revisit them when a decision becomes difficult.
  3. Try the practice decisions. Feedback explains the reasoning and you can retry.
  4. Create the required product in a document, presentation, drawing or recording. Use the response record to summarize decisions and identify your files.
  5. Check the rubric, revise, then choose Save to Portfolio. Print or download a backup when needed.

Plan your time

Each CTS code represents approximately 25 hours of access to instruction and practical learning. A suggested pattern is 6 hours of lessons and source investigation, 4 hours of examples and practice, 9 hours developing evidence, 4 hours testing and revising, and 2 hours evaluating and preparing submission. Adapt the pace with your teacher; there is no timer or automatic credit for hours.

Independent work and assessment

Use fictional customers and supplied data. You do not need to buy anything, travel, contact a business, recruit a partner or attend a live session. Written role responses and solo recordings let you practise applied skills. Some official outcomes require participation or performance evidence that a simulation alone may not demonstrate. Your teacher must validate those requirements before the affected module is released or awarded.

Project connections must refer to successfully completed eligible CTS courses, including one at the project’s level and in the same occupational area. Advanced projects connect to another intermediate or advanced course. Project and practicum codes cannot supply the connections. Teacher confirmation is separate from this page’s progress indicators.

Keep your work

Draft records save in this browser on this device. Different browsers and devices have separate storage. Download a JSON backup before moving devices or clearing browser data. A valid restore replaces the current course record; it does not merge drafts. Keep your original external files separately.

TOU1010 · The Tourism Sector
TOU1010 · Learning module · Introductory


Create a visitor-experience map that shows how tourism services work together and how visitor spending can affect a host community.
Learning focus: see the whole visitor journey; compare benefits, costs and perspectives; design and research responsibly.


1. See the whole visitor journey2. Compare benefits, costs and perspectives3. Design and research responsiblyPracticeYour work


Words to know
Tourism product
the combined services and experiences offered to visitors.
Inbound tourism
travel into a destination by people who live elsewhere.
Seasonality
variation in demand across the year.
Stakeholder
a person or group affected by tourism activity.
1. See the whole visitor journey
A visitor experiences more than an attraction. Transportation, accommodation, food, information, recreation, events and public services shape the journey from planning to returning home. Classify a service by what it does rather than assuming every tourism business is a hotel. A resident's local leisure activity and a visitor's trip can use the same facilities but have different planning needs.


Map before, during and after the visit. Before arrival, the visitor needs reliable information, availability and an accessible way to book. During the visit, signs, staff, transfers and opening hours must connect. Afterwards, feedback, lost-property support and accurate records matter. One weak connection can undermine an otherwise excellent experience; explain the relationship instead of producing only a list of attractions.
Next lesson
2. Compare benefits, costs and perspectives
Tourism can support employment, business revenue and cultural exchange, but benefits and burdens are not shared equally. Congestion, seasonal work, housing pressures and impacts on natural or cultural places require attention. Separate money received by a business from profit, local wages and broader economic effects. Do not claim that every dollar remains in the community or that a busy destination is automatically successful.


Supplied fictional place: Willow Junction has a small museum, a lakeside trail, two inns and a weekend market. Summer Saturdays bring 300 day visitors; winter Saturdays bring 60. A trail entrance is step-free, but the last 400 metres have loose gravel. The market closes at 15:00. These facts support questions about seasonality, access, staffing and coordination without requiring a real trip or business contact.
Previous lessonNext lesson
3. Design and research responsibly
Create a visitor profile describing purpose, budget, time, access requirements and interests without reducing people to stereotypes. Use the profile to select services and explain trade-offs. A family, solo traveller or older visitor can have many different preferences; ask for relevant requirements rather than inferring them from identity. Do not request medical diagnoses to describe an access need.


Use official destination and operator information for live research, recording the page and observation date. Treat a commercial description as a claim to verify, not an independent review. If a page changes, the Willow Junction dataset remains available for the core task. Research one tourism occupation through ALIS and connect its responsibilities with a stage of your visitor journey.
Previous lessonGo to practice
Worked example
  1. A visitor arriving at 13:30 cannot comfortably fit a two-hour museum visit before the 15:00 market closing. The map places the market first or changes the arrival plan.
  2. Calling the whole trail 'accessible' overstates the supplied evidence. A stronger description states the step-free entrance and loose-gravel section so a visitor can judge suitability.
  3. A winter proposal uses the museum and an indoor activity instead of assuming the summer market operates year-round. The change responds to the dataset, not a generic slogan.

TOU1030 · Quality Guest Service
TOU1030 · Learning module · Introductory


Create a guest-service response toolkit and independently demonstrate how you would handle a booking error, an access question and a service recovery.
Evidence boundary for teacher review
Solo scripted responses do not verify actual interaction or teamwork performance; teacher validation remains required.
Learning focus: translate welcome into observable behaviour; solve the issue within policy and authority; close the loop and evaluate the response.


1. Translate welcome into observable behaviour2. Solve the issue within policy and authority3. Close the loop and evaluate the responsePracticeYour work


Words to know
Service standard
an observable expectation for consistent service.
Service recovery
a response intended to address a service failure.
Escalation
referring an issue to someone with appropriate authority.
Data minimization
collecting only information necessary for the stated purpose.
1. Translate welcome into observable behaviour
Good service is not simply being cheerful. It includes accurate information, attentive listening, respectful language, realistic commitments and follow-through. A service standard should be observable: confirm the booking date and room type, summarize the request, and explain the next step. Avoid promising a result outside your authority or using friendliness to hide an unresolved problem.


Written, telephone and face-to-face communication need different cues. In writing, use a clear subject, short paragraphs and explicit dates. In a spoken response, check understanding and avoid interrupting. An access request should be addressed through practical features and options; a guest should not need to disclose a diagnosis. Independently perform the supplied roles, but label them as a simulation rather than real customer feedback.
Next lesson
2. Solve the issue within policy and authority
Fictional Willow Inn policy: front-desk staff may correct a booking record and offer up to $25 of service credit for a documented inconvenience. Room upgrades and larger compensation require manager approval. Scenario: a guest booked a quiet room for two nights at $120 per night; the record shows one night. The confirmation email supports two. The guest has an early appointment and needs a prompt answer.


A strong response acknowledges the discrepancy, confirms the evidence, checks availability and explains the authorized options. It does not blame the guest or guarantee an unavailable room. If a decision needs escalation, identify who will review it and when the guest can expect an update. Document necessary facts without unnecessary personal details or hostile characterizations.
Previous lessonNext lesson
3. Close the loop and evaluate the response
Service recovery includes confirming the action taken and whether the original need is addressed. A refund or credit is not the only relevant result: accurate information, usable alternatives and respectful treatment also matter. Create a brief handover with the issue, verified facts, action, unresolved item and follow-up owner. Keep fictional identifiers rather than real guest records.


Privacy obligations depend on the organization and jurisdiction. Alberta's former FOIP framework was replaced for public bodies in 2025; private-sector and federal contexts require their own checks. Use current official guidance, not an old blanket statement that one law applies to every hotel. Evaluate your response against accuracy, empathy, authority, access and follow-through, then revise a line that could mislead.
Previous lessonGo to practice
Worked example
  1. 'Your confirmation shows two nights; I will check the second-night availability now' acknowledges evidence without promising an outcome before checking.
  2. If no room is available, the response offers a manager-assisted alternative and a definite update time. It does not invent an upgrade or exceed the $25 authority.
  3. The handover says 'second night missing from record; confirmation received; manager reviewing alternative' rather than labelling the guest difficult.

TOU1050 · The Accommodation Industry
TOU1050 · Learning module · Introductory


Compare accommodation options and recommend a suitable stay for a fictional traveller using cost, service, access and operating constraints.
Learning focus: compare products, not just labels; use a transparent comparison dataset; recommend and manage a limitation.


1. Compare products, not just labels2. Use a transparent comparison dataset3. Recommend and manage a limitationPracticeYour work


Words to know
Occupancy
occupied rooms divided by available rooms for the period.
Room rate
the price charged for the specified accommodation period and terms.
Distribution channel
a route through which accommodation is marketed or booked.
Operating department
a team responsible for a function such as front desk or housekeeping.
1. Compare products, not just labels
Hotels, motels, hostels, lodges, campgrounds and other accommodation types offer different combinations of location, facilities, privacy and service. A label does not guarantee a particular quality or access feature. Compare the actual offer: room configuration, bathroom arrangement, check-in limits, meals, transport links, cancellation terms and relevant physical features.


Accommodation departments depend on each other. Front desk needs accurate room status; housekeeping needs a prioritized turnover list; maintenance must communicate unavailable rooms; reservations must avoid selling inventory that cannot be delivered. Create a service chain showing how an incorrect room-status update could affect a guest and how a clear handover reduces the risk.
Next lesson
2. Use a transparent comparison dataset
Fictional options for two nights: Willow Inn charges $120 per night plus a $10 total booking fee; breakfast and parking are included. Junction Lodge charges $105 per night plus $15 parking per night and $12 breakfast per person per day. River Hostel charges $70 per night for a private room with a shared bathroom and no parking. Taxes are excluded from all supplied prices; identify them as an unresolved real-world cost rather than inventing a rate.


For one traveller needing parking and breakfast on both mornings, Willow totals $250 before tax. Junction totals $210 + $30 + $24 = $264. River's lower room price does not establish suitability if the traveller needs a private bathroom. State assumptions and compare the same number of nights, people and included services.
Previous lessonNext lesson
3. Recommend and manage a limitation
A good recommendation balances the traveller's must-haves and preferences. Separate a required feature from a nice-to-have and identify what remains unverified. Do not promise a room's accessibility solely because a building has an elevator. A useful confirmation asks for the relevant entrance, room, bathroom and route details without requiring health information.


For a small property, calculate occupancy to describe use of inventory: 18 occupied rooms out of 24 available is 75%. Occupancy is not profit; room rates and operating costs still matter. Explain seasonality and the role of reservation channels. Finish with a sourced accommodation comparison and a career connection showing how a department contributes to the guest experience.
Previous lessonGo to practice
Worked example
  1. A room-only comparison suggests Junction is cheaper, but the like-for-like two-night total with parking and breakfast is $264 versus Willow's $250.
  2. A recommendation can select Willow for the stated needs while identifying taxes and exact room features as items to verify. It should not claim certainty beyond the dataset.
  3. The 75% occupancy calculation describes rooms used, not a 75% profit margin. The distinction prevents a common operations error.

TOU1060 · Learning module · Introductory


Build a travel-service comparison and a booking-information checklist that helps a fictional client understand roles, terms and risks before making a decision.
Learning focus: trace who provides and who arranges; compare terms alongside price; build a reliable information handover.


1. Trace who provides and who arranges2. Compare terms alongside price3. Build a reliable information handoverPracticeYour work


Words to know
Supplier
an organization providing a travel component.
Intermediary
an organization connecting travellers with suppliers.
Package
a combined set of travel components offered under stated terms.
Booking condition
a rule governing purchase, changes, cancellation or use.
1. Trace who provides and who arranges
The travel industry connects clients with transportation, accommodation, activities and support. A supplier operates a service; an intermediary may arrange or sell it. A booking platform, travel advisor, tour operator and destination organization do not necessarily have the same responsibilities. Map who receives payment, who delivers each component and who handles a change.


An appealing bundle can still contain separate terms. Read the dates, inclusions, exclusions, eligibility and cancellation conditions. Distinguish an estimate from a confirmed reservation and a marketing claim from a contractual commitment. Classroom work must not involve making a real booking, contacting a business or providing personal documents.
Next lesson
2. Compare terms alongside price
Fictional two-night package A costs $420 and includes a $60 attraction ticket, transfers and accommodation. It is non-refundable after confirmation. Package B costs $455 with the same inclusions and permits cancellation until seven days before arrival with a $25 fee. Package C costs $390 but excludes $40 transfers and the $60 ticket. All are before tax and use the same dates and room type.


The comparable C total is $490, so the lowest advertised number is not the lowest like-for-like price. A client uncertain about dates may value B's cancellation option, but it still has a deadline and fee. Write the recommendation using the client's stated risk tolerance rather than assuming the cheapest or most flexible option is always best.
Previous lessonNext lesson
3. Build a reliable information handover
A booking checklist should confirm names only through the eventual authorized process, dates, route, accommodation, total cost, payment schedule, terms and relevant documents. In this course use fictional names and no identity numbers. Direct live document, entry, safety or health questions to current official sources, including travel.gc.ca; requirements can change and vary by traveller and destination.


Create a disruption handover identifying the booking reference placeholder, affected component, verified terms, available options and unresolved question. Explain which organization should be approached through the official channel in a real situation. Evaluate source quality and record observation dates so a later reader knows when the comparison was accurate.
Previous lessonGo to practice
Worked example
  1. Package C's $390 headline omits $100 in components included in A and B. Adding them yields $490 before tax.
  2. For a client whose dates may change, B's $35 higher purchase price may be justified by its stated cancellation terms. The recommendation still notes the seven-day deadline and $25 fee.
  3. The checklist points to official document guidance and records a verification date instead of guessing that all travellers need identical documents.
