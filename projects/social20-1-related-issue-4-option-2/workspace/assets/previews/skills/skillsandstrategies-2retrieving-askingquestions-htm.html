Asking Questions

It is important to ask good questions for your inquiry. Good questions lead to more than a simple “yes” or “no” answer. They require high level thinking; asking these types of questions will help you to thoroughly understand a topic.
Divergent questions are good questions to ask. They have many possible answers. Descriptions of different kinds of divergent questions are provided below. Use them to formulate your own questions. Click here to download a copy of the chart below.

Knowledge: These questions get you to recall information using words such as describe, name, list, label, who, what, where, or when.






Comprehension: These questions help you understand facts, how they relate to each other, and what they mean all together. They use words such as create, explain, summarize, or rewrite.






Application: These questions help you understand how and why figures or events are significant. They use words such as identify, example of, or related to.






Analysis: These questions help you distinguish facts from opinions. They use words such as outline, classify, compare, or contrast.






Synthesis: These questions involve creativity, as they get you to make inferences and predictions. They use words like predict, infer, design, or create.






Evaluation: These questions get you to formulate opinions and judgments. They use words such as agree, prioritize, assess, or decide.
