## Lead-review constraints to incorporate

1. Lesson 1's independent warm-gym/sweating task in the progression is too similar to the worked exercise/sweating example. Change the reasoning demand, for example a supplied interruption in an already-taught control route, and require the learner to explain the consequence. A new proper noun or the same sequence with a new temperature does not establish independent transfer. Teach every fact required by a question before asking it.
2. Teach homeostasis as regulation within workable ranges, not perfect constancy. Not every nervous-system response is homeostatic. In the temperature example, avoid claiming all detection must come from skin/PNS: central temperature-sensitive structures can monitor internal conditions too. Explain how sweat evaporation helps remove heat if that is your worked example, without a digression into later brain anatomy. Verify any consequential added mechanism against the full attached textbook or a clearly identified authoritative source; keep that evidence outside student copy.
3. The systems-table check needs minimum structure AND function teaching for CNS, PNS, sensory/afferent, motor/efferent, somatic, autonomic, sympathetic and parasympathetic. Teach nesting and information direction. Somatic means skeletal-muscle control, not necessarily conscious choice; withdrawal is a somatic reflex. Keep detailed organ effects for lesson-08.
4. A neuron is one cell; a nerve is a bundle of axons/nerve fibres, not whole-cell drawings. Use the existing neuron as a typical multipolar example, not every neuron's shape. Explain structure/function, continuous axon, insulating myelin and regeneration at nodes. Schwann/PNS versus oligodendrocyte/CNS. Introduce an action potential as a travelling electrical event before using the term, but reserve ion-channel detail for lessons-04/-05. No teleported signals, grey/white exclusive neuron binary, myelination-based regeneration rule, 10:1 glial ratio or simple genetic/inevitably progressive MS claim.
5. Lesson 3 must distinguish a deliberately selected visually guided action from spinal withdrawal; rapid movement alone does not establish a reflex. Keep the phone example and a fresh transfer distinct from the exact required tennis-ball prompt. Information can also travel toward the brain while a spinal reflex starts. Not every reflex has an intervening interneuron and not every reflex is spinal. Keep job-versus-shape qualification concise, not a new taxonomy memorization demand.
