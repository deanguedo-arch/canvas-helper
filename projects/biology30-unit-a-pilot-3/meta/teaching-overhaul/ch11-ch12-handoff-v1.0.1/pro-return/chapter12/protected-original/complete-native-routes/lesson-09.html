Explain a result from the affected step

An eye may focus light correctly but have damaged photoreceptors. An ear may have working hair cells but poor transmission through the middle ear. Identify the affected step before predicting the consequence.

Worked example

Build a full explanation

“The person has difficulty hearing” is an observation, not a complete mechanism.

  1. Name the stated damaged structure.
  2. Identify whether it conducts, transduces or carries neural signals.
  3. Explain the effect on the next stage.
  4. State the likely consequence without diagnosing beyond the supplied evidence.

Stop and think

Try this before opening the explanation

Can the same all-or-none type of signal carry information for both hearing and vision?

Show the explanation

Yes. The receptor and pathway that are active, together with their patterns of activity, help the brain interpret the information.

Written explanations

Written explanation 1

Maximum 3200 characters. Explain the biology in your own words.

Compare after saving your own response

Vision begins with light guided by cornea, humours, pupil and lens to retinal rods and cones; retinal processing leads to ganglion-cell activity and visual pathways. Hearing begins with pressure waves conducted by pinna, canal, tympanum and ossicles to cochlear movement; hair cells transduce movement and sensory neurons carry information into auditory pathways.

Check your explanation for:

  • Correct support structures
  • Correct receptors and transduction
  • Neural transmission distinguished from original energy

Written explanation 2

Maximum 3200 characters. Explain the biology in your own words.

Compare after saving your own response

Balance combines semicircular canals, utricle/saccule, vision and proprioception. Taste categories are distributed, not confined to exclusive zones. The optic nerve carries ganglion-cell action potentials. For example, otolith organs use a weighted membrane to detect gravity and linear acceleration, rather than using the canal rotation mechanism.

  • One causal mechanism explained
