Explain a result from the affected step

An eye may focus light correctly but have damaged photoreceptors. An ear may have working hair cells but poor transmission through the middle ear. Identify the affected step before predicting the consequence.

Complete the reasoning rather than stopping at a label

Here is a fully stated hearing case: a hypothetical record says that the tympanum vibrates, the stapes cannot move the oval window effectively, the hair cells remain capable of responding to suitable movement, and auditory nerve fibres remain functional. Start with the first observed interruption: transfer from the ossicles to the inner-ear fluid is limited. Therefore less useful fluid and basilar-membrane movement reaches the receptors. The resulting receptor input to auditory neurons can be reduced even though the cells and nerve are functional.

The supported conclusion is a mechanical-conduction limitation at the stated stage. It is not “every part of the inner ear is damaged,” and it does not establish the medical cause of the stapes limitation. The general method is to retain the evidence for working structures, locate the first stated failure, explain its downstream consequence, and stop before an unsupported diagnosis.
