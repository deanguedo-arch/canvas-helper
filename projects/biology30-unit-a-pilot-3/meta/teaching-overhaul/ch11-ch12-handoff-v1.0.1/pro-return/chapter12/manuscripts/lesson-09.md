## Words for this lesson

Use these definitions when you need help with a term.

sensory adaptation  
Reduced responsiveness to a stimulus that remains relatively constant.

transduction  
Conversion of stimulus energy into a change that the nervous system can signal.

mechanoreceptor  
A sensory receptor that responds to force, movement, pressure or stretching.

accommodation  
Adjustment of lens shape to focus on objects at different distances.

## Use one pathway to organize the chapter

For each sense, trace stimulus → receptor → transduction → sensory pathway → interpretation. Then identify the structures that help the stimulus reach the receptor. The lens focuses light and the ossicles pass on vibration, but neither is the sensory receptor.

Reconstruct the eye route, the two retinal routes, the hearing route and the two balance mechanisms. Then change one condition: the object moves closer, the sound frequency increases or the head starts moving straight upward. Explain which part responds and why.

### Use a comparison to keep the stages separate

Consider two hypothetical systems. In the eye, a cloudy lens scatters light before it reaches otherwise responsive photoreceptors. In the ear, immobile ossicles reduce the vibration reaching otherwise responsive cochlear hair cells. Both cases can reduce useful sensory information, but neither starts with a receptor that has lost its ability to respond. The shared failure is preparation or conduction of the input before transduction.

For the eye, the causal link is scattered incoming light → a degraded retinal pattern → less useful information about the scene. For the ear, it is reduced ossicle movement → reduced movement at the oval window and cochlea → reduced stimulation of the hair cells. Saying only “both send weak signals” hides the mechanism. Conversely, the evidence does not show damaged optic or auditory nerves simply because the final experience is impaired.

### Complete the reasoning rather than stopping at a label

Here is a fully stated hearing case: a hypothetical record says that the tympanum vibrates, the stapes cannot move the oval window effectively, the hair cells remain capable of responding to suitable movement, and auditory nerve fibres remain functional. Start with the first observed interruption: transfer from the ossicles to the inner-ear fluid is limited. Therefore less useful fluid and basilar-membrane movement reaches the receptors. The resulting receptor input to auditory neurons can be reduced even though the cells and nerve are functional.

The supported conclusion is a mechanical-conduction limitation at the stated stage. It is not “every part of the inner ear is damaged,” and it does not establish the medical cause of the stapes limitation. The general method is to retain the evidence for working structures, locate the first stated failure, explain its downstream consequence, and stop before an unsupported diagnosis. ## Use a selective improvement as evidence

These hypothetical records describe changes made to models, not treatment results. All unstated conditions are held constant for the comparison.

| Record        | Before                                                      | After a stated change                                                                                                                 |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Vision model  | Near and far targets are blurred through a cloudy lens      | The lens becomes clear with its focusing shape otherwise unchanged; the far target becomes sharp, but the near target remains blurred |
| Hearing model | Mechanical input is weak and the neural response is reduced | Mechanical input to the cochlea is restored; hair cells still fail to change signaling normally                                       |

For each record, explain what improved or was restored, what limitation remains, and why the result does not show that every part of the sensory pathway is now normal. Identify one additional observation that would help locate the remaining limitation without assuming a diagnosis.

A hint if you need one

A successful change can isolate one contribution without resolving another. In the vision case, separate clear transmission from adjustment for near focus; in the hearing case, separate movement from transduction.

Compare your reasoning

In the vision model, clearer transmission supports the improved far image, but persistent near blur indicates that transparency alone did not solve the near-focus demand. Observing the muscle–ligament–lens response while the model attempts to focus from far to near would test whether accommodation is working and help locate any failure. The record alone does not identify a failed accommodation link or its cause. In the hearing model, restored mechanical input does not restore normal hair-cell signaling, so a receptor-conversion limitation remains. Evidence of auditory-nerve responsiveness would help separate that known receptor problem from an additional neural limitation. Improvement at one stage is evidence about that stage, not proof of complete pathway function.

### Check your explanation

- Uses the before-and-after change in each record
- Distinguishes transparency/accommodation and conduction/transduction
- Names a relevant missing observation for each case
- Avoids claiming complete restoration or an unsupported diagnosis

If you concluded that a successful intervention proves a normal system, look at the stated residual failure. Causal chains can contain more than one limitation, and a partial improvement can make the remaining one easier to identify.

## What a sensory technology changes

A sensory experience requires more than an open route into an organ. Inputs must reach appropriate receptors, be converted into useful neural patterns, and be processed by functioning pathways. When a technology assists a sense, ask exactly which of those steps it changes. The optional extension applies that question to a display and a receptor-bypass device.

## Feedback for the required selections

### A clear lens forms an image on a region of retina that lacks photoreceptors. What is the immediate limitation?

Hint: Identify the step after focusing.

Answer: There is no local photoreceptor transduction

Focusing cannot replace receptors.

### Which combination describes near accommodation?

Hint: Use the full four-step chain.

Answer: Contracted ciliary muscle, reduced ligament tension, rounder lens

Ciliary contraction reduces the tension that flattens the lens.

### Which pair correctly distinguishes hearing and rotation receptors?

Hint: Which apparatus responds to each kind of movement?

Answer: Cochlear hair cells / semicircular-canal hair cells

Both are mechanoreceptive systems but have different structures and stimuli.

### A tone becomes higher in pitch without becoming louder. Which main wave property increases?

Hint: Separate pitch from loudness.

Answer: Frequency

Frequency is the cycles-per-second property associated with pitch.

### Which route carries retinal information toward the brain?

Hint: Start with transduction, not with light entry.

Answer: Rods/cones → bipolar pathways → ganglion cells → optic nerve

Ganglion-cell axons form the optic nerve.

### A study changes contact pressure and body region at the same time. Why is its conclusion limited?

Hint: Ask whether the cause of the difference is isolated.

Answer: Either change could contribute to a difference in discrimination

A fair comparison controls other influential variables.

### A student says, “A brighter object and a nearer object require the same eye adjustment.” Explain why this is incomplete. Name the main adjustment for each change and trace the muscle–ligament–lens sequence for the nearer object.

Greater brightness mainly calls for the iris to reduce pupil size and limit light entry. A nearer object calls for accommodation: the ciliary muscle contracts, ligament tension decreases, and the lens becomes rounder to bend light more strongly. The two adjustments have different jobs and can occur together.

- Brightness linked to iris/pupil
- Distance linked to lens accommodation
- Contraction → reduced ligament tension → rounder lens
