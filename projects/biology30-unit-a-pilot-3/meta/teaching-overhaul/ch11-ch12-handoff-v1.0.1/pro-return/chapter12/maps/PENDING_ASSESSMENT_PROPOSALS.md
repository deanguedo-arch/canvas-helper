## P1 Retinal numerical sequence

Existing: Word quiz numerical response 1; D2L OBJ_2131004 / QUES_51798_85531. Original six events are:

1. rods and/or cones transduce light energy into an action potential
2. nerve impulse travels to the thalamus area of the brain
3. nerve impulse travels to the occipital lobe of the cerebral cortex for interpretation
4. action potential is transferred to axons of ganglion cells
5. nerve impulse leaves eye via the optic nerve
6. light energy enters the eye and strikes the retina

The original key is 614523. This is a wording/science defect, not a problem solved by changing the digits.

Proposed future item ID: ch12-assessment-proposed-retinal-sequence-v2. Stem: “Arrange the following events in the order of a simplified retinal-to-cortex pathway.” Proposed six event texts:

1. Light absorption changes photoreceptor signaling and transmitter release
2. Visual information is relayed through the thalamus
3. Visual information reaches the occipital visual cortex for processing
4. Bipolar pathways influence ganglion cells, which generate action potentials
5. Ganglion-cell action potentials travel out of the eye in the optic nerve
6. Incoming light reaches retinal photoreceptors

Proposed ordering remains 614523, but applies only to the new wording. Proposed feedback: “Light is first transduced into changed photoreceptor signaling. Retinal pathways influence ganglion-cell firing; ganglion axons carry the action potentials in the optic nerve. Rods and cones do not send an action potential through each successive cell.” Source: current lesson04, textbook p415–416 with its inherited transmitter shortcut explicitly corrected; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10806/

## P3 Hearing-aid prompt and absolute model

Existing Word written2(f): “A hearing aid is a technology designed to amplify the sound waves that enter the middle ear. Why wouldn’t this technology be of much use for a person with NIHL?” Original key ends: “No amount of amplification will increase the person’s ability to hear sound waves if the hair cells (receptors) have been damaged.”

Proposed future ID ch12-assessment-proposed-amplification-limits-v2. Prompt: “Explain why amplification can help some people with damaged cochlear hair cells, yet may offer limited benefit when too little usable hair-cell function remains.” Model: “Amplification increases sound vibrations. Surviving hair cells may respond to the stronger input and communicate with auditory neurons. Amplification does not regrow missing receptors; if too little usable transduction remains, stronger input may not provide useful signals. The amount and location of remaining function matter.” Criteria: surviving-cell mechanism, distinction from repair, no universal outcome. Source: current lesson06; https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/hearing-aids

## P4 Otolith stimulus terminology

Existing Word MC17 stimulus and textbook p424: “The otoliths lie in a cupula over a layer of hair cells.” The question asks where the utricle/saccule are and has an intended correct inner-ear answer; the surrounding stimulus is the issue.

Proposed replacement sentence for a new stimulus version: “The otoliths weight an otolithic membrane over hair cells in the utricle and saccule. Gravity or linear acceleration can shift that membrane relative to the hair cells, bending their projections and changing vestibular signaling.” Keep the original question/options/key in the protected record. Do not rename a semicircular-canal cupula as an otolithic membrane globally. Source: actual Fig12.24 p425, current lesson07; https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/balance-disorders

## P5 Held-posture teacher model

Existing comprehension Q25 asks how the brain perceives that the body is lying down. Teacher key printed5-62 says: “When the person is in an established position, the crystals stop rolling around. The vestibule doesn’t send any impulses to the brain, thereby indicating that the individual is still holding that static position.”

Proposed reviewed model for future use: “The head's orientation relative to gravity affects the pull on the weighted otolithic membranes in the utricle and saccule. The resulting hair-cell signaling provides information about that orientation even when the head is held still. The brain combines vestibular information with proprioception and vision when available. A still posture does not mean the vestibule stops sending all impulses.” Source: current lesson07 and Fig12.24, NIDCD balance reference above. Preserve the original teacher key as source evidence; approval is needed before using the replacement for marking.

## P8 Adaptation bank consistency

Existing ch12-mc-002 stem: “A student stops noticing the hum of a ventilation fan after several minutes. Explanation: “The CNS reduces attention to constant, low-priority sensory input.” Existing ch12-sensory-adaptation-recognize defines it as “reduced awareness of a constant, unchanging stimulus so that new or changing information receives more attention”.

## P10 Specific misconception recovery

Current ch12-cornea misconception: “The cornea changes shape during accommodation to focus near objects.” Its correction is only the definition: “The transparent front part of the outer eye that provides much of the eye's refraction.”

Proposed future feedback: “The cornea contributes substantial refraction, but accommodation changes lens shape. Ciliary contraction reduces ligament tension, letting the lens become rounder for near focus. Naming the cornea's focusing role does not make it the adjustable structure in this mechanism.” Apply only after a reviewed bank-version decision; no blanket regeneration of all 64 concept records is authorized.
