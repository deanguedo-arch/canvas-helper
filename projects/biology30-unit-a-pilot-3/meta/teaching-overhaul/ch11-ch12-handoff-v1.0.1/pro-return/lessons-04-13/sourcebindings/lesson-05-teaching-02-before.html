3. Repolarization and hyperpolarization

After the peaks, sodium channels become inactivated and K⁺ moves out through potassium channels. Positive charge leaves, making the inside negative again. This is .

Potassium channels close slowly, so K⁺ may keep leaving briefly. The voltage drops below the resting level. This is , or the undershoot. The example here reaches about −90 mV before recovering.

The sodium-potassium pump maintains the ion concentration differences over time. Ion movement through channels causes the rapid rise and fall of an action potential.

Voltage is not concentration. The membrane voltage changes during an impulse, but the overall Na⁺ and K⁺ concentration gradients do not reverse.
