## Resting-potential teacher erratum, not a prompt replacement

- Route04 source-checkpoint-10-written, paired required check-resting-potential-written; source comprehensionQ10, PDF4/printed5-21; textbook373–374/PDF14–15; slides22–23
- Historical key treats chloride as trapped and overstates direct pump-only explanation
- Proposed approved marking model: A resting neuron commonly has higherK inside and higherNa outside, maintained by ATP-dependent pumping3Na out/2K in. ## Repolarization/recovery teacher erratum

- Route05 source-checkpoint-13-written and source-checkpoint-14-written; related check-action-potentials-written. ComprehensionQ13, PDF5/printed5-23; textbook377–378/PDF18–19; slides26–29
- Proposed model: Na-channel inactivation and delayedK-channel opening shift the rapid net movement toward K efflux, returning voltage toward its resting negative level. ## Blood–brain barrier versus meninges teacher erratum

- Route09 source-checkpoint-20-written; route11 source-review112-q3-written; route12 source-chapter-review-q22-written
- Historical evidence: textbook388/PDF29 and comprehensionQ20/PDF8/printed5-29; chapter-review keyQ22/PDF4/printed5-40
- Proposed model: meninges are enclosing membranes; CSF supports cushioning and the fluid environment; the BBB depends principally on tightly joined capillary-lining cells and specialized transport. Blood andCSF are different compartments, so CSF analysis can provide evidence specific to the CNS surrounding fluid that is not equivalent to routine blood sampling. This does not mean that blood cannot contain medically useful information or that meninges themselves are theBBB
- Criteria: distinguish mechanical surroundings from selective blood-to-tissue exchange; avoid absolute claims that all molecules are blocked or all blood tests are useless
- Verification: https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/stroke/stroke-overview, checked6October2026. No test procedure or individual medical recommendation is proposed
