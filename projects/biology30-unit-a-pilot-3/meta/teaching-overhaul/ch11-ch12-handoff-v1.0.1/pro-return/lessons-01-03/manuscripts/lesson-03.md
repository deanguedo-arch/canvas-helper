# Pathways and reflexes

## Why can movement begin before awareness?

If a hand contacts something painfully sharp, withdrawal can begin before you consciously recognize what happened. Yet you can also see an obstacle, decide to avoid it and choose a movement. Both responses use neurons, but they do not require the same processing sequence.

To compare them, follow information from the receptor to the effector. Ask where it enters the CNS, what processing is required and how instructions leave. Speed alone will not tell you which pathway is involved.

## Three jobs in a neural pathway

A **sensory neuron**, also called an **afferent neuron**, carries information from a sensory receptor toward the CNS. The receptor might detect a change at the skin or provide information involved in a sense such as vision. Sensory describes the input job.

An **interneuron** operates within the CNS. It can connect and process information between other neurons. In a withdrawal circuit, it can link incoming sensory information with a motor neuron. In more complex pathways, many interneurons participate in processing.

A **motor neuron**, also called an **efferent neuron**, carries information from the CNS toward an effector. The effector produces the response. If the effector is a skeletal muscle, its contraction produces movement.

| Structure | Direction or location | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory receptor | At the point of detection | Detects a particular stimulus |
| Sensory neuron | Toward the CNS | Carries sensory information inward |
| Interneuron | Within the CNS | Connects and processes information |
| Motor neuron | From the CNS toward an effector | Carries output information |
| Effector | At the responding tissue | Carries out the response |

These are functional descriptions. Words such as multipolar, bipolar and unipolar describe a neuron's shape and branches. A question about what a neuron does is different from a question about its appearance. Trace its connections and direction before assigning a functional role.

## Work through a deliberately directed movement

A driver sees a cat move onto the road and presses the brake. Where do sensory input, integration and motor output occur?

Start with the information that matters: light from the scene is detected by the visual system. Sensory information reaches the CNS. Processing in the brain allows the driver to recognize the situation and organize a response. Motor pathways then carry instructions toward the skeletal muscles used to move the foot. Muscle contraction presses the brake.

The eyes do not send a motor command directly to the foot. Their contribution is sensory information. The foot muscle does not decide that the cat is an obstacle. It is an effector carrying out motor output. The processing between these stages is essential to explaining a response selected from visual information.

This is a simplified account of a complex movement. Its purpose is to keep the direction and jobs clear. We have not described every intervening neuron or all the feedback needed for precise driving.

## A withdrawal reflex uses a rapid circuit

A **reflex** is a rapid, involuntary response to a stimulus. A **reflex arc** is the neural pathway that produces it. In the withdrawal example below, the spinal circuit can initiate output without waiting for the conscious recognition required in the driver example.

Begin at the skin on the left. Follow the sensory route into the spinal cord, then follow the interneuron and motor route to the muscle on the right. After tracing that circuit, look for the separate path carrying information toward the brain.

![A simplified spinal withdrawal circuit. A receptor in the skin connects through a sensory neuron and an interneuron in the spinal cord to a motor neuron and muscle. A separate ascending route carries information toward the brain.](../assets/source/integrated-withdrawal.svg)

A receptor detects the potentially harmful stimulus. The sensory neuron carries information into the spinal cord. An interneuron in this circuit passes information to a motor neuron. Motor output reaches the muscle, which contracts and moves the hand away. The response reduces continued contact with the harmful stimulus.

Information also travels toward the brain. That ascending communication contributes to conscious awareness of the stimulus. The spinal route does not have to wait for that awareness before beginning the withdrawal response. Saying that withdrawal can start before conscious pain does not mean that no information reaches the brain.

The motor neuron's cell body is inside the spinal cord in this drawing; its axon extends outward toward the muscle. The sensory pathway enters the cord. Use those locations to distinguish central processing from the peripheral connections. The picture is a schematic, not a scale drawing or a complete map of every branch.

Not every reflex uses the exact same circuit. Some do not require an interneuron between the sensory and motor neurons. Other reflexes use different CNS regions. The five-component withdrawal route is the model being explained here, not a claim that all involuntary activity follows these exact five structures.

## Compare the two responses using the same questions

| Question | Driver sees an obstacle and chooses to brake | Spinal withdrawal model |
|---|---|---|
| What starts the pathway? | Visual information about the scene | A potentially harmful stimulus detected at the skin |
| Where does the relevant processing occur? | Brain processing helps recognize the situation and organize the chosen action | A spinal circuit can organize the initial motor response |
| Does the initial movement wait for a conscious decision? | The example is a deliberately directed action | The withdrawal can begin without waiting for one |
| How does the instruction reach muscle? | Motor pathways carry information to skeletal muscles | A motor neuron carries output from the spinal circuit to muscle |
| Does the brain receive information? | Yes, as an essential part of the described visual response | Yes, through ascending pathways as the rapid circuit acts |

Both examples involve sensory information and motor output. The useful difference is the processing required for the initial response. An action can occur quickly and still depend on brain processing. Therefore, “it was fast” is not enough evidence to identify a spinal withdrawal reflex.

### Repair an incomplete pathway

A student has written the following withdrawal pathway:

| Position | Student's entry |
|---|---|
| 1 | Receptor detects the stimulus |
| 2 | Motor neuron carries information into the spinal cord |
| 3 | Interneuron connects the pathway within the spinal cord |
| 4 | Sensory neuron carries instructions out toward the muscle |
| 5 | Muscle contracts |

Correct positions 2 and 4 and explain why each correction is needed. Then add one sentence about how the brain can become aware of the stimulus even though the movement has begun. Save your response before opening the comparison.

**Hint:** sensory and motor name opposite directions relative to the CNS. Use the separate ascending route in the figure for the awareness sentence.

**Model correction:** a sensory neuron carries the input toward the spinal cord at position 2. A motor neuron carries output toward the muscle at position 4. The student reversed their jobs. An ascending pathway also carries information toward the brain, where further processing can contribute to conscious awareness. The existence of that route does not require the spinal withdrawal circuit to wait for a conscious decision.

**Check your explanation:** correct both directions, retain the interneuron within the CNS and explain the separate information route toward awareness.

## Use intermediate evidence to reason about a pathway

A final observation such as “the muscle did not contract” could have several causes. To locate an interruption, look for evidence of where signalling still occurs.

For example, suppose a hypothetical record shows that the receptor detects the stimulus and the sensory signal enters the spinal cord normally, but no motor signal leaves toward the muscle. This evidence rules out failure of detection or the incoming sensory route in the stated model. The interruption must be investigated at the processing-to-output part of the pathway. It does not by itself distinguish every possible cell or junction there.

That last limitation matters. A useful explanation says what the evidence supports and what it leaves unresolved. It should not turn a simple pathway exercise into a certain diagnosis.

### Predict two different outcomes

Use the withdrawal figure and the two hypothetical modifications below. Assume every part not identified as interrupted continues to function as shown. These are simplified teaching models, not clinical records.

| Model | Specified interruption |
|---|---|
| A | The separate ascending route toward the brain is interrupted after information has entered the spinal withdrawal circuit. The local sensory-to-interneuron-to-motor route remains intact |
| B | The motor route from the spinal circuit to the muscle is interrupted. The sensory route into the spinal cord and the ascending route toward the brain remain intact |

For each model, predict whether the pictured withdrawal movement can begin and whether information can reach the brain through the pictured ascending route. Explain why “no withdrawal” and “no sensory information” are different conclusions. Save your first response before comparing it with the model.

**Hint:** trace the two routes separately. Mark where each route encounters its stated interruption rather than assuming that damage to one branch disables every branch.

**Model explanation:** in model A, the local withdrawal circuit is intact, so it can still send motor output to begin the pictured response. Information cannot continue to the brain through the interrupted ascending route. In model B, sensory information can enter the spinal cord and continue toward the brain, but the interrupted motor route cannot deliver the pictured instruction to the muscle. The withdrawal movement therefore cannot be produced through that route. These predictions concern only the explicitly described pathways. They do not establish every sensation or possible movement in a real person.

**Check your explanation:** give both predictions for each model, trace the relevant intact or interrupted route, and avoid using absence of movement as proof that detection failed.

## Connect the pathway back to the cell

A pathway explanation needs more than an ordered list of names. It identifies what each structure does, how information travels between stages and why the response follows. It also distinguishes a circuit that can act locally from a response that depends on further brain processing.

So far, an impulse has been the signal carried by a neuron. We will now look at the axon membrane itself. To understand how a neuron fires, first establish what “resting” means and why the inside and outside of the membrane have different electrical potentials.
