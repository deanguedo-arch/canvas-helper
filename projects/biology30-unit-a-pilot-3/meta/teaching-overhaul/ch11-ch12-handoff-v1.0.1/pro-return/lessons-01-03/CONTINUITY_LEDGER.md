# Continuity after lessons 1–3

The learner has now encountered:
- Stimulus versus response, receptor versus effector, sensory input/integration/motor output, and homeostasis as relative internal stability
- CNS/PNS by location; sensory/motor by direction; a small somatic/autonomic and sympathetic/parasympathetic preview sufficient for the unchanged first systems-table demand. Detailed autonomic physiology remains in lesson 8
- Neuron as one cell, nerve as a bundle of fibres, glial support, typical dendrites/soma/axon/terminals, axon hillock as a region in the diagram, and chemical communication at a synapse
- Action potential as a brief all-or-none membrane-voltage change regenerated along the axon. Voltage is introduced as an inside/outside comparison; ions, gradients, channels, threshold mechanism and detailed recovery are still to be taught in 4–6
- Myelin, nodes, continuous axon, node regeneration, Schwann/PNS versus oligodendrocyte/CNS, with limited white/grey preview. Tissue arrangements belong in 9
- Afferent sensory and efferent motor roles, interneurons within the CNS, shape versus function, brain-mediated driver response versus local withdrawal, and separate ascending communication
- Evidence-based location of an interruption with limits on what a final missing response proves. All new failure records are hypothetical models, not clinical observations

Examples used: cold skin/surface-vessel response; deliberate sound-to-button action; sensory versus motor interruption; myelin versus terminal release failure; misleading myelin drawing; wrong-location proposed repair; driver/cat/brake; withdrawal before awareness; reversed sensory/motor labels; different interruption of ascending versus motor branches.

Avoid using those exact new examples as fresh independent mastery evidence later. Later work can reuse the methods while changing a consequential condition, representation or inference. The original tennis-ball written task is a taught-level comparison, not a blind assessment. The current required drawing and source-glia questions remain unchanged.
