## 2. Plan the teaching, not a collection of facts

For each section record: prerequisite understanding; the idea being introduced; why it matters now; the observation students make; the explanation that follows; the independent task that becomes possible.

Build a chapter-wide conceptual progression before requesting prose. Introduce people, experiments, processes and comparisons when needed; do not write generic references such as “In lesson01 you learned…”. Refer to the actual biological idea in natural language. Distinguish purposeful teacher phrasing from slide shorthand requiring spoken explanation.

Every lesson must make clear what we are examining and why. Start with a meaningful question, situation or observation, then teach the mechanism. Reuse the teacher's progression where it works; verify and qualify misleading historical or scientific shorthand rather than copying it blindly.
