Extra: Levels of Paralysis

Paraplegia: If the injury occurs below the first thoracic vertebrae in the upper back region, the paralysis is likely to be confined to the legs. A paraplegic patient will retain full use of their arms and hands.

Quadriplegia: If the injury occurs above the first thoracic vertebrae in the cervical vertebrae or neck region of the body, the paralysis generally results in loss of movement in all limbs. Abdominal and chest muscles may also be affected, resulting in weakened breathing and the inability to properly cough to clear the chest. 

A spinal cord injury usually results in the area of the body below the injury site being partially or completely unable to communicate with the brain. 

Paraplegic patients are not necessarily confined to a wheelchair; modern prosthetics and stem cell treatment can provide an increased level of mobility
