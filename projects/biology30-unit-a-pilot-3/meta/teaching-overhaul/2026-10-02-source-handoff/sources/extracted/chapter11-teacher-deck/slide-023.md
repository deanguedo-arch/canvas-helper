Resting State & the Sodium-Potassium Pump 

Inside the resting cell, there exists a higher concentration of potassium (K+) than sodium (Na+); outside the cell, the opposite is true.

Potassium ions are able to passively diffuse out of the cell more easily than sodium ions can diffuse into the (K+ = “leaky” ion channels)

In an attempt to balance this difference, the sodium-potassium pump along the membrane surface will pump two K+ back into the cell in exchange for three Na+  

As a result, as excess of positive charge accumulates outside of the cell membrane, causing the inside of the cell to become negatively charged relative to the outside. A constant membrane potential of -70 mV is maintained.
