Extra: The Stress Response and Anxiety

The stress response, which triggers when our bodies perceive a threat, served an important evolutionary purpose in helping our ancestors survive dangerous situations. However, in modern times, stressors often aren't physical threats but rather psychological ones like exams, social pressures, or work deadlines. While the stress response can still help us cope with these challenges, if it's triggered too frequently or intensely, it can lead to chronic stress and anxiety. This is because our bodies aren't designed to handle prolonged periods of stress, and the constant activation of the stress response can have negative effects on both our physical and mental health, contributing to conditions like anxiety disorders.
