The PNS links the CNS with the rest of the body

The connects the brain and spinal cord with sensory organs, muscles, glands and internal organs. Sensory pathways carry information toward the ; motor pathways carry instructions away.

There are 12 pairs of cranial nerves associated with the brain and 31 pairs of spinal nerves associated with the spinal cord. You do not need to memorize the individual spinal regions here.

Somatic and autonomic systems

The includes somatic and autonomic systems. The somatic system carries sensory information and controls skeletal muscles. It includes , such as moving your hand, and skeletal-muscle reflexes.

The autonomic system controls involuntary activity in smooth muscle, heart muscle and glands. Changes in heart rate and digestion help maintain without a conscious decision.

Motor output is not always voluntary. It can also carry instructions to an that produces an involuntary .

Sympathetic: preparing for action

The produces the fight-or-flight . Pupils and air passages dilate; heart rate and blood pressure rise. Digestion and bladder emptying slow. The liver releases glucose, and adrenal secretion increases.

These changes prepare the body for action. Notice that heart activity increases while digestion decreases: not every organ speeds up.

Parasympathetic: rest and digest

The supports rest and digest. Pupils constrict, heart rate and blood pressure fall, and digestion and urination increase. is the key in these pathways.

The often have on the same organ. Together, they adjust body functions as conditions change.

Apply it: what does a polygraph measure?

A polygraph records changes such as heart rate, blood pressure, breathing and sweating. These can reflect sympathetic activity, but they do not directly show whether someone is lying.

check: a racing heart can have several causes. The measurement alone cannot explain why it increased.
