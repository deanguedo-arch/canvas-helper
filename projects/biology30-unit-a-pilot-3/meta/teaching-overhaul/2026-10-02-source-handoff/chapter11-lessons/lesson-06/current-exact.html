Release, movement and binding

An reaches the . Calcium channels open, and Ca²⁺ enters. Calcium causes vesicles containing to fuse with the presynaptic membrane. This releases the neurotransmitter by exocytosis.

Neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane. This changes ion movement and makes the receiving cell more or less likely to fire.

Excitatory and inhibitory effects

An excitatory signal makes an more likely. For example, Na⁺ entering the cell makes the inside less negative and moves it toward . The neuron fires only if threshold is reached.

An inhibitory signal makes an action potential less likely. In the examples here, K⁺ leaves or Cl⁻ enters. The inside becomes more negative and moves away from threshold.

Remember: excitatory input helps the neuron reach threshold; inhibitory input makes firing less likely.

Adding inputs: summation

A receives excitatory and inhibitory inputs. Summation is their combined effect. The receiving neuron fires if the combined change brings it to .

Spatial summation: inputs from different locations act together. Temporal summation: repeated inputs arrive close together in time. Both can help determine whether an occurs; they do not make it taller.

At a neuromuscular junction, acetylcholine stimulates the muscle membrane and contributes to contraction. breaks down acetylcholine so that stimulation can end. Be able to explain both roles: the neurotransmitter carries the message; the enzyme helps terminate it.

These examples show why changing synaptic communication can affect many functions. Keep acetylcholine, norepinephrine and GABA as your three memorization priorities.
