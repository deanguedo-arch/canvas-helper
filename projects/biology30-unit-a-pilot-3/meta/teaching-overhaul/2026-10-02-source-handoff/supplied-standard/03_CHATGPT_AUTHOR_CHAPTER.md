# Reusable ChatGPT authoring prompt

You are authoring the teaching for an existing Alberta Biology 30 online course, not designing a new interface. Read the attached `02_TEACHING_STANDARD.md`, chapter source package, preservation manifest, and any approved calibration lessons before writing.

Target: the chapter and exact lesson IDs identified in the attached batch manifest. Default first run: the first two or three coherent Chapter 11 lessons already selected in the source package. Do not infer missing IDs or rewrite another chapter.

## What to produce
Produce complete, student-ready teaching copy and an exact sequence for this batch. Do not produce only an outline, improvement recommendations, a topic summary, or instructions for a future writer. Write every opening, explanation, worked example, transition, question, hint, model answer, and feedback message that the student needs.

Use the supplied textbook and verified outcomes for science and scope, teacher materials for appropriate voice and level, and the current approved course for delivery patterns. Keep provenance and editorial notes in a separate developer-only map. No learner-facing references to teacher slides, missing materials, source conflicts, author comments, or source corrections.

Preserve valuable existing explanations; deepen or replace what is weak. Do not rewrite a good passage merely to make it different. Do not assume Chapters 11–15 pass because the reported feedback prefers them. Do not shorten later lessons to fit an output budget: finish fewer complete lessons and clearly identify the remaining lessons instead.

## Establish the progression first
Briefly map the chapter's central question and the role of each lesson. Then, for each target lesson, establish the opening question, needed prior knowledge, new ideas in dependency order, closing answer, and bridge to what follows. Keep this planning outside the learner-facing manuscript.

## Write like the teacher is teaching
Teach one meaningful step at a time. Explain causes, structures, relationships, and changes rather than listing terms. Introduce vocabulary when needed, explain it in context, and connect to the preserved vocabulary system. Use respectful high-school language and biologically accurate simplifications. Include sufficient detail for a first-time learner without turning the lesson into a university lecture.

Use the lesson's question to create continuity. Point to the relevant part of a visual and explain how to read it. In examples, show why each step follows. Include additional examples when a contrasting condition or new representation is needed to establish transfer. Then reduce support in guided practice and use a fresh independent attempt with an explained answer. Do not turn this sequence into repetitive, mechanical headings.

## Media
Reuse approved images when suitable. For each necessary new or revised visual, supply a precise developer-only board-style storyboard, labels, scientific constraints, alt text, intended placement, and the learner-facing explanation that accompanies it. Keep locked answer mappings intact. A visual brief is not a finished asset; mark its status honestly and do not call an essential-asset-dependent lesson release-ready.

For video, specify the exact teaching purpose, placement, viewing prompt, and follow-up application. Use only supplied/verified resources; do not invent video titles, URLs, or timestamps. If a clip is missing, provide a proposed recording script or storyboard separately. The in-course text must still teach the essential content.

## Deliverables
1. Complete learner-facing manuscript for each target lesson, clearly separated from developer information.
2. Integration map: existing lesson ID/anchor, ordered content blocks, exact asset/reference IDs, preserved elements, and proposed new formative items.
3. Worked examples, guided and independent practice, hints, solutions, and explanatory feedback. New practice is distinct from unchanged required checks.
4. Source/outcome coverage map with exact evidence locations and factual uncertainties.
5. Asset and video briefs/statuses, plus any proposed structural or assessment changes requiring approval.
6. A self-review that names concrete remaining weaknesses and release blockers rather than awarding unsupported confidence percentages.
