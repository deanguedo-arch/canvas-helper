## Textbook p. 370, question 5

Sensory neurons carry impulses from receptors towards the brain or spinal cord. Interneurons connect neurons within the central nervous system. Motor neurons carry impulses from the central nervous system to effectors, such as muscles or glands, which produce a response.

## Textbook p. 371, investigation 11.A, application 4

Heat stimulates receptors in the hand. A sensory neuron carries an impulse to the spinal cord, where an interneuron connects it to a motor neuron. The motor neuron stimulates a muscle to withdraw the hand. Another pathway carries information to the brain. The spinal response can occur before the brain produces conscious awareness of pain, so the hand moves before the student feels the pain.

## Textbook p. 372, question 8

A myelinated neuron has dendrites that receive signals, a cell body containing the nucleus, and an axon that carries impulses away from the cell body. A fatty myelin sheath surrounds much of the axon. Gaps between myelin segments are nodes of Ranvier. Axon terminals communicate with other cells. Schwann cells make peripheral myelin; different glial cells make myelin in the central nervous system.

## Textbook p. 384, question 3

Both types have a cell body and processes that receive and transmit signals. A myelinated axon has an insulating sheath interrupted by nodes. An unmyelinated axon lacks that sheath. For otherwise similar axons, transmission is generally faster with myelin because the impulse is renewed mainly at nodes, rather than continuously along the exposed axon membrane. Axon diameter also affects speed, so myelin is not the only factor.
