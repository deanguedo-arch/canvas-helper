# Teacher review: answers and expected evidence

This is feedback-rich local practice, not a secure test. The HTML necessarily contains concept keys. Written responses are evaluated using the rubric, not automatically scored.

## Confidence for a particular task
**p4-task-confidence-check-1: C.** The statement concerns an ability to carry out a particular action. It does not guarantee the outside decision.

**p4-task-confidence-check-2: A.** Prepared skills and remaining uncertainty can exist together. Clarifying the arrangement addresses the gap while retaining useful preparation.

**Written evidence:** State the specific task.; Connect two pieces of preparation evidence to it.; Identify one uncertainty without turning it into a global judgment.

## Find evidence of preparation
**p4-preparation-evidence-check-1: B.** The demonstration supplies information. The learner still needs an opportunity to understand, try and review the sequence.

**p4-preparation-evidence-check-2: C.** Specific feedback links an observation to an action. It can support preparation without making a claim about the whole person.

**Written evidence:** Name two different sources.; Link each to a particular part of the task.; Explain the limit of each source and a useful next question.

## Keep useful evidence
**p4-useful-evidence-check-1: A.** Progress requires a comparison across time. Attendance describes an action; a broad affirmation does not identify a change.

**p4-useful-evidence-check-2: B.** The account is a metaphor. A programmed balance does not measure confidence, identity or emotional control.

**Written evidence:** Separate effort, success and change over time.; Use specific case evidence.; State what the record supports without promising a result.

## Use feedback after setbacks
**p4-feedback-setbacks-check-1: C.** The statement identifies a task feature and a possible adjustment. It does not judge the whole person.

**p4-feedback-setbacks-check-2: A.** Protecting personal worth should not remove useful corrections. Evaluate the information and seek support for harmful comments.

**Written evidence:** Identify the observation, correction and global judgment.; Retain relevant feedback without denying the error.; Name a useful action or support request.

## A routine you can use
**p4-cba-routine-check-1: A.** The statement connects preparation to a practical action without promising the result.

**p4-cba-routine-check-2: B.** A routine cannot supply information that has not been given. Clarification addresses the actual need.

**Written evidence:** Make C believable and evidence-based.; Describe B as optional, comfortable and appropriate to a safe pause.; Explain the task information selected in A and the next action.

## Rehearse in a way that works for you
**p4-rehearsal-formats-check-1: C.** The task assesses preparation and reasoning. Another representation can demonstrate those decisions without requiring a private imagery experience.

**p4-rehearsal-formats-check-2: A.** Different perspectives can highlight different information. The learner should justify the choice for the stated purpose.

**Written evidence:** Name the format and purpose.; Explain the relevant cues, perspective or sequence.; State what the rehearsal can and cannot demonstrate.

## Rehearse disruption and recovery
**p4-disruption-rehearsal-check-1: B.** It is a coping-rehearsal metaphor. The useful outcome is a response and return point, not certainty or a required emotional experience.

**p4-disruption-rehearsal-check-2: C.** Changing the representation can preserve the preparation purpose. The learner can explain the sequence without forcing an unhelpful image.

**Written evidence:** Name a plausible interruption and its signal.; Give the response, return cue and relevant timing.; Add a second option and label the rehearsal as hypothetical when appropriate.

## Put the toolkit together
**p4-combine-toolkit-check-1: A.** The parts should support the same demand. A long list of techniques or a claim about feelings does not establish that connection.

**p4-combine-toolkit-check-2: B.** The changed task adds coordination demands. Preserve useful preparation while adapting the cues and recovery plan to those demands.

**Written evidence:** Use one consistent task.; Link evidence, routine and rehearsal decisions.; Name an observation for review and one limitation.

## Written review

**1.** Task confidence can be grounded in evidence of preparation and an action the performer can carry out. Certainty claims the outcome is settled. A prepared performer can act while still uncertain about judging, opponents or conditions.

**2.** Keep the accurate observation and task-based correction. Do not turn one error into a whole-person judgment or deny the error to protect confidence. Clarify advice when needed and seek support for disrespectful treatment.

**3.** Choose the representation and perspective for a purpose. A written, spoken, storyboard or walkthrough format can demonstrate planning. Rehearsal and actual performance provide different evidence; this course does not claim neurological equivalence or measured tissue change per repetition.

**4.** A useful revision may replace a guarantee with preparation evidence, preserve a specific correction, shorten a routine or change a rehearsal format. Explain the task-based reason and identify what remains uncertain.
