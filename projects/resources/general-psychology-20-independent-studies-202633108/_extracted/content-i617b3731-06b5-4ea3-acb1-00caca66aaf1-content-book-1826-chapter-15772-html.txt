<p>Information is thought to be stored permanently - for your entire lifetime. It is now thought possible that some memories may be genetically inherited and therefore last longer than a lifetime.</p>
<p><span>The issue with duration in long-term memory relates to recall and forgetting.</span></p><h5><strong>Encoding in Long Term Memory</strong></h5>
<p><span>Two types of encoding are thought to operate in Long Term Memory</span></p>
<p><strong><img width="420" height="322" style="margin-right: auto; margin-left: auto; display: block;" src="http://www.s-cool.co.uk/assets/learn_its/alevel/psychology/human-memory/long-term-memory/a-psy-mem-dia05.gif"></strong></p>
<div id="cm-001"><em class="red"><strong>Research into semantic encoding in long term memory Baddeley (1966)</strong></em></div><div id="cm-001"><em class="red"><strong><br></strong></em></div>
<p><strong>Baddeley presented participants with four lists to remember:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>List 1:</strong>�man map can cap</li>
<li><strong>List 2:</strong>�try pig hut pen</li>
<li><strong>List 3:</strong>�great big huge wide</li>
<li><strong>List 4:</strong>�run easy tug end</li>
</ul>
<p>Participants had to recall as many words as possible immediately after presentation of lists and then try again 20 minutes later.</p>
<p>Baddeley found that the immediate recall was better for list 2 than for list 1 and with little difference in recall between lists 3 and 4. List 1 contains similar <i>sounding</i> words and list 2 contains <i>non-similar sounding</i> words.�</p><p>When participants were then asked to recall words after twenty minutes they recalled list 4 better than list 3, list 4 contains words with <i>non similar meaning</i> words and list 3 contains words with <i>similar meanings</i>. There was little difference in recall for lists 1 and 2.�</p><p>This shows that the short-term memory tends to store information according to sounds rather than meaning and that the long-term memory tends to store information according to semantics (meaning) rather than simply sound.</p>
<p>Baddeley used a laboratory experiment and can therefore be criticized in terms of ecological validity, demand characteristics, participant variables/individual differences, experimenter bias and representativeness (Baddeley used undergraduate students as participants). Although it has good reliability.</p>
