<p>Multi-store model is basic and limited in explaining such a complex phenomena as memory.</p>
<div>
<h5><b>Working Memory - Baddeley & Hitch (1974)</b></h5>
</div>
<p>An alternative to the Multi-store Model. Emphasises workings of STM. It is a far more complex explanation of STM.</p>
<p>Rather than the STM being a single inflexible store, Baddeley and Hitch suggested that the STM was made up of several subsystems, each having a specialised function.</p>
<p>They suggested that these subsystems were involved in complex cognitions/thought processes, including analysis and judgements about information input.</p>
<p>There is little empirical evidence to support the Working memory Model but the recognition of the complexity of the STM makes sound theoretical sense. However some brain damaged patients appear to suffer impairment to some functions of STM and not others (Shallice & Warrington '1974) therefore suggesting existence of several specialised systems within STM.</p>

<h5><b>Levels of Processing Model - Craik & Lockhart (1972)</b></h5>
</div>
<p>This model of memory concentrates on the LTM and the semantic processing occurring there.</p>
<p>It presents another alternative to the Multi-store model which suggests information is transferred to LTM through rehearsal (repetition).</p>
<p>This model suggests that the depth or level at which we process information determines its place in LTM and also how well we recall that information.</p>
