<p><strong>Confirmation Bias�</strong></p>
<p>Imagine that you have tried to reach a friend (who you already believe is often unreliable) by phone, leaving messages, yet have not received a call in return. In situation like this, it is easy to jump to conclusions in an intuitive manner that your friend wants to avoid you. The danger, of course, is that you leave this belief unchecked and start to act as though it were true.�</p>
<p>Confirmation bias�occurs from the direct influence of desire on beliefs. When people would like a certain idea/concept to be true, they end up believing it to be true. They are motivated by wishful thinking. This error leads the individual to stop gathering information when the evidence gathered so far confirms the views (prejudices) one would like to be true.</p><p><i>Example: You believe that your English teacher hates you. Whenever you get a bad mark on an English assignment you attribute it to the fact that your teacher holds a grudge against you. Each bad mark only reinforces your belief that your teacher doesn't like you. Even when you get a good mark, it doesn't change your existing belief.</i></p>

<p><strong>Availability Heuristic</strong></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><strong><img src="http://jamesclear.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/availability-heuristic.jpg" alt="comic" width="500" height="333" class="img-responsive atto_image_button_text-bottom"></strong></p>
<p>An availability heuristic is a mental shortcut that relies on immediate examples that come to mind. When you are trying to make a decision, a number of related events or situations might immediately spring to the forefront of your thoughts. As a result, you might judge that those events are more frequent and possible than others. You give greater credence to this information and tend to overestimate the probability and likelihood of similar things happening in the future.</p>
<p><i>Example: after seeing several news reports about car thefts, you might make a judgment that vehicle theft is much more common than it really is in your area.�</i></p><p>This type of availability heuristic can be helpful and important in decision-making. When faced with a choice, we often lack the time or resources to investigate in greater depth. Faced with the need to an immediate decision, the availability heuristic allows people to quickly arrive at a conclusion.</p>
