It is commonly thought that reinforcement is a pleasant response and punishment is an unpleasant response to a behavior. <b>THIS IS INCORRECT!!</b></p>
<p>As you have already learned, reinforcement is something that increases a behavior. It may be pleasant or the removal of something unpleasant but in any case it increases a targeted behavior. �By contrast, punishment decreases a targeted behavior. Just like reinforcement, there is <strong>positive punishment</strong> (adding something. i.e. if you don't do your homework I will add more chores) and <strong>negative punishment</strong> (taking something away. e.x. if you don't do your homework you will not get to watch Netflix).�</p>

<p>In general, reinforcing desired behavior is more effective than punishing undesired behavior.�</p>
<ul>
<li>A child is rewarded with a snack for good behavior (reinforce to increase good behavior)</li>
<li>a child is given a time out for bad behavior (Punish to decrease bad behavior)</li>
</ul>
<p>Reinforcement does more to <strong>motivate good behavior in the future</strong> than the fear of punishment does to make the person avoid negative behavior. Therefore, reward is usually a more significant influence in shaping or changing one s behavior. Fear and anxiety generated by punishment usually hamper the learning process.�</p>
<p></p>
<ul>
<li>Punishment stops undesirable behavior, but the behavior may reappear if the punishment ceases. Therefore, punishment is not a permanent solution for improving behavior.</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li class="_mce_tagged_br">Punishment may make a person or animal so fearful that they make even more mistakes than usual.</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li class="_mce_tagged_br">Punishment may lead to behavior that is less predictable than behavior that comes from rewarding desirable responses.</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li>Punishment tells the individual what not to do; it does not indicate what the person should do.�</li>
</ul>
