<p>This may be a�<strong>membership group</strong>�(family or peers), or it may be a�<strong>reference group</strong>�(pop or sports stars). This group can be either a majority�<strong>or</strong>�a minority group.</p>
<div class="subsection">
<h4><a id="why-people-conform"></a>Why people conform</h4>
</div>
<p>Two main processes have been identified in causing people to conform:</p>
<h5><span class="red"><strong style=""><span class="" style="color: rgb(0, 0, 0);">Normative influence:</span></strong></span></h5>
<p>This type of influence often comes from <b>peer pressure</b>. Reasons include; fear of rejection, wanting approval, simply to be identified as part of a group and to show loyalty.</p>
<p><span><i>How often do people look at their friends when they vote for something with their hands up?</i></span></p>
<div id="cm-001" style="text-align: center;"><span class="red"><strong style=""><img src="http://www.s-cool.co.uk/assets/learn_its/alevel/psychology/social-influence/conformity/a-psy-socinf-dia01.gif" width="366" height="209"></strong></span></div>
<h5><span class="red"><strong style=""><span class="" style="color: rgb(0, 0, 0);">Informational influence:</span></strong></span></h5>
<p>This type of influence is concerned with the <b>fear of looking unintelligent</b> and believing others know better, especially if the task is difficult or unfamiliar.</p>
<p><span><i style="">Would you trust someone else's answer more than your own when you haven't revised very hard for a test?</i></span></p>
<div id="cm-002" style="text-align: center;"><img src="http://www.s-cool.co.uk/assets/learn_its/alevel/psychology/social-influence/conformity/a-psy-socinf-dia02.gif" width="407" height="193"></div>
<div><br></div><h5><b>Minority influence:</b></h5>
<p>A minority group can also strongly influence an individual, this is more likely when the minority are the 'in group', possibly due to social status or age, or they are acting out of principle and have made personal sacrifices in the pursuit of their beliefs.</p>
<p><strong>Moscovici</strong>�designed an experiment to investigate the influence of a "<strong>stooge minority</strong>", that is a group of people who have been 'planted' in the experiment. The stooges will already have been told what to say by the researcher.</p>
<p>In this experiment, volunteers had to judge the color of slides that were blue-green. If the minority were�<strong>consistent</strong>, for example, saying "green" all the time, they had a definite influence in changing the opinions of others in the group.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img src="..\image_chapter_15844.jpeg" alt="Image result for Moscovici designed" width="401" height="300" class="img-responsive atto_image_button_text-bottom"><br></p>
