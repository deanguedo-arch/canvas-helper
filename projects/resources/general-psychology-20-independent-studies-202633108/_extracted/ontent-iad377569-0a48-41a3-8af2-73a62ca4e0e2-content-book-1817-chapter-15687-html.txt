<p>In 1879 at the University of Leipzig in Germany, an important development occurred which gave psychology a new beginning when Wilhelm Wundt decided it was time to set up the first real psychological <strong>laboratory</strong> so that many of the thought-provoking ideas that had been debated for years could actually be tested.<img src="http://image.slidesharecdn.com/grouppresentation-101003122208-phpapp01/95/psychologists-32-728.jpg?cb=1286108636" width="428" height="321" style="display: block; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"></p>
<p></p>
<p>Moving psychology into the category of<strong> experimental science</strong> was a very significant step in promoting psychology s acceptance in the academic community. Establishing a laboratory to test data <b>l</b><strong>ed to a major shift; psychology moved from being a collection of philosophical ideas to being an area for experimentation and research.</strong></p>
<p></p>
<p>Wundt was interested in examining the relationship between sensations that people experiences (like warm or cold temperatures, or bright or dim light) and the�<a href="http://moodle.eipsnextstep.ca/mod/glossary/showentry.php?courseid=330&eid=158&displayformat=dictionary" title="Globalization 10-1/10-2 Glossary: effect" class="glossary autolink concept glossaryid64">effect</a>�these factors have on judgments that people make. Sensations are external factors from one s surroundings; feelings are personal or inner reactions to one s surroundings. Data on the ways in which senses operate was collected, tested, and published. Wundt believed that the conscious mind brings together the sensations a person experiences with the feelings that are aroused by those experiences.</p>
<p>Wundt s psychological laboratory investigated people s sensations, feelings, attention (awareness of an event), perceptions (the data one absorbs), reaction times, and associations (grouping similar factors together). Although Wundt s laboratory testing was simple and sometimes lacked thoroughness, it did succeed in giving psychology scientific acceptance and credibility.</p><p style="text-align: center;"><img src="http://workabletechnology.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/credibility.png" alt="" width="499" height="320" role="presentation" class="img-responsive atto_image_button_text-bottom"></p>
