BIO 30 · Unit A

How does the body stay stable while the world keeps changing?

Your nervous and endocrine systems detect change, exchange information, and coordinate responses. In this unit, you will connect ion movement in a single neuron to whole-body regulation of sensation, stress, water, salts, metabolism, and blood glucose.

Learning intention

I am learning to explain how the nervous and endocrine systems detect change, communicate information, and coordinate responses that maintain homeostasis.

Success criteria

  • I can trace information through neurons, synapses, nervous-system pathways, and sensory systems.
  • I can explain how hormones, target cells, and negative-feedback loops regulate internal conditions.
  • I can interpret models and data, design safe investigations, and use evidence to revise a scientific explanation.
  • I can evaluate how drugs, disorders, and technologies affect regulation while stating evidence limits.
  • I can connect nervous and endocrine control in an unfamiliar whole-body regulation case.

The learning journey

You will begin with homeostasis, then move from cellular communication to organs and integrated control. Each lesson follows the same rhythm: observe a phenomenon, build a scientific explanation, test the explanation with a model or dataset, receive feedback, and save a short piece of evidence.

  1. Systems and signalsHomeostasis, nervous control, endocrine control, and negative feedback
  2. Neural communicationNeurons, membrane voltage, action potentials, synapses, drugs, and toxins
  3. Nervous controlBrain organization, reflex arcs, response evidence, and experimental design
  4. Sensory systemsReceptors, vision, hearing, equilibrium, photoperiod, and sensory technologies
  5. Endocrine controlHormone pathways, feedback, glands, imbalances, stress, water, salts, and metabolism
  6. IntegrationA novel case that combines neural, endocrine, glucose, and osmolality evidence

What you will make

Your Investigation Notebook gathers seven portfolio artifacts. These include models, graph interpretations, investigation evidence, and a final integrated case. Your drafts are saved as you work, and you can print or save each artifact as a PDF when it is ready to submit.

Practice helps you check and improve your understanding. Use the immediate feedback to identify the reasoning behind an answer, revise your thinking, and try again.

Safety and health information

Investigations use safe, low-material procedures and always include supplied-data alternatives. Classroom models and datasets are educational, not diagnostic. Never change medication or health decisions based on a course activity; use a qualified health-care professional for personal concerns.
