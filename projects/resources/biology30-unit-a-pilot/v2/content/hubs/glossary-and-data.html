A reliable data-reading routine

  1. Read the axes, units, and conditions. Identify what was measured and when.
  2. Describe before explaining. Name the baseline, direction, magnitude, timing, and any overlap.
  3. Connect a mechanism. Use a receptor, neural route, hormone, target, or feedback effect that predicts the pattern.
  4. Test an alternative. State another explanation and the evidence that would distinguish it.
  5. Bound the claim. Label synthetic data, design limits, and what the evidence cannot diagnose.
