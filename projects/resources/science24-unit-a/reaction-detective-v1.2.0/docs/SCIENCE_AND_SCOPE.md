# Science, assessment scope and sources — A1 v1.2.0

## What this game prepares

A1 is deliberately a focused evidence/reasoning activity. It retains one worked example, four practice cases and one new transfer task. It is not all eight games, a full Unit A course or an official examination.

The curriculum source consulted is Alberta's published **Science 14–24 Program of Studies**, Science 24 Unit A, printed/PDF pp. 31–33 [S1]. The labels W01/P01–P04/T01 are our case identifiers, not official outcome codes.

| Targeted portion of the program | Where practised | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| General 2: evidence and reactant/product comparison, p.31 | All cases | Structured selection plus ungraded explanation |
| General 2: energy interpretation, p.31 | W01; P01 contrast | Worked/supported, not an independent energy assessment |
| General 2: reaction classification, pp.31–32 | W01 decomposition; P02 corrosion; P03 neutralization; P04 combustion | Decomposition is demonstrated, not independently tested |
| General 4: antacid context, p.32 | P03 | Model classification, not practical dosing |
| General 4: corrosion protection, p.32 | P02 | Required barrier decision |
| General 4: combustion emissions, p.32 | P04 | One product connection, not complete pollution coverage |
| Interpretation/communication skills, p.33 | Claim–evidence–reasoning, revisions, T01 | Authored records, not a performed experiment |

General 1's broader materials/history and General 3's equation-balancing/experimental demands are not covered. Nor are all General 4 emission-control technologies. Current lesson placement and actual teacher exam items were not supplied as a live course source. Those need separate alignment review; task completion cannot establish complete curriculum attainment. [S1]

## From game task to assessment task

| Game interaction | Conventional task being rehearsed | Evidence retained |
|---|---|---|
| Predict before opening records | State a provisional interpretation | Frozen prediction, not scored |
| Choose informative records | Select relevant observations and identify limits | Exact selected records and seen history |
| Compare substance identities | Distinguish physical/chemical change using given information | Structured claim and explanation |
| Choose a reaction label | Infer a reaction pattern from reactants/products | Checked label in practice |
| Choose a response in P02/P04 | Apply the concept in a practical or environmental context | Checked response plus prose |
| Revise using targeted feedback | Identify why an initial answer is inadequate | Immutable complete attempts and support counts |
| T01 without the investigation board | Read an unfamiliar written record/table and justify a classification | First complete response separately from retries |

The first T01 answer is a useful, narrow independent-performance observation. It is **not** enough to infer general mastery of reaction types, all exam question formats or long-term retention. The six-case package deliberately does not invent such a claim.

## Scientific safeguards by case

### W01 — hydrogen peroxide

The supplied before/after identities support hydrogen peroxide decomposition into water and oxygen [S2]. A catalyst is not consumed in the overall reaction. The warming statement is bounded to the explicit no-external-heating setup; it is consistent with energy release, not proof that every decomposition is exothermic. Identity is stronger than bubbling alone. The displayed word equation is HTML. No student mixing/procedure is specified.

### P01 — cooling solution

This is an authored **identity-accounting model**, not a claim that every solid dissolving in water is a simple physical process. Its decisive record explicitly reports no new substances and matching recovered material. Temperature change, recovery without identification, rate differences and unchanged pH alone are insufficient for that conclusion. Physical change does not change chemical identity [S3]. The justified uncertainty branch is retained until the decisive identity record is encountered.

### P02 — corrosion

The analysis establishes a changed coating containing iron and oxygen. Oxygen/moisture are relevant to iron corrosion, and a suitable barrier can reduce contact [S4]. The game does not give rust a falsely universal pure formula. Its mass observation explicitly weighs the tool with all attached coating, without flaking loss. The application is a conceptual protection choice, not an industrial maintenance certification.

The deterministic accepted label is corrosion. An ungraded written answer cannot automatically qualify a more nuanced “composition” alternative, so that unconditional path was removed. A teacher may still consider a defensible overlapping classification outside the game's checked label.

### P03 — acid–carbonate antacid model

The source reaction relationship is calcium carbonate with acid forming a calcium salt, carbon dioxide and water [S5]. The game joins the specific acid–carbonate pattern to the course's neutralization context in one answer label. Its wording does not teach that every neutralization produces gas. It contains no medication recommendation, real dose, experiment steps or handling instructions. Product identities, not bubbles alone, establish the chemical-change claim.

### P04 — complete combustion

The model supplies methane and oxygen at the inlet and identified carbon dioxide/water after complete combustion [S6]. Both inlet and exhaust records are required, so merely selecting a hot surface or an energy observation cannot substitute for substance accounting. The game does not pretend all real furnaces always have only these products.

The environmental question was corrected to ask for a **carbon-containing product**. CO₂ fits; water vapour is also a greenhouse gas but has no carbon [S7]. Methane is identified as an inlet reactant in this record. This is one emissions connection, not a complete lesson about climate processes, incomplete combustion or pollution-control technology.

### T01 — boiling-water transfer

The written identity record remains H₂O before heating and after condensation. The supported interpretation is physical change in this supplied record [S3]. The new task deliberately removes art, selectable-record guidance, tokens, hints and early exemplars. Students still need to explain the connection rather than merely recall a “bubbles = chemical” shortcut.

## Written-explanation review

The runtime checks **presence**, not correctness, of the prose. It has no keyword or AI reasoning score. After a supported structured answer, it provides an exemplar and four self-review statements. The UI and review explicitly distinguish this self-check from teacher grading.

Teacher review should examine: (1) a defensible claim, (2) relevant identities/observations, (3) a causal or evidential link rather than repetition, and (4) appropriate uncertainty/model limits. Do not report “scientifically valid explanation” solely because the student checked four boxes.

## Source and integration limits

No source validates these unpiloted games' educational effectiveness. Numbers and observations are original fictional teaching records, not real experimental measurements. The canonical screenshot supplies appearance only: its photographed/generated text, chemistry choices and sample values are not the answer source for v1.2.0. The historical handoff's curriculum and mockup claims are superseded by this narrower, explicit coverage record where they conflict.
