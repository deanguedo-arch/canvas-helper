## W01 — Peroxide Record — WORKED

**Purpose:** Model the full reasoning process.

**Record:** A supplied virtual laboratory record identifies hydrogen peroxide before the reaction. After the change, water and oxygen are identified. The mixture warms while no external heating is applied. A catalyst is unchanged after the reaction.

**Correct conclusion:** Chemical change.  
**Reaction classification:** Decomposition.  
**Strongest evidence:** New substance identities — water and oxygen are present after the change.  
**Supporting evidence:** Temperature rises in the stated setup; gas is observed.  
**Misconception to teach:** Bubbles alone are not proof of chemical reaction.

**Model explanation:** The substance identity changes from hydrogen peroxide to water and oxygen. That product evidence supports a chemical change, and one compound forming simpler products is a decomposition reaction. The warming is consistent with energy being released to the surroundings in this supplied record.

**Boundary:** Virtual record only; not a procedure.

## P01 — Cooling Solution

**Purpose:** Evidence sufficiency; temperature change and dissolving are not automatically chemical change.

**Base observation:** A white crystalline solid disappears into water and the container cools from 22 °C to 18 °C. A clear solution remains.

**Optional evidence choices:**
1. Recover the solid after water is removed.
2. Compare recovered solid with the original identity record.
3. Measure pH.
4. Repeat at a different water temperature.

**Authoritative revealed record for options 1–2:** The recovered solid is identified as the same substance as the starting solid.

**Initial prediction:** “Not enough evidence” is defensible before identity/recovery evidence is revealed.  
**Final conclusion after identity/recovery evidence:** Physical change.

**Strongest evidence:** Same substance recovered; no new substance identity established.

**Misconceptions:**
- “It cooled, so it must be chemical.”
- “The solid disappeared, so it became a new substance.”

**Feedback:** Temperature change can occur during a physical process. The strongest record here is that the same substance can be recovered unchanged.

## P02 — Rusting Tool

**Purpose:** Corrosion/rusting, new substance evidence, mass from surroundings.

**Record:** An iron-containing tool is exposed to air and moisture over time. A reddish-brown corrosion coating develops. A supplied analysis identifies the coating as iron-containing oxide/hydroxide corrosion products, different from the original metallic iron. The final mass is slightly greater than the initial mass.

**Correct conclusion:** Chemical change.  
**Accepted classification:** Rusting / corrosion. “Composition reaction” may be accepted only when the learner clearly describes formation of a new iron–oxygen compound from simpler reactants; do not require this label.

**Strongest evidence:** New corrosion product identity.  
**Supporting evidence:** Mass increase is consistent with oxygen from the surroundings becoming part of the product.

**Important science wording:** Do not simplify rust to a single exact formula such as Fe₂O₃ in production copy. Rust can contain mixtures of iron oxides/hydroxides.

**Practical response:** A barrier coating can reduce contact with air/moisture and help limit corrosion.

## P03 — Antacid Record

**Purpose:** Everyday neutralization; separate superficial signs from product/reactant evidence.

**Record:** In a virtual model, a calcium-carbonate antacid is placed in a model acidic solution. The pH moves toward neutral. Gas is produced. The supplied product record identifies carbon dioxide, water and a dissolved calcium salt.

**Correct conclusion:** Chemical change.  
**Accepted classification:** Acid–base neutralization / acid–carbonate reaction.  
**Strongest evidence:** Identified products differ from reactants.  
**Supporting evidence:** pH moves toward neutral; gas forms.

**Misconception:** “Bubbles prove neutralization.” Bubbles are supporting evidence, not enough by themselves to identify the reaction.

**Boundary:** This is a model record, not health or dosing advice and not a student procedure.

## P04 — Furnace Combustion

**Purpose:** Combustion classification and energy evidence in an everyday technology.

**Record:** A fictional natural-gas furnace model supplies methane and oxygen to a controlled burner. The supplied exhaust record identifies carbon dioxide and water. Heat and visible light are released.

**Correct conclusion:** Chemical change.  
**Reaction classification:** Combustion.  
**Strongest evidence:** Reactants/products are consistent with methane combustion: methane + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.  
**Supporting evidence:** Heat/light released.

**Environmental connection:** Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. This single case is not full coverage of combustion pollution or emissions technology.

**Boundary:** No instructions for igniting fuel or adjusting a real furnace.

## T01 — Boiling Water — INDEPENDENT TRANSFER

**Scenario:** Water is heated until bubbles form and water vapour is produced. The vapour is cooled and condenses back to liquid water. The supplied record identifies the substance before heating and after condensation as water (H₂O).

**Prompt:** Do the bubbles prove a chemical reaction took place?

**Correct conclusion:** No. This is a physical change / change of state in the supplied record.

**Required reasoning:** Bubbles are an observation, but the substance remains water. No new substance identity is established.

**No hints before first submission.**
