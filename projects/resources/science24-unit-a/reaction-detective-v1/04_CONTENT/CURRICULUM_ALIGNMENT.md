## Relevant curriculum demand

The official Unit A key concepts include:
- chemical change in everyday life;
- evidence of chemical change;
- combustion and rusting;
- acid–base reactions;
- simple composition and decomposition;
- chemical reactions at home and in the workplace;
- environmental effects of chemical-change technologies.

A1 most directly practises General Outcome 2, which asks students to **investigate and classify chemical reactions**. Relevant specific outcomes include investigating evidence of chemical change; describing and comparing changes to reactants/products in combustion and rusting; defining endothermic/exothermic reactions operationally; investigating simple composition/decomposition; describing reactant/product properties; and identifying simple composition, decomposition, combustion and neutralization reactions from supplied information.

The game also touches General Outcome 1 through everyday/workplace contexts and parts of General Outcome 4 through antacid, corrosion and combustion contexts.

## What this game does not claim

A1 does **not** by itself cover all of Unit A. It does not replace:
- full compound naming/formula work;
- balancing equations and conservation of mass (A2 is the stronger game for that);
- the full environmental-technology analysis of Outcome 4;
- laboratory skill assessment;
- WHMIS instruction;
- all required word/chemical equation practice.

## Exam-preparation intent

The game deliberately rehearses assessment actions rather than only content recognition:
- read a scenario;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- select relevant evidence;
- classify the change/reaction;
- reject insufficient evidence;
- justify the conclusion in words;
- transfer the idea into a less scaffolded prompt.
