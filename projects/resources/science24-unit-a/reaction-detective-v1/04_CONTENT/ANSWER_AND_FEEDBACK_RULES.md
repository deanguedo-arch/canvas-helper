# Answer and Feedback Rules — A1 v1.0

## 1. Structured answer checking

The game may deterministically check:
- selected conclusion;
- selected reaction type;
- selected evidence IDs;
- selected practical response when one is authored.

The game must not use a keyword count to decide whether a written explanation is scientifically good.

## 2. Evidence quality levels

Each evidence record has one of three roles:

- **strong** — directly establishes substance identity / product relationship relevant to the claim;
- **supporting** — consistent with the claim but not conclusive alone;
- **weak_or_irrelevant** — true observation, but insufficient for the specific claim or reaction classification.

A strong response generally selects at least one strong record. Where two strong records exist, selecting either can be accepted unless the scenario explicitly requires two.

## 3. General misconception feedback

### Bubbles-only error
“Bubbles are an observation. Which record identifies a new substance or shows that the substance changed identity?”

### Temperature-only error
“A temperature change can occur in physical and chemical processes. What evidence tells you whether a new substance formed?”

### Colour/appearance-only error
“Appearance can be useful evidence, but it does not automatically identify a new substance. Look for an identity or product record.”

### Overclaim from incomplete evidence
“The current observations do not establish that conclusion. ‘Not enough evidence’ is a valid scientific answer when the available record cannot distinguish the possibilities.”

### Correct conclusion, weak evidence
“Your conclusion may be defensible, but your selected evidence does not strongly establish it. Which record is most directly connected to substance identity?”

### Correct evidence, wrong reaction type
“You found evidence of chemical change. Now compare the stated reactants and products with the definitions of the reaction types.”

## 4. Hint ladder

### Hint 1 — orient
Point to what kind of evidence matters; do not reveal the answer.

### Hint 2 — narrow
Identify the relevant evidence category or eliminate one misconception.

### Hint 3 — near-complete support
Point to the specific record that resolves the question, but still require the learner to select/construct the conclusion.

## 5. Explanation rubric shown after structured submission

Learner self-check:

- **Claim:** I clearly say what type of change/reaction occurred.
- **Evidence:** I name specific evidence from the supplied record.
- **Reasoning:** I explain how that evidence supports the claim.
- **Limits:** I do not claim more than the evidence establishes.

For the independent transfer, all four are shown after submission. Completion is recorded separately from explanation quality.

## 6. Retry behavior

- Keep the learner’s previous structured choices visible.
- Keep their explanation text in the current session.
- Highlight the specific reasoning issue.
- Do not erase the attempt.
- Allow unlimited retries.
- Do not reduce a numeric score because of retries; v1 has no numeric score.
