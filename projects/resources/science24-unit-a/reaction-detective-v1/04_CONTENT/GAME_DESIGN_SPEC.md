# Game Design Specification — A1 Reaction Detective v1.0

## Game identity

**ID:** A1  
**Title:** Reaction Detective  
**Unit:** Science 24 Unit A — Applications of Matter and Chemical Change  
**Primary learning action:** Use evidence to explain a change.

## Learning objective

Students use observations and authored records to determine whether evidence supports a physical change, chemical change, a specific reaction classification, or an insufficient-evidence conclusion. They then justify the claim using the strongest relevant evidence.

## Prerequisites

Students should already have encountered:
- reactant and product;
- physical vs chemical change;
- system and surroundings;
- basic names/formulas used in the supplied cases.

The game may refresh these ideas, but it is not the first full lesson on every term.

## Primary mechanic

**Evidence investigation.** Learners cannot win by clicking the visually obvious item. They must decide which evidence actually bears on the claim.

### Actions available
- inspect base observation;
- make an initial prediction;
- reveal up to two optional evidence records;
- select up to two evidence records as strongest support;
- choose a conclusion;
- select reaction type when the case supports one;
- enter a short explanation;
- submit;
- request one hint at a time after an attempt or when allowed;
- revise and resubmit;
- reset case.

## No score economy

Do not add:
- coins;
- XP;
- leaderboards;
- streak pressure;
- timers;
- reflex challenges;
- avatar upgrades.

Case completion and support use are enough for v1.

## Progression

| Stage | Case | Main demand | Scaffold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worked | W01 Peroxide Record | See complete evidence reasoning | Full modelling |
| Practice 1 | P01 Cooling Solution | Evidence sufficiency; physical vs chemical | High |
| Practice 2 | P02 Rusting Tool | New substance + corrosion evidence | Medium |
| Practice 3 | P03 Antacid Record | Neutralization; products vs superficial clues | Medium-low |
| Practice 4 | P04 Furnace Combustion | Reaction classification + energy evidence | Low |
| Transfer | T01 Boiling Water | Reject bubbles-as-proof; explain phase change | No hints before submit |

## Success evidence

A student product consists of:
- valid structured conclusion;
- relevant selected evidence;
- reaction classification when required;
- submitted explanation;
- fresh transfer response.

Completion does not equal mastery. Open explanation quality is not established by keyword matching.

## Visual structure

Default desktop layout uses three zones:
1. The Case
2. The Evidence / Investigation
3. Your Reasoning

On narrow screens, stack in that same order. The bottom strip holds feedback, key idea, stage status and primary navigation.

## Generated-art boundary

Generated images establish context only. They can show:
- a lab bench;
- rusted metal;
- a model antacid reaction;
- a furnace flame;
- boiling water.

Do not rely on generated art for:
- chemical formulas;
- temperatures;
- pH values;
- substance identities;
- reaction equations;
- answer choices;
- evidence wording.
