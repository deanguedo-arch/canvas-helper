              <h1>CTS Program and Pathway Planning for Teachers</h1>
              <p>&nbsp;</p>
<div class="accordion-container"> 
     <h2 class="accordion-header">Considerations for CTS Program and Pathway Planning</h2> 
     <div class="accordion-content">
     <p>While schools are encouraged to consider flexible methods of planning and delivering CTS courses that best meet the needs of students, care is needed when planning to ensure that</p>
<ul>
<li>all the prerequisites and parameters of CTS courses are followed<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li> all the general and specific outcomes of CTS courses are delivered and assessed<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>the student’s right to access a minimum of 25 hours of instruction per course is observed<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>credits are not awarded twice for the same learning<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>students are apprised of their registration in CTS courses and that their right to choose CTS courses is maintained<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>teachers who provide or supervise the instruction have the appropriate certification and are knowledgeable about the CTS course<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>there are designated times when teachers are available to students<br>
  <br>
</li>
<li>students know, prior to enrolling in CTS courses, how and when they will be able to access the instructional expertise of teachers</li></ul>
</div>

<h2 class="accordion-header">Involving Students in Pathway Planning</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p>CTS pathways are designed at the school and/or school authority level by combining 1-credit CTS courses into pathways that best suit the needs of the students, school, school authority, and community. Schools determine which CTS courses and pathways to make available to students, the extent to which students are involved in planning their own CTS pathways, and whether courses will be teacher-directed or student-managed using multi-activity learning. CTS pathway and program design and CTS
course selection must address prerequisites and other delivery  equirements defined in the CTS course parameters.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Teachers and counsellors should provide students with guidance and support during their individual pathway planning process. The <a href="https://curriculum.learnalberta.ca/curriculum/en/area/CTS?id=5d46bd31-6360-407b-829d-1398742badb3" target="_blank">Alberta Education: Program Supports</a> can help organize student pathway planning and provide a record for regular reflection and revision.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Teachers plan for instruction to ensure that students meet all the learner outcomes defined in the program
of studies by the end of the school year. In CTS, planning for instruction is focused on CTS course selections that lead to a student’s pathway of choice and deliberate planning to ensure that all learner
outcomes for each course are met.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p> 
<div class="screen">For more information about planning for instruction, refer to the <em><a href="https://www.alberta.ca/guide-to-education.aspx" target="_blank">Guide to Education: ECS to Grade 12</a></em>.
For more information on career planning, see <a href="imp_teach_car.html" target="_blank">Career Planning with Students</a>.
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="accordion-header">CTS Prerequisites</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p><strong>Prerequisite</strong> courses have been identified as essential to build on further learning in a specific area, to
  cover content required for an external credential, and/or for safety reasons. Students will benefit from the
  learning covered in prerequisite courses, and every effort should be made to ensure students acquire the
  necessary prerequisites as specified for their CTS programming.  </p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>To support the transition from existing CTS courses to new and revised CTS courses, documents entitled
  <em>from Existing to New</em> are available for each occupational area to indicate course correlations. Where applicable,
  current prerequisite courses will be aligned with new courses and accepted as prerequisites (as indicated).  </p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Alternative delivery strategies may include concurrent delivery of CTS courses over the course of a term
  or semester. Teachers must ensure that all outcomes for prerequisite courses are successfully completed
  by the end of the term or semester.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div class="screen">To review the <em>from Existing to New</em> documents, visit the <a href="https://curriculum.learnalberta.ca/curriculum/en/area/CTS" target="_blank">Alberta Education: Career and Technology Studies</a> website. Each document is found with its occupational area. For example, you can find <em>REC Courses - from Existing to New</em> by first clicking on the link for HRH: Health, Recreation & Human Services and then by opening the REC document folder.</div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>A CTS course prerequisite may be waived by the school principal when a student can demonstrate competency in prerequisite CTS course outcomes, attitudes, skills, knowledge, and values. Funding will not be provided for a CTS course if the prerequisite(s) was not completed and/or waived in the same term or a prior term.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div class="screen">For more information on waiving prerequisite courses for CTS, refer to the <em><a href="https://www.alberta.ca/guide-to-education.aspx" target="_blank">Guide to Education: ECS to Grade 12</a></em>.</div>
	</div>

<h2 class="accordion-header">Course Challenges in CTS</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p>Course challenge is a provision that allows a senior high school student to challenge, for credit, the outcomes of a CTS course. This may be appropriate for a student who, as a result of prior learning, can demonstrate the ability to meet the assessment standards established for specific CTS courses. Course challenge cannot be applied to dual credit CTS courses.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>Course assessment may occur through:</p>
<ul>
  <li>a traditional comprehensive examination</li>
  <li>teacher observation over several classes</li>
  <li>teacher evaluation of a student’s portfolio or work sample</li>
  <li>student demonstration of skills through the performance of set tasks</li>
  <li>external credentialing that acknowledges successful completion of course outcomes</li>
</ul>
<div class="screen">For more information on challenge courses for CTS, refer to the <em><a href="https://www.alberta.ca/guide-to-education.aspx" target="_blank">Guide to Education: ECS to Grade 12</a></em>.</div>
</div>

<h2 class="accordion-header">Project Courses</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p>A CTS project course provides students with an opportunity to develop project design and management
skills to extend and enhance competencies and skills gained in other CTS courses through contexts
that are personally relevant. There are five CTS project courses available in each occupational area:
one introductory project course, two intermediate project courses, and two advanced project courses.
Additionally, CTS project courses</p>
<ul>
  <li>must connect with a minimum of two successfully completed CTS courses, one of which must be at
    the same level and be in the same occupational area as the CTS project<br>
    &ndash; at the <strong>introductory</strong> level, the other CTS course(s) can be either at the same level or at the
    intermediate level from any occupational area<br>
    &ndash; at the <strong>intermediate</strong> level, the other CTS course(s) can be at any level from any occupational area <br>
    &ndash; at the <strong>advanced</strong> level, the other CTS course(s) can be either at the same level or at the
    intermediate level from any occupational area<br><br></li>
  <li>may not be delivered as stand-alone courses<br><br></li>
  <li>may not be combined with core courses or connected to other CTS project courses or CTS practicum courses<br><br></li>
  <li>must include a course outline or student proposal for projects and/or performances that are teacher-led or student-led</li></ul>
  
<div class="screen">For more information about project courses, see <a href="imp_teach_faqs.html" target="_blank">CTS Project Course FAQs</a> and <a href="https://curriculum.learnalberta.ca/curriculum/en/area/CTS?id=5d46bd31-6360-407b-829d-1398742badb3" target="_blank">Alberta Education: Program Supports</a>.</div>
	</div>
 
<h2 class="accordion-header">Practicum Courses</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p>A CTS practicum course enables students to apply prior learning and demonstrate the attitudes, skills,
  knowledge, and values required by an external organization to achieve a credential, articulation, or credit.
  There are two CTS practicum courses available in each occupational area: one intermediate practicum
  course and one advanced practicum course. CTS practicum courses</p>
<ul>
  <li>may be delivered on- or off-campus<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>may be accessed by students continuing to work toward attaining a recognized credential, articulation,
    or credit awarded by an external organization <br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>must be connected to at least one successfully completed CTS course from the same occupational
    area <br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>cannot be delivered as a stand-alone course or combined with a CTS project course<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>cannot be used in conjunction with the Registered Apprenticeship Program or the Green Certificate
    Program<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>cannot, at the intermediate level, be used in conjunction with any advanced (3000-) level course<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>cannot, at the advanced level, be used in conjunction with any introductory (1000-) level course</li>
</ul>
<div class="screen">For more information about practicum courses, see <a href="https://curriculum.learnalberta.ca/curriculum/en/area/CTS?id=5d46bd31-6360-407b-829d-1398742badb3" target="_blank">Alberta Education: Program Supports</a>.</div>
	</div>
    
<h2 class="accordion-header">Students Enrolled in Knowledge and Employability (K&amp;E) and CTS</h2> 
	<div class="accordion-content"> 
	<p>K&amp;E occupational courses are designed for students who need unique learning support systems; e.g.,
  hands-on learning experiences and remediation in language arts, mathematics, science, and social studies.  </p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>In general, the K&amp;E learning environment may be characterized by:  </p>
<ul>
  <li>smaller class sizes, allowing teachers to provide students with individual assistance in building
    self-esteem and developing specific competencies<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>hands-on learning, with minimal reference to occupation-related theory<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>the recognition of students’ ability to demonstrate practical skills necessary for success in the specified occupational area<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>the awarding of grades based on generic skills development, rather than for the
    equivalent career-specific competencies in CTS courses </li>
</ul>
<p>Both the K&amp;E occupational courses and the CTS program are designed to </p>
<ul>
  <li>involve students in hands-on learning experiences <br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>assist students in the development of occupational competencies within a career-related context that
    will assist them in their transition into the workplace<br>
    <br>
  </li>
  <li>provide opportunities for students to explore a variety of career pathways</li>
</ul>
<div class="screen">For more information about K&amp;E, see <em><a href="../ke/ke.html" target="_blank">Knowledge and Employability&ndash;Career and Technology Studies (CTS) CONNECTIONS</a></em>.</div>
	</div>
