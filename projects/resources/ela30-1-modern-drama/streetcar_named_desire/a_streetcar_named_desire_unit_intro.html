Introduction to A Streetcar Named Desire

Pictured above is the set model, designed by Michael Yeargan, for the world premiere performances of Andre Previn's opera A Streetcar Named Desire. Photo: Terrence McCarthy; Courtesy: San Francisco Opera

It will be necessary for you to purchase a copy of the play A Streetcar Named Desire by Tennessee Williams from a bookstore, or you may find the play at your local library.  If you are unable to locate a copy, please email your instructor for assistance.

Williams' play explores many of the playwright's personal struggles in a society which he felt was often repressive and narrow-minded.  Williams said that the belief which inspired and dominated his writing was the "need for understanding and tenderness among individuals trapped by circumstance."

Before you begin to read the play, research the playwright Tennessee Williams. Biographical information about the playwright may help you enjoy the play more and to be more aware of some of the issues the writer is exploring. Every writer is a part of the world in which he lives. Therefore, as you study any piece of literature, consider how it reflects the personal experiences of the author. Remember that writers frequently explore issues in their own lives through their writing.
