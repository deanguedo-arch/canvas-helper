Sketchbook Activity 1: Observing Landscape Shapes
Begin by looking at a landscape image or observing a natural scene outside. In your sketchbook, lightly sketch the horizon line that separates land from sky. Next, identify the large shapes in the landscape. Instead of drawing small details, focus on the biggest forms you see, such as hills, groups of trees, shorelines, or rock formations.
Think about how the artists of the Group of Seven simplified landscapes into bold shapes. Your drawing should focus on these larger forms rather than small textures or details.
Sketchbook Activity 2: Foreground, Middle Ground, Background
On the next page of your sketchbook, create a new landscape drawing that clearly shows three areas: the foreground, the middle ground, and the background. Begin by sketching a strong object in the foreground such as a tree, rock, or branch. Next, add shapes in the middle ground that lead the viewer’s eye deeper into the scene. Finally, create a background with distant hills, mountains, or sky.
As you draw, think about how the Group of Seven organized their landscapes to guide the viewer’s eye through the artwork.
Sketchbook Activity 3: Drawing Trees the Group of Seven Way
Trees were a common subject in many Group of Seven paintings. Instead of drawing every individual leaf, the artists often simplified trees into strong shapes with curved trunks and clusters of foliage.
In your sketchbook, draw three different tree studies inspired by this style. Focus on the shape of the trunk, the movement of the branches, and the overall form of the tree. Try exaggerating the curves or angles to give the tree a sense of movement and energy.
Sketchbook Activity 4: Landscape Inspired by the Group of Seven
Using the ideas you practiced in the earlier exercises, create a final landscape drawing in your sketchbook inspired by the style of the Group of Seven. Your drawing should include a clear foreground, middle ground, and background, and it should focus on bold shapes rather than small details.
Think about how you can simplify forms such as trees, hills, and clouds to make the landscape feel strong and expressive.
Reflection
After completing your sketches, write a short reflection in your sketchbook. Consider how simplifying shapes helped you draw the landscape more easily. Think about how the foreground, middle ground, and background influenced the composition of your drawing. Reflect on how the artists of the Group of Seven used exaggeration and strong shapes to communicate the feeling of the Canadian wilderness.
