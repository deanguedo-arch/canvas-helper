Sketchbook Tasks
Page 1 — Identity Map
Begin by creating an identity map in your sketchbook. Write the word “ME” in the center of the page. Around it, add words, quick sketches, or objects that represent important aspects of your life.
You might include things that matter to you, places that feel important, hobbies you enjoy, challenges you have faced, or objects that remind you of meaningful memories.
This page does not need to look neat or organized. The purpose is to generate ideas that might become symbols in your artwork.
Page 2 — Symbol Exploration
Choose three ideas from your identity map that feel meaningful to you. For each idea, draw one or two possible symbolic images that could represent that idea.
These sketches should remain simple and exploratory. For example, if one idea involves perseverance, you might draw a plant growing through cracked pavement or a mountain climber reaching a peak.
Write a short note beside each sketch explaining what the symbol could represent.
Page 3 — Artwork Planning
Choose one symbol that you would like to develop into your final artwork. On half a page sketch how that symbol will appear in your composition.
As you plan the image, think about whether the symbol should appear realistic or simplified. Consider where the symbol should be placed so it becomes the focal point. You may also consider how colour, value, or composition might strengthen the meaning.
This planning step helps artists clarify their ideas before beginning the final artwork.
Page 4 — Reflection
After completing your planning sketches, write a short reflection explaining your choices. Describe why you selected this particular symbol and what it represents to you.
Consider whether the symbol is personal, universal, or a combination of both. Finally, explain what you hope viewers might feel or think when they see your artwork.
Reflecting on your choices helps you understand how images communicate meaning.
