Sketchbook Tasks
Page 1 — Blind Contour Practice
Choose a simple object that you can place in front of you. Good choices include your hand, a shoe, a mug, or a small plant.
Place the object where you can see it clearly. Begin drawing the outline of the object while keeping your eyes focused on the object itself. Do not look at your paper while drawing.
Move your pencil slowly across the paper as your eyes trace the edges of the object. Try to match the speed of your hand with the movement of your eyes.
Do not erase any lines and do not stop to judge the drawing. The purpose of the exercise is observation, not appearance.
At the bottom of the page write one sentence explaining what felt most difficult about drawing without looking at the paper.
Page 2 — Observational Contour Drawing
Use the same object you used for the blind contour drawing. This time you may look at both the object and your paper.
Begin by observing the largest shapes of the object. Draw the main outline slowly and carefully. Pay attention to where lines curve or change direction.
Look frequently at the object rather than focusing only on your paper. The goal is to observe the proportions and relationships between shapes.
At the bottom of the page write one sentence explaining what improved when you slowed down and focused on observation.
Page 3 — Value Practice
On the next page create a five-step value scale. Begin with the lightest value on one end of the scale and gradually darken the pencil marks until you reach the darkest value.
After completing the value scale, draw a simple object such as a sphere, cube, or small household item. Observe where the light hits the object and where shadows appear.
Shade the drawing so that the lightest areas remain mostly white while the darker areas gradually become deeper in tone.
At the bottom of the page write one sentence explaining where the lightest and darkest areas appeared on the object.
Reflection
At the end of the lesson you will write a short reflection describing your experience with observational drawing.
Explain which drawing exercise felt most challenging and why. Describe anything that surprised you about the process of observation. Finally, explain how slowing down affected your drawing.
The purpose of the reflection is to help you recognize how observation improves drawing skills.
