Translating Influence into New Ideas
After researching and analyzing an artist, the next step is learning how to transform what you have learned into original artwork. Artists rarely copy the work of others directly; instead, they study ideas, techniques, and themes, and then reinterpret them in their own way. This process is called translating influence. It involves taking inspiration from another artist’s work and using it as a starting point for developing new ideas. One way to do this is through theme translation, where you explore a similar concept or subject as the artist you studied but apply it to a modern situation or your own personal experiences. Another approach is technique translation, where you experiment with the methods or visual processes used by the artist, but apply them to a completely different subject or image.
Artists can also translate influence through material experimentation. This means exploring materials or processes that relate to the artist you researched while adapting them to suit your own artistic direction. For example, if the artist used collage, layering, or textured surfaces, you might experiment with similar materials while developing new imagery and ideas. To begin this process, you will create visual brainstorming pages in your sketchbook. These pages should include quick sketches, written notes, diagrams, and possible compositions that explore several different directions for your artwork. The goal of this stage is not to create a finished piece but to generate possibilities. By experimenting with multiple ideas, you begin moving from research and analysis toward creative development, allowing you to transform artistic influence into an original concept for your own work.
