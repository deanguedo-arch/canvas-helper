In your Art 30 portfolio, one artwork must respond directly to an artist, art movement, or historical period that you researched during the course.
This does not mean copying someone else’s artwork. Instead, it means studying how artists from another time or movement worked and then creating your own artwork inspired by their ideas, techniques, themes, or visual style.
Artists throughout history have learned from each other in this way. Painters, sculptors, and designers study the work of others to understand how artists solve problems, communicate ideas, and use materials. When you respond to an artist or movement, you are joining this long conversation between artists across time.
Your artwork should show that you understand:
Who the artist or movement is
What ideas or themes their work explores
What visual techniques or materials they use
How their work connects to culture, history, or society
Your final artwork should clearly show how their work influenced your own piece, while still expressing your own ideas, style, and artistic choices.
Think of this project as a visual conversation between you and another artist.
What a “Visual Response” Can Look Like
Your response might include:
Technique Response You use a similar technique or process. Example: layering like a mixed media artist.
Style Response You adapt visual characteristics such as colour, mark making, abstraction, or composition.
Conceptual Response You explore similar themes but with your own perspective.
Material Response You experiment with similar materials.
Cultural or Historical Response You reinterpret ideas from a different time in a modern context.
What Evidence Must Be Visible in the Work
To meet Art 30 expectations, your project should show:
• Clear connection to the researched artist/movement• Personal interpretation rather than copying• Thoughtful visual decisions• Technical skill and craftsmanship• Reflection on artistic influence
