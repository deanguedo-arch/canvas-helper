Lesson 3: Composition as Meaning
Composition is not decoration. Composition is decision-making. Where things are placed, how space is used, and what is emphasized all shape how meaning is communicated.
You will be solving visual problems rather than following a single “correct” solution.
What You Are Learning
In this lesson, you will:
Understand how composition controls meaning
Learn to develop and solve design problems visually
Analyze relationships between visual elements
Use thumbnails and value studies to test multiple solutions
This lesson moves you from drawing objects to designing images intentionally.
Technique Focus
1. Compositional Thumbnails
www.youtube.com/watch?v=gYTuKZ3TbMk
Thumbnails are small, fast sketches used to test ideas.
They help you:
Try many options quickly
Avoid committing too early
See patterns and weaknesses
Thumbnails are about thinking, not detail.
2. Dominance & Visual Hierarchy
Every composition answers this question:
What do I want the viewer to notice first?
Dominance can be created through:
Size
Placement
Contrast
Isolation
Directional lines
Changing dominance changes meaning.
3. Value Structure
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tOGDmamxQwE&t=25s
Value (light and dark) is one of the strongest compositional tools.
Working in value only:
Removes the distraction of colour
Reveals balance and emphasis clearly
Strengthens composition before detail
Art History & Context
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, many artists moved away from detailed realism and began focusing on structure, balance, and abstraction.
They discovered that:
Meaning can be communicated through arrangement alone
Simplifying forms can strengthen impact
Composition affects emotional response
In contemporary art, composition is understood as a visual language that guides how an image is read.
Artists
Lawren Harris
www.youtube.com/watch?v=LOD4VzvBjSc
Lawren Harris was a Canadian painter associated with the Group of Seven. His work is known for simplified forms, strong structure, and careful balance. Harris believed that reducing a scene to its essential shapes could communicate spiritual calm, isolation, or clarity.
Rather than filling his compositions with detail, Harris:
Simplified landscapes into bold shapes
Used symmetry and balance intentionally
Created a strong sense of focus and stillness
Key Characteristics of Harris’s Work:
Simplified forms
Clear focal areas
Strong balance and structure
How This Connects to Your Work: Harris shows that removing detail can make meaning clearer. Thoughtful placement and balance can communicate emotion without complex imagery.
Christi Belcourt
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UmNdUwH91oE
Christi Belcourt is a contemporary Indigenous artist whose work is inspired by Métis beadwork traditions. Her compositions are often dense and intricate, built from repetition, pattern, and interconnected forms.
Belcourt’s work emphasizes:
Relationship between all living things
Balance through repetition
Meaning built from many small parts
Key Characteristics of Belcourt’s Work:
Highly detailed pattern
Repetition and rhythm
No single dominant object; meaning emerges from relationships
How This Connects to Your Work: Belcourt’s compositions demonstrate that dominance is a choice. Sometimes meaning comes from equality, repetition, and interconnectedness, not a single focal point.
Wassily Kandinsky
https://youtu.be/yDWLq3wQPwk
Wassily Kandinsky was an international artist and one of the pioneers of abstract art. He believed that shape, line, and colour could communicate emotion in the same way music does.
Kandinsky’s compositions often:
Use floating shapes and lines
Avoid realism entirely
Rely on balance, tension, and movement
Key Characteristics of Kandinsky’s Work:
Abstract shapes
Dynamic movement
Emotional use of visual relationships
How This Connects to Your Work: Kandinsky shows that composition alone can carry meaning. Even without recognizable objects, the relationships between elements can create emotion and impact.
