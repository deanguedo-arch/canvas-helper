Lesson: Facial Expressions & Body Language
Communicating Emotion with Simple Lines
Artists do not need complex drawings to communicate emotion. A few simple lines—especially the eyes, eyebrows, and mouth—can completely change how a face feels to the viewer. Even very simple drawings such as stick figures can communicate powerful emotions through posture and gesture. In this lesson you will learn that small changes in lines can change meaning. By adjusting the curve of a mouth, the angle of eyebrows, or the tilt of a body, a neutral character can quickly appear happy, angry, worried, or surprised. The focus of this lesson is expressive drawing, where the goal is not perfect realism but clear communication of feeling.
What You Are Learning
In this lesson you will learn how artists use facial features to communicate emotion and how small changes in eyes, eyebrows, and mouth shapes can dramatically change how a character appears. You will practice drawing nine different facial expressions using only these features. You will also explore how body language communicates emotion by creating stick figures that show different feelings through posture and gesture. By the end of the lesson you will better understand how artists use simple lines to express complex ideas and emotions.
Technique Focus
One of the most powerful parts of a facial expression is the eyebrows. Eyebrows often communicate emotion before any other feature. When eyebrows angle downward toward the center of the face, the expression often appears angry or intense. When they rise high above the eyes, the face appears surprised or shocked. When they tilt upward in the middle, the face can appear worried or sad. Even very small changes in eyebrow angle can dramatically change the emotional message of a drawing.
Eyes reinforce the emotional message created by the eyebrows. Happy eyes often appear slightly curved or relaxed, while angry eyes may appear narrow or sharply angled. Fear or surprise is often shown with wide open eyes. Tired or bored expressions may show drooping eyelids. Artists frequently exaggerate eye shapes slightly so that the emotion is easier for viewers to recognize.
The mouth is another feature that strongly influences expression. A curved upward line suggests happiness, while a downward curve suggests sadness. A straight line may feel neutral or serious. An open mouth may communicate surprise, excitement, or shock. Because the mouth is such a recognizable shape, even a tiny adjustment in the curve of the line can change the emotion the viewer interprets.
Why Artists Study Expression
Artists study expression because art is a form of visual communication. Just as writers use words to communicate ideas and emotions, artists use lines, shapes, and gestures to express feelings and meaning. Many artists intentionally simplify their drawings so that emotion becomes the most important part of the image. Learning to communicate emotion clearly is an important step in developing expressive artwork.
Artist Connections
Shuvinai Ashoona
Shuvinai Ashoona is an Inuit artist from Cape Dorset (Kinngait), Nunavut, a community well known for its strong tradition of drawing and printmaking. She is recognized internationally for her large, detailed drawings created with pencil, ink, and coloured pencil. Her artwork often combines real-life experiences from the Arctic with imaginative scenes that include animals, people, and unusual dreamlike imagery. Many of her drawings contain expressive figures that interact with each other in complex and sometimes mysterious environments.
Ashoona’s work demonstrates how drawing can communicate emotion and storytelling through line and gesture. Even when her figures are simplified, their body language and facial expressions help viewers understand relationships between characters and the feelings present in the scene. Her drawings also reflect Inuit perspectives and northern life, connecting personal imagination with cultural knowledge. Through her expressive style, Ashoona shows that drawing can be both deeply personal and culturally meaningful.

Kent Monkman
Kent Monkman is a Cree artist from Manitoba and one of Canada’s most influential contemporary artists. His work includes painting, drawing, installation, and performance. Monkman is known for creating dramatic scenes that reinterpret Canadian and colonial history from an Indigenous perspective. A recurring character in his work is Miss Chief Eagle Testickle, a gender-fluid figure who challenges traditional ideas about identity, power, and history.
Monkman’s drawings and paintings often include expressive human figures that communicate emotion through body language, gesture, and posture. The characters in his artwork frequently appear in moments of tension, conflict, or storytelling. Because of this, the way bodies move and interact becomes very important in communicating meaning. His work demonstrates how artists use the human figure not only to represent people but also to express ideas about identity, culture, and social history.
