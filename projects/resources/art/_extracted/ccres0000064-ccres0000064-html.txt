Sculpture Challenge
Your task is to design and build a small sculpture using materials you can find at home. The sculpture should be large enough to stand on a table or desk and should be stable enough to remain upright on its own.
The sculpture should show creative thinking about form and structure. It does not need to represent something realistic. Your sculpture may be abstract, imaginative, or inspired by objects, animals, buildings, or natural forms.
Material Options
You may use any materials that are safe and available at home. Choose one of the options below or combine materials if you wish.
Cardboard Construction
Cardboard is one of the easiest materials to use when building sculptures. You can cut, fold, and stack cardboard to create structures. Corrugated cardboard from boxes works especially well because it is strong but lightweight.
Possible approaches include creating layered shapes, cutting slots so pieces connect together, or stacking shapes to build height.
Paper Sculpture
Paper can be folded, rolled, twisted, or layered to create three-dimensional forms. Artists sometimes use techniques similar to origami or paper engineering to build structures.
You might roll paper into tubes, fold geometric shapes, or cut and connect paper strips to form a structure.
Recycled Object Sculpture (Assemblage)
Assemblage sculptures are made by combining found objects. These might include bottle caps, packaging materials, plastic containers, cardboard tubes, or other everyday objects.
Think about how different objects can connect to create an interesting structure. Glue, tape, or string can help attach pieces together.
Natural Material Sculpture
Nature provides many interesting sculptural materials. You might use sticks, rocks, leaves, pinecones, or other natural objects.
These materials can be arranged and connected to create organic shapes inspired by nature.
Aluminum Foil or Wire Sculpture
Aluminum foil can be twisted and compressed into strong shapes. Wire (such as craft wire or twist ties) can also be bent and shaped to create structural forms.
These materials allow artists to build flowing shapes and lines in space.
Clay or Dough Sculpture (Optional)
If you have access to clay or homemade salt dough, you may choose to sculpt by shaping and modeling the material with your hands.
This method allows you to create smooth forms and add texture using simple tools such as toothpicks or forks.
Planning Your Sculpture
Before you begin building, spend a few minutes planning your idea in your sketchbook. Draw two or three small sketches of possible sculpture ideas. Think about what materials you will use and how the pieces will connect together.
Consider how the sculpture will stand and whether it needs a base for support.
Building Your Sculpture
Begin constructing your sculpture using your chosen materials. Work slowly and experiment with different ways of connecting pieces together. You may need to adjust your design as you build.
Remember that sculptures are meant to be viewed from multiple sides. Walk around your sculpture while working to see how it looks from different angles.
Documenting Your Sculpture
Once your sculpture is complete, take two or three photographs of it from different angles. Good lighting and a simple background will help viewers see your work clearly.
Reflection
In your sketchbook or as a short written response, describe your sculpture and explain your artistic decisions. Reflect on what materials you used and why you chose them. Describe any challenges you faced while building the sculpture and how you solved them. Consider how your sculpture changes when viewed from different angles.
