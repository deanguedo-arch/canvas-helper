Lesson 7: Identity & Mixed Media
Meaning Through Materials
Artists make decisions not only about what images they create, but also about the materials they use to create them. The materials in an artwork can communicate meaning just as strongly as the images themselves. A rough surface might suggest struggle or memory, while soft fabrics might suggest comfort or identity. The way materials are layered together can suggest complexity, time, history, or personal experience.
Mixed media is an approach to art in which artists combine multiple materials within a single artwork. Instead of using only one medium such as pencil or paint, artists may combine drawing, painting, collage, text, photographs, or found objects. These combinations allow artists to communicate ideas in richer and more layered ways.
In this lesson you will explore how artists use materials, textures, and layers to communicate identity and experience. You will experiment with combining different materials and will begin thinking about how those materials connect to personal meaning.
This lesson is designed for independent and distance learners. You are encouraged to experiment and take creative risks. Not every experiment will work perfectly, and that is an important part of learning. The goal is to explore how materials contribute to meaning in an artwork.
What You Are Learning
In this lesson you will begin understanding how artists combine materials intentionally rather than randomly. You will explore how textures and layered surfaces can represent identity, memory, or emotional experiences.
You will also learn how art can reflect cultural background, time, and community values. Artists often draw inspiration from cultural traditions and personal experiences, and learning about these influences helps us understand artworks more deeply.
Through experimentation and planning, you will create a mixed media artwork that communicates something meaningful about identity, place, or experience.
This lesson connects personal expression with cultural awareness and encourages you to think about how materials themselves can carry meaning.
Technique Focus
Understanding Mixed Media
Mixed media refers to artworks created using more than one material. Instead of limiting themselves to a single medium, artists combine materials such as drawing tools, paint, collage, fabric, photographs, text, or found objects.
For example, an artist might combine pencil drawing with paint, attach pieces of fabric to represent clothing, or include fragments of printed text to suggest memory or narrative. Each material contributes something different to the artwork.
Artists often choose materials because of what they represent. A torn newspaper might symbolize information or history. A piece of fabric might represent family traditions or personal memories. The choice of material becomes part of the meaning of the artwork.

Texture and Layering
Texture refers to how a surface feels or appears to feel. In art, texture can be actual, meaning it can be physically felt, or implied, meaning it only appears textured through drawing or painting techniques.
In mixed media work, texture often becomes an important visual element. Thick paint, rough paper, fabric, or raised objects can create surfaces that feel layered and dynamic.
Layering occurs when materials overlap or build on top of one another. Artists may glue papers over drawings, paint over photographs, or attach objects to a surface. Each layer contributes additional meaning.
Layered artworks often suggest ideas such as memory, time, and experience because the surface shows evidence of multiple stages of creation.

Materials as Meaning
When working with mixed media, artists often ask themselves why they are choosing a particular material. The choice of material can suggest ideas about culture, memory, environment, or personal experience.
For example, using pages from an old book might represent memory or storytelling. Recycled materials might comment on environmental concerns. Fabric might suggest family traditions or personal identity.
Thinking about materials in this way helps artists create artworks where the medium itself contributes to the message.
Instead of choosing materials simply because they are available, artists begin selecting materials because they strengthen the meaning of the artwork.
Art History and Context
Contemporary artists frequently experiment with mixed media to communicate complex ideas. Rather than restricting themselves to traditional materials, they often combine drawing, painting, photography, sculpture, and found objects.
These materials may be chosen because of what they represent culturally, historically, or personally. A work created with rusted metal might evoke history and decay. A collage built from magazine images might comment on consumer culture.
Mixed media allows artists to create layered visual stories that cannot always be expressed through a single medium.
Understanding how materials contribute to meaning helps viewers interpret contemporary artworks more thoughtfully.
Artists Who Use Materials to Communicate Identity
Nadia Myre
Nadia Myre is an Algonquin artist from Canada who creates mixed media artworks that explore identity, history, and healing. Her work often combines materials such as beadwork, textiles, photography, and installation. One of her most well-known projects involved beading over the printed text of the Indian Act, a Canadian law that historically controlled many aspects of Indigenous life. By covering the text with beads, Myre transformed a painful historical document into a collaborative artwork created by many people. Her work shows that materials can carry cultural meaning. Beadwork connects to traditional Indigenous craft practices, but Myre uses it in contemporary art to tell stories about culture, resilience, and identity.

Kim Dorland
Kim Dorland is a Canadian painter known for his highly textured paintings. He often applies paint in thick layers that create rough, physical surfaces.
These textures contribute to the emotional intensity of his work. The heavy paint and energetic brushstrokes make the paintings feel raw and expressive.
Dorland’s work shows how the physical surface of an artwork can communicate emotion and atmosphere.

Anselm Kiefer
Anselm Kiefer is an international artist who uses unusual materials such as straw, ash, clay, and metal in his paintings and sculptures. These materials often represent themes of memory, history, and cultural trauma.
The rough surfaces and unconventional materials in Kiefer’s work create a strong emotional atmosphere. The materials themselves suggest ideas about destruction, rebuilding, and historical memory.
Kiefer’s work demonstrates how materials can function as powerful symbols within an artwork.
