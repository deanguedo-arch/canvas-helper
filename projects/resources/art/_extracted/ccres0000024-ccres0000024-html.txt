Sketchbook Activities for Lesson 3
Composition & Design — Learning to See on Purpose
Choose 3 of the activities below for this lesson's sketchbook activities.
Activity 1 — Same Object, Different Placement
Goal: Understand how placement changes meaning and attention.
Students draw the same simple object (cup, shoe, plant, phone, etc.) four times on the page.
Each drawing must place the object in a different location:
Center of the page
Near one corner
Very close to the edge
Off-center using the Rule of Thirds idea (not middle)
Students should keep the object simple and quick.
Reflection Prompt
Students write 3–4 sentences:
Which drawing feels the most interesting?
Which feels the most stable?
Where does your eye look first in each drawing?
What you should see:  Composition changes the feeling of an image.
Activity 2 — Finding the Focal Point
Goal: Understand emphasis and visual hierarchy.
Students create three small rectangles (thumbnail sketches).
Each drawing should contain three simple shapes (circle, square, triangle).
Students must make one shape clearly the focal point using different methods:
Sketch 1 Use size (one shape much larger).
Sketch 2 Use contrast (one shape shaded dark).
Sketch 3 Use placement (one shape placed where the eye goes first).
Reflection Prompt
Students answer:
Which method made the focal point most obvious?
Did your eye move anywhere after the focal point?
Activity 3 — Symmetrical vs Asymmetrical Balance
Goal: Understand visual balance.
Students divide a sketchbook page into two halves.
Left Side — Symmetrical Balance
Draw a simple design where both sides mirror each other.
Examples
Tree reflections
Geometric pattern
Butterfly style shape
Right Side — Asymmetrical Balance
Draw a design where the two sides are different but still feel balanced.
Example ideas
Large shape on one side + small shapes on the other
Dark object balanced by several light shapes
Reflection Prompt
Students write:
Which side feels calmer?
Which side feels more dynamic or interesting?
Activity 4 — Eye Movement Path
Goal: Practice directing the viewer’s eye.
Students draw a simple composition using 3–5 objects or shapes.
They must arrange them so the viewer’s eye moves across the page intentionally.
Ways to guide movement:
Repeating shapes
Curved lines
Diagonal placement
Objects pointing toward each other
After drawing, students draw arrows showing the path their eye follows.
Reflection Prompt
Did your eye follow the path you expected?
Where did your eye start and end?
Activity 5 — Composition Cropping Experiment
Goal: Understand how framing affects composition.
Students choose one everyday scene:
Examples
A chair
A plant on a desk
A window
A group of objects
They draw three cropped versions of the scene.
Full view
Close-up crop
Unusual crop (cutting objects off the edge)
Reflection Prompt
Students answer:
Which crop feels the most interesting?
Which one creates the strongest focal point?
This connects directly to Modernist composition ideas.
Activity 6 — Artist Composition Study
Goal: Learn from real artists.
Students choose one of the artists from the lesson:
Bill Reid
Emily Carr
Henri Matisse
They quickly sketch the basic shapes of the composition from one artwork.
Students should simplify it into shapes only (no details).
Then they label:
Focal point
Direction of movement
Balanced areas
Reflection Prompt
How did the artist guide your eye?
What shapes create the balance?
