Before beginning your final artwork, you must develop a clear plan for what you will create. Strong artists spend time planning their ideas before starting a finished piece.
Your planning pages should include the following:
Thumbnail Sketches
Create at least 4–6 small thumbnail sketches exploring different ideas.
Thumbnails should test different subjects, arrangements, or compositions.
These sketches should be quick and exploratory rather than detailed.
Composition Planning
Choose your strongest thumbnail idea and develop a larger composition sketch.
Consider how the image will be organized on the page.
Think about where the viewer’s eye will move through the artwork.
Material Testing
Experiment with possible materials or techniques you may use.
Test how different materials behave (paint, ink, collage, pencil, mixed media, etc.).
Record notes about which materials work best for your idea.
Colour Palette Exploration
Test possible colour combinations.
Consider how colour might influence mood or meaning.
Develop a planned colour palette for your final work.
Planning Questions
In your sketchbook or planning sheet, answer the following questions:
What parts of the researched artist’s work are influencing my idea?
How am I changing or adapting those ideas to create something original?
What message, theme, or idea do I want my artwork to communicate?
What visual elements will help communicate that idea?
This planning process should result in a clear direction for your final artwork.
