Sketchbook Tasks for Lesson 4
Begin this assignment by watching the short videos below. These demonstrations introduce the basic ideas behind expression drawing and show how artists simplify facial features in order to make emotions clear and readable.
Sketchbook Activity: Facial Expression Grid
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GWEte7NUmxs
For the first sketchbook activity, draw a three-by-three grid on one page of your sketchbook so that you create nine squares. Inside each square you will draw a simple face that shows a different emotion. Each face should be created using only three features: eyes, eyebrows, and a mouth. You do not need to include details such as noses, shading, hair, or realistic anatomy. The purpose of the activity is to explore how small line changes affect emotional expression.
Create nine different expressions. Your grid should include the emotions happy, sad, angry, surprised, confused, scared, tired, excited, and bored. Begin each drawing with a simple circular head shape and then experiment with the angles of eyebrows, the shapes of eyes, and the curves of the mouth until the emotion becomes clear. The goal of this activity is not to create perfect drawings but to make the emotion easily recognizable.
Sketchbook Activity: Stick Figure Emotions
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DkM6FgbQIGs
On the next sketchbook page you will explore how emotions can also be communicated through body language. Draw another grid with nine spaces and fill each space with a stick figure. These stick figures should communicate emotion through posture and movement rather than facial detail. You may use arm position, body angle, head tilt, or the position of the legs to show how the character is feeling.
Your stick figures should show nine different emotions such as pride, nervousness, laughter, sadness, anger, excitement, exhaustion, confidence, and embarrassment. Think about how real people stand or move when they feel these emotions. For example, someone who is excited might raise their arms upward, while someone who is sad might have slumped shoulders and a lowered head. Someone who is angry may clench their fists or stand rigidly. This activity demonstrates that the body itself can communicate emotion even when the face is not detailed.
Reflection
After completing your drawings, write a short reflection in your sketchbook. In your response, consider which facial feature seemed to change the emotion the most. Think about whether it was easier to communicate emotion through the face or through body language. Reflect on which expression was the most challenging to draw and explain why. Finally, consider how artists are able to communicate strong emotions even when their drawings are very simple.
