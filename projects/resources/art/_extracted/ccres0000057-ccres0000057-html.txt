Sketchbook Tasks
Page 1 — Artist Research
Choose one of the artists from this lesson and create a research page in your sketchbook. Write the artist’s name and include a few short facts about their background, cultural context, and artistic focus.
Look closely at examples of their work and describe what you notice about how they create their artwork. You might observe the materials they use, the types of shapes or marks they repeat, or the themes they explore.
Finish the page with a short sentence explaining what you find most interesting about the artist’s work.
Page 2 — Inspiration Without Copying
On the next page create several small sketches inspired by the artist you studied. Instead of copying their imagery, focus on borrowing aspects of their approach.
You might experiment with their process, such as repeating shapes, using bold outlines, creating layered textures, or working with expressive gestures.
Beside each sketch write short notes explaining what aspect of the artist’s work inspired your idea and how you changed it to create something new.
Page 3 — Process Experiments
On another page conduct at least two creative experiments. Try using different drawing tools or materials. You might experiment with repetition, movement, layering, or mark-making techniques.
Write short notes describing what worked well in each experiment and what did not work as expected. These notes help you reflect on your creative decisions and guide your next steps.
Page 4 – Artist Study and Response Artwork
For this project you will create one artwork inspired by the process or approach of an artist from this lesson. Your artwork should clearly show how the artist’s ideas influenced the way you worked.
Your sketchbook pages should show evidence of planning and experimentation before the final artwork. The finished piece should represent your own idea rather than copying the artist’s imagery.
The goal of this project is to demonstrate how studying artists can expand your creative thinking and help you develop your own artistic approach.
Page 5 — Reflection
After completing your experiments and final artwork, write a short reflection describing what you learned from studying the artist.
Explain how experimentation influenced your ideas and whether any unexpected results changed your direction. Finally, describe what you might try differently the next time you begin a new artwork.
Reflection helps artists understand their own creative process and recognize how their work develops.
