In Art 30, you will write a portfolio critical statement that analyzes and evaluates your own artwork. This statement is not just a description of what you made. Instead, it is a thoughtful analysis where you explain how and why your artwork was created, and how your work relates to ideas, techniques, or approaches used by professional artists.
A strong critical statement shows that you understand how to think about artwork using the language of art criticism. This means discussing your work using art vocabulary such as the elements of art, principles of design, composition, symbolism, materials, and technique. You will explain how these visual decisions helped communicate your theme or concept. In addition, you will compare your work to professional artists whose work influenced you. This comparison helps show that you understand how artists learn from other artists and how artistic ideas develop over time.
Your written statement should also include evaluation and reflection. This means thinking about what worked well in your work, what challenges you encountered, and what you learned during the process. The goal is to demonstrate that you can analyze your own artwork the same way an art critic or art historian might analyze the work of a professional artist.
Your statement should clearly explain three main things:
• the ideas and themes explored in your artwork • the visual decisions you made while creating your work • how your work connects to or was influenced by professional artists
This written reflection helps show that you are not only capable of creating artwork, but also capable of thinking critically about art.
