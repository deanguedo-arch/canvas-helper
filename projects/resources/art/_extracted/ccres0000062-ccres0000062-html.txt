Breaking it down - how to get started and what would your next steps be?
A Simple Formula to create your thematic works:
One big idea + four different explorations + one connected visual language = a strong Art 30 series
If you need a more detailed explanation see below.
NOTE: you do not have to follow all of these steps in order to create your theme - this is a guideline if you are stuck or need help and ideas.
Step 1 — Choose One Clear Theme, Concept, or Technique
Begin by deciding what your series will be about. Your four artworks must connect to one main idea. This could be a theme such as identity, memory, place, change, isolation, power, nature, consumerism, or belonging. It could also be based on a concept such as transformation, contrast, routine, or perspective. In some cases, your series may be connected by a technique, such as collage, monochromatic painting, layered photography, or ink drawing.
Your theme should be broad enough that you can explore it in four different ways, but focused enough that the pieces still feel connected.
Step 2 — Write a One-Sentence Focus Statement
Once you have chosen your theme, write a simple focus statement that explains what your series is exploring.
For example:
My series explores how identity can change depending on place and situation.
My series examines the impact of human activity on the environment.
My series investigates memory through layered imagery and symbolism.
This statement will help keep your work focused as you plan all four pieces.
Step 3 — Research Visual Ideas and Artists
Look for artists, artworks, photographs, textures, symbols, or visual references connected to your theme. Study how other artists explore similar ideas. Notice their use of colour, composition, materials, mood, and symbolism.
Collect this research in your sketchbook or digital notes. Include:
artist names
images or references
written observations
possible symbols or visual ideas
notes on materials or techniques you may want to try
This step gives you starting points for your own work.
Step 4 — Brainstorm More Than One Idea
Do not settle on your first idea immediately. Create a brainstorming page with quick written ideas and rough sketches. Think of different ways your theme could be shown visually.
Ask yourself:
What images connect to this theme?
What symbols could represent this idea?
What emotions or moods fit this topic?
What materials would strengthen the meaning?
How could I show this idea from different angles?
Your goal is to generate several possibilities before choosing your best directions.
Step 5 — Plan How the 4 Works Will Connect
Before creating the final artworks, decide how the four pieces will feel like a series. They should not look random or unrelated.
Think about how you will create cohesion through things such as:
repeated colours
similar materials
repeated symbols
similar compositions
related subject matter
a shared mood or tone
one technique used across all four works
You should also decide how the pieces will be different from each other. A strong series uses repetition with variation. That means the works are connected, but not identical.
Step 6 — Decide What Each Artwork Will Explore
Now assign each of the four works a specific role within the series. Each piece should explore the theme in a slightly different way.
For example:
Work 1 may introduce the main idea
Work 2 may explore a different perspective or mood
Work 3 may experiment with composition, symbolism, or scale
Work 4 may deepen the idea or act as the strongest conclusion
You are not making the same artwork four times. You are exploring one idea across four connected pieces.
Step 7 — Create Thumbnail Sketches
For each of the four works, make small thumbnail sketches. These should be quick planning drawings that help you test:
composition
placement of objects
size relationships
focal point
viewer eye movement
Create more than one thumbnail for each artwork if needed. Thumbnails help you solve visual problems before you begin the final piece.
Step 8 — Choose Materials and Test Them
Decide what materials you will use for the series. You may use the same material for all four works, or you may vary the materials slightly while keeping the overall look connected.
Do small tests for:
colour palettes
mark-making
texture
layering
collage combinations
shading or painting approaches
These material tests help you make better decisions before starting final work.
Step 9 — Make a Series Plan Page
Before beginning the finished artworks, create one planning page that clearly shows your direction for the full series.
Include:
your theme or focus statement
the title or idea for each of the 4 works
small sketches or composition plans
colour palette ideas
materials list
notes about symbols or repeated visual elements
This page becomes your guide while you work.
Step 10 — Create the First Artwork
Start with the piece that feels clearest or strongest. As you work, focus on craftsmanship and clear communication of your idea.
Ask yourself:
Does this artwork connect clearly to my theme?
Does it fit the series visually?
Does it still have its own unique role?
Take process photos or record notes about changes you make along the way.
Step 11 — Reflect Before Starting the Next Piece
After finishing the first work, pause and evaluate it before moving on. Look at what worked well and what could improve.
Consider:
Is the theme clear?
Is the mood effective?
Are the materials working well?
What should stay consistent in the next piece?
What should change or develop?
This reflection helps the next artwork become stronger.
Step 12 — Create the Remaining 3 Works
Complete the remaining works one at a time, using each piece to build on the one before it. Keep your series connected, but allow each work to contribute something new.
As you continue, pay attention to:
consistency across the series
variation between the works
growth in your idea
refinement of technique
stronger symbolism or clearer communication
Your series should feel more developed as it progresses.
Step 13 — Lay All 4 Works Together and Evaluate the Series
Once all four works are complete, place them side by side and examine them as a group. This is important because a series must work both as individual artworks and as a connected whole.
Ask yourself:
Do these clearly belong together?
Is there a shared theme, concept, or technique?
Are there enough similarities to create cohesion?
Are there enough differences to keep the series interesting?
Do the works build meaning when viewed together?
You may need to revise one or more pieces so the series feels more unified.
Step 14 — Title the Series and Individual Works
Give your series an overall title that reflects the central idea. Then title each individual work if required.
The title should support the meaning of the series and help viewers understand the focus of your in-depth study.
Step 15 — Write a Reflection or Artist Statement
When the series is complete, explain your thinking in writing. Your reflection should describe:
your theme or concept
how the works are connected
what changed from one work to the next
what symbols, colours, or techniques you used
what you learned while developing the series
This shows that you understand not only how to make art, but also how to develop ideas across multiple works.
A Strong 4-Work Series will have...
one clear central theme, concept, or technique
visual cohesion across all four pieces
repetition with variation
thoughtful planning
experimentation and development
technical care and craftsmanship
evidence that the idea deepened across the series
