Grade 10 Art Assignment

Personal Choice Artwork

In this assignment you will create a completed artwork based on a subject or idea of your own choosing. Artists constantly make decisions about what they want to communicate and how they want to communicate it. The subject, materials, colours, and style used in an artwork are all choices made by the artist. This project is designed to give you the opportunity to explore your own interests while applying the artistic skills and concepts you have been learning in Art 10. Rather than following a strict theme, you will decide what you want to create and how you want to express your idea visually.

The goal of this assignment is to help you develop independence as an artist. You will practice using the Elements of Art and Principles of Design, make creative decisions, plan an artwork, and complete a final piece that demonstrates effort and craftsmanship. Artists rarely begin a finished piece without planning, so part of this project will involve thinking through your idea and making some preliminary sketches before beginning the final artwork. This process allows artists to test ideas, make improvements, and solve problems before committing to the final piece.

Your artwork can be about any topic that interests you personally. Some students may choose to draw landscapes or nature scenes, while others may focus on portraits, animals, still life objects, fantasy imagery, or symbolic ideas. You may also choose to create artwork that expresses an emotion, a memory, or something meaningful from your own life. There is no single correct subject for this assignment; what matters is that you make thoughtful decisions about what you want to create and how you want to present it.

You may also choose the materials or medium you would like to work with. Drawing materials such as pencil, coloured pencil, charcoal, ink, or markers are all acceptable options. Painting materials such as watercolour or acrylic paint can also be used if they are available to you. Students may also choose to work in mixed media, collage, digital art, or even create a small sculpture or three-dimensional artwork. The material you choose should allow you to demonstrate effort, control, and attention to detail.

Before beginning your final artwork, you will spend some time planning your idea in your sketchbook. This planning stage should include a simple sketch of what you intend to create along with a few notes explaining your idea and the materials you plan to use. Planning helps artists think through their work before starting the final piece and can make the creative process much smoother.

Once your planning is complete, you will begin working on your final artwork. The finished piece should show clear effort, attention to detail, and thoughtful artistic choices. Your artwork should demonstrate the use of at least two Elements of Art, such as line, shape, colour, texture, value, or space, as well as at least one Principle of Design, such as contrast, emphasis, balance, movement, or unity. These elements and principles are the visual tools artists use to organize and strengthen their work.

After completing the artwork, you will write a short reflection paragraph about your work. In this reflection you should explain the idea or subject you chose and why it interested you. You should also describe the materials you used and identify which Elements of Art or Principles of Design appear in your piece. Finally, reflect on what part of the artwork you are most proud of and what you might improve if you had the opportunity to create the artwork again.

Your final submission should include your planning sketch in your sketchbook, the completed artwork, and the written reflection. Together, these parts demonstrate both your creative thinking and the artistic process you used to complete the project.

Assessment Rubric

Your artwork will be evaluated using the following criteria.

Idea and Creativity refers to how original and thoughtful your concept is. Work at the highest level demonstrates strong personal ideas and creative thinking. Work at the middle levels communicates a clear but simpler idea. Work at the lower levels shows limited originality or unclear intent.

Use of the Elements of Art and Principles of Design looks at how effectively visual tools such as line, colour, shape, value, balance, contrast, or emphasis are used. Strong work demonstrates clear and intentional use of these concepts to strengthen the artwork, while developing work may show only limited understanding of how they influence the image.

Skill and Craftsmanship evaluates the care and effort put into the artwork. High-level work demonstrates control of materials, attention to detail, and patience throughout the process. Lower-level work may appear rushed, unfinished, or lacking in effort.

Planning and Artistic Process assesses the planning sketch and preparation completed before the final artwork. Strong work shows thoughtful preparation that clearly influenced the final piece, while limited work may show little or no evidence of planning.

Reflection and Explanation evaluates the written reflection. At the highest level, the reflection thoughtfully explains the artist’s decisions, materials, and learning. At lower levels the reflection may be brief, incomplete, or unclear.

Each category contributes equally to the final mark, allowing the teacher to evaluate both the creative idea and the artistic process behind the finished artwork. The overall goal of this project is not simply to produce a finished picture, but to practice thinking and working like an artist.
