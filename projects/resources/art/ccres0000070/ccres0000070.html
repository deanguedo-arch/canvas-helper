What To Do If You’re Stuck

A Sketchbook Survival Guide for Artists Who Don’t Know What to Draw

Being stuck does not mean you are bad at art.

Being stuck usually means:

  • You care about the outcome
  • You are thinking more deeply
  • You are between ideas

Professional artists get stuck all the time. The difference is that they know what to do next. This guide gives you tools to keep moving forward.

First: What NOT to Do When You’re Stuck

✘ Do not wait for inspiration
✘ Do not close your sketchbook
✘ Do not scroll endlessly for ideas to copy
✘ Do not erase everything and start over repeatedly

Being stuck is a signal to change strategy, not stop working.

Strategy 1 — Lower the Stakes (The 5-Minute Rule)

Tell yourself:

“This page does not matter.”

Set a timer for 5 minutes and fill a page with:

  • Scribbles
  • Shapes
  • Words
  • Symbols
  • Messy lines

No planning. No judging.

Most blocks disappear once your hand starts moving.

Strategy 2 — Draw the Same Idea 5 Different Ways

Choose one simple idea (an object, symbol, or feeling).

Draw it:

  1. Realistically
  2. As a symbol
  3. From memory
  4. With your non-dominant hand
  5. Using only shapes or lines

You are not looking for the best version — you are looking for options.

Strategy 3 — Steal a Method (Not an Image)

Look at an artist you’ve studied and ask:

  • How do they use line?
  • How do they use space?
  • How do they simplify or exaggerate?

Borrow the approach, not the picture.

Example:

  • Use bold outlines like Morrisseau
  • Use symbolic objects like Kahlo
  • Use controlled realism and composition like Colville

Strategy 4 — Write First, Then Draw

If drawing feels impossible, write:

  • What am I frustrated by right now?
  • What do I keep thinking about?
  • What am I avoiding drawing?

Then circle one word and draw that.

You don’t need a full idea — just a starting point.

Strategy 5 — Change the Rules

Stuck often means too many choices.

Limit yourself:

  • One colour only
  • No outlines
  • Only symbols
  • Only abstract shapes
  • Only 3 minutes per sketch

Constraints create momentum.

Strategy 6 — Make a Bad Page on Purpose

Give yourself permission to make:

  • An ugly page
  • A boring page
  • A page that goes nowhere

Bad pages are not failures.
They are bridges to better ones.

Strategy 7 — Ask Better Questions

Instead of asking:

“What should I draw?”

Ask:

  • What am I reacting to?
  • What feels unresolved?
  • What matters more than looking good?

Art grows from questions, not answers.

Strategy 8 — Use Words + Images Together

Your sketchbook is not just for drawings.

Try:

  • Writing beside sketches
  • Crossing words out
  • Arrows pointing to ideas
  • Notes like “this didn’t work because…”

Thinking on the page counts as art learning.

Strategy 9 — Copy One Thing, Then Change It

Copying can help unstick you — but only if you transform it.

After copying, change:

  • The mood
  • The scale
  • The meaning
  • The context

Copy → change → personalize.

Strategy 10 — Keep Going Anyway

You do not need to feel inspired to:

  • Fill a page
  • Test a symbol
  • Try a new approach
  • Write a reflection

Action creates clarity.
Not the other way around.

What Teachers Are Looking For When You’re Stuck

✔ You kept working
✔ You tried multiple approaches
✔ You documented thinking
✔ You reflected honestly

We are not looking for:

  • Perfect ideas
  • Fast solutions
  • Constant confidence

If You’re Still Stuck After Trying These

Do this:

  1. Choose one strategy from this guide
  2. Use it for 10 minutes
  3. Write one sentence about what changed

That counts as progress.

Final Reminder

Being stuck is not a problem.
Stopping is.

Your sketchbook is where stuckness turns into discovery.

Open it. Make a mark. Keep going.
