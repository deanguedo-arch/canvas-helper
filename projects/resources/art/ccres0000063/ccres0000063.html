Lesson: Found Object Sculpture

Creating 3-Dimensional Art at Home

Sculpture is a form of art that exists in three dimensions. Unlike drawing or painting, which are created on a flat surface, sculptures occupy real space. Artists can walk around them and view them from different angles. Sculptures can be created from many materials including clay, metal, wood, stone, or even everyday objects.

Many contemporary artists create sculptures using materials that are easily available in everyday life. These might include cardboard, paper, recycled materials, wire, fabric, or natural objects. This approach encourages creativity and problem solving because artists must think about how to build forms, connect materials, and make structures that stand or balance.

In this lesson you will design and build a small sculpture at home using materials you can easily find around you. The focus of the activity is on experimentation and creative thinking rather than perfection.

What You Are Learning

In this lesson you will explore how sculptures are constructed and how artists work with different materials. You will learn how to build forms in three dimensions and how to experiment with structure, balance, and stability. You will also practice making artistic decisions about shape, texture, and composition while using materials available at home.

What Makes a Sculpture?

A sculpture is a three-dimensional artwork that has height, width, and depth. Sculptures can be created using several different techniques. Some sculptures are formed by carving material away from a larger block. Others are built by adding materials together in a process called construction or assemblage.

When artists create sculptures they often think about several important ideas. One is form, which refers to the overall three-dimensional shape of the artwork. Another is balance, which describes how the sculpture stands or supports itself. Artists must also think about texture and how the surface of the sculpture looks or feels.

Because sculptures exist in real space, artists also consider how viewers move around the artwork and how it looks from different angles.
