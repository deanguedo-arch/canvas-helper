In Art 30, creating a series of at least four artworks that form an in-depth study means developing several pieces that are connected by a shared idea. Instead of creating four completely unrelated artworks, you will explore one central theme, concept, or technique across multiple pieces. Think of the series as a visual investigation. Each artwork should contribute something new to the idea you are exploring while still clearly belonging to the same group of work.

A cohesive series usually shares recognizable connections between the artworks. These connections might come from a common theme, such as identity, memory, environment, or social issues. They might come from a consistent visual approach, such as similar colour palettes, repeated symbols, or related compositions. A series can also be unified through technique, where each artwork experiments with the same process or material in different ways. When viewers see the pieces together, they should be able to recognize that the artworks belong to the same exploration.

In a strong series, each artwork should build on the others. One piece might introduce the theme, another might explore it from a different perspective, and another might experiment with a different composition or emotional tone. Rather than repeating the same image four times, the goal is to develop the idea more deeply with each artwork. By the end of the series, viewers should be able to see how your thinking evolved and how your artistic decisions helped you investigate the theme.

This type of project reflects how many professional artists work. Artists often create bodies of work that explore a single idea over multiple pieces. Working in a series allows artists to test different approaches, refine their techniques, and communicate more complex ideas than a single artwork might allow. Through this process, you begin to develop a personal artistic voice and direction, which is an important goal of Art 30.
