Module 7—The Digestive and Respiratory Systems
Lesson 3—The Respiratory System
 Structure 
 Function and Features 
 alveoli
 site of the actual exchange of gases
 tiny sacs enclosed by a membrane called the alveolar wall that is surrounded by a network of capillaries 
 bronchiole
 cleans foreign particles out of incoming air
 small and fine tubes that branch off of the bronchi; lined with cilia and mucous-producing cells 
 bronchus (pl. bronchi)
 cleans foreign particles out of incoming air
 smaller branches off of the trachea; have cartilage “c” rings to provide support; lined with cilia and mucous-producing cells 
 diaphragm
 contraction contributes to inspiration by increasing the volume of the thoracic cavity
 a muscle layer that separates the region of the lungs (thoracic cavity) from the region of the stomach and liver (abdominal cavity) in mammals
 epiglottis
 prevents food and drink from entering the trachea and passing into the bronchi
 a flap of cartilage that lies behind the tongue and in front of the larynx and closes over the glottis 
 glottis
 allows passage of air to lower respiratory tract
 opening from pharynx to the trachea 
 larynx
 holds the vocal cords, which vibrate to make sound; called the voice box
 made from cartilage 
 lung
 principal organ of respiration
 located deep within the body and protected by bone and muscle of the chest cavity 
 nasal passages
 warms, moistens, and cleans incoming air
 lined with ciliated cells and cells that secrete mucus 
 nostril
 entry point for incoming air
 external nasal opening 
 pharynx
 a passageway for air into respiratory system and food and water into the digestive system; commonly known as the throat
 located behind the mouth; connects the mouth and nasal cavity to the larynx and esophagus 
 pleural membrane
 enables lungs to expand and contract with chest movement
 double-layered membrane; outer layer attaches to the inside of the chest wall and inner layer attaches to lung 
 thoracic cavity
 protects the lungs
 structure made of bone and muscle 
 trachea
 passageway for air moving to the bronchi
 commonly called the wind pipe; strengthened by semicircular cartilaginous arches
