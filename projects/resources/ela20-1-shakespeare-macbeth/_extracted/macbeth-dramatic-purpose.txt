Dramatic Purpose
The Techniques of Drama

To hold the attention of an audience and provoke a progressively strong emotional response
within a short period of time, a dramatist must meticulously structure a play. Every conversation,
soliloquy, and action must serve a purpose in the unfolding drama. This is known as dramatic
purpose, a term applied to the importance a scene or speech has in fulfilling a writer’s objective.

What follows is a list of dramatic purposes a dramatist may have for a conversation, soliloquy, or
incident, and may even include a whole scene in a play. The dramatic purpose may be to

a. advance the plot (to start the conflict; depict the victories and defeats of the protagonist;
depict some incident or decision that will precipitate further events in the conflict)

b. indicate setting

c. provide exposition

d. develop character (to reveal the nature of a person)

e. show parallels between characters and plot of a previous or forthcoming scene

f. create atmosphere and impart mood

g. develop theme

h. create suspense

i. create dramatic irony (and heighten tension)

j. provide dramatic relief (to break tension after a particularly dramatic or tense scene)

k. provide comic relief (a type of dramatic relief, comic relief relieves tension by provoking
laughter from the audience)

|. foreshadow coming events
m. arouse emotion in the audience (to deliberately make the audience’s sympathies lie where
the dramatists wants them to lie, e.g. to create pathos; it may direct the audience’s sympathies

toward a certain character, or withdraw sympathy from a character)

Note that a conversation, soliloquy, or incident may have more than one dramatic purpose.
