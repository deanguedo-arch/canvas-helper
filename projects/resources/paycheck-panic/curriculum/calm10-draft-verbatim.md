## ORGANIZING IDEA 1

**Career Exploration:** Purposeful exploration of past and present experiences can support a healthy work-life balance, contribute to self-knowledge, and assist in navigating transitions while pursuing career and educational goals.

### Guiding Question: How can planning support the pursuit of educational and career goals?
**Learning Outcome:** Students investigate how engaging in career planning shapes readiness for the future.

**Knowledge (cluster 1):**
- Career planning involves exploring interests and strengths while building skills that support informed decision making.
- Career planning includes managing transitions to post-secondary education and the world of work, such as:
  - exploring career clusters and online employment tools
  - completing interest inventories
  - creating or updating resumés, cover letters, portfolios, and online employment profiles
  - using a high school program plan to support decision making
  - networking
- Career plans can change based on internal and external factors such as:
  - relocation
  - labour market information
  - evolving interests in career pathways
  - entrepreneurial opportunities
  - available resources and support
  - unforeseen circumstances
- Career portfolios can include career plans, documentation of curricular and extracurricular achievements, credentials and certificates, references, and evidence of work-related experiences.
- High school program plans, based on career research, contribute to high school completion by identifying courses, interests, and strengths.
- High school program plans can support transition to life beyond high school through transition management strategies, including:
  - accessing supports
  - managing responses to change, e.g., emotions or stress
  - adapting to new situations
- Life goals reflect personal values that guide decisions about education, careers, and well-being.
- Setting career goals and life goals involves recognizing how education, career, finances, and well-being are interconnected.
- Effective development of career plans involves considering how career goals and life goals work together and may change over time.
- Career clusters are groups of occupations that share similar skills or fields, such as business administration and finance, skilled trades, and human services.
- Occupational classification systems, such as the National Occupational Classification (NOC), categorize occupations based on detailed skills, training, and job duties.
- Occupational profiles serve as a resource to support career pathway decisions by outlining labour market information such as job specific duties, wage ranges, and educational or training requirements.
- Labour market information for the same occupation can vary across Canada due to regional differences in economic and industry conditions, wages, employment demand, and cost of living.
- Skills and strategies, such as using scheduling tools for time management, developing healthy digital habits, practising stress management techniques, breaking goals into manageable steps, being flexible, and seeking support, can help achieve work-life balance.

**Understanding:** Career planning is influenced by shifting priorities, possibilities, and contexts.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Identify personal career goals and life goals.
- Investigate various career clusters and occupational classification systems.
- Relate career clusters to high school program plans and career plans.
- Create a high school program plan that supports career goals and life goals.
- Identify supports and actions for managing transitions to post-secondary education and employment.
- Explore skills and strategies to achieve work-life balance.
- Discuss internal and external factors that can change career plans.
- Investigate occupational profiles and local labour market information.

**Knowledge (cluster 2):**
- A career pathway is a linear or non-linear progression of formal education, training, and various jobs an individual follows over time as part of their career journey.
- Intentional exploration and analysis of career pathways helps individuals identify personal strengths and opportunities for growth, which can inform career planning and shape future occupational direction.
- Aspirations and career goals evolve with reflection and feedback from others.
- Occupations, including entrepreneurship, have specific knowledge and skill requirements, and researching occupations helps identify required academic qualifications and essential employability skills.

**Understanding:** Career pathway exploration supports informed planning and decision making about future opportunities.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Discuss possible career pathway progressions.
- Reflect on aspirations and career goals related to career pathways of interest.
- Relate personal strengths and opportunities for growth to career pathways of interest.
- Research academic qualifications and employability skill requirements for various occupations.
- Research multiple career pathways for various occupational directions.
- Incorporate feedback from experiences and from others to revise and strengthen career plans and goals.

## ORGANIZING IDEA 2

**Career Opportunities:** Volunteer or work-integrated learning experiences can influence career development and promote world-of-work and community interactions.

### Guiding Question: How do work-related experiences influence career development?
**Learning Outcome:** Students explore work-related experiences related to career pathways of interest.

**Knowledge:**
- Continuous growth involves regularly assessing and refining employability skills and career plans.
- Work-related experiences, e.g., volunteering, job shadowing, community service, field trips, career fairs, and entrepreneurship, provide opportunities to develop and strengthen employability skills aligned with career pathways of interest.
- Ongoing self-assessment and feedback from others can contribute to evaluation and refinement of employability skills.
- Participation in work-related experiences requires an understanding of employability skills, job-specific responsibilities, and workplace safety expectations.
- Contributing to a positive workplace culture and fulfilling workplace responsibilities can influence future opportunities in work-related experiences.
- Work-related experiences in school or the community strengthen familiarity with career pathways of interest.
- Networking with professionals through experiences such as job shadowing, mentorship programs, internships, and mock interviews builds awareness of career pathways.
- Work-related experiences can provide insight into academic qualifications and training requirements for career pathways, including:
  - on-the-job training
  - skilled trades and apprenticeships
  - micro-credentials
  - dual credit programs
  - diplomas and certificates
  - post-secondary degrees
- Different types of employment, e.g., full-time, part-time, temporary, seasonal work, self-employment, or having multiple jobs, have varying impacts on career and financial security, benefits, and lifestyle choices.
- Interview preparation strategies involve knowing how personal skills, experiences, and goals can be communicated effectively during an interview or a mock-interview scenario.

**Understanding:** Work-related experiences build capacity to support informed career pathway decisions.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Assess personal employability skills.
- Discuss ways to network and enhance employability skills related to career pathways of interest.
- Research job-specific responsibilities and safety training required to participate in a work-related experience.
- Participate in a supervised work-related experience in school or the community.
- Reflect on a work-related experience.
- Create or update high school program plans and career plans based on a work-related experience.
- Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of different types of employment.
- Explore interview preparation strategies.

### Guiding Question: How do employment rights and responsibilities support employees in the world of work?
**Learning Outcome:** Students investigate employee rights and responsibilities in the workplace.

**Knowledge:**
- Employers are required by law to protect employee health and safety in the workplace.
- Laws and regulations guide workplace practices, e.g., the Alberta Human Rights Act, Occupational Health and Safety legislation, and the Alberta Employment Standards Code, and can exist at either the federal or provincial level.
- Employment standards can be set by legislation and outline rules related to:
  - payment of earnings
  - hours of work, breaks, and overtime
  - vacations and statutory holidays
  - termination of employment
- Employment standards outline specific restrictions and permissions for youth employment.
- Employers and employees have rights and responsibilities, established by legislation, to promote health, safety, and well-being.
- Employees have the responsibility to be familiar with legislation and regulations that apply to their work, including:
  - safety in the workplace
  - workplace hazards
  - proper training
  - right to refuse dangerous work
  - basic health and safety information
- Legislation provides protection against workplace discrimination, and employers and employees have rights and responsibilities to ensure a discrimination-free workplace.

**Understanding:** Awareness of and advocacy for employee rights and responsibilities promotes health and safety in the workplace.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Investigate current legislation and regulations related to health and safety of employees in the workplace.
- Summarize rights and responsibilities of both employers and employees in the workplace.
- Investigate employment standards.
- Discuss awareness of and advocacy for rights and responsibilities in the workplace.

## ORGANIZING IDEA 3

**Financial Literacy:** Informed financial decision making contributes to the well-being of individuals, groups, and communities.

### Guiding Question: How do economic and financial decisions support personal and financial well-being?
**Learning Outcome:** Students analyze factors and strategies that impact economic and financial decisions.

**Knowledge:**
- Economic decision making involves evaluating how costs, benefits, and trade-offs contribute to achieving life and career goals while considering short-term and long-term consequences of financial decisions.
- Strategies that support economic decision making include:
  - assessing and comparing costs of major purchases
  - researching and evaluating products and services before making a purchase
- Financial decision making involves prioritizing spending to align with economic decisions, including understanding a pay stub, assessing and comparing costs, and researching and evaluating products and services before purchasing.
- Earnings information captured on a pay stub includes current pay period and year-to-date totals and details about additional earnings, deductions, and overtime.
- Financial decision making may be influenced by contextual factors, such as professional financial advice, family, culture, and community.
- Emotions, habits, and attitudes are psychological factors that can affect financial decision making.
- Beliefs and values about money, debt, saving, and credit can differ across cultures, families, communities, and lived experiences.
- Major purchases, such as vehicles, can be made through a variety of purchasing options, including fixed-term contracts, month-to-month agreements, leases, or one-time purchases.
- Overextending is overspending or borrowing more than can be repaid, which can reduce money available for the future and can affect financial security.
- Long-term financial considerations, such as home or vehicle insurance, maintenance, and interest payments, are often associated with major purchases and can lead to financial overextension.
- When choosing between a fixed-term contract or a month-to-month agreement, such as a phone plan, considerations include:
  - level of commitment
  - ease of cancellation
  - financial penalties
  - payment predictability
  - payment frequency

**Understanding:** Effective economic and financial decision making supports independence and builds confidence.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Reflect on contextual factors that influence financial decision making.
- Explain elements on various pay stubs.
- Explore economic decision-making scenarios related to potential future purchases.
- Examine psychological factors that affect financial decision making.
- Explore beliefs and values about money, debt, saving, and credit.
- Examine potential costs related to making a major purchase.
- Investigate the implications of overextending.
- Discuss considerations between a fixed-term contract and month-to-month agreement.

### Guiding Question: In what ways can lending and debt affect financial planning?
**Learning Outcome:** Students explore lending products and debt management as part of financial planning.

**Knowledge (cluster 1):**
- Financial decisions, including those influenced by lifestyle choices, may have both positive and negative consequences and impacts, including accumulation of debt.
- Strategies are used to manage debt and include:
  - making additional lump-sum payments on loans
  - prioritizing paying off high-interest debt
  - adjusting budgets and spending priorities
  - having an emergency fund
- Financial decisions involving risk and low odds of return, such as gambling, lotteries, and online sports betting, can impact an individual's financial security through loss of money or unexpected costs.
- Strategies to save money vary and can impact financial decisions, e.g., bundling services, comparing financial services, products, and fees for low-cost options.
- Financial leveraging is using lending products and accumulating debt for the purpose of investing, and can impact financial security by increasing both financial gain and associated risks.

**Understanding:** Effective debt management requires understanding implications of short-term and long-term financial decisions.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Examine potential consequences and impacts of financial decisions.
- Investigate implications of financial leveraging.
- Explain strategies to manage debt.

**Knowledge (cluster 2):**
- Lending products have different features, including terms and conditions, interest rates or borrowing costs, and application requirements.
- Lending products are a form of credit available in a variety of formats, such as:
  - vehicle loans
  - cash advances
  - lines of credit
  - mortgages
  - student loans
  - buy-now-pay-later arrangements
- Lending products are often designed to appeal to consumer financial behaviours, such as digital money management through apps, and these designs should be considered when evaluating offers, options, and products.
- Lending products can either be secured to collateral, e.g., vehicle loans, or unsecured, e.g., credit cards.
- Lenders calculate and charge borrowers interest by using either a fixed or variable interest rate, which is used to determine the cost of borrowing.
- Interest rates and associated fees vary between banks and other financial institutions and can influence length of repayment and cost of borrowing.
- Amortization is the life of a loan expressed over a period of months or years based on a regular payment schedule.
- Payments made during an amortization period include payment toward the principal, or original loan amount, and the cost of borrowing, or interest.
- Some lending products may require a co-borrower, a person who agrees to be responsible for repayment if the original borrower cannot repay it.
- A mortgage is a loan individuals may apply for through a bank or financial institution to purchase a property.
- When applying for a mortgage, multiple factors are taken into consideration, such as:
  - income confirmation requirements
  - down payment
  - loan amortization and repayment
  - credit history, e.g., credit score
  - lump-sum or prepayment options

**Understanding:** Effective use of lending products can support individuals in meeting their needs and goals at various stages of life.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Explore how interest rates on lending products can influence debt, debt repayment, and amortization.
- Compare fixed and variable interest rates.
- Explore implications of amortization over the life of a loan.
- Investigate the purpose and application requirements of various lending products.
- Compare cost of borrowing in relation to principal amount, interest rate, fees, and amortization, using a financial calculation tool.
- Explore features of a mortgage.

### Guiding Question: How can financial plans support long-term goals?
**Learning Outcome:** Students examine the role of saving, investing, and strategies for future financial planning.

**Knowledge:**
- Financial plans outline steps and strategies, e.g., budgeting, to achieve financial goals and may change depending on circumstances and through different stages of life, e.g., starting a job or retiring.
- Saving and investing are both pathways to financial growth, where saving is typically money set aside for short-term and unanticipated costs, and investing involves intentional planning to meet long-term goals.
- When deciding whether to save, invest, or pay off debt, individuals should be aware of opportunity cost, which is the cost of making one financial decision over another.
- Financial planning supports and services are available through professional organizations, financial institutions, and government agencies.
- A financial plan can include various types of savings and investment products, e.g., mutual funds and other investments, which can be held in multiple registered and non-registered investment accounts.
- Savings and investment products exist to meet the needs and goals of consumers, and each have their own specific features, terms and conditions, risks, and rates of return.
- Non-registered investment accounts allow for investment of savings in investment products, with money earned being subject to income tax.
- Registered investment accounts allow for investment of savings that can potentially offset income taxes, including:
  - tax-free savings accounts, TFSAs
  - registered retirement savings plans, RRSPs
  - first home savings accounts, FHSAs
  - registered education savings plans, RESPs
- Factors used to assess the alignment between personal financial goals and investment products and accounts include:
  - purpose and objective of savings or investment
  - investment duration
  - stage of life
  - risk tolerance
  - fees and associated costs
  - return on investment
  - investor's level of knowledge
- Financial calculation tools and simulators can provide support in comparing financial products and services.
- Market fluctuations affect the value of investments, with some investments being more volatile and appreciating or depreciating over time.
- Investments, e.g., cryptocurrency and bonds, vary in financial risk, uncertainty, and potential benefit.
- Inflation refers to the decrease of purchasing power over time relative to the price of goods and services, and inflation can have an impact on the value of savings and investments.

**Understanding:** Effective financial planning aligns saving and investment decisions with financial goals, while adapting to risk and changing conditions.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Explore scenarios for how financial plans can change throughout different stages of life.
- Examine financial planning supports and services for various circumstances.
- Investigate strategies for saving and investing.
- Research types of investment products and investment accounts.
- Analyze investment products and accounts for alignment with personal financial goals.
- Evaluate a financial plan based on a scenario.
- Engage with financial calculation tools and simulators.
- Explain how market fluctuations impact the value of investments.
- Investigate how inflation influences the value of savings and investments.
- Discuss benefits, uncertainty, and risks of different types of investments.

### Guiding Question: In what ways can protecting personal information impact financial security?
**Learning Outcome:** Students examine strategies for protecting personal information.

**Knowledge:**
- An individual's digital identity is created by a collection of data based on online transactions and activities, which can be called a digital footprint.
- Financial security can be impacted by an individual's digital footprint; e.g., publicly available personal information may lead to identity theft, fraud, scams, and financial loss.
- Consumer protection laws outline consumer rights and safeguards that help individuals recognize and respond to unfair or deceptive practices affecting financial security.
- Fraud involves the deceptive or improper use of personal information to unlawfully gain financial or other benefits and can pose a risk to financial security.
- Scams are a type of fraud and can be classified as investment, job, emergency, online purchase, or romance scams.
- Protecting personal information can enhance financial security and includes an awareness of the indicators of potential fraud, such as:
  - asking for personal information
  - demands for money transfers or payments through unusual methods
  - a sense of urgency or secrecy
  - offers that seem too good to be true
  - messages or websites that impersonate trusted sources
- The risk of being affected by fraud can be reduced by engaging in strategies to protect personal information, including:
  - keeping devices and passwords secure
  - regularly reviewing credit card and bank transactions
  - reporting signs of phishing and spam from unknown links or attachments
  - sharing personal information only when necessary
  - strengthening privacy settings on social media
  - healthy digital literacy habits
  - educating others
- If experiencing a fraud, it is essential to notify necessary authorities and agencies, e.g., law enforcement, financial institutions, and credit bureau reporting agencies.

**Understanding:** Protecting personal information can enhance financial security.

**Skills & Procedures:**
- Investigate the impact of digital footprints on financial security.
- Explore scenarios involving Alberta's consumer protection laws.
- Recognize types and indicators of fraud.
- Classify various scams.
- Investigate strategies to protect personal information.
- Relate the protection of personal information to enhancing financial security.
- Research healthy digital literacy habits to protect personal information.
- Analyze scenarios of frauds or scams.
- Identify authorities and agencies to contact in case of fraud.
