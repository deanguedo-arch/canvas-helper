The Crucible – Act Two
Arthur Miller

Responding Critically
Answer the following items completely.

1. What  evidence  can  be  found  that  Abigail  and  her  party  of  accusers,  including  Mary
Warren,  have  undergone  a  change  in  status  since  the  beginning  of  the  play?  Use
examples to support your answer.

2. What is the central conflict that John Proctor faces?

3. Does the Reverend Hale still believe as firmly in the truth of the accusations, or is he
beginning to have some doubts? Cite examples to support your answer.

4. What commandment does Proctor fail to recollect? Why is this significant?

5. Someone  once  said  that  the  best  defense is  a  good  offense.  Do  you  think  that  John
Proctor  may  be  hinting  at  this  when  he  asks  why  the  accusers  are  always  innocent
now? Explain your answer.

6. Identify a voice of reason in Act Two. Argue your answer with careful evidence.

7. At the end of Act Two, what is learned about Mary Warren’s motives?

8. The theme of human cruelty versus righteousness is particularly important in this act.
Go  through  Act  Two  and  select  three  quotes  that  represent  this  theme.  Critically
explore these quotes in a five-paragraph essay, discussing what they reveal about the
characters and plot of The Crucible.

Ongoing Activity (I)

a. his beliefs about the court system
b. witchcraft, and his ability to identify witches
