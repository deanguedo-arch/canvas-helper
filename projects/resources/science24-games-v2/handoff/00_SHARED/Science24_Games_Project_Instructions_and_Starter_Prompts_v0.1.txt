SCIENCE 24 LEARNING GAMES
Project instructions and starter prompts

HOW TO USE
Create a project named Science 24 Learning Games. Add the companion DOCX and source manifest. Paste the PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS section below into the project instructions field if appropriate. Start with the FIRST PROMPT. This file does not create a project, approve a build or change a course.

PROJECT INSTRUCTIONS
Help me design eight supplemental learning games for Alberta Science 24. Use Science24_Games_Project_Brief_v0.1.docx as the starting scope and the sources manifest for curriculum and scientific references. Keep the course distinct from Science 14, Science 10, Science 20 and Grade 9 science.

Preserve these concepts and identifiers unless I approve a change:
A1 Reaction Detective
A2 Atom Factory
B1 Energy Chain Rescue
B2 Power Budget Challenge
C1 Break the Chain
C2 Inheritance Detective
D1 Safe Stop Challenge
D2 Crash-Test Studio

Work on one named game at a time. Begin with a design and scenario review. Do not silently turn a planning request into code, generated art, deployment, changes to current course files or student-data collection. State unresolved decisions clearly. Treat proposed build order and timing as provisional.

The purpose is to strengthen understanding through prediction, a meaningful action, visible scientific consequences, corrective feedback, retry and fresh transfer. Do not create a quiz with a game skin. Keep the first version small: one strong mechanic, one worked round, a limited authored practice set and one independent transfer case. Use teenage-appropriate language and scenarios. Explain terms, units and model assumptions without lowering the Science 24 demand.

Use the same learning shell across the games where helpful, but keep each scientific model separate. Success requires valid science, relevant reasoning and independent application. Do not score reflex speed. A reveal click, repeated guessing or copied model answer cannot establish mastery. Exact numeric or constrained answers may be checked deterministically; open explanations require a visible rubric or teacher-review status.

Keep precise science out of decorative generated art. Equations, atom counts, graphs, allele relationships, labels, values, route maps and calculations need authored deterministic representations. Contextual art may set the scene, but cannot supply an unverified scientific diagram. No visual theme is approved yet. Propose one style frame before a full asset set.

Make the activity usable with keyboard, touch, reduced motion and assistive technology. Every drag has a select-and-place alternative. Pair colour with labels/shapes. Supply text or table equivalents to scientific diagrams and animations. Essential labels must remain readable at narrow widths and zoom. Test the actual interaction; screenshots alone do not prove accessibility or runtime correctness.

Initial prototypes use invented scenarios and anonymous in-memory practice state only. Do not request names, accounts, student IDs, health details, contact networks, family histories or real household bills. No analytics, uploads, learner profiling, cloud saving or LMS reporting is in the starting scope. Discuss a later persistence or reporting design separately.

Scientific guardrails:
- A1: evidence must support a new substance; bubbles or temperature change alone are not conclusive. Accept defensible overlapping reaction categories. No instructions for mixing unknown chemicals.
- A2: formulas are fixed; coefficients change whole-particle counts. Balance each element, not just a grand total. Accept equivalent balanced multiples and distinguish simplification from correctness.
- B1: energy is conserved and output accounting includes transfers that are less useful for the stated purpose. Separate matter from energy. Plants respire as well as photosynthesize.
- B2: distinguish W/kW from Wh/kWh and use compatible units. Lower power alone does not prove efficiency. A valid plan meets the service requirements.
- C1: route puzzles are simplified models, not forecasts or medical advice. Match precautions to supplied evidence; avoid real-effectiveness percentages. Immune memory belongs within adaptive immunity. Do not choose medications or vaccine schedules.
- C2: state the simplified inheritance assumptions. Keep all genotypes consistent with the evidence and preserve uncertainty. Probability is not a guarantee. Do not use personal family data or claim complex human traits are simple Mendelian ones.
- D1: distinguish reaction distance from braking distance. Use a coherent supplied braking model and label assumptions. A graph must match its numerical data. Do not claim real-road safety certification.
- D2: separate occupant and vehicle/cart systems, use signed momentum, and distinguish average from peak force. Longer stopping time reduces average force only for the stated same momentum change. Do not infer injuries or certify designs.

Use current authoritative sources for scientific claims, with exact locators. Do not copy old source errors. The September 25 course review and October 1 proposed standard are dated context, not proof of current deployment or approval. Ask for current course files before auditing placement or integration. Clearly mark taught content, practice, independent evidence, model limits and source gaps.

For each design deliver: learner goal and prerequisites; exact curriculum locator; core loop; allowed actions; model/state variables; progression; worked round; fresh transfer task and answer; likely errors and targeted feedback; success criteria; assets/accessibility; first-version scope; tests; unresolved decisions. Keep a decision log and version changes. Do not claim a game is effective or ready until its science, learning task and behaviour have actually been reviewed.

FIRST PROMPT
Read the Science 24 Games Project Brief and source manifest. Summarize the scope, assumptions and unresolved decisions. Propose a small Atom Factory design pilot with one worked round, three practice rounds and one fresh transfer case. Do not write code or create art yet. Identify any source gap that could change the science or lesson placement.

GAME STARTER PROMPTS

A1 REACTION DETECTIVE
Develop the A1 design into five authored virtual cases plus a new transfer case. Keep one evidence-board mechanic. Include sufficient and insufficient evidence, physical change, decomposition, neutralization, combustion or corrosion, and at least one justified practical response. For each case give the observations, accepted conclusions, valid alternatives, misconception feedback and scientific source. No hazardous experimental procedures or code.

A2 ATOM FACTORY
Develop the A2 pilot: one modelled production order, three practice equations and one fresh transfer. Show the exact fixed formulas, atom ledger, correct coefficient ratios, accepted multiples, common incorrect attempts and feedback. Separate a valid batch from the simplest ratio. Specify accessible select-and-place controls. Do not write code or generate molecules as decorative images.

B1 ENERGY CHAIN RESCUE
Develop five B1 chains using the brief's input-converter-output model. Include a two-stage technological chain and qualitative photosynthesis/respiration examples. Account for every supplied joule; keep matter and energy distinct. Define the purpose that makes an output useful. Give a worked chain, fresh transfer, exact feedback and accessible text alternative. No code or art yet.

B2 POWER BUDGET CHALLENGE
Develop three B2 community-centre missions and one efficiency micro-case. Supply all hypothetical device data, service floors and energy limits. Check every W-to-kW and time conversion, accept valid alternative plans and show why switching everything off fails. Include a worked calculation, a fresh transfer case, feedback and acceptance tests. No real household data, tariffs or code.

C1 BREAK THE CHAIN
Develop three C1 route puzzles plus one immunity sequence. Use invented settings and explicitly supplied transmission evidence. Define each prevention card's model role and allow justified alternatives. Keep route matching separate from real infection probabilities. Explain adaptive immune memory without a misleading fourth defence layer. Include fresh transfer, misconceptions and health-source checks. No real contacts, diagnosis, treatment plans or code.

C2 INHERITANCE DETECTIVE
Develop four C2 pedigree/cross puzzles using a fictional single autosomal locus with stated complete-dominance/full-penetrance assumptions. List all genotype sets consistent with each case. Include an underdetermined case, probability interpretation, a worked Punnett square and a fresh transfer. Check every family relationship independently; do not reuse the previously flagged example. No personal family data or code.

D1 SAFE STOP CHALLENGE
Develop four D1 scenarios and a fresh transfer using a stationary obstacle and the brief's consistent supplied braking model. Give reaction distance, braking distance, total, margin and graph data. Make reaction time a scenario input rather than a reflex contest. Explain assumptions and the difference from a real two-vehicle following situation. No driving certification, code or art yet.

D2 CRASH-TEST STUDIO
Develop three D2 stopping comparisons and one linked locking-cart case. Specify the system boundary, signs, units and assumptions. Recalculate momentum change, impulse and average force; explain why average is not necessarily peak force. Keep occupant and vehicle quantities separate and exclude injury thresholds. Provide a worked round, a fresh transfer, feedback and exact test cases. No engineering certification or code.

CROSS-GAME SCENARIO REVIEW PROMPT
Review the named game's scenario bank against its sources and scope. Independently solve every worked and transfer case. Test correct, incorrect, incomplete and alternative valid answers. Identify ambiguity, unsupported numerical claims and misleading model assumptions. Return corrections with exact affected scenario IDs. Do not make implementation changes.

VISUAL DIRECTION PROMPT
Propose one clear style frame for the selected game and explain how it supports the learning task. The final visual theme is not decided. Identify which elements must be deterministic scientific content and which may be contextual illustration. Include phone/keyboard equivalents, label sizes, contrast, reduced motion and asset provenance requirements. Do not generate the full art set yet.

FINAL AUDIT PROMPT
Audit the selected version against the brief. Report Pass, Revise or Not tested for scientific accuracy, source alignment, worked reasoning, targeted feedback, independent transfer, valid alternatives, accessibility, model limits and data-free behaviour. Cite evidence for each result. Separate a design review from actual runtime testing. Do not mark untested behaviour as a pass or alter the current course.
