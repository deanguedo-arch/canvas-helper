Learn · Science 24 · Unit B · Lesson 8

Efficiency and conservation choices

How do you judge whether a device uses energy well?

Learning goal

Compare devices by efficiency and choose actions that reduce wasted energy.

Before you begin

Energy is conserved: output energy equals input energy, split into useful and wasted.

Useful out, divided by total in

Efficiency is a comparison: useful energy output ÷ total energy input, usually as a percent. A bulb that turns 100 J of electrical energy into 30 J of light is 30% efficient. The other 70 J is not “lost” — it left as heat and sound you did not want.

That one idea reorganizes every device you own. The question is never “does it use energy?” It is “how much of what I paid for became what I wanted?”

Pattern 4 — Sticker callout (decision point) What changed: the “which would you pick?” decision moment is pulled out of the paragraph flow into a sticker-style callout placed at the decision point, mid-lesson — not stacked with the guide cards at the top. When to use: max one per lesson section, only for genuine decision/choice moments (“which device wastes less?”, “what would you measure first?”). Do not: use for core instruction, definitions, or warnings — those keep their existing treatments. Markup: <aside class="s24x-sticker"> with a <strong> label line.
Your call

Two bulbs give the same brightness. One draws 60 W, the other 9 W. Which one wastes less — and what numbers prove it?

Answer with the efficiency comparison, not a feeling: useful output over total input, for each bulb. The practice set below uses the same move three times before the required check asks you to make it alone.

Try it: read the split

Use the diagram on the right for all three questions. For each device: (1) name the useful output, (2) name the wasted outputs, (3) compute the efficiency.

1. The bulb in the diagram takes in 100 J electrical. Useful light out: 30 J. Efficiency = (work it out before peeking)

2. A motor takes in 200 J and delivers 140 J of motion. The rest becomes heat and sound. Efficiency?

3. Which device wastes a larger share of its input — and does the larger share mean larger waste in joules?

Answers: 1) 30%. 2) 70%. 3) The bulb wastes the larger share (70% vs 30%), but compare joules before judging total waste.

What stays the same

The required check, the practice items, the save behavior, the vocabulary buttons, the “How to complete this lesson” guide and the textbook band are untouched. These four patterns only change how visuals and video meet the learner — never what is assessed or how progress is recorded.

Pattern checklist for Codex (self-verify per lesson) 1) Opener: one key visual, lesson question directly under it. 2) Video (if moved): at point of need with a “watch-for” prompt; data-video-card verbatim; progress guarantee kept. 3) Sticky figure: max one per lesson, only beside the practice set that references it; figure markup unchanged; labels ≥14px at phone width. 4) Sticker: max one per section, decision moments only. 5) Every figure keeps caption + credit + alt text + “View larger”. 6) No IDs, save keys, check structure, or assessment boundaries changed. 7) Phone width (390px): no horizontal scroll, opener/sticker/figure stack cleanly. 8) 200% zoom: no clipped text. 9) Keyboard: all new controls focusable with visible focus.
