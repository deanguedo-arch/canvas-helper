Judgement Sampling
Judgment sampling is a common non-probability method where researchers select the sample based on their judgment.  For example, researchers may decide to draw the entire sample from one northern town although the target population includes all northern towns.  When using this method, the researcher must be confident that the chosen sample is truly representative of the entire population (in other words, one northern town is very similar to all other northern towns).
Snowball Sampling
Snowball sampling is a special non-probability method used when the desired sample characteristic is rare.  It may be extremely difficult or cost prohibitive to locate respondents in these situations.  With this method, existing study subjects are used to recruit more subjects into the sample.  Although this technique may reduce subject recruitment costs, bias is introduced into the study because the technique decreases the likelihood that the sample represents a reasonable cross-section of the population.
