Case Study B
One hundred forty (140) male college students were asked to read a summary of a
fictitious date between Brad and Barb.  The students were divided into two groups of 70
people each.  The summary described going to a movie and gave much detail about what
Brad did on the date.  The only difference in the descriptions that each group was given
related to the amount of popcorn Brad ate during the movie.  In one version of the
summary Brad ate “a couple of handfuls” of his popcorn.  In the other version Brad ate
“almost all” of his popcorn.  Researchers asked the two groups of men how much weight
(in pounds) they thought Brad could bench press.  Students who read that Brad ate
almost all of his popcorn imagined Brad lifting, on average, 21 more pounds than Brad who
only ate a couple of handfuls of his popcorn.

3.Describe the importance of the control group in an experiment.(2 marks)
Good experiments compare different groups of trials with each other.   This
comparison is done using a Control group to ensure that the changes observed are
caused by the independent variable.
4. Larry performs an experiment on his two best friends.  Explain why his sample size is too
small.  (2 marks)
More than one test subject is used for much the same reason more than one trial is
performed.  Scientists want results that are not considered flukes or anomalies.  The
sample size must be large enough to rule out such extremes or abnormalities in
individuals.
