Part Two:  Written-Response Questions(12 marks)
1.Is it possible to score in the 65th percentile yet not pass an exam?  Explain.(2 marks)
A percentile is not the same as a percentage.  If Lance scored 32 of a possible 50
marks on a test for introversion, his percentage score would be 64%.  To determine
the percentage, you divide 32 by 50 then multiply the quotient by 100.  If Lance
wanted to compare his score to the scores of others on the same test, he would use
his percentile.  If 70 percent of the other scores were less than Lance's score (less
than 32 out of 50) then Lance's score would be the 70th percentile.  To summarize,
percentiles compare your score to the scores of others who have taken the same test.
2.Define reliability as it pertains to scientific experimentation and state why reliability is
important.(4 marks)
Reliability refers to the results of an experiment being obtained that are similar when
the experiment is repeated by others.  The three types of reliability are test-retest,
internal, and inter-rater. The conclusions reached are considered reliable if they can
be transferred to similar situations in other parts of the world.
3.Define negative feedback and provide an example of a system that uses negative
feedback.  Do not repeat an example provided in the course content.(4 marks)
Negative feedback tends to reduce the future output of a system. Examples should not
include a home heating system, personal heating system (body), or human hormone
systems.
4. Can the results of an experiment be ascribedto groups other than those that participated
in the experiment?  Explain.(2 marks)
No - Researchers discovered that the results from studies that only focus on one
subset of individuals could not simply be transferred over to other demographic
groups.  How women, children, and members of other races react to drugs, therapies,
suggestions, and other treatment methodologies is often different from how a
Caucasian adult male reacts so you could not transfer results from Caucasian adult
males to other demographic groups..

1.  To what do the terms of central tendency (mode, median, and mean) refer?
Central tendency refers to how clustered a group of numbers is.  Mathematically,
central tendency refers to the average in a set of data.
2.  What is the mode?
The mode is the number that occurs most often in a set of data.  To determine the
mode, you should first organize the numbers in order of size. 7 78 8 89 10 10 11 12
Mode = 8
3.  What is the median?
The median is the number in the middle of the set of data after you arrange the
numbers in order of size.  In many cases, you will have the same number of values on
the left and right of the median value.  If, however, there is an even number of data
points, you will have two numbers as your median and the values must be averaged.
7 7 8 8 89 10 10 11 12median: 8 + 9 = 17, 17 ÷2 = 8.5
4.  What is the mean?
The mean is the value obtained when you add all the values in a data set and then
divide the sum (total) by the number of values in the data set.
Mean: 7 + 7 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 9 + 10 + 10 + 11 + 12  = 90, 90 ÷ 10 = 9

1.  To what do the terms of variability (range, variance, standard deviation) refer?
The measures of variability refer to how spread out a set of numbers is. The terms
variability, distribution, and dispersion are synonyms and refer to the spread of a data
set.
2.  What is the range?
The least complicated measure of variability is the range.  The range is the difference
between the highest value in a data set and the lowest value in a data set.
Range: 12 - 7 = 5
