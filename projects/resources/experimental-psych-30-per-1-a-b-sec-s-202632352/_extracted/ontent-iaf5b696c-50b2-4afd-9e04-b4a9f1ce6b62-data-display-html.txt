Communicating Results
After scientists have outlined the procedures used in their experiments, selected the sample populations, and tested the variables, they are left with raw data and observations.  They must then organize and tabulate the data into a format that allows interpretation and analysis between the variables.
The data can be presented in various formats including observation checklists, data tables (charts), graphs, maps, flow charts, and illustrations.
The format in which data is displayed influences the interpretation of the results.  Although some scientists have deliberately lied about or exaggerated the results of experiments or they have displayed data in ways to skew the interpretation, ethical scientists try to present their findings clearly and truthfully.  Each format of data display has strengths and weaknesses.  In this course, you will learn about common display formats -- graphs and tables or charts.
