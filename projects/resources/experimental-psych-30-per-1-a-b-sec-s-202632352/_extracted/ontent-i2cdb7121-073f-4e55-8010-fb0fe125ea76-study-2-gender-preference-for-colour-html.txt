Study Two:  Gender Preference for Colour
Theories based on stereotypes, opinions, and attitudes have been prevalent over the centuries.  One study, summarized in the journal Current Biology, indicates that one stereotype might have some scientific basis.  Researchers asked young men and women to select as quickly as possible their preferred colour from a sequence of paired coloured swatches.  Although blue was an overall favourite, women had a preference for the red end of the spectrum making pinks and purples more popular than other colours.
The results of the experiment were such that researchers were able to predict the gender of a test subject based on the colour choices.  To determine whether the results were culturally based instead of biologically based, people from non-Caucasian races were tested.  The results support the notion that biology (genetics) may have an influence on colour preference.  One of the researchers, Anya Hurlbert, theorized the female preference for red could originate from "humans' hunter-gatherer days when women - the primary gatherers - would have benefited from an ability to key in on ripe, red fruits."  The researchers plan to test infants to gain further insight into colour preferences.
