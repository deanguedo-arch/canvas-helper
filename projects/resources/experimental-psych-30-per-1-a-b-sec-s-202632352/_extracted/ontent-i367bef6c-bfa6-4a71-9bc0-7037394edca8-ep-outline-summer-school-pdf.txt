Course Description:
The focus of Experimental Psychology 30 is to provide an overview of the scientific approach to understanding human
behaviour and mental processes. The aim of the course is to familiarize students with methods and concepts used in
experimental psychology, including the key steps in the psychological research process. Throughout the course
students  are exposed to a variety of past and current psychological research and a practice project is completed to
experience  how the scientific method is applied in psychology. Students will be able to develop their skill to think more
like a  psychologist about information encountered in day-to-day living. Experimental Psychology 30 is a 3-credit
course.

Assessment:
Assignments are considered formative, meaning you have an opportunity to apply feedback from the teacher to
improve your achievement prior to completing the unit summative assessments. Summative assessments have
two  parts: Part A consists of multiple choice, true and false, and matching questions to demonstrate knowledge of
the  lesson content; and Part B consists of one of the three written responses: WRI (responding to a
straightforward  question – explanation and support, communication), WRII (three source analysis – interpretation
of sources,  defense of position, communication), or WRIII (responding to others’ views on a particular topic
exploration and  analysis, defense of position, communication).
As per EIPS Administrative Procedure 360: Learning Assessment Students
will:
1.1. be active learners demonstrating their learning through opportunities provided by the teacher;
1.2. complete tasks to the best of their ability to demonstrate their achievement; and
1.3. fulfill the requirements of a missed summative assessment or demonstrate a different level of understanding through a
reassessment opportunity.
