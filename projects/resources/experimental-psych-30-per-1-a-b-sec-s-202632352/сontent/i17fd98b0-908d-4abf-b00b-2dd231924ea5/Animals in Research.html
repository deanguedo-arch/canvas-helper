Animals in Research

Non-human animals continue to be used in experiments, many of which would be considered torture-like by some. 

Humans have struggled to determine what constitutes acceptable parameters for research with animals.  In first world countries, humans can no longer experiment on other humans without first doing extensive tests on "lower" animals. 

For example, a drug company may have developed a medication to help reduce high blood pressure.  Before the medication can be mass marketed, the medication has to be tested on humans.  Before the medication can be tested on humans, it has to be tested on "lower" animals.  Researchers might have to obtain mice, for example, induce high blood pressure in them, then give the new medication to the mice and record the results.  If the results are positive, showing that blood pressure is reduced, then further tests are performed to confirm the results.  Only after years of development and testing are humans given the drug.  If these tests show positive results, the drug may be approved for general use.  From inception to market, it takes years for a drug to be made available for use.
