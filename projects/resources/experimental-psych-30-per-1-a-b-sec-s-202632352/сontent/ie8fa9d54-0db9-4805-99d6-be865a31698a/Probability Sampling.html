Probability Sampling

Random sampling is the purest form of probability sampling.  Each member of the population has an equal and known chance of being selected.  When the population is very large, however, to identify every member is difficult thus bias is introduced into the sample.

Systematic sampling is often used instead of random sampling and may also be referred to as the Nth name selection technique.  After the required sample size has been calculated, every Nth record is selected from a list of population members.  

For example, a researcher could select every 12th name from the white pages of a telephone book to contact for an opinion poll.  Depending on the size of the town or city, the researcher might need to contact every 5th person or every 50th person.  The formula to determine the sampling interval (5th, 12th, or 50th) is:

Calculating the Sampling Interval

To illustrate how to calculate the sampling interval, please review the scenario below.

Scenario:  You are conducting research about shopping behaviour.  After completing sample size calculations, you find you need to observe 63 people.  The small town grocery store where you live serves approximately 900 customers per day.  

Using the formula above, your calculation would be:

k = N ÷ n

k = 900 ÷ 63

k = 14.285

This means you will include every 14th customer in your sample.  Please note that you do not need to start your interval count with the first customer.  The first customer you observe should be randomly selected from the first 14 people.  After this, you begin your regular interval count of every 14th customer -- that is, 14 after the first customer you interview.
