Transferability of Research Results

Researchers in the late twentieth century designed experiments that often focused on one subset of individuals and then assumed the results could be generalized (applied) to everyone.  For example, much research was done using Caucasian adult males. The results were then "carried over" or ascribed to women, children, and men who were not Caucasian.

Researchers discovered that the results could not simply be transferred over to other demographic groups.  How women, children, and members of other races react to drugs, therapies, suggestions, and other treatment methodologies is often different from how a Caucasian adult male reacts.

With psychological research, any study that uses only one subset of individuals (such as Caucasian males), the results must be ascribed only to Caucasian males, not to children, women, or members of other races.  In some cases, a medication that works for an adult does not work for a child; in some cases it even has the opposite effect.  In other cases, sometimes a smaller dose or larger dose of a medication has the same effect on a child as a standard adult dose has on an adult.

Fortunately, current researchers try to take into account the fact that marked differences occur between adults, children, and adolescents in addition to men, women, and people of various cultural and racial groups.
