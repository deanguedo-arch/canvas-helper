Measures of Variability
The terms of central tendency refer to how clustered (central) a group of numbers is.

The measures of variability refer to how spread out a set of numbers is.  The terms variability, distribution, and dispersion are synonyms and refer to the spread of a data set.  The most commonly used measures of variability (or spread) are the range, percentile, interquartile range, variance, and standard deviation.

The least complicated measure of variability is the range.  The range is the difference between the highest value in a data set and the lowest value in a data set.

In the weather example, the range is 9 ºC

This is the difference between 19 ºC (the highest value) and 10 ºC (the lowest value).


To understand interquartile range one must first understand percentiles.  A percentile is not the same as a percentage.  If Lance scored 32 of a possible 50 marks on a test for introversion, his percentage score would be 64%.  To determine the percentage, you divide 32 by 50 then multiply the quotient by 100.  If Lance wanted to compare his score to the scores of others on the same test, he would use his percentile.  If 70 percent of the other scores were less than Lance's score (less than 32 out of 50) then Lance's score would be the 70th percentile.  To summarize, percentiles compare your score to the scores of others who have taken the same test.

In any set of data the total number of percentiles is 100.  Researchers may divide the total number of percentiles into 10 equal parts called deciles, or they can divide the total number of percentiles into four equal parts called quartiles.  If a person's score was in the first decile, he or she would have scored less than 90% of others on the same test.  If a person scored in the forth quartile, he or she scored higher than 75% of the population who took the test.  The median of all percentiles is the 50th percentile.  The difference between the first quartile (the 25th percentile) and the third quartile (the 75th percentile) is the interquartile range.  This range contains the middle 50% of the scores in a distribution.
