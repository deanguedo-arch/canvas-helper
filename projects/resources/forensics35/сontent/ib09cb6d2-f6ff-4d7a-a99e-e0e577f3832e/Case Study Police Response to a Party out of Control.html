Case Study: Police Response to a "Party out of Control"

On a warm autumn night, hundreds of people filed in and out of a raucous hall party, located near the downtown core of a large Canadian city. A local rugby association had organized the event in the spirit of celebration as the season ended, but shortly after midnight, the party began breaking into a series of tense confrontations between members of rival rugby teams. 

As a night of heavy drinking began to take its toll, taunts and jeers were replaced with threatening gestures and insults. Soon, the first fistfight broke out among the large number of intoxicated people milling about the hall. Various people attending the party made several emergency calls to police as the party continued to spill outside. 

Several patrol officers showed up minutes later, but they realized quickly that the crowd, numbering in the hundreds, was highly agitated and unpredictable, a situation that presented an unknown risk to the officers. Additional police units were requested, but because it was a busy night, only a few additional officers were available to assist in crowd control. 

The primary objective for responding officers was to contain the crowd and escort everyone out of the hall. However, the over-consumption of alcohol and late hour meant that fights would likely be occurring sporadically. Responding officers moved into the hall in an effort to clear out remaining patrons, only to find that the crowd was rapidly developing a mob mentality. The sudden outbreak of several fistfights emphasized the severe nature of the problem and requiring swift action by police to prevent innocent people from being injured. 

A police supervisor responded quickly to the scene after being alerted to the urgent nature of this call. He scanned the immediate area upon arrival and realized that a riot could develop if something was not done to quell the uproarious crowd. From the trunk of his police vehicle, he obtained an oversized can of pepper spray commonly referred to as a "fogger". Rather than deploying a narrow and limited stream of oleoresin capsicum (OC) spray, as was the case with personal issue pepper spray commonly carried by patrol officers, this device is capable of emitting a dense cloud of OC spray up to a range of 10 to 15 metres. 

The supervisor fastened the large can of OC spray to his equipment belt and walked around the crowd, entering the hall through a side door. He located several officers who were ushering people out of the hall. A group of several large males within the crowd suddenly began fighting outside the main doors. The supervisor, realizing that the situation could quickly spiral out of control if he did not take appropriate action, decided to utilize the fogger to break up the fight. He detached the fogger from his belt and pointed it at the group of combative males, announcing his presence in a loud voice. Then, the supervisor instructed the crowd to disperse. However, people were too intoxicated to notice. Consequently, the supervisor discharged the fogger, spraying a long burst of OC spray into the group of males who were still fighting outside the hall. 

The effect was immediate. People began moving away from the area, coughing and sneezing as the cloud of OC spray drifted about. The crowd dispersed quickly to a manageable size, and several officers were able to separate the group of males without further incident because they were now in various stages of incapacitation due to the OC spray. Several of the males were arrested for causing 
a disturbance and were transported to a nearby police facility for decontamination, which involved flushing their eyes with water. Everyone else, who had been in the area of the OC spray deployment recovered within minutes and left the area in a more peaceful manner. Police were able to clear remaining patrons from the hall and surrounding area within 30 minutes. 

The police supervisor who deployed the OC spray completed a report describing his involvement in this "use of force" situation and submitted it to the investigating officer.
