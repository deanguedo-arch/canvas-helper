The Novel
The Big Picture

A. THE NOVEL IS PEOPLE.
1. Identify the major characters and the subordinate characters.
2. Detailed study of characters should include:
a. Who is the protagonist of the novel?
b. Point  out  how  the  protagonist  is  developed,  what  its  motivation  is,  and  explain  its
attitude, philosophy, and personality traits.
c. Characterize the remaining people in the novel in a similar fashion.

B. THE NOVEL IS SETTING.
1. What is the geographical setting of the novel? Describe and provide a map if possible.
2. What is the historical setting of the novel? Explain.
3. What is the atmosphere of the novel? Explain.
4. Discuss the importance of setting in the novel. Remember, setting is often used for more than
a physical backdrop to the action.
a. Setting may suggest mood.
b. Setting may help delineate character.
c. Setting may reveal the novelist’s attitude.

C. THE NOVEL IS ACTION.
1. Discuss the plot of the novel. Include a thorough analysis of the following elements:
a. What are the major conflicts in the novel? Name them and discuss their significance
and interrelationships.
b. What are the major complicating incidents in the novel?
c. How  is  suspense  developed  and  maintained?  Commonly  used  devices  include
conflict, mystery, an apparently insoluble problem, foreshadowing, and a see-saw of
victories and defeats for the protagonist.
d. What is the climax of the novel? What is the outcome? Explain.
2. Are there subplots in the novel?
a. Describe them.
b. How are they related to the main plot?
c. What purpose do they serve in the novel?
3. How is the plot developed?
a. Do any coincidences occur in the novel? How does the novelist make them credible?
b. Explain the role fate plays in the novel.
c. Classify the novel as either comic or tragic.

D. THE NOVEL IS TRUTH.
1. A good novel conveys universality.
a. What is the controlling purpose of the novel? Explain.
b. Does the novel contain supplementary themes? Elaborate on them.
2. It  is comforting  to  learn  from  the  mistakes  of  others  through  the  reading  of  novels.  What
mistakes are evident in this novel and what can be learned from them? In other words, does
the novel contain a moral?

E. THE NOVEL IS STYLE.
1. What point of view is used to tell the story? Explain its advantages.
2. What is the author’s tone (its attitude toward its material)?
3. Does novelist create pathos or bathos in its novel? Explain.
4. Does the author use any of the following literary devices?
a. Imagery: Are figures of speech or vivid description used? If so, for what purpose(s)?
b. Symbolism:  Are  symbols  used  to  reinforce  meaning  or  to  add  explosive  force  to  the
narrative? Give examples and discuss their importance.
c. Irony: Discuss the use of irony. Give examples and explain their significance.
