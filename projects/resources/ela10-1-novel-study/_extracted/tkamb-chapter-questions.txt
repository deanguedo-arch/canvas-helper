CHAPTER 1 (7-19)
1. What is the function of relating the Finch family history at the outset of the novel?

By relating the Finch family history at the outset of the novel, author Harper Lee
illustrates how society is changing. The narrator states that it “was customary for the
[Finch] men [...] to remain on Simon’s homestead” (8), but Atticus and his brother both
attend university and, rather than return to the landing, pursue professions other than
farming. This shows how family traditions are shifting, and suggests that Alticus is at the
forefront of change in the small town of Maycomb, Alabama. The narrator also discusses
how Simon Finch started the homestead with slaves, and how eventually the slaves were
freed, revealing that society in general is beginning to accept people of colour as people.
It is likely that To Kill a Mockingbird will continue to explore the societal evolution
occurring at this time. By examining the past, the author equips the reader to better
understand the present and, furthermore, predict future events in the novel.
