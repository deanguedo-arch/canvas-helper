2. Why was the Bill of Rights added to the constitution?



3. Which amendment in the Bill of Rights most clearly draws from Locke’s idea of natural rights and the of the social contract that requires government not to unduly infringe upon those rights?



4. Canada and the United States both use the federal system. This means that power is divided between the national government and provincial, state or territorial governments.  Which level of government gets what power is usually set out in the constitution.  For example, in Canada, health and education are a provincial responsibility, national defence and the postal system are federal responsibilities.  Those who draw up constitutions must make provisions for the regulation of unforeseen circumstances.  For example, nuclear reactors were not invented until the 1940s.  Yet surely this potentially dangerous technology needs to be regulated.  Recognizing that such circumstances arise, most constitutions make reference to residual powers.  Residual powers refer to the constitutional right of a level of government to legislate matters not explicitly referred to in the constitution.  


Examine the Bill of Rights.  Which level of government has the residual powers in the United States?



5. Research how residual power is handled in Canada.  How does it compare with the U.S. approach?
