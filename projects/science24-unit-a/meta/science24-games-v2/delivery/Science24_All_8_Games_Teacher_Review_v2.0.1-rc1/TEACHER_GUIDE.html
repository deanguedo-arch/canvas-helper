W01 - Peroxide Record
Stage: worked
Situation: A virtual laboratory record identifies hydrogen peroxide before a change. Afterwards, water and oxygen are identified. The mixture warms with no external heating.
Base observations:
  Observation: Gas bubbles appear.
  Energy: The mixture warms; no external heat is supplied.
All optional evidence records:
  A - Product identity (w01_e1, strong): Water and oxygen are identified after the change.
  B - Starting substance (w01_e2, strong): Hydrogen peroxide is identified before the change.
  C - Temperature record (w01_e3, supporting): The mixture warms while no external heating is applied.
  D - Catalyst record (w01_e4, supporting): The catalyst is unchanged after the reaction; it is not consumed in the overall change.
Checked conclusion: chemical
Checked reaction: decomposition
Hint ladder:
  None. W01 is a demonstration; T01 has no hints.
Model explanation: The identified substances change from hydrogen peroxide to water and oxygen. New substances support a chemical change. One compound forms simpler products, so the reaction is decomposition.
Key idea: Identify what the substances were before and after. Bubbles describe an observation, not a substance identity.
Science note: In this stated setup, warming without external heating is consistent with an exothermic reaction: energy is released to the surroundings.

P01 - Cooling Solution
Stage: practice
Situation: A white crystalline solid disappears into water. The container cools from 22 °C to 18 °C and a clear solution remains. What does that establish?
Base observations:
  Appearance: The solid is no longer visible.
  Temperature: 22 °C → 18 °C
All optional evidence records:
  A - Recovery record (p01_e1, supporting): After the water is removed in the supplied record, a solid is recovered.
    Feedback rationale: Recovery matters, but a solid appearing again does not by itself identify it.
  B - Identity comparison (p01_e2, strong): The supplied identity analysis reports no new substances in this model. The recovered solid matches the starting solid.
    Feedback rationale: This record addresses whether the substance changed identity.
  C - pH record (p01_e3, weak): The record reports that the pH stayed the same.
    Feedback rationale: An unchanged pH does not establish whether all substance identities stayed the same.
  D - Warm-water comparison (p01_e4, weak): The solid dissolves at a different rate in warmer water.
    Feedback rationale: A rate difference does not answer whether a new substance formed.
Checked conclusion: physical
Checked reaction: none
Accepted evidence pairs: p01_e1 + p01_e2
Uncertainty rule: before p01_e2 has ever been seen, insufficient evidence is a justified unfinished checkpoint. Free follow-up reveals p01_e2. Replace one selected record, conclude physical, submit again and review the rubric to advance. Seen identities survive reopening.
Hint ladder:
  1. Separate the temperature observation from the question of substance identity.
  2. Recovery is useful, but the recovered material must also be identified.
  3. Pair the recovery record with the identity comparison.
Model explanation: The recovery and identity records support a physical change in this supplied model. The original substance is recovered and no new substances are reported. Cooling alone was not enough to decide.
Misconception feedback: The cooling and disappearance are observations. Neither alone establishes a new substance. Compare the recovery and identity records.
Key idea: A physical change can involve an energy transfer. Temperature change alone does not prove that a new substance formed.
Teacher decision: UNREVIEWED

P02 - Rusting Tool
Stage: practice
Situation: An iron-containing tool is left in moist air. A reddish-brown coating develops. In this record, the tool and all attached coating are weighed together; none has flaked away.
Base observations:
  Surface: A reddish-brown coating develops.
  Mass: The tool with its coating has gained mass.
All optional evidence records:
  A - Coating analysis (p02_e1, strong): The coating contains iron and oxygen and has different properties from the starting metallic iron.
    Feedback rationale: This compares the new material with the starting metal.
  B - Atmosphere record (p02_e2, supporting): The tool was exposed to air containing oxygen and to moisture.
    Feedback rationale: This supplies the conditions and a source of matter from the surroundings.
  C - Surface observation (p02_e3, supporting): The coating is reddish-brown.
    Feedback rationale: The appearance is consistent with corrosion but does not identify the substance alone.
  D - Location record (p02_e4, weak): The tool was stored in a garage.
    Feedback rationale: A location alone does not establish a chemical change.
Checked conclusion: chemical
Checked reaction: corrosion
Accepted evidence pairs: p02_e1 + p02_e2; p02_e1 + p02_e3
Application: Which response best limits further corrosion?
  [ACCEPTED] Use a suitable barrier coating to limit air and moisture contact.
  Polish it, then leave the metal unprotected.
  Do nothing; only the colour has changed.
Incorrect application feedback: A suitable barrier reduces contact with oxygen and moisture. Polishing alone leaves fresh metal exposed.
Hint ladder:
  1. Colour is suggestive. Which record compares the coating with the original metal?
  2. Use the coating analysis, then connect it to the exposure conditions or surface observation.
Model explanation: The coating has a different composition and properties from the starting iron, supporting a chemical change: corrosion. Oxygen from the surroundings contributes to the retained coating and the measured mass gain. A suitable barrier limits further contact with oxygen and moisture.
Misconception feedback: Do not explain the coating as a colour change alone. Compare the coating analysis with the original metal.
Key idea: Matter from the surroundings becomes part of corrosion products. A barrier can slow further corrosion.

P03 - Antacid Record
Stage: practice
Situation: In a virtual model, a calcium-carbonate antacid encounters an acidic solution. Review the supplied records to classify the change. This is not a dosing or laboratory activity.
Base observations:
  Observation: Bubbles appear.
  Acidity: The pH moves towards neutral.
All optional evidence records:
  A - Product record (p03_e1, strong): Carbon dioxide, water and a dissolved calcium salt are identified as products of the change.
    Feedback rationale: Product identities establish the new substances, not just visible bubbles.
  B - Reactant record (p03_e2, supporting): The starting reactants are the acidic solution and calcium carbonate.
    Feedback rationale: The reactant identities identify the acid–carbonate reaction pattern.
  C - Bubble observation (p03_e3, supporting): Gas bubbles are visible during the change.
    Feedback rationale: Visible bubbles support the record but do not identify the gas.
  D - Tablet appearance (p03_e4, supporting): The tablet becomes smaller during the supplied record.
    Feedback rationale: Disappearance alone could have another explanation; connect it to product identity.
Checked conclusion: chemical
Checked reaction: neutralization
Accepted evidence pairs: p03_e1 + p03_e2; p03_e1 + p03_e3; p03_e1 + p03_e4
Hint ladder:
  1. Classify using the identities of reactants and products, not the bubbles alone.
  2. The product record and the reactant record together identify the acid–carbonate pattern.
Model explanation: The identified products differ from the starting reactants, so the change is chemical. Acid reacting with calcium carbonate is an acid–carbonate neutralization, producing a calcium salt, water and carbon dioxide. The pH trend supports reduced acidity; bubbles alone do not identify the reaction.
Misconception feedback: Use the product record to determine whether new substances formed. Bubbles and a pH trend do not identify the reaction by themselves.
Key idea: This acid–carbonate reaction neutralizes acid and produces a salt, water and carbon dioxide. Not every acid–base reaction produces a gas.

P04 - Furnace Combustion
Stage: practice
Situation: A fictional natural-gas furnace supplies heat. Use its inlet and exhaust records to classify the reaction and connect a product to an environmental consequence.
Base observations:
  Energy: Heat is released.
  Observation: Light is produced in the burner.
All optional evidence records:
  A - Inlet record (p04_e1, strong): Methane and oxygen are supplied to the model burner.
    Feedback rationale: This identifies the reactants; compare them with the exhaust products.
  B - Exhaust record (p04_e2, strong): Carbon dioxide and water are identified as products in this complete-combustion model.
    Feedback rationale: This identifies products to compare with the inlet record.
  C - Energy record (p04_e3, supporting): The burner releases heat and visible light.
    Feedback rationale: Energy release is consistent with combustion, but does not identify its reactants and products.
  D - Casing record (p04_e4, weak): A metal surface becomes hot during operation.
    Feedback rationale: A hot casing is an effect of energy transfer, not evidence identifying the reaction products.
Checked conclusion: chemical
Checked reaction: combustion
Accepted evidence pairs: p04_e1 + p04_e2
Application: Which carbon-containing product links this model to greenhouse-gas emissions?
  Water vapour
  [ACCEPTED] Carbon dioxide
  Methane
  Oxygen
Incorrect application feedback: Carbon dioxide is the carbon-containing product. Water vapour is also a greenhouse gas, but it contains no carbon. Methane is an input in this model, not an identified exhaust product.
Hint ladder:
  1. Use the inlet and exhaust identities together. Decide which substance is a product, not an input.
Model explanation: Methane and oxygen react to form carbon dioxide and water. These new products support a chemical change classified as combustion. Energy is released. Carbon dioxide is the carbon-containing greenhouse-gas product identified in this model.
Misconception feedback: Compare the inlet and exhaust identities. Heat or light alone does not identify a reaction.
Key idea: Complete methane combustion forms carbon dioxide and water and releases energy. This model does not claim that every real furnace burns completely.

  After cooling: Liquid water (H₂O)
All optional evidence records:
