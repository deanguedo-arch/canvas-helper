## Curriculum and learner evidence

Authority: [Alberta Science 24 program, Unit B, printed pages 36–39](https://education.alberta.ca/media/159714/sc1424.pdf). General outcome 2 includes energy-use planning, power/energy units and quantitative efficiency. Unit B skills include proposing solutions, interpreting numerical accounts and correcting system problems. This pilot gives partial practice, not full Unit B coverage.

| Skill | Learner action | Fresh response / evidence limit |
|---|---|---|
| Apply power × time and use W, kW, h and kWh | W01/P01 select lighting; P02 repair computer schedules; P03 compare projectors | T01 independently calculates original and repaired totals. Practice totals are calculated support, not independent arithmetic evidence. |
| Improve energy use while preserving service | P01–P03 and centre lab preserve device counts and operating floors | T01 meets the cap and required services. Replay variants change quantity, runtime or equipment constraints. |
| Calculate useful-output/input efficiency | P04 chooses a motor and enters a percentage; R1/R2 change the account and targets | Motor trial preserves the entered percentage and explanation. The separate next-lesson task checks a new ratio without support. |
| Predict, test, revise and communicate | All supported missions record predictions, configurations, outcomes and claims | Immutable trials show revisions; teachers assess causal explanations. Digital simulation does not establish physical measurement or teamwork skills. |

Power consumption alone does not establish conversion efficiency. Projector alternatives are explicitly authored as providing equal service. Thermal comfort, room bookings and actual building operation are not simulated. Energy-distribution tracing, fossil fuels and broader Unit B content require separate teaching.

## Solutions and acceptable alternatives

`B2_SOLUTIONS.json` contains independently authored calculations for all six core cases, eight replay cases and the centre lab. Expected results are not generated by the validator. The learner runtime does not load that solution file.

Any offered equipment/count/hour configuration meeting every service floor and cap is valid. Hours use half-hour increments; quantities must be whole numbers. The centre lab's all-minimum plan uses **10.390 kWh**. Reducing only heater runtime from six to four hours uses **11.920 kWh**, saving **3.000 kWh**. Both are acceptable.

The core transfer is the corrected canonical projector event: **0.580 kWh** original, **0.460 kWh** with the 140 W alternative, against **0.500 kWh**. The supplied ChatGPT reference's workstation transfer is preserved as reference only. It does not silently replace `B2-T01`.

Common misconceptions: forgetting device quantity; treating W as kW; confusing power and energy; removing mandatory services to fit a cap; using useful/input as a decimal when a percentage is requested; choosing the lowest input energy without checking useful output and efficiency separately.

## Explanation review

Each explanation remains **Teacher review pending**, even when its deterministic model result is correct. Assess whether the learner names the change, cites a correct numerical account with units, connects it to service/useful-output requirements and recognizes the authored model's limits. Nonempty text and an evidence selection establish a submitted explanation, not its quality.

Independent transfer hides derived calculations and earlier support before its first complete submission. The learner's two entered totals are its quantitative prediction, recorded before the model reveal. Its first response is immutable; revisions remain separate. Review and printing report completion, model correctness, support and explanations separately. Work exists only in memory; closing or reloading clears the session.
