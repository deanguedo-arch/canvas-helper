What is the Great Binding Law and by what other name(s) is it known?

The Constitution of the Iroquois Nations, Gayanashagowa, The Great Law of Peace


Approximately how long have the Haudenosaunee lived under the principles of the Great Binding Law?  How have scholars determined this?

It is believed that the Haudenosaunee have lived under the principles of the Great Binding Law since 1142.  Haudenosaunee records refer to an eclipse of the sun in the Haudenosaunee territories around the time the Great Binding Law was adopted.  A firm date has been arrived at by calculating the date of solar eclipses in the area.


Find a statement or statements within the Great Binding Law that suggests the Haudenosaunee, like other advocates of liberalism, believed that the protection of property rights was important.  Record the relevant passage(s) below.

 

Protection of the House


107.  A certain sign shall be known to all the people of the Five Nations which shall denote that the owner or occupant of a house is absent.  A stick or pole in a slanting or leaning position shall indicate this and be the sign.  Every person not entitled to enter the house by right of living within it upon seeing such a sign shall not approach the house either by day or by night but shall keep as far away as his business will permit.

What evidence is exists in Benjamin Franklin’s writings to prove that Franklin was impressed by the Haudenosaunee’s approach to governing themselves.  Provide a quotation and explain its significance.

“It would be a very strange thing if Six Nations of Ignorant Savages should be capable of forming a Scheme for such an Union and be able to execute it in such a manner, as that it has subsisted Ages, and appears indissoluble, and yet a like Union should be impracticable for ten or a dozen English colonies

-- Benjamin Franklin to James Parker, 1751


Despite his reference to the Haudenosaunee as “ignorant savages” it is clear that he admires the “Union” of tribes the Haudenosaunee had created.  He admires the length of time it has been in effect (“subsisted Ages”) and the strength of the bond between tribes (“appears indissoluble”).  Moreover, he is implying that a similar union would be practical for the English colonies that would later make up the United States.

What role did women play in the government of the Haudenosaunee?


It was the role of women to choose the chiefs.  If the chief was not seen to be serving the interests of the people, women also had the power to remove the chief from office.


When did women in the United States obtain the power to participate fully in the government of the nation?

In the United States, women’s right to vote, and thereby have a role in choosing their own government, was not enshrined in the U.S. constitution until 1920.
