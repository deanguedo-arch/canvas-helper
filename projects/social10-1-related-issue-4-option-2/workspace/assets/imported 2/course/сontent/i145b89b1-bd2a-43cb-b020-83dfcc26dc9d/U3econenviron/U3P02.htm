Defining Prosperity

What does prosperity mean to you? What does it mean to people in other parts of the world? Is it something that can be measured? There are many ways of looking at prosperity for Canadians and for people all around the world. In our increasingly globally connected world, people are becoming more and more aware of differences in prosperity within and between countries. In this lesson, you will explore different understandings of prosperity, beyond simple monetary considerations.

What is prosperity? 

Although there are many definitions of prosperity, the word prosperity usually refers to money. The desire for prosperity is a powerful globalizing force. It’s often the reason why people immigrate, why nations trade with one another, and why people communicate with people in other nations. People, corporations, and nations connect with each other so that they can become more prosperous.

In today’s world, 40 percent of the world, over 2.6 billion people, live on less than two Canadian dollars per day. While there are many perspectives on what prosperity means, without money, people’s basic needs cannot be met. Without increased wealth, people in the poorest nations of the world will never have enough food, an education, proper clothing or shelter, or a high enough standard of health care to live long and healthy lives.

If the gap between rich and poor continues to grow, what possible consequences are there for both the rich and the poor? What does this kind of inequality mean to the global community? You will continue to explore these questions in this module and the modules that follow.

Your understanding of what prosperity really means is an important factor in understanding economic globalization and how it affects people around the world.

What’s the difference between standard of living and quality of life?

Prosperity can be described in many ways. It can be defined in terms of standard of living and quality of life.

Standard of living is measured in terms of income. It’s usually called per capita income and calculated as GDP per capita. GDP, or Gross Domestic Product,is one way to show how wealthy a country is.

For example, in 2006 Canada's GNP was $1 095 000 000. Its population was 32 422 000. Therefore, 1 095 000 000 divided by 32 422 000 = $33 770. The per capita income of Canadians in 2006 was $33 770.

Did each person in Canada make $33 770? Of course not—some made more and some made much less. One of the biggest problems with looking at prosperity in terms of per capita GDP is that money is not evenly shared in any country. Some people make a lot of money, and others can make almost nothing.

GINI Index

One way of looking at the lack of economic equality in a nation is to look at the distribution of money. If there is a huge gap between the richest and poorest people in the country, there is a very unequal sharing of wealth. That means that some people are very prosperous and others are very poor. Such inequality is more extreme in some countries than in others. The GINI Index is one way of measuring inequalities in income.

Quality of life describes the achievement of life goals. These goals can include the amount of money you have but also your happiness, freedom, equality, personal satisfaction, and love of family and friends. Quality of life is a difficult concept because every human being has different experiences and values. People have different perspectives about what matters.

What are some different perspectives on prosperity?

People all over the world have different ideas about what “prosperity” means. For some, prosperity refers only to their income, while others are more concerned other aspects of their lives, such as their emotional and spiritual well-being. Still others value their relationship with the natural environment, their political freedom, and their relations with friends and family.

Gross National Happiness

In our world today, we usually describe prosperity as economic well-being. However, there is a growing movement of people worldwide who believe that things like standard of living and per capita income don’t tell the whole story about the overall well-being about people who live in a country.

In 1972, the king of Bhutan, a small country in the Himalayas, proposed something he called Gross National Happiness. Gross National Happiness is based on the Buddhist philosophy that the ultimate purpose of life is inner happiness. As the leader of a Buddhist country, the king of Bhutan felt that it was his responsibility to define the development of his country in terms of the happiness of its people, rather than how much money they made.

The ideology of GNH connects Bhutan’s development goals with the pursuit of happiness. This means that the ideology reflects Bhutan’s vision on the purpose of human life, a vision that puts the individual’s self-cultivation at the center of the nation’s developmental goals, a primary priority for Bhutanese society as a whole as well as for the individual concerned.

—Dasho Meghraj Gurung, Director General Bhutan Post

Should prosperity matter?

What do you think of when you hear the terms underdeveloped or developing world? Generally speaking, the “developed world” includes North America, Australia, New Zealand, and most of Europe. Countries in these parts of the world are industrialized and wealthy. They also hold most of the world's economic power, but they don’t hold the majority of the world's population. The “developing world” includes nations that have lower levels of industrial development, higher levels of poverty, and more people.

What impressions do those terms create for us? The term developed suggests that a nation is advanced and that it has fully matured or has fulfilled its potential as a nation. Do you think the word developed suggests that these nations are in some ways superior to other nations? Does it say that certain countries have reached a goal that other nations should try to reach? Canada is considered developed. Do you think Canada has reached its ultimate level of development, or are there still ways Canadian society could improve itself?

On the other hand, a developing nation is defined by what it is not. Does the term imply that these nations (or cultures) are inferior? Does it suggest that these economically poorer countries will only reach their full potential when they become like countries in the developed world? What else does the term developing suggest? It implies that all nations are moving forward in economic and social development. In fact, many of the world's poorest countries have not moved ahead in a very long time. In many countries, living standards are becoming worse from year to year.

Vocabulary

standard of living: a level of material comfort as measured by the goods and services available to an individual, group, or nation, including access to health care and education

quality of life: a measure of how good one's life is, including standard of living and other, more difficult to measure aspects of well-being, such as health, happiness, human rights and freedoms, environmental well-being, and creativity

per capita income: the average income of the people in a country calculated by dividing the gross national product by the number of people in the country

GDP per capita: the average income of each person in a country, calculated by dividing the gross national product by the number of people in the country

Gross Domestic Product: the total value of goods and services produced within a country in a year

GINI Index: a measurement of the inequality of wealth distribution in a country. The smaller the number, the more equal the distribution of wealth.

Lesson Summary

In this lesson you examined different perspectives about prosperity and considered what prosperity means to you. Prosperity includes traditional measures of wealth including standard of living and per capita income, as well as other ways in which people measure their sense of well-being. The prosperity of people within most countries varies a great deal, and prosperity in one country is not equal to prosperity in another country. These inequalities, and differing perspectives about what prosperity means, have influenced the growth of economic globalization. Your understanding of prosperity will help you answer the question: To what extent does globalization contribute to sustainable prosperity?
