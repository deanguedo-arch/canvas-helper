Contemporary Events

In this lesson you will explore the events leading from the Age of Imperialism from Unit 2 and 3 until the modern day. The events of the past century have included two devastating world wars; competition between the United Sates and the Soviet Union, the world’s two superpowers; incredible population growth; and increasing destruction of the natural world. To deal with these issues, the nations of the world have chosen to act in order to create a sustainable future. Have those actions led to greater economic connections? Have they led to sustainable prosperity for all, or have they made some people richer and others poorer?

What events led to economic globalization as we know it today?

Think back to Evan and his pop stand. His small local business earned a few dollars every time there was a football game. He provided a service to local football fans. He had the freedom to work when he wanted and how he wanted. However, when those same fans discovered a convenience store nearby with a larger selection of goods at cheaper prices, they stopped buying Evan’s pop. A store that was part of a large global chain of convenience stores was a challenge to Evan’s ability to prosper. Could he compete with a global chain? He could sell his pop at just a few cents above cost, but his limited economies of scale would mean that he could never sell enough to make any kind of money. He could offer different products and better customer service, but there are limits to what he can do. He could join with Su Yin and come up with some creative strategies to expand their market. Or, he could get a job at the convenience store. He would make more money in an hour than he did selling pop outside his house, but he would lose some of his freedom in doing so.

History is the story of people coming together and pulling apart, of finding common ground and better ways to co-operate, and of finding reasons to disagree. As your study of imperialism showed, it’s also the story of the strong and the weak and how they interact. Over the past century, nations have united for many reasons, especially to protect their own interests. Nations have also come into conflict because of differing values, perspectives, and competing interests. Conflict can lead to change, and change can be for better or for worse.

Historic Events of the Twentieth Century

World War I, 1914–1918 

World War I was the world’s first truly global war. The initial event that started the war was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria, but there were many real causes. The reasons included these:

  • competition between European nations for global power, including power over colonies
  • military build up, especially between Great Britain and Germany
  • alliances and secret agreements between nations, in which countries promised to support each other in times of war

While the war began as a dispute between European powers, almost every nation in the world eventually became involved because many countries were either a colony or former colony of a European country. For example, Canada was a colony of Great Britain, so it went to war when Britain went to war. This war had devastating effects on the world’s people. Over 40 million people died in battle or because of disease or poverty caused by the war. The war disrupted the global economy because of the amount of money that went into creating weapons and later into rebuilding war-torn Europe.

Creation of the Soviet Union, 1922

Russia was a huge country that was controlled by a powerful and extremely rich royal family and the upper class of people. There was a great deal of inequality between the rich and the poor, and the average person had no say in what happened in government. While many other nations of the world (especially in Europe) had industrialized and become somewhat democratic, Russia had not. During the October Revolution of 1917, the Communist Party took power in Russia in an attempt to improve the lives of the people. After a lengthy revolution, which partly took place during World War I, the Soviet Union was formed in 1922. The establishment of a communist country set the stage for future conflict.

Formation and Collapse of the League of Nations, 1920–1946

After World War I, the powerful nations of the world decided that some kind of international organization should be created in order to prevent future conflicts such as World War I. The League of Nations had many goals, including the right of nations to rule themselves, ending slavery and prostitution, and reducing the number of weapons produced around the world. The League of Nations was formed with representatives of most European nations. Many important nations, including the United States, never joined, while others came and went. The league did not have any military force and was unable to resolve many of the conflicts that occurred. Its ultimate failure to achieve its goals was shown when World War II began.

The Great Depression, 1929–1939


The Great Depression was a worldwide downturn in the economy. It devastated industrialized nations and the less-industrialized countries that sold raw materials. It had a negative impact on international trade, including heavy industry, mining, logging, and farming. It forced governments to react in different ways in order to protect their people. Some governments developed more protectionist policies in order to promote and preserve local jobs and income.

World War II, 1939–1945

World War II was the second global war that happened in the twentieth century. It involved over 100 million military personnel and resulted in over 70 million deaths, most of them civilian.

There were many causes for World War II, including unresolved disagreements resulting from World War I. An important reason was the Treaty of Versailles, the international agreement that ended the First World War. In this treaty, Germany was forced to accept most of the blame for the war and make war reparations, or repayments, to other nations. This, coupled with the Great Depression, led to a lot of unhappiness in Germany. The German people elected Adolf Hitler as their leader, and he eventually turned Germany into a fascist dictatorship. Germany rebuilt its military and began to aggressively expand into other nations. By the time other nations of the world stepped in, Germany had a powerful war machine. Again, colonies and former colonies joined the war. The war was further intensified by military action in Asia, as Japan also fought to expand its territory in other parts of Asia. Two nuclear bombs were exploded in Japan, which effectively ended the war.

How did the competition between communism and capitalism affect economic globalization?

The Cold War, 1945–1990

The ideas behind the ideologies of communism and capitalism are very different. Capitalists think that if people are given a lot of freedom to own property and make money, everyone will work hard and everyone will benefit. On the other hand, communists believe that prosperity should be shared; they do not agree with the large differences between rich and poor that take place in capitalist nations. Instead, communists believe the government should manage the factors of production in order to benefit everyone equally.

These two ways of thinking divided the world for many years. Two “superpowers,” the United States and the Soviet Union, competed to have their ideas dominate the world economy. As a result, the world became polarized into two large blocks, with capitalist nations on one side and communist nations on the other. These two sides traded primarily among themselves. There was limited movement of people between the two sides, and most communication was hostile. 

The communist way of managing the economy was not entirely successful. People often would not work as hard as they could if they did not see a direct benefit for themselves. Government mismanagement also led to economic failure. Failures in the communist system, along with limits on trade, movement, and communication, prevented a lot of economic growth, especially in the communist nations. But capitalism isn't a perfect system either, and it often results in the huge gaps between rich and poor that the communists have tried to eliminate.

As well, this period in history saw a huge amount of money being spent on arms, including nuclear weapons. While these weapons were never used, both the United States and the Soviet Union and their allies were involved in a number of armed conflicts that sometimes threatened to become another world war. These included the Berlin Blockade, the Korean War, the Cuban Missile Crisis, the Vietnam War, and the Soviet Afghan War.

Was non-alignment possible?

Though a number of countries did not take sides during the cold war, each superpower felt that in order to protect themselves and become prosperous, they needed to pursue their own interests, no matter the cost. 

In 1951, Iran elected a man named Mohammed Mossadegh Prime Minister. A popular politician, Mossadegh was responsible for the nationalization of Iran's oil industry; this meant that Iran would be in full control of it's oil industry, which had been under control of the British government since 1913. He believed that the money from the oil industry could be used to make the lives of the Iranian people better. However, Britain was aligned with the United States against the Communist countries, and this was an economic blow to Britain. In 1953, the CIA would overthrow Mohammed Mossadegh and replace him with General Fazlollah Zahedi, which let the Shah of Iran rule as a dictator. 

How has increased technology led to the expansion of globalization?

Although economics has been the main driving force that has led to the expansion of globalization, it is technology that has made the expansion of globalization possible.

From the days of the Industrial Revolution until today, transportation and communications technology has continued to improve, especially over the last 100 years. Large corporations can operate with economies of scale; because they sell larger quantities of products, they can sell them for lower prices. Shipping technology continues to allow corporations to move their goods from the place they are grown or made to the places where they are sold. The components of many manufactured goods can come from various places, be assembled in another locations, shipped to a central depot, and then shipped all over the world.

Communications Technology and Globalization 

Developments in communications media have also contributed to the expansion of globalization. Telegraphs, telephones, and radio broadcasts began in the 1800s and early 1900s, allowing people to communicate more easily across borders. Television, then satellite television, and the development of the Internet in the early 1990s further enabled the sharing of information and culture. This free flow of information was one of the factors that led to the downfall of the Soviet Union because people there were able to find out what was happening in the rest of the world. The Soviet people realized very quickly that people in Western countries such as Canada had far more consumer goods available to them and far more freedom to express themselves in music, television, film, and print. They rebelled. This same free flow of information has led to a push for democracy in China. It allows people in the Middle East to see what is happening in wartime. It shows everyone what is happening when disaster strikes. It keeps everyone up-to-date with the latest trends.

The Media and Freedom of Information

Instant communication made possible by satellites and the Internet has caused countries and international organizations throughout the world to react more quickly and sometimes more honestly. Once upon a time, a government could easily hide what was happening in others part of the world, but such information control is becoming increasingly difficult. Satellite dishes can be prohibited, but they are now so small that they are easily hidden. Likewise, a government can attempt to block certain sites on the Internet or outlaw them completely, but that is very difficult and impractical. If the citizens of a country want to find out what is happening outside their borders, a government can do very little to stop them. 

How did the downfall of the Soviet Union affect economic globalization?

The Collapse of the Soviet Union, 1991

By the end of the 1980s, the Soviet Union was having a great deal of difficulty meeting the needs of its citizens. There were complaints about limited products including food. People in the Soviet Union wanted more political and economic freedoms. They wanted to own property and have the opportunity to prosper by diligent work and innovation. They wanted to choose their own leaders. Non-violent revolutions occurred across the Soviet Bloc, and by 1991, the former Soviet Union was dissolved and democratic capitalism arrived in some form to the nations of the Soviet Union. They broke into smaller countries based on their history and linguistic and cultural ties.

While the collapse of the Soviet Union led to greater economic integration between nations, the fall of communism did not necessarily lead to greater prosperity for all Soviet society. 

In the former Soviet Union, the government owned not only the land and the forests but also almost every factor of production. That included factories, airlines, banks, stores, medical services, and many other money-making businesses that are privately owned in capitalist countries. All of these were opened up to private ownership.

This huge change, sometimes called “shock therapy,” caused huge problems for the people of the former Soviet Union. The economy collapsed and millions of people were forced into poverty. Corruption and crime spread. Prices were out of control, with the prices of some goods increasing by more than 3000 percent in one year. State-owned businesses fell into the hands of a few, including members of crime organizations, who became extremely rich. A lot of money left the country. Many educated people moved away. Today, however, the Russian economy is growing. More and more people are working.
