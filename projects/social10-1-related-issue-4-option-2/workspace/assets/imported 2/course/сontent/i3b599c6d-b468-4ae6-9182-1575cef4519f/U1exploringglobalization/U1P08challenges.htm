Homogenization

Monoculture is a term used to refer to the uniformity of traditions, beliefs, customs, traditions, values and attitudes. Critics claim that globalization is leading to an increase in homogenization of culture.

If teens in Brazil are wearing the same jeans as the teens in Japan, how does that affect the local traditions?

Assimilation

Assimilation refers to a minority group adopting the traditions, language, values and beliefs of the dominant group to the extent that the identity of the minority group disappears. Assimilation can be a voluntary or non-voluntary process.

Marginalization

Marginalization occurs when individuals are excluded from groups, prevented from gaining power or influence, with identity and culture not respected. Although governments have acknowledged that these practices are unlawful, many individuals are facing these challenges today.

Black Lives Matter is a grassroots group within the United States demanding equality and respect for Black Americans. The movement has spread across North America as statistics on such issues as the large wage gap between black and white Americans, levels of police brutality, and the over-representation of African Americans in US prisons, has been publicized by the group.

Another example of a group which faces marginalization in modern society is the LGBTQ community. Though all members of the community are more likely to face discrimination, harassment, and violence, the transgender (someone whose internal gender identity does not align with their assigned sex at birth) community is especially vulnerable.

According to a survey of Canadian schools on the impact of this marginalization, LGBTQ youth experience higher rates of verbal harassment (insults and prejudiced comments), physical harassment (assault and physical aggression), and sexual harassment than their peers. Of all groups, transgender youth are most likely to experience all three. For example, almost half of transgender youth report that they have been sexually assaulted. You can see the report with additional statistics here. LGBTQ youth are also more likely to be truant or drop out of school, use drugs, or attempt suicide, according to this US report.

An important factor to note is that different kinds of marginalization can and do intersect. Someone who is both a visible minority and a part of the LGBTQ community may experience discrimination or prejudice based both on their race and their sexual orientation or gender identity. The recognition of this is known as intersectionality.

Traditions and Change Around the World

We know that collective identities and traditional ways of life are always changing.

In your grandparents’ day, your grandmother probably worked in the home, and families always sat down together for a meal. In your parents’ day, that began to change as more women worked outside the home, and more members of the household took responsibilities for meals. Today it is estimated that 20% of all meals are eaten in the car.

Changes in way of life is happening all over the world.

With the change in lifestyle come many changes to collective identity. Some possible examples include the following:

  • The role of women has changed, giving women more rights.

  • The family unit has become less important for many people.

  • More money might lead to possessions becoming more important.

There are many challenges to your collective identity. Today people are connected to each other in many ways. These connections influence how people live, what they believe, and how they act. While in the past, most aspects of cultural identity were locally created; many aspects of today’s culture in the form of food, music, art, and entertainment are mass produced and mass marketed. This mass production by large corporations also influences identity.

Transnational corporations can test the uniqueness of collective identities by providing products that everyone around the world uses, rather than the locally made food, clothing, and other items they have used in the past.

This brings changes to people’s traditional ways of producing, buying, and selling items. It can bring changes to daily life as well. For example, people who once grew their own food organically, or created their own artistic crafts, cannot compete financially with large chains. If their traditional crops are no longer grown, their local food is no longer produced or their handicrafts no longer made, the world is a less diverse place as these cultural groups lose their distinctiveness. They lose their income and also their some of their freedom to do and be who they are.

One World, One Taste? McDonald’s ran a campaign called “One World, One Taste.” Coca-Cola’s logo is a rainbow coloured map of the world flowing out of a Coke bottle. Coke and McDonald’s operate all around the world, encouraging people to eat and drink their North American-based products. Their campaigns are designed to encourage everyone around the world to eat and drink the same food and pop. Is that necessarily a good thing or a bad thing for collective identity?

Lesson Summary

Identities can express many things. As in the lesson on the relationship between language and identity, your choice of words can reflect where you are from. Your beliefs can reflect who you are. You can observe values, beliefs, and connection to the land in the identities of those around you. Some of these ideas and beliefs are passed down by your family over generations. You may choose more values and beliefs from other peoples and communities as communication and transportation technologies allow you to observe and exchange diverse values, beliefs, traditions, and ideas. This can result in both unique and shared expressions of identity for both individuals and collectives. This may also challenge which values and ideas will shape identities in the future.
