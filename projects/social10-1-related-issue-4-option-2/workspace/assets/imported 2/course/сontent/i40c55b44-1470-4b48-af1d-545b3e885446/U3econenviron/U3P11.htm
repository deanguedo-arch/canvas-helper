Sustainable Growth

In the previous few lessons you discovered that people around the world want to prosper. In order to succeed financially, they need to obtain resources, manufacture goods, and trade goods and services. These activities have an impact on the environment. As people prosper, they tend to be healthier and live longer. They eat more. They travel farther in vehicles, and they live in bigger houses. With greater prosperity comes decreased population growth, and with lower prosperity comes increased population growth. Overall, the world’s population is increasing at a rapid rate. 

In the news, we hear about an international food shortage, the depletion of the ozone layer, the control of the world’s water, global warming, the destruction of the polar ice cap, and other environmental issues caused by human activity. Food, water, clean air, and other natural resources are needed so people can survive and prosper.

If economic growth continues at its current rate, will the world continue to support human life, or are there limits to what the planet can support? In this lesson you will investigate the question: Is sustainable economic growth possible?

Is sustainable economic growth possible?

In 1999 there were six billion people in the world. In 2011, seven billion. In 2020, about 8 billion. The United Nations predicts that by the year 2030 the world’s population will be almost nine billion with the highest growth in Africa. Other regions' populations are predicted to stay level or decline.

What changes will occur in people’s lives around the world if the population continues to grow at this rate? 

Environmental sustainability means the ability to meet present needs without compromising the future. Earth and its resources are limited, and if people want the human race to survive, everyone needs to make sure that the people of today do not use up or destroy all the resources that will be needed tomorrow. They have to make sure that future generations can prosper.

Many people believe that we must act as stewards of the land. Stewardship means taking good care of the world’s resources. Stewardship involves the idea that all living things have a right to exist and that people should understand the natural world and encourage and maintain natural areas and biological diversity for future generations.

How does human activity affect the environment?

Look out your window. Almost everywhere you look, no matter where you live, you see the impact of the human race on the environment. You may see fields of grain blowing in the breeze, a logging truck hauling freshly cut trees to the pulp mill, a pump jack relentlessly sucking oil from the ground, a neatly manicured suburban garden, or a network of city streets and the vehicles that drive on them. Humankind’s footprint is enormous. No one lives without creating some kind of impact on the world, and the more people who live on our planet, the bigger that impact is.

While we know that economic development depletes natural resources and often has a negative impact on the environment, most of us want new cars, better televisions, more clothes, nicer homes, and money in the bank. As well, people who currently live in poverty in both the developed and the developing world want to experience the same material well-being that we enjoy. How do we balance our desire for prosperity with our impact on the environment?

In our drive for prosperity, how much economic growth can be sustained? Consider specifically the relationship between sustainability, population, and the global food supply.

Along with prosperity comes improved health care, lower infant mortality rates, and longer life expectancies. This leads to greater population growth. The world's population reached the one billion mark in 1804, but it now stands at over six billion people.

The natural population growth of a nation is calculated by determining the number of live births and subtracting the number of deaths per year. The overall population growth rate also includes the number of people who migrate into a country in any given year.

The current world rate of growth is 1.3 percent per year. If it continues at the same rate, the world’s population will double by 2054 to twelve billion people. The more people who live on this planet, the greater our impact on the environment. Can the planet support 12 billion people? Right now, the planet produces enough food and water to nourish everyone, but it is not equally distributed. What about the future?

Do rich nations have higher rates of growth? You might think that wealthy nations and wealthy families would have the most children because they can best afford to provide for them. In fact, the wealthier industrialized nations have lower population growth rates than developing nations. Canada has an overall growth rate of just over one percent, and some European countries actually have shrinking populations.

Ninety-nine percent of global population increase is occurring in the developing world. Several countries are on a growth curve that will lead to their population doubling (Nigeria) or even tripling (Ethiopia) in 2050. These areas can least afford the increase in population. Not only are they already overcrowded, but they lack the water, the arable land, the infrastructure, and the finances to increase food production.

How does local economic activity affect the global environment?

Many Canadians pride themselves on their love of the environment. However, the Canadian economy is largely based on the sale of natural resources, including oil and gas, lumber, fish, and agricultural products. Canada is the world’s largest exporter of forest products. Many people are employed in the oil, gas, and forestry industries. 

Eight thousand years ago, forests covered most of North America's surface. Human activities, including agriculture and urbanization, have greatly reduced the forests of North America. Along with the Russian boreal and the Amazon basin, Canada’s boreal contains all that remains of the world’s large frontier forests.

Can technology limit the impact of global economic growth?

The world’s resources are not infinite. There are renewable and non-renewable resources. Food and water are renewable, but they still need to be managed appropriately or there will not be enough to go around. Other resources are limited.

For years, people have been concerned about the world’s dwindling supply of oil and gas. Scientists have worked long and hard to find alternative fuels. One such alternative is “biofuels.” Biofuels are obtained from biological material, usually plants such as corn or sugarcane, as opposed to the traditional fossil fuel, which comes from long-dead plant matter. Biofuels provide a clean and efficient source of energy. Some governments are pushing for increased use of biofuels to reduce reliance on fossil fuels. However, since biofuels are produced on agricultural land, there is less land free to grow food. When less food is produced, it becomes more expensive. This is one factor in the global food-supply crisis.

Enrichment

Genetically Modified Food/Organisms (GMOs)

You may have heard news stories about genetically modified organisms, or GMOs. This refers to food that has been genetically modified in order to produce certain traits in crops, such as resistance to pesticides or herbicides or additional vitamins. They are also the subject of a great deal of controversy.

On one hand, some make the case that GMOs allow more food to be produced, and ensure that the food produced is more nutritious. Golden rice, for example, has been genetically modified to be a source of Vitamin A, and some have made the case that this will enable people in regions without access to Vitamin A-rich foods to get their recommended dosage. They argue that this is a benefit of globalization, in that it allows a plentiful, nutritious food supply.

An image of Golden Rice taken from wikipedia.org

On the other hand, while the majority of scientific studies have concluded that GMOs themselves are safe, some make the case that herbicide or pesticide-resistant crops can encourage irresponsible use of these chemicals, which could be damaging to humans or the environment. Some also make the case that some European countries are hesitant to import them. Others point to the business practices of companies who produce genetically engineered crops such as Monsanto, who have pursued legal action against farmers for replanting their seeds without permission.

Vocabulary

stewardship: responsible management of land and resources so that future generations will enjoy a healthy environment

Lesson Summary

In this lesson you looked at some of the challenges and opportunities that globalization presents to sustainable development. As people everywhere struggle to become more and more prosperous, strains occur on the world’s ability to sustain itself. There are challenges to the food and water supplies and to resource development, as well as associated damages to the environment. Technology, government controls, and global action may limit the negative impacts of economic growth.
