One World

As the nations of the world have become increasingly interconnected, economic development has increased. Sometimes development is designed to profit a few people, and sometimes the goal is prosperity for all. Progress may involve one huge transnational corporation trying to create profit for its shareholders, it may involve providing employment and sustainable income for the world's poor. Either way, the environment is always affected.

Transnational corporations operate in various countries around the world, sometimes where there are few laws to protect the environment. Global development projects funded by organizations such as the World Bank often sacrifice environmental protection in order to create prosperity. With large-scale projects, economic development can have large-scale and often devastating effects on the environment. 

Whether development projects are spearheaded by transnationals trying to create profits or international agencies trying to improve life in the developing world, all have an impact on the environment. In this lesson you will investigate the question: If environmental concerns are ignored, can prosperity be sustained? 

How does global economic growth affect the world’s environment?

Global economic growth has a powerful effect on the production and consumption of products. As people get richer, they are able to buy more products. Because our world is globally connected through trade, people all over the world are able to use up more of the world’s resources, while at the same time using up more land for larger homes and using agricultural land to build cities.

Does global economic growth affect everyone’s environment equally?

Economic activity affects everyone. Previously, you explored supply and demand. When supplies are limited, prices increase. If people in the developed world are using up Earth’s energy, water, and food, then those same resources will get more expensive for everyone—including those who are least able to afford them. When there are fewer resources to go around, everyone pays, especially the poor. As the cartoonist predicts, limited supplies of the goods people need can lead to conflict.

One of the strongest influences of economic growth on the environment is the impact on the world climate. Earth's climate is continuously changing, but human activity, including industrialization and increasing world population growth, has an important role today. Climate change can result from natural changes to Earth's weather, but most of the world's scientific community—including the UN’s Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change and the U.S. National Academy of Sciences—supports the position that “most of the warming observed over the last 50 years is attributable to human activities.”

Global warming is the most significant effect of climate change. Global warming is the observed increase in the average temperature of Earth's atmosphere and oceans in recent years. Earth’s average temperature rose from 0.2 to 0.6 degrees C during the twentieth century. That does not sound like much change, but the effects are significant and greater changes are predicted for the future.

 

Global warming is caused primarily by the greenhouse effect, the process in which the absorption of infrared radiation by the atmosphere warms the planet. The human-induced causes of the greenhouse effect include burning of fossil fuels, land clearing (burning), and agriculture—all of which lead to increased amounts of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases in the atmosphere.

An increase in global temperatures can also lead to other changes, including rising sea levels and changes in the amount and pattern of precipitation—all of which lead to increases in extreme weather such as droughts, floods, heat waves, hurricanes, and tornados. As well, increase in global temperatures can lead to higher or lower agricultural production, species extinction, the retreat of glaciers, and reduced river and stream levels.

Can human life as we know it continue if the trends in climate change continue at the same rate? Obviously, reduced agricultural output, reduced amounts of water, and extreme storm activity will have negative effects on the planet for most species, including humans. That is the reason many governments support action on climate change.

What would an appropriate response be? Should all nations control their greenhouse gas emissions equally? Wealthy countries do emit more and perhaps can better afford to make changes in their industries and vehicles. Should richer nations take the lead and make their changes first to show the way for poorer nations?

How does the globalization of resource development affect sustainability?

In the past, people could only use the resources that were nearby. In today’s globalized world, people can use resources from anywhere. Canadian forest products are shipped all over the world for use as wood products and paper as well as disposable diapers and other products made with pulp.

Corporations operating in Canada ship their oil, gas, fish, lumber, and minerals all over the globe. Since they operate with a profit motive, they will sell these products wherever the price is highest. Without government intervention, this can mean that some of the goods Canadians need can be sold to people in other countries. Sometimes that leads to destruction of our own resources and higher costs for us. What if the world wanted Canadian water? Would our government or corporations sell it if the price was right?

Transnational corporations will buy their resources wherever they are cheapest and sell them wherever they get the best price. Canadian coal, for example, has been in high demand by Japanese industry. However, if the large corporations in Japan that need coal can buy it for less in Australia, they will do so. This can lead to economic challenges.

You learned about the increasing demand for biofuels. Plants for biofuels are grown on land that was previously used to grow food crops. In our global economy, if agribusiness owns the land and can make more money growing maize for biofuels than it can growing corn for people to eat, it will do so. This can contribute to the global fuel crisis.

Lesson Summary

In this lesson you looked at the impact of economic activity on the global environment. You learned that the environmental impact of economic activity is worldwide. Pollution and climate change do not respect borders. You also learned that while some environmental challenges affect all people, they may affect the poor in a more serious way than they do the rich. Solutions to the challenges presented by economic growth may need global co-operation to resolve.
