Steps to Being Successful in an Independent Study Course

1. Take Responsibility

You have enrolled in this course because you have a need to learn outside a traditional classroom and a desire to be successful.  Are you prepared to do what it takes to achieve success?  Be disciplined and committed.  Be willing to problem solve and search for answers.  Actively seek help when you need it - not just from your teacher, but from all the resources you have access to.  In an independent study environment, your teacher is a facilitator of learning; they do not simply deliver the knowledge and you simply open your mind to receive it.  Confidently seek it and pursue learning.  It may take some time to reframe your mindset to embrace this way of learning as a lifelong skill that will serve you well.  You will be supported and encouraged along the way, but ultimately the effort must come from you.  Are you ready?  Great!

2.  Invest Your Time

Remember that in a traditional high school course, you would be spending 80 minutes per day in class (160 minutes if you’re on a quarter schedule), plus doing additional homework and/or studying in the evening.  You should expect and plan to spend the same amount of time per day doing the course by independent study, perhaps more if you encounter a topic/concept that is particularly challenging for you.  Working at your own pace means there is the flexibility to spend less time on concepts you master easily and more time on concepts you struggle with, but the total time invested should be about the same as a traditional course.

3.  Manage Your Learning

Learning no longer follows the format of a classroom, but you can still apply a structure at school and at home.

 

  1. Gather information.
    - Carefully read your textbook/literary text.  You should always read the content at the beginning of each lesson to learn new vocabulary and have a general idea of what you will be learning.  Information is provided to demonstrate what processes and strategies you need to understand.
    - Use the online resources and videos to help you or search online for videos.  It is not expected that you watch every video, it is expected that you watch enough videos to gain an understanding of the concepts in each unit.  Remember that you have control over how fast the videos go, as well as the ability to pause, rewind or fast-forward as you feel necessary. Reading the news enhances your understanding and increases evidence that you can use when writing.
    - The more methods you use to learn, the more familiar you will become with the content. 

  2. Take notes. 
    Record new definitions, important events in your reading, structures and any other information that you think is relevant.  Write it in your own words. Write thoughts and questions. Follow up with your questions.  Develop a system of communication that works for you so that when you go back to your notes later, they still make sense and are helpful when you get stuck.  A good place for support is https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/purdue_owl.html

  3. Write, write, write. Read, read, read.  
    You do not need to write an essay for every question, but increase your writing skills and stamina by crafting longer answers where possible. Incorporate supporting evidence (specific/precise examples and/or effectively embedded quotations in English) and current events (Social) to substantiate your response. Repetition is important.  We often need to re-read sections to gain understanding, and when re-reading we can see new information that we missed the first time.  Try to keep information fresh by attempting to add to a question from a previous lesson each day.  Our brains need information to be layered in order to have comprehension and recall.You may understand something right away, but in order for it to “stick”, the pathways in your brain have to be solidified. If you were walking through a forest, it would be very difficult to follow a trail that had only been travelled once or twice.  A well-worn path with recognizable landmarks would be much easier to navigate!  It is the same with your memory.  Repetition is important.  (See what I did there?)  

 

  1. Challenge yourself. 
    Once you understand a concept, add to a piece of writing using your new understanding.  Don’t just move on to the next lesson; you need more than just the basics to be successful.  You need to be comfortable enough with your understanding that you can adapt to new situations.

 

  1. Seek help.
    Help is available in many forms.  Many students struggle with the same concepts and ask the same questions.  You will likely find support for frequently asked questions on the internet.  If your question is not answered there, be organized and deliberate so you can use your time with your teacher most effectively.  Make a list or use a symbol like a star or question mark to indicate what you need help with.  Identify what you already know, and ask something specific.  Make sure to review these questions later and practice until you can explain the info to someone else.

  2. Assess your learning. 
    Complete any chapter reviews that are available.  In English, be sure to thoroughly answer all questions in the booklet so that you have a solid understanding of characterization and themes. Check your understanding with Exam Bank. Look at Chapter reviews in the textbook (Social). Use a rubric and grade your own work and analyze your mistakes or stumbling blocks. For BOTH English and Social, read diploma exam student exemplars and the mark rationales available on the Alberta Ed website.
