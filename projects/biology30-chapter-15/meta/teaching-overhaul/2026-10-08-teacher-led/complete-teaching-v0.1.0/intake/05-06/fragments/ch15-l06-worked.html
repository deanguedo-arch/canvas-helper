Worked example

Separate a visible change from a functional claim

A classroom observation record gives three findings: a region of tissue folds into a hollow tube; some cells begin making different proteins and develop new functions; and a small limb bud becomes visible. A student labels all three findings “growth” and concludes that development is complete. How should the record be interpreted?

  1. Classify the folding. Changing a sheet into a tube is a change in shape and organization, so it illustrates morphogenesis. If the stated tissue is the neural plate and its folds close, that particular process is neurulation. Shape alone without tissue identity would not identify every tube as a neural tube.
  2. Classify the new cell functions. Different proteins and specialized activities illustrate differentiation. They describe what cells do, not simply how many cells exist.
  3. Interpret the limb bud. It establishes that limb formation is under way as part of organogenesis. It does not establish mature muscles, joints or coordinated movement.
  4. Check the conclusion. The findings describe ongoing developmental processes. None proves completion, and later growth and maturation are still necessary.

Now apply the reasoning to the actual neural-tube figure. Follow surface ectoderm into a plate, raised folds and a tube below the surface. The changed position is the result of morphogenesis; it does not change the tissue's ectodermal origin. That tube can develop into the brain and spinal cord even though those structures are internal later.
