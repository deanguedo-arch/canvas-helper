Three kinds of change work together

Development is not only growth. Cell division increases the number of cells, produces specialized cell functions, and changes their arrangement into a body form. is the formation of early organs from those tissues.

These events overlap. A developing structure can contain cells that are still dividing while others become more specialized. Use the terms to describe the kind of change being discussed, rather than imagining completely separate switches that turn on one at a time.

Follow neural-tube formation

During , a region of becomes the neural plate. Its edges rise as neural folds, producing a groove between them. The folds move together and fuse, forming the neural tube below the surface.

The neural tube develops into the brain and spinal cord. This sequence explains why nervous tissue has an ectodermal origin even though it lies inside the body later. Tissue position changes during folding.

Neurulation occurs early, during the third and fourth weeks after . A disruption of closure can affect the organization of the nervous system and surrounding structures. The position and timing of a disruption matter; the same word “developmental change” does not describe one identical outcome.

A basic organ is not a mature organ

Most basic organ structures become established during the embryonic period, through about eight weeks after fertilization. The fetal period then involves substantial growth and functional maturation. Lungs can begin forming long before they can support breathing outside the uterus.

Look at developmental images for the appearance of structures, but do not infer full function from visible shape. A limb bud is evidence of a developing limb, not a completely formed hand. A neural tube is a foundation for the nervous system, not an adult brain.

Developmental questions are often asking for this distinction: what has begun to form, what is continuing to differentiate, and what still needs to mature?

Worked example

Explain the origin of an internal structure

A student says the spinal cord cannot arise from ectoderm because ectoderm is the outer layer.

  1. Begin with early position: neural tissue begins in a specialized region of ectoderm.
  2. Follow the movement: the neural plate forms folds, which meet and close.
  3. The resulting neural tube lies below the surface.
  4. Therefore an initially outer layer can give rise to an internal structure through morphogenesis.
