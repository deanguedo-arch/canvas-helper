## 2. Two ordinary repairs

**Location:** lesson 05, `ch15-l05-teaching-02`, immediately before/after `ch15-source-membranes`.

**Evidence:** the protected image and its caption request locating a connecting stalk. The actual p. 516 image labels amnion, amniotic cavity, chorion, yolk sac, allantois, endometrium, developing placenta and maternal blood vessels, but has no separate connecting-stalk label. **Recheck:** read this sentence together with both actual figures; verify the learner uses E for connecting stalk, follows the separate allantois endpoint in the original picture, and does not equate the two. Recheck the worked E explanation and unchanged A–F key.

### R2: Identify the migrating cell population and qualify the source’s broad derivative claim

**Location:** lesson 06, `ch15-l06-teaching-02`, paragraph beginning “The source figure also shows cells near the folds…”.

**Evidence:** original Figure 15.8D and the candidate pixels describe cells leaving the fold margins as forming bones/muscle; the embedded caption adds other organs. The existing lesson warns against concluding that *all* bones and muscles arise from the neural tube. That prevents an extreme generalization but leaves the identity and actual meaning of the labelled cells unresolved.

This is an underspecified claim, not grounds to assert that neural crest forms no muscle or no bone. Human neural-crest research identifies migration from dorsal neural tube and includes craniofacial skeletal and peripheral-neural derivatives. Animal lineage studies distinguish neural-crest-derived connective tissue/tendons and skeletal elements from mesodermal skeletal-muscle progenitors. Those studies have different species/method limits and should not be presented as direct human lineage tracing. Sources: [Thomas et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18689800/) and [Grenier et al., 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0004381).

**Recommended replacement within the permitted teaching paragraph, leaving source pixels/captions untouched:**

“The cells leaving the fold margins are neural crest cells, distinct from the cells that remain in the neural tube. Depending on their position, neural crest cells contribute to structures such as peripheral nerves and some craniofacial bone. The source’s broad ‘bones and muscle’ wording is not a general origin rule for the skeleton or skeletal muscles: much of the skeleton and most skeletal muscle develop from mesoderm. For this sequence, keep the neural tube’s future brain and spinal cord separate from the migrating neural crest cells.”

A slightly shorter explanation is acceptable if it preserves that population distinction and avoids the false categorical assertion that neural crest cannot contribute to any muscle. The protected source figure can be retained with a sufficiently local clarification. If the owner later wants the visible figure label itself changed, that is a separate protected-content decision.

**Recheck:** have a reader explain which cells remain in the tube, which migrate, what the neural tube forms, and why the generic muscle label is not a skeletal-muscle origin rule. Reinspect A–D and the final cross-section; preserve all required assessment wording/models/keys.

## 3. Scientific/source findings

- **Yolk-sac correction: supported.** Original p. 516 makes an absolute no-nutrition statement. Lesson 05 correctly separates absence of a large yolk store from early nutritional/metabolic roles. Human yolk-sac transcriptomics and protein/spatial data support the limited positive correction and early blood formation. The later liver/bone-marrow succession avoids the teacher slide’s overly direct handoff to bone marrow. [Goh et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37590359/). The independently found [Cindrova-Davies et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5474779/) supports nutrient-processing/transport potential, while its more specific pathway model is explicitly inferential; the candidate does not improperly turn that model into a measured detailed route.
- **Allantois wording: appropriately limited.** The candidate describes a small outgrowth associated with connecting stalk and development of the bladder–umbilical connection. It does not repeat the old source’s implication that allantois alone equals the cord or the entire bladder. Human histology supports the connection/urachus association while underscoring developmental complexity. The candidate need not add the research paper’s specific stage claims, especially because its abstract uses gestational age. [Liu et al., 2022, first online 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34854363/).
- **Placenta/cord physiology: sound within this scope.** The explanation matches original pp. 516–517 and teacher slide 20 for exchange direction and separates maternal/fetal blood. Two arteries away from fetus and one vein returning toward it are correctly described as the usual anatomy. Direction, not oxygenation, defines artery/vein. The maternal contribution is more accurately stated as uterine tissue than the textbook’s incomplete blood-pool wording.
- **Neurulation sequence: supported with appropriate model limits.** Ectodermal neural plate, folds, enclosed tube and subsequent separation are consistent with original p. 514 and teacher slide 19. Notochord is correctly distinct from neural tube; the candidate does not repeat p. 513’s broad skeletal-framework fate claim. The third/fourth-week statement uses “mainly,” and the text avoids an instantaneous whole-length closure. Original human-embryo studies support progressive primary neurulation and caution against mapping animal models directly onto humans. [O’Rahilly and Müller, 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/tera.10007); [Santos et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11620743/). Secondary neurulation continues beyond the primary closure event, so retaining the qualifier is important; a detailed secondary-neurulation extension is unnecessary for these objectives.
- **Organ formation versus function: sound.** The candidate does not reproduce the teacher timeline as a claim of mature function, does not treat the approximate organ percentage as percentage of adult capability, and does not treat an early lung bud as independent air-breathing capacity. Prenatal oxygen delivery via placental exchange is correctly used. Beginning of week nine after fertilization is consistent with the supplied source’s transition after week eight.
- **“Eyes open” caution: useful and not a required local-assessment fact.** The candidate treats old textbook wording as early appearance, explicitly excluding normal mature vision. The broad distinction is reasonable; do not turn this into a new detailed eyelid timeline without an exact source. I located a primary human-eyelid study by Byun et al. (2011), but full publisher retrieval failed, so I do not claim a full independent detailed ocular-timeline audit.
