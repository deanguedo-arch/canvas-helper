Begin by locating the embryo. The amnion encloses the amniotic cavity around it. Amniotic fluid cushions the embryo and later the fetus, permits movement and helps provide a stable physical environment.The chorion is farther outward. Chorionic villi extend toward maternal tissues and contribute to the fetal part of the placenta. The words chorion and amnion therefore identify different structures, even though both help support development.Early extraembryonic support structuresView largerThe amnion surrounds the embryo and amniotic cavity; the chorion lies outside it. The yolk sac is separate from the amniotic cavity, and the connecting stalk links toward the outer tissues. Schematic — not to scale.Small early structures still have important rolesThe yolk sac lies outside the embryo's body early in development. In humans it does not contain a large reserve of yolk like a bird egg. It contributes to early blood-cell formation and other developmental processes before later organs take over these roles.The allantois is a small outgrowth associated with the connecting stalk. It contributes to an early connection involved in bladder development. Avoid transferring the picture of a large waste-storage allantoic sac from a bird egg directly to a human embryo.As development proceeds, the connecting structures become organized into the umbilical cord. The cord contains fetal blood vessels linking the fetus with the placenta. It is a transport connection, not a tube through which maternal blood directly enters the fetus.Membranes around the developing embryoView largerLocate the amnion, amniotic cavity, chorion, yolk sac and connecting stalk. The yolk sac is outside the amniotic cavity. Inquiry into Biology, Chapter 15, p. 516.Use both location and functionIn a labelling activity, follow the endpoint of each leader line. A line inside a space can indicate a cavity, while a line on its boundary can indicate a membrane. Do not give the same answer to both just because they are close together.For a functional question, make the connection explicit. The amniotic environment provides physical support; chorionic villi participate in exchange; the cord transports fetal blood; early sacs have developmental roles. No single membrane performs every function.Worked exampleDistinguish a membrane from a vesselA diagram shows a fluid-filled space around the fetus and a separate cord connecting it with the placenta.The boundary around the fluid-filled space is the amnion.The contents are amniotic fluid, not the membrane itself.The cord contains fetal vessels for transport to and from the placenta.The placenta is the exchange organ at the uterine interface; the cord is the connection to it.Practise with supportApply the explanationTry both questions.
