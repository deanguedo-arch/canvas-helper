## R2 reattempt: migrating cells and neurulation writing

**Figure interpretation:** The plate’s edges rise and meet; the closed neural tube separates beneath the surface ectoderm. Cells leaving near the folds are identified in the new paragraph as neural crest cells, distinct from those retained in the tube. Their illustrated migration does not mean the neural tube becomes the whole skeleton or skeletal-muscle system. The tube supplies the central nervous system; the migrating population has other contributions, such as peripheral nerves and some craniofacial bone. The explanation explicitly limits the old broad muscle label and avoids the opposite error of denying every possible neural-crest muscle contribution.

**Reattempted required neurulation writing:** A specialized ectodermal region becomes the neural plate above the mesodermal notochord. The plate’s edges rise into neural folds with a groove between them. The folds meet and fuse around a hollow space. The resulting neural tube separates from surface ectoderm and develops into the brain and spinal cord. The notochord helps organize this development through signals and is not another name for the tube. Some neural crest cells leave the fold region; they should be distinguished from the cells retained in the neural tube.

**Support:** every step is in the revised visible section and source sequence. The revised paragraph is consistent with the independently inspected primary evidence identified in the initial source report. It preserves the ectoderm/mesoderm distinction, does not change the CNS answer key, and adds no claim of mature function or deterministic medical outcome. Human primary/secondary neurulation timing qualifications remain intact. **R2: Pass.**
