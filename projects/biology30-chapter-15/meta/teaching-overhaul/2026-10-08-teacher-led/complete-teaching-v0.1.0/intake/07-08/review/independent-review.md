## Scientific findings and source reconciliation

1. **Placental exchange and vessels: Pass.** The teaching separates maternal intervillous blood from fetal capillaries, traces the two-artery/one-vein circuit, distinguishes direction from oxygen content and avoids making separation an absolute filter. Gas directions agree with original pp.516–517 and teacher20. Hormone and temporary passive-antibody roles are supported. Normal bulk separation does not claim that absolutely no cells ever cross.
2. **Transport mechanism qualification: Pass after repair R1.** The original p.517 image caption groups nutrients and oxygen under diffusion. The teaching correctly distinguishes mechanisms rather than endorsing a universal passive mechanism. Primary human perfused-placenta work demonstrates carrier-mediated glucose movement and active transport contributions for amino acids: [Challier et al., 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3836401/) and [Schneider et al., 1979](https://www.nature.com/articles/pr1979148). These support the course-level distinction; they do not imply every nutrient follows one identical route.
3. **Ten-week/600-g correction: Pass.** The problematic paired statement is visibly printed on p.516. Human placental villi continue growing and maturing after ten weeks; [Jackson et al., 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1438084/) directly studied human placentae at10–41 gestational weeks. Delivered-term mass around500–600g is a reasonable broad association, not a diagnostic threshold: one term cohort reported a mean around494g and wide variation, while an untrimmed term cohort reported590g; method/population matter. [Sri Lankan term study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10192661/), [Nigerian term study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3530253/). The candidate explicitly retains variation and rejects using mass alone to assess individual function.
4. **Dating and period boundaries: Pass.** Original p.520 says the fetal period starts in week nine and lasts until birth, then later incorrectly equates266 days with40 weeks after fertilization. The candidate correctly computes266/7=38 and distinguishes the LMP clock. [ACOG's due-date explanation](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/when-pregnancy-goes-past-your-due-date) places40 weeks/280days from LMP. The candidate treats a two-week offset as a stated classroom assumption rather than an exact personal date. Teacher22's9–12 label is correctly bounded to its first-trimester panel. The source p.518 graph visibly uses weeks after implantation, and the candidate preserves that exception.
5. **Developmental sensitivity: Pass, with model limits.** The retained slide21 chart has compressed, unequal intervals, early “usually not susceptible” wording and qualitative sensitivity bands. The candidate explains all three limitations and does not infer probability or viability. [MotherToBaby, critical periods](https://mothertobaby.org/fact-sheets/critical-periods-development/) supports timing-dependent structural versus functional effects and an early all-or-none pattern with exceptions. It is not evidence that every later exposure has the same effect or that early exposure is safe.
6. **Blastocyst differentiation: Pass.** Actual teacher32 says blastocyst cells have not begun differentiating. The candidate rejects that absolute. Direct human-embryo research identifies distinct blastocyst lineages: [Blakeley et al., 2015](https://journals.biologists.com/dev/article/142/18/3151/46887/Defining-the-three-cell-lineages-of-the-human), [human founder-lineage study, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37102672/). No mouse-only result or stem-cell model was substituted for human evidence.
7. **Vitamin C: Pass as a cautious correction; protected question remains unresolved.** Original p.523 asserts teratogenicity and newborn deficiency; p.528 Q3 builds that into a single-answer classification. [MotherToBaby's vitamin-C fact sheet](https://mothertobaby.org/fact-sheets/vitamin-c/) reports that increased birth-defect risk is not established, evidence is limited, and the cited postnatal-deficiency report involved two babies. The candidate appropriately separates lack of established risk, limited evidence, a reported deficiency outcome and a claim of unlimited safety. It does not give treatment instructions.
8. **Concrete alcohol example: Pass after repair R2.** The added example connects prenatal exposure with continuing brain development and possible later learning/memory/attention difficulties, retaining variability and avoiding diagnosis from photographs. This restores a concrete agent-to-effect link from teacher32 and p.522 without reproducing stigmatizing generalizations. [NIAAA's FASD explanation](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/understanding-fetal-alcohol-spectrum-disorders) supports this account; [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/fasd/about/) independently supports variable effects and continuing brain-development vulnerability.

No source of this review supports inferring an individual's diagnosis, behavior, culpability or prognosis from an exposure label, photograph, placental size or broad week number. The candidate maintains that boundary.
