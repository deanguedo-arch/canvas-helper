The placenta and exchange

How can two blood supplies exchange substances without becoming one circulation?

Learning goal

Trace placental exchange and umbilical blood flow, and explain why the placenta is neither a complete barrier nor a site of normal bulk blood mixing.

Before you begin

Chorionic villi contribute to the fetal part of the placenta. Diffusion moves substances down concentration gradients across suitable surfaces.

Two circulations meet across an exchange surface

The has both fetal and maternal contributions. Fetal capillaries pass through chorionic villi, while maternal blood flows in spaces around the villi. Substances can move across the intervening tissues. The circulations do not normally join into one continuous stream.

Oxygen and nutrients move from the maternal side toward fetal blood. Carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes move from fetal blood toward the maternal side for removal by maternal organs. Different substances use different transport mechanisms, including diffusion and carrier-mediated transport.

Large surface area, a short exchange distance and continuing blood flow help maintain exchange. A placenta is therefore more than a wall; it is an organized interface whose structure affects its function.

Name umbilical vessels by direction

The umbilical cord usually contains two arteries and one vein. The arteries carry fetal blood away from the fetal heart toward the placenta. The vein returns blood from the placenta toward the fetus, carrying oxygen and nutrients obtained across the exchange surface.

This can seem backwards when you associate arteries with oxygen and veins with carbon dioxide. The naming rule is direction relative to the heart. Use that rule first, then describe oxygen content in the particular circuit.

Trace a round trip in the figure: fetus → umbilical arteries → placental fetal capillaries → → fetus. Keep that loop separate from the maternal vessels.

Protection is not complete exclusion

The placenta also releases hormones, including and estrogen later in pregnancy, and allows transfer of some maternal antibodies. It does not block every chemical or infectious agent. Alcohol and some other harmful agents can cross or affect the developing system.

For an evidence question, distinguish transport from its consequence. Showing that a substance reaches fetal blood establishes exposure, not automatically its exact effect or severity. Those depend on the substance, amount, timing and other conditions.

The essential comparison is clear: the placenta supports useful exchange and some protection, but it is not an impenetrable filter.

Worked example

Trace oxygen without mixing the blood

Explain how an oxygen molecule from maternal blood can reach a fetal tissue.

  1. Locate maternal blood near the chorionic villi.
  2. Follow oxygen across the placental exchange tissues into fetal capillaries.
  3. Follow fetal blood through the umbilical vein toward the fetus.
  4. The fetal circulation distributes oxygen to tissues.
  5. The molecule crossed an interface; the explanation does not require maternal blood to enter the fetal circulation as a bulk stream.
