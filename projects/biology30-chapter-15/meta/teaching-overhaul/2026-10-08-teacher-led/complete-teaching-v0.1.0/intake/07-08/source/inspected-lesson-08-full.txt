Gestational age is conventionally counted from the first day of the last menstrual period, commonly about two weeks earlier in an illustrative cycle. Mixing these conventions creates apparent contradictions.The embryonic period extends through about eight weeks after fertilization, with the first two weeks sometimes described separately as pre-embryonic. The fetal period begins in week nine after fertilization and continues until birth. It does not stop at twelve weeks.Growth and maturation continueDuring early development, the main body plan and basic organs become established. During the fetal period, these structures grow and mature. Brain development, skeletal changes and lung maturation continue; the appearance of an organ does not mean it is ready for independent function.Pregnancy is also divided into three trimesters. These are broad time groupings, not three abrupt biological switches. Early pregnancy emphasizes establishment of structures; the middle and later periods include substantial growth and functional maturation.A developmental timeline helps organize changes, but individual milestones vary. Do not use a single classroom diagram to predict an individual fetus's exact capacity to survive outside the uterus.Match the influence to the developmental processA teratogen is an agent that can disturb prenatal development. Examples include alcohol, some drugs and some infections. Whether an exposure causes harm depends on the agent, dose, timing and the susceptibility of the developing system.An exposure during formation of a structure may affect its organization. A later exposure can still affect growth or function, particularly in systems that continue developing. “Later” does not mean “safe,” and “early” does not mean that every exposure has the same effect.Some very early damaging exposures are described using an all-or-none pattern, but this is not a guarantee of safety. For course analysis, use the evidence supplied and identify the affected process. Do not make a personal diagnosis or suggest changing medication from a hypothetical case.Worked exampleCompare timing while holding the agent constantTwo hypothetical exposures involve the same agent and dose, but one occurs during formation of a structure and the other during its later growth.Identify the developmental process occurring at each time.During formation, changes may affect the organization of the structure.During later growth, effects may concern size, maturation or function.The comparison shows why timing matters; it does not prove either exposure is harmless.Other factors, including susceptibility, still limit individual predictions.Practise with supportApply the explanationTry both questions.
