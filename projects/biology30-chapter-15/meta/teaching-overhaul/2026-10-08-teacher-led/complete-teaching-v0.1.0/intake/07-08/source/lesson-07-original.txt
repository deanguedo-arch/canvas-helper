Fetal capillaries pass through chorionic villi, while maternal blood flows in spaces around the villi. Substances can move across the intervening tissues. The circulations do not normally join into one continuous stream.Oxygen and nutrients move from the maternal side toward fetal blood. Carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes move from fetal blood toward the maternal side for removal by maternal organs. Different substances use different transport mechanisms, including diffusion and carrier-mediated transport.Large surface area, a short exchange distance and continuing blood flow help maintain exchange. A placenta is therefore more than a wall; it is an organized interface whose structure affects its function.Placental exchange: two separate circulationsView largerMaternal blood surrounds a chorionic villus. Fetal blood flows through vessels inside that villus. Oxygen and nutrients cross inward; carbon dioxide and wastes cross outward. The blood streams stay separate. Schematic — not to scale.Name umbilical vessels by directionThe umbilical cord usually contains two arteries and one vein. The arteries carry fetal blood away from the fetal heart toward the placenta. The vein returns blood from the placenta toward the fetus, carrying oxygen and nutrients obtained across the exchange surface.This can seem backwards when you associate arteries with oxygen and veins with carbon dioxide. The naming rule is direction relative to the heart. Use that rule first, then describe oxygen content in the particular circuit.Trace a round trip in the figure: fetus → umbilical arteries → placental fetal capillaries → umbilical vein → fetus. Keep that loop separate from the maternal vessels.Placenta and chorionic villiView largerFetal vessels extend into chorionic villi, next to maternal blood spaces. Substances cross the placental interface rather than both circulations becoming one continuous blood stream. Inquiry into Biology, Chapter 15, p. 517.Protection is not complete exclusionThe placenta also releases hormones, including progesterone and estrogen later in pregnancy, and allows transfer of some maternal antibodies. It does not block every chemical or infectious agent. Alcohol and some other harmful agents can cross or affect the developing system.For an evidence question, distinguish transport from its consequence. Showing that a substance reaches fetal blood establishes exposure, not automatically its exact effect or severity. Those depend on the substance, amount, timing and other conditions.The essential comparison is clear: the placenta supports useful exchange and some protection, but it is not an impenetrable filter.Worked exampleTrace oxygen without mixing the bloodExplain how an oxygen molecule from maternal blood can reach a fetal tissue.Locate maternal blood near the chorionic villi.Follow oxygen across the placental exchange tissues into fetal capillaries.Follow fetal blood through the umbilical vein toward the fetus.The fetal circulation distributes oxygen to tissues.The molecule crossed an interface; the explanation does not require maternal blood to enter the fetal circulation as a bulk stream.Practise with supportApply the explanationTry both questions.
