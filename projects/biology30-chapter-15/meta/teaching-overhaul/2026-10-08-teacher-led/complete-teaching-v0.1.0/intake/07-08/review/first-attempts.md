5. Required selection 2: **It permits selective exchange but does not exclude all harmful substances**. 6. Required writing 1: Fetal blood travels away from the fetal heart toward the placenta through the umbilical arteries. In the fetal capillaries of the placenta, oxygen crosses from maternal blood through intervening tissues into fetal blood, while carbon dioxide crosses from fetal blood toward maternal blood. Maternal blood can then carry that carbon dioxide to maternal organs for removal. The fetal blood returns toward the fetus through the umbilical vein, relatively richer in oxygen and poorer in carbon dioxide after exchange. These are two separate blood streams exchanging substances, not normally becoming one bulk circulation. Support: all route/gas/separation steps appear in the exchange explanation and “Follow the fetal circuit”; removal appears in the worked example. No inference from red/blue alone is required.
7. Required writing 2: The placenta supports development by transferring oxygen and nutrients toward fetal blood, moving carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes toward maternal blood, and producing hormones such as progesterone and estrogen. Some maternal antibodies also cross and give temporary passive protection. The separating tissues are selective rather than an impenetrable wall, so some harmful substances or infectious agents can cross or affect the developing system. Exposure therefore remains possible. Whether an exposure produces a particular injury depends on the agent, amount, timing and susceptibility; crossing alone does not prove a particular injury. Support: first exchange section, “Useful exchange does not make a perfect filter,” and explicit exposure-versus-outcome paragraph.
