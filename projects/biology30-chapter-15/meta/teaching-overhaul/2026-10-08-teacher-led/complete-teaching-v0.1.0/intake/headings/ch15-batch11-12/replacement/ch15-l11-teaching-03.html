Separate mechanism, evidence and values

A mechanism explains how a method could change reproduction. Effectiveness asks how often a specified outcome occurs under stated conditions. These are different questions. For an IVF claim, ask whether “success” means fertilization, implantation, an ongoing pregnancy or birth. Evidence for one stage does not automatically establish success at every later stage.

Compare evidence using the same outcome, population and time period. For a contraceptive method, correct use and typical use are not interchangeable conditions. For safety, identify the possible effect, who experiences it and how often it occurs. The old source's percentages and treatment descriptions should not be treated as current, universal predictions or individual instructions.

Decisions also involve values and circumstances, including informed consent, access, cost, preferences and the handling of embryos or genetic information. For example, evidence might describe a chance of pregnancy, but it cannot by itself decide how a family should weigh cost or embryo-related concerns. A reasoned response distinguishes the biological evidence, its uncertainty and the value judgment instead of claiming one choice is right for everyone.
