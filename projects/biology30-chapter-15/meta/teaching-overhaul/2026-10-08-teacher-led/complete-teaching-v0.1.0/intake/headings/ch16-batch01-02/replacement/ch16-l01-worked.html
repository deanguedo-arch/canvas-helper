Worked example

Identify the relationship before doing the arithmetic

Given: A model cell contains a long maternal chromosome, a long paternal chromosome, a short maternal chromosome and a short paternal chromosome. The long chromosomes are one type and the short chromosomes another. Question: How many sets and chromosomes are present, and what would replication change?

  1. Identify the types: long and short give two types. One complete set therefore contains two chromosomes, so n = 2.
  2. Group the homologues: the maternal and paternal long chromosomes form one pair; the two short chromosomes form the other. Each pair can carry different alleles at corresponding loci.
  3. Count the sets: two of every type means two complete sets. The cell is diploid, with 2n = 2 × 2 = 4 chromosomes.
  4. Change the condition: after replication, each of those four chromosomes has two joined sister chromatids. There are still four replicated chromosomes before the sisters separate, not eight independently counted homologues.

Check: pairing by parental origin describes homologues; copying one chromosome produces sisters. Neither condensation nor the X-shaped drawing alone tells you how many chromosome sets a cell has.
