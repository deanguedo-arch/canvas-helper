Interphase contains different kinds of work

During G1, a cell grows and carries out its normal activities. During , it replicates its DNA. During G2, it continues preparing for division, including producing and organizing materials needed for movement. Together G1, S and G2 make up .

Interphase is not inactivity. Many observed cells are in interphase because it occupies much of the cycle. The proportion of cells seen in each stage can provide evidence about relative time spent there, if the sampled population and observation method are appropriate.

Replication prepares two copies for distribution

In S phase, each chromosome's DNA is copied. The replicated chromosome consists of two associated at the centromere region. This provides copies that can later be distributed to daughter cells.

Keep chromosome number and DNA amount separate. A human body cell in G1 normally has 46 chromosomes. After S phase, before the sister chromatids separate, it still has 46 chromosomes but now has 92 chromatids. The DNA amount has doubled.

The X-shaped drawing is a simplified way of showing a condensed replicated chromosome. DNA is not necessarily condensed into that shape throughout S phase. A drawing can make relationships clearer without being a literal image of every stage.

Division has nuclear and cytoplasmic parts

Mitosis distributes replicated chromosomes into two nuclei. Cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm, producing separate daughter cells. These processes are coordinated but not identical. A cell can divide its nucleus without immediately completing cytoplasmic division.

Cell-cycle checkpoints help regulate whether a cell continues through the cycle. If regulation fails, uncontrolled division can contribute to cancer. “Dividing quickly” and “cancer” are not synonyms: normal growth and repair can also involve rapid cell division.

Follow the sequence as a preparation-and-distribution process. Replication supplies copies before mitosis separates them; cytokinesis then establishes separate cells.

Worked example

Follow one chromosome through preparation

A G1 cell has an unreplicated chromosome with one DNA molecule.

  1. During S phase, the DNA is copied.
  2. The copied structures remain associated as sister chromatids of one chromosome.
  3. DNA amount has doubled for that chromosome, but the chromosome has not yet separated into two counted chromosomes.
  4. During anaphase, separation will change the chromosome count within the still-undivided cell.
