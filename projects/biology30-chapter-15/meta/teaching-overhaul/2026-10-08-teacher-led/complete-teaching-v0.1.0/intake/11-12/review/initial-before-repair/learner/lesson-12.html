Rebuild the sequence by explaining each change

Use this review to connect the mechanisms already taught, then revisit a topic in the table if a step is uncertain. In typical human fertilization, usually in an oviduct, one sperm and one oocyte contribute haploid chromosome sets: 23 + 23 restores a diploid complement of 46 in the zygote. Cleavage then increases cell number with little overall growth, dividing the starting cytoplasm among smaller cells.

A compact morula develops into a blastocyst with a cavity, an outer trophoblast region and an inner cell mass. Implantation involves attachment to and interaction with the uterine endometrium; merely being a blastocyst in the uterus does not establish that attachment. Gastrulation reorganizes embryonic cells into three primary germ layers. Ectoderm contributes epidermis and nervous tissue, mesoderm contributes tissues such as skeletal muscle, and endoderm contributes digestive and respiratory epithelial linings.

Differentiation produces specialized cells; morphogenesis shapes structures; organogenesis establishes organs. Neurulation forms the neural tube from ectoderm. These processes are connected, but appearance of an early organ does not prove mature performance. Continued growth and functional development are still required.

Check the clock before assigning a stage: age from fertilization and gestational age from the last menstrual period have different starting points. Gestational age is conventionally about two weeks greater, not an exact conversion for every pregnancy. Using post-fertilization age, the fetal period begins in week nine. A report saying only “week ten” leaves its dating convention unclear; seeing an organ also leaves its level of function unclear.

Trace support, exchange and feedback separately

Early trophoblast-derived hCG supports the corpus luteum, which continues progesterone and estrogen secretion supporting the endometrium. Later, the placenta contributes these hormones. Compare that support pathway with labour: cervical stretch sends sensory information to the hypothalamus; posterior-pituitary oxytocin travels in blood to uterine muscle; stronger contractions can increase cervical stretch. This return to a larger initiating stimulus makes the labour loop positive feedback. Delivery reduces its main stretch input. Do not label the hCG support chain negative feedback merely because it maintains pregnancy; classify feedback only when the return effect on a starting signal is specified.

Placental exchange also depends on structure. Maternal and fetal circulations normally remain separate while substances cross the interface. Umbilical arteries carry fetal blood toward the placenta; the umbilical vein returns blood toward the fetus. Less exchange area or a thicker barrier can reduce diffusive exchange, even if blood still flows. Neither change means the two circulations must mix.

After birth, keep lactation's functions distinct: anterior-pituitary prolactin supports milk production; posterior-pituitary oxytocin supports contraction that moves existing milk toward the nipple. A tissue can receive a hormone yet respond poorly, so a normal signal alone does not prove a normal target response. Apply the same reasoning to technologies: normal hormone release does not prove an unobstructed reproductive duct.

Worked example

Compare two different developmental barriers

Case A forms a blastocyst that cannot attach to the endometrium. Case B implants normally, but a later placenta has reduced exchange area.

  1. Case A concerns implantation, before a stable uterine attachment is established.
  2. Case B concerns exchange after implantation; reduced villus area can reduce transport capacity.
  3. Do not assign both cases the explanation “fertilization failed,” because fertilization already occurred.
  4. Identify the affected stage and its mechanism before predicting downstream effects.
