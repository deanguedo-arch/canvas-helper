Worked example

Compare two interventions without merging their mechanisms

Given: Model A completely blocks both vasa deferentia but leaves testicular hormone release into blood intact. Model B suppresses the usual ovarian LH surge. Question: How can both reduce the chance of fertilization while acting at different steps?

  1. Locate A: the altered structure is a sperm-transport duct. Sperm cannot follow that route into the ejaculatory pathway, although the stated blood-borne hormone release continues.
  2. Locate B: the altered event is a hormonal signal that helps trigger ovulation. Suppressing the surge makes release of an oocyte less likely in this model; it is not a physical duct blockage.
  3. Connect to a shared outcome: fertilization requires sperm and oocyte to meet. A changes sperm transport; B changes oocyte release. Different mechanisms can reduce the same later event.
  4. Check the limit: neither model supplies a clinical success rate or says all reproductive organs stop functioning. Keep the stated intact function in the explanation.

If a different model thickened cervical mucus while ovulation continued, an oocyte could still be released while sperm passage became more difficult. That is another reason to identify the affected step before predicting the outcome.
