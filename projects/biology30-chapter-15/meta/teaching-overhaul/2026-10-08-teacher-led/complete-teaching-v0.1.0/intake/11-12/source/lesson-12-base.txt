Follow development at three levels
At the cell level, fertilization restores a diploid chromosome complement and cleavage increases cell number. At the tissue level, the blastocyst has distinct regions and gastrulation establishes germ layers. At the organism level, morphogenesis and organogenesis establish structures that continue growing and maturing.
Alongside these changes, supporting tissues provide exchange, protection and hormones. Keep the embryo or fetus, its membranes, the placenta and maternal tissues distinct. They cooperate without becoming one indistinguishable structure.
Explain a pathway rather than listing terms
For each mechanism identify a starting change, a signal or process, its target and the resulting effect. hCG maintains corpus-luteum activity early in pregnancy. Placental exchange moves substances across an interface. Cervical stretch and oxytocin reinforce labour. Prolactin and oxytocin support different lactation functions.
When a condition changes, ask which step is affected. Normal hormone concentrations do not prove normal transport or receptor function. A visible organ does not prove mature function. A measured exposure does not by itself establish an exact developmental outcome.

Topic
What you should be able to explain
Fertilization and the zygote
Explain where fertilization normally occurs, how one sperm contributes its chromosomes, and how the diploid chromosome number is restored.
Cleavage, morula, blastocyst and implantation
Distinguish cleavage, morula, blastocyst and implantation, and connect each stage with its location and cell organization.
hCG and maintaining early pregnancy
Trace hCG through the corpus luteum to progesterone and the endometrium, and explain the later placental contribution.
Gastrulation and the three germ layers
Describe gastrulation and connect ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm with representative tissues.
Extraembryonic membranes and support structures
Distinguish amnion, chorion, yolk sac, allantois and umbilical cord, using their positions and functions.
Organogenesis and neurulation
Explain differentiation, morphogenesis, organogenesis and neural-tube formation without equating early structure with mature function.
The placenta and exchange
Trace placental exchange and umbilical blood flow, and explain why the placenta is neither a complete barrier nor a site of normal bulk blood mixing.
Fetal development and developmental influences
Distinguish embryonic and fetal development, identify the age convention used, and explain how timing, dose and susceptibility affect developmental outcomes.
Labour and birth as positive feedback
Trace cervical stretch, neural signalling and oxytocin, then distinguish the three stages of parturition.
Lactation and hormonal control after birth
Distinguish prolactin-driven milk production from oxytocin-driven milk ejection and connect both with suckling and post-birth hormone changes.
Reproductive technologies and informed decisions
Compare the physiological or mechanical action of reproductive technologies and identify what evidence can and cannot establish.
Worked example
Compare two different developmental barriers
Case A forms a blastocyst that cannot attach to the endometrium. Case B implants normally, but a later placenta has reduced exchange area.
Case A concerns implantation, before a stable uterine attachment is established.
Case B concerns exchange after implantation; reduced villus area can reduce transport capacity.
Do not assign both cases the explanation “fertilization failed,” because fertilization already occurred.
Identify the affected stage and its mechanism before predicting downstream effects.
