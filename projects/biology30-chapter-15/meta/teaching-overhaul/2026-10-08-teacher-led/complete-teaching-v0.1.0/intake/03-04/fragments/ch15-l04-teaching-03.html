How can related cells do different jobs?

Differentiation is the process through which cells become specialized in structure and function. For example, muscle cells develop machinery for contraction while nerve cells develop structures used for communication. They generally retain the same inherited genome, the complete set of genetic information, rather than acquiring a completely new chromosome set for each job.

What changes is how cells use that information. Gene expression means using the information in a gene to make functional products. Different patterns of gene expression, together with signals from surrounding cells and tissues, help cells take different developmental paths. Establishing cell positions during gastrulation matters because position influences which signals cells encounter.

Keep the processes connected but distinct. Cleavage divides the early structure into more cells. Gastrulation reorganizes cells into tissue-forming layers. Differentiation develops specialized cell types, and morphogenesis produces the shape and organization of structures. These processes interact during development; they are not four interchangeable names for cell division.

When a question names an organ, look for the particular tissue it asks about. Intestinal smooth muscle and intestinal epithelial lining have different functions and origins even though they lie beside each other. A developmental change affecting one contribution need not affect the other identically. Do not turn a tissue-origin explanation into a diagnosis or a prediction that every tissue from that germ layer must be damaged.

The embryo's own layers are also different from the tissues and membranes that support it. Having explained its tissue-forming organization, we will next examine the surrounding support structures and what each contributes.
