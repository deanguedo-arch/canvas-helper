## hCG source, target and limits
Main supplied evidence: textbook printed510–512, actual teacher11–13. Slide13 states trophoblast is the only possible hCG source and any presence confirms pregnancy. The candidate instead keeps the normal early-pregnancy chain and limits what detection alone proves.

Primary corroboration: Cole & Gutierrez (2009), *Production of human chorionic gonadotropin during the normal menstrual cycle*, J Reprod Med54:245–250, PMID19438167; authoritative indexed abstract read at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19438167/ . Daily samples across normal cycles detected hCG, contradicting the absolute pregnancy-only source claim. This does not imply all commercial tests detect those low amounts, establish false-positive rates, or supply a clinical test-interpretation protocol. No concentrations, diagnoses, retesting schedules or treatments were added to learner copy.

Authoritative physiology: Tal & Taylor, *Endocrinology of Pregnancy*, Endotext, updated6February2024, publisher PDF https://www.endotext.org/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/endocrinology-of-pregnancy-2.pdf , especially pp9–10 on corpus-luteum support and luteal–placental transition. It supports hCG stimulating luteal progesterone production and a first-trimester shift toward placenta. Its weeks are gestational dating, so the candidate deliberately avoids importing exact week numbers into a fertilization-age convention. Only bounded physiology was used, not clinical interventions from the reference.

The revised03 differentiates hormone source, receptor-bearing target and downstream hormone. It does not say hCG turns into progesterone, progesterone is absent everywhere before pregnancy, or measured hormone guarantees target response. Declining corpus-luteum output is not universal zero hormone production.

## Gastrulation timing and diagram boundaries
Main supplied evidence: textbook511–513 and actual teacher14–16. Slide14's week-two heading and the textbook's transition from an earlier two-layer disc conflate stages. Native lesson04 already says third week after fertilization; this correction is retained and the visible source conflict clarified.

Primary corroboration: Tyser et al. (2021), *Single-cell transcriptomic characterization of a gastrulating human embryo*, Nature600:285–289, DOI10.1038/s41586-021-04158-y; primary-study abstract and indexed figure descriptions read at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34789876/ . It places human gastrulation in the third post-fertilization week and characterizes one embryo staged16–19days. One specimen does not establish an exact universal onset day. No molecular atlas/experimental method was added as required Biology30 content.

Slide14 image11 is generic blastula invagination, not a literal human three-dimensional sequence. Slide16 image15 is another simplified migration picture and includes broad whole-organ derivative language. Both were inspected for source meaning, not copied as definitive human anatomy. The original native three-band SVG remains exact. Learners are told it summarizes resulting layers and tissue examples, not cell movements or body cavities.

Germ-layer names describe embryonic relationships, not final adult position. Epidermis and nervous tissue share ectodermal origin; muscle/support/circulatory tissues illustrate mesoderm; much digestive/respiratory epithelium illustrates endoderm. Organs include multiple tissues. Source whole-organ shorthand must not imply every cell in the gut or skin has one origin. Differentiation generally involves different expression of shared genetic information and responses to developmental signals, not a newly acquired genome for every specialized cell.
