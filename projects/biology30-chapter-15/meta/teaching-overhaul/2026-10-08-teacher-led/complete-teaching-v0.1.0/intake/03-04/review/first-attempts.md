## Lesson 03: seven local items

1. Guided selection: **Corpus luteum**. Visible support: “hCG travels through maternal blood and acts on the corpus luteum in the ovary” and keeps it active to release progesterone and estrogen. No unstated step needed.
2. Guided selection: **Placenta**. Visible support: progesterone production shifts toward placenta during first trimester; continued placental production explains support after early hCG peak. The other choices are not taught as hormone sources.
3. Stop question: **No. hCG and progesterone are different hormones. hCG supports the corpus luteum; the corpus luteum releases progesterone.** Visible support explicitly says hCG does not change into progesterone and is not another name for it.
4. Required selection 1: **Reduced corpus-luteum hormone support for the endometrium**. Step-to-support: the prompt makes hCG unable to act on corpus luteum → lesson explicitly distinguishes signal availability from responsive receptors → normal luteal support cannot be assumed → its progesterone/estrogen support for lining is reduced. No later fully established placental rescue is a given.
5. Required selection 2: **That implantation is definitely in the normal uterine location**. Visible support: detectable hCG alone does not show usual uterine implantation or normal development. This is the uniquely explicit unsupported diagnostic conclusion. Minor wording issue: the option “That hCG is being used as a pregnancy-associated signal” can sound like a physiological event established in this tested individual, which the teaching also does not strictly establish because hCG can occur outside pregnancy. Read as the course's classification of hCG, it is a reasonable distractor; read as a test-specific inference, it is also unestablished. I selected the clear intended answer without importing knowledge.
6. Required writing 1: **As implantation develops, trophoblast-derived tissue releases hCG. Maternal blood carries it to its direct target, the corpus luteum in the ovary. Responsive corpus-luteum cells keep this temporary ovarian structure active. The corpus luteum continues to release progesterone and estrogen, which support the endometrium. Maintaining this hormonal support helps prevent the loss of support and menstrual shedding that normally follow corpus-luteum decline.** Support: explicit source–blood–target–downstream hormone chain and preceding no-pregnancy comparison. The sample answer does not claim that hCG acts directly on the uterine lining or changes into progesterone.
7. Required writing 2: **A decline in hCG is not the same as a loss of progesterone because they are different hormones. Early on, hCG supports the corpus luteum, which releases progesterone. As the placenta develops during the first trimester, it increasingly produces progesterone. Thus progesterone support can continue even when hCG falls below its early peak. In the displayed schematic the blue curve remains supported while the red curve declines; the red curve also remains above zero. The unnumbered graph does not supply exact concentrations or an individual pregnancy's health.** Support: main explanation, graph pixels and worked example all supply these steps. No missing quantitative given is needed.

## Lesson 04: seven local items

8. Guided selection: **Ectoderm**. Support: main prose and figure key A associate epidermis with ectoderm.
9. Guided selection: **Mesoderm**. Support: main prose lists muscle and heart; figure B lists muscle and key B is mesoderm. The “heart muscle” wording names the relevant tissue.
10. Stop question: **No. Adult position alone does not reliably identify germ-layer origin. A spinal cord lies internally but is ectoderm-derived, and an organ may include different tissue origins.** Support: explicit counterexample and tissue-versus-organ teaching.
11. Required selection 1: **Epidermis and nervous tissue**. Support: both share figure band A, which the visible figure key and prose identify as ectoderm.
12. Required selection 2: **Digestive organs include muscle and connective tissue with other germ-layer origins**. Support: endoderm is taught as contributing much of epithelial lining; muscle/connective tissues are mesodermal examples. The lesson directly warns against assigning every part of an organ to its lining's origin.
13. Required writing 1: **Gastrulation changes the arrangement and developmental organization of embryonic cells. Cells move and reorganize into three primary tissue-forming germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm. It is more than another increase in cell number through cleavage. The three layers contribute to different tissue lineages, and their positions affect the developmental signals they encounter.** Support: first teaching section and later process comparison. Timing is not demanded; if included, lesson supplies third week after fertilization and distinguishes earlier two-layer disc.
14. Required writing 2: **Intestinal muscle and intestinal epithelial lining are different tissues even though they are in the same organ. Muscle fits the mesodermal contribution, whereas much of the digestive epithelial lining comes from endoderm. A developmental disruption can therefore affect intestinal muscle without affecting its epithelial lining in the same way. Their shared adult location does not give them a single germ-layer origin. This explanation does not show that every other mesodermal tissue is damaged.** Support: the teaching names muscle versus intestinal epithelial lining and warns about both organ-wide assignment and overgeneralizing developmental damage. The question does not need a specific disease mechanism, diagnosis or absent measurements.
