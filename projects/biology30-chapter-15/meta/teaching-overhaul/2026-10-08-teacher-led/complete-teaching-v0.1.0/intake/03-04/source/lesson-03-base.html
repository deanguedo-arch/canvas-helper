hCG and maintaining early pregnancy

How can a developing conceptus keep the uterine lining in place?

Learning goal

Trace hCG through the corpus luteum to progesterone and the endometrium, and explain the later placental contribution.

Before you begin

Without pregnancy support, the corpus luteum loses activity, ovarian hormone levels fall and menstruation follows.

Connect early pregnancy with the menstrual cycle

Before pregnancy, an LH surge helps trigger ovulation and formation of the . This temporary ovarian gland releases and some estrogen. Progesterone supports the endometrium so it can provide an appropriate environment for development.

Without a continuing signal associated with pregnancy, corpus-luteum activity declines. Progesterone and estrogen then fall, and the lining is shed. Early pregnancy therefore needs a way to maintain hormonal support instead of simply beginning another menstrual cycle.

Follow the signal through its target

-derived tissue releases human chorionic gonadotropin, or . In early pregnancy, hCG maintains the corpus luteum. The supported corpus luteum continues releasing progesterone and estrogen, which help maintain the uterine lining.

Say the whole chain: trophoblast-derived tissue → hCG → corpus luteum → progesterone and estrogen → supported endometrium. Skipping the corpus luteum loses the important distinction between the source of a signal and the tissue that responds to it.

As the placenta develops, it becomes able to provide substantial progesterone and estrogen. Support shifts toward placental production during the first trimester. hCG can decrease after its early peak without meaning that all hormone support has ended.

What a hormone measurement can show

Many pregnancy tests detect hCG in urine. This makes hCG an indicator associated with pregnancy, rather than a direct image of the embryo. A test result alone does not reveal location, developmental stage with precision or whether development is proceeding normally.

For a course question, use the stated evidence. A graph of rising hCG supports a conclusion about hormone concentration. To explain the effect on the lining, connect that hormone to the corpus luteum and its secretions. Questions about interpreting an individual's test belong with a qualified health professional.

Worked example

Explain a changing graph

A schematic shows an early hCG peak followed by a decline while progesterone remains high.

  1. Identify both curves; do not treat every pregnancy hormone as the same signal.
  2. Early hCG supports the corpus luteum, which contributes progesterone.
  3. The developing placenta increasingly contributes progesterone and estrogen.
  4. Continued progesterone support can therefore occur while hCG decreases from its early peak.
  5. The graph alone does not determine the health of an individual pregnancy.
