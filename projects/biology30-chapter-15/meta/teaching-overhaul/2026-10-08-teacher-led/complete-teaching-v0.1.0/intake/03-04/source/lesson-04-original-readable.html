Three layers arise through cell movement

During , cells change position and establish the embryo's three primary germ layers. In human development this process begins in the third week after . It follows earlier and formation, rather than occurring at fertilization.

The layers are called , and . Ecto- suggests outer, meso- middle, and endo- inner. These names provide an orientation aid, but the layers do not remain three simple sheets in the adult. Later folding, migration and differentiation produce complex tissues and organs.

Connect a layer with representative tissues

Ectoderm contributes to the nervous system and the epidermis, the outer layer of the skin. The nervous system is not on the outside of the adult body; its ectodermal origin becomes easier to understand when you study neural-tube formation.

Mesoderm contributes to skeletal and muscle tissues, the heart and blood vessels, blood, kidneys and much of the reproductive system. These examples connect the middle layer with support, movement, transport and several internal organ systems.

Endoderm contributes to the epithelial lining of much of the digestive and respiratory systems, and to parts of associated organs such as the liver and pancreas. Be precise: the lining of the intestine and the muscle around it have different tissue origins.

Differentiation is not a new set of genes in every cell

Cells become specialized as different genes are expressed and cells respond to signals from their surroundings. A developing nerve cell and a developing muscle cell generally do not need completely different genomes. They use genetic information differently.

When solving a germ-layer problem, identify the tissue rather than relying on the adult organ's location. A question about intestinal lining points toward endoderm; a question about intestinal smooth muscle points toward mesoderm. The words after the organ name can change the correct answer.

Worked example

Read the tissue named

Match three developing tissues: spinal-cord neurons, intestinal smooth muscle and respiratory lining.

  1. Spinal-cord neurons are nervous tissue: ectoderm.
  2. Smooth muscle is muscle tissue: mesoderm, even when it surrounds the gut.
  3. Respiratory epithelial lining: endoderm.
  4. Check that you classified the named tissue, not the entire organ as one uniform material.

Gastrulation reorganizes embryonic cells through movement and produces ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm. Each layer contributes to particular tissue lineages as development continues. This is a change in organization and developmental pathways, not just an increase in cell number.
