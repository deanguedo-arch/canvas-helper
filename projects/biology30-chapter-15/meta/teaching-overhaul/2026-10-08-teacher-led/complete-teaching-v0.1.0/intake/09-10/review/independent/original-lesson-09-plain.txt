Birth involves interacting signals Labour involves hormonal and mechanical changes. Oxytocin and locally acting prostaglandins help promote uterine contractions. The full initiation of labour is more complex than a single hormone suddenly switching on, but the contraction–stretch loop is a useful model of positive feedback . The baby's position and uterine contractions increase stretch at the cervix. Sensory information travels to the hypothalamus. The posterior pituitary releases oxytocin, which travels in blood and helps strengthen uterine contractions. Labour: an amplifying feedback loop View larger The first five features reinforce cervical stretch; birth removes the principal stimulus. Prostaglandins can also support contractions. Schematic — arrows show the specified causal sequence. Explain why the loop is positive Stronger contractions can push the baby more firmly against the cervix, increasing stretch. That additional stretch promotes further signalling and oxytocin release. The response reinforces the initiating change instead of opposing it. This is why the loop is called positive feedback. The word positive does not mean “good.” It describes the relationship between response and stimulus. Compare negative feedback, in which a response reduces a deviation or decreases the signal that initiated it. Delivery removes much of the cervical-stretch stimulus from this particular loop. A feedback explanation is incomplete unless it identifies what ends or limits the amplification. Distinguish the stages of parturition The dilation stage includes widening of the cervix as contractions continue. During the expulsion stage, the baby is delivered. During the placental stage, the placenta and associated tissues are expelled. These stages describe a sequence, not three different types of fertilization . After birth, continued uterine contraction helps the uterus reduce in size and limits bleeding at the placental attachment site. The endocrine changes also prepare the transition to lactation, which you will examine next. When interpreting a birth diagram, name the structure and the change. “Cervix” is a structure; “dilation” is what happens to it. “Oxytocin” is a signal; “uterine contraction” is a response. Worked example Classify the feedback A contraction increases cervical stretch. Further oxytocin release strengthens the next contractions. Identify the starting change: cervical stretch. Identify the response: more contraction-promoting signalling. Ask whether the response increases or reduces the starting change. Here it increases stretch. Classify the relationship as positive feedback. Name an ending event: delivery removes the main stretch stimulus in this loop. Practise with support Apply the explanation Try both questions.
