## Scientific conflicts and source limitations

1. **Prolactin during pregnancy: genuine source error already corrected natively.** Textbook526 states it is not secreted during pregnancy and that estrogen/progesterone suppress its production. Actual PPTX28 says the pituitary begins secretion after birth. The correct distinction is that prolactin is present and rises during pregnancy while placental steroid effects restrain copious secretion; their post-delivery decline permits increased milk secretion. WHO's official teaching source directly supports this, as well as the separate prolactin/oxytocin actions: https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK148970/ , sections2.4–2.5; official manual https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/333675/9789240008953-eng.pdf . The parent independently verified a pregnancy PRL study, Hu et al.2018, DOI10.1515/cclm-2017-0644; that study was not independently read in this preflight.

2. **Birth onset and continuation must stay distinct.** Teacher26 says onset is regulated by positive feedback; its image23 opens with fetal growth filling uterine space and generic uterine stretch. Teacher27 and textbook523/525 provide the clearer cervical-stretch reinforcement route. Textbook Figure15.14 explicitly qualifies the model and incomplete understanding of initiation. Retain the native qualification; do not describe this loop as a complete explanation of why human labour starts.

3. **Source wording around lactation and colostrum is loose or misleading.** Teacher28 identifies actual milk release as lactation and says a crying infant stimulates oxytocin production. For this task retain production versus ejection, with posterior release and hypothalamic synthesis distinguished. Textbook526 calls colostrum a thin fluid before milk; colostrum is early milk. Historical OBJ_2131140 asks which colostrum statement is false and keys “thicker than breast milk.” Current official sources describe colostrum as thick and as first milk, so that historical key should not be restored. The current native adaptation asks about prolactin versus oxytocin and is scientifically sound. Official support: https://www.unicef.org/uganda/key-practice-exclusive-breastfeeding and https://www.who.int/tools/elena/interventions/early-breastfeeding . This is not a new authorization to edit the historical assessment.

4. **Vitamin C task premise: carry the prior correction forward.** Original523/528 and Q3 imply a general teratogenic classification. The direct MotherToBaby fact sheet, dated1November2024, states that birth-defect risk from these intakes is not known and the evidence is limited; its rebound-deficiency discussion describes two reported babies, not a universal outcome. https://mothertobaby.org/fact-sheets/vitamin-c/ . After independent inspection of the source, this matches the previous07–08 preflight finding. Do not reintroduce categorical advice or an invented replacement key.

5. **Stages are a sequence, not universal durations or a clinical protocol.** Figure15.15's stage labels and spatial sequence support the course task. Its printed “Explusion” and “umbillical” are typos; do not propagate them. The illustrated durations, membrane-rupture timing, breech/operative-delivery discussion and waterbirth rationale are not needed to answer the protected core checks. Avoid presenting them as fixed rules for every birth. Caesarean definition can be explained without clinical recommendations.
