## Lesson 09

1. Supported practice 1 — **Stronger uterine contractions**. Teaching 01 paragraph 2 says oxytocin is released into blood and carried to uterine muscle, where it “helps strengthen contractions.” Figure C→D ends at “Uterine muscle contracts more strongly.” This directly supports the selected response. No missing step.
2. Supported practice 2 — **Placental stage**. Teaching 03 paragraph 1 defines dilation, expulsion and placental stage in that order. Paragraph 2 explicitly says if the baby is delivered but the placenta remains, expulsion is finished and the placental stage remains. No missing step.
3. Stop and think — **Positive describes a response that reinforces the starting change, not whether the outcome is healthy. Here stronger contractions increase cervical stretch, so the response can cause more of the initiating stretch.** Teaching 02 paragraph 1 explicitly says “response and starting change reinforce one another” and that positive does not mean healthy, pleasant or beneficial. Paragraph 2 contrasts an opposing negative-feedback response. No outside knowledge used.
4. Required selection 1 — **The response can increase the stretch that initiated it**. Teaching 02 paragraph 1 supplies the response→increased starting change relationship; paragraph 3 rules out immediately stopping all contractions. Teaching 01 paragraph 2 supplies the blood route, excluding the nerve distractor. No missing step.
5. Required selection 2 — **Cervical stretch → sensory signalling → oxytocin release → uterine contraction**. Teaching 01 paragraph 2 explicitly supplies cervical sensory receptors→nerve impulses→hypothalamus→posterior-pituitary oxytocin in blood→uterine muscle. Figure A→B→C→D presents the same ordered sequence. No missing step.
6. Required writing 1 — **Stretch at the cervix is detected by sensory receptors. Nerve impulses carry information to the hypothalamus. Oxytocin, made in the hypothalamus and released into the blood by the posterior pituitary, travels to uterine muscle and strengthens contractions. The contractions push the baby more firmly against the cervix, increasing stretch and renewing the signal. This is positive feedback because the response reinforces the initiating change. Delivery removes much of the pressure and limits this loop, although uterine contractions can continue afterward.** Every consequential step maps to teaching 01 paragraph 2 (sensory input, neural route, synthesis/release and blood route), teaching 01 text after the figure and teaching 02 paragraph 1 (contraction→pressure→more stretch), and teaching 02 paragraph 3 (delivery limits the loop while contractions can continue). The worked example repeats each causal link. No outside premise needed.
7. Required writing 2 — **Dilation is the stage when the cervix thins and opens. During expulsion, the baby passes through the opened cervix and birth canal and is delivered. In the placental stage, the placenta separates from the uterine wall and is delivered with associated membranes. The baby's delivery therefore completes expulsion but does not complete placental delivery.** Teaching 03 paragraphs 1–2 explicitly supply each event and the distinguishing example. No duration is assumed. No missing step.

## Lesson 10

8. Supported practice 1 — **Prolactin**. Teaching 01 paragraph 2 says anterior-pituitary prolactin supports milk production by secretory cells. Left figure branch A→B is keyed prolactin→milk production. No missing step.
9. Supported practice 2 — **Sensory nerve impulses**. Teaching 02 paragraph 1 says nipple/areola sensory receptors respond to suckling and nerve impulses carry information to the hypothalamus. Paragraph 2 distinguishes nerves, blood and ducts. Source figure 1→2 draws suckling→neural pathways. No missing step.
10. Stop and think — **Yes. Milk can already be present in the sacs or ducts but fail to move toward the nipple if the surrounding myoepithelial cells do not contract. That is impaired ejection even though production has occurred.** Teaching 01 paragraphs 1–2 distinguish collection of milk from contraction moving it. The final paragraph of teaching 01 and the worked example apply exactly this distinction. I cannot infer from that alone whether oxytocin release or the tissue response is the cause, or that prolactin is absent. No missing step.
11. Required selection 1 — **Prolactin—anterior pituitary—milk production**. Teaching 01 paragraph 2 names the release site and action together; the left figure branch repeats them. No missing step.
12. Required selection 2 — **Estrogen and progesterone concentrations fall, changing their restraint on secretion**. Teaching 03 paragraph 1 says high concentrations limit copious secretion during pregnancy and fall after placental delivery, allowing prolactin-supported secretion to increase. Paragraph 2 explicitly rejects the idea that prolactin first appears only after birth. No missing step.
13. Required writing 1 — **Prolactin is released by the anterior pituitary and supports secretory cells making milk. Oxytocin is released by the posterior pituitary and causes myoepithelial cells around the secretory structures to contract, moving milk already present toward the ducts and nipple. Thus prolactin supports production, while oxytocin supports milk ejection or let-down. Oxytocin is made in the hypothalamus before release from the posterior pituitary.** Teaching 01 paragraph 2 explicitly supports all release-site and action comparisons; paragraphs 1 and 3 separate production from movement. Figure A/B and C/D repeat the comparison. No missing step.
14. Required writing 2 — **Suckling stimulates sensory receptors in the nipple and areola. Neural impulses carry that information to the hypothalamus, which influences pituitary hormone release. Prolactin from the anterior pituitary and oxytocin from the posterior pituitary then travel in blood to mammary tissues. Prolactin supports milk production; oxytocin causes the surrounding myoepithelial cells to contract and eject available milk. This integrates a neural input and an endocrine output. The milk itself travels through ducts toward the nipple.** Teaching 02 paragraphs 1–2 support input→hypothalamus→pituitary control and distinguish the three routes; teaching 01 paragraph 2 identifies the hormone sites and responses. The overview figure shows two branches, while its accompanying text says the hypothalamic control step is compressed. The numbered source figure visibly supports only the oxytocin branch; the teaching expressly explains that limit. No outside repair needed.
