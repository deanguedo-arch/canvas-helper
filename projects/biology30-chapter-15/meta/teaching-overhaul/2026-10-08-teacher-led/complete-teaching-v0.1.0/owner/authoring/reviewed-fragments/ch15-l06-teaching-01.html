More cells, different jobs and a changing shape

Cell division increases cell number, but number alone does not explain an organ. Cells also become specialized and arranged into structures. A sheet with the usual number of cells can still have the wrong shape if its cells do not move or fold appropriately.

During , cells develop specialized structures and functions. Different patterns of gene expression mean that cells use inherited information to make different functional products, including proteins. A developing muscle cell and nerve cell generally retain the same inherited genome but use it differently. Differentiation does not require another fertilization event or a new chromosome set for each type of cell.

Cell movements, changes in cell shape and folding help turn tissue regions into organized structures. is the formation of early organs from developing tissues. These terms identify different aspects of development, but the processes overlap: cells may divide, specialize and change arrangement within the same developing organ.
