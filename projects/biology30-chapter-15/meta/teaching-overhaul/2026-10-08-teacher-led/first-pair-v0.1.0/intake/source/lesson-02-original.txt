These early divisions are called cleavage. The cells become smaller because they are dividing the starting material without a matching increase in the size of the whole structure.Imagine cutting a piece of modelling clay into two, then four pieces. You have more pieces, but you have not created four times as much clay. The comparison explains size and number only; real cleavage also requires DNA replication and controlled cell division.Cleavage: more cells, little overall growthView largerThe sequence distinguishes a single zygote, a solid morula and a blastocyst with an inner cell mass and cavity. Schematic — not to scale.Recognize organization, not only cell numberA compact ball of cells is called a morula. It commonly develops during the first few days after fertilization. A fluid-filled cavity then forms, producing a blastocyst. Do not identify these stages only by a memorized exact cell count; look for the cavity and the arrangement of cells.The blastocyst has an inner cell mass, which contributes to the embryo, and an outer trophoblast, which contributes to supporting structures including the fetal part of the placenta. The distinction is important: support tissues and the embryo develop together, but they are not the same thing.Before attachment, the blastocyst emerges from its surrounding zona pellucida. It can then interact with the uterine lining. Use the sequence figure to connect changing cell organization with movement from the oviduct to the uterus.Implantation is a later eventImplantation begins around the end of the first week after fertilization and continues into the second week. The trophoblast attaches to and grows into the endometrium. The lining provides a suitable, well-supplied environment for further development.Timing varies, so these days are approximate developmental landmarks rather than a personal pregnancy calendar. A blastocyst forming normally does not guarantee implantation. The condition of the endometrium and the interaction between tissues also matter.Keep four events in order: fertilization makes the zygote; cleavage increases cell number; a blastocyst organizes distinct regions; implantation establishes attachment to the uterus.Worked exampleIdentify a stage from evidenceA diagram shows many small cells, a large fluid-filled cavity and a compact cell cluster at one side.The presence of many cells rules out the one-cell zygote.The large internal cavity rules out a solid morula.The cluster is an inner cell mass; the outer layer is trophoblast.Together these features identify a blastocyst. Attachment to the endometrium would indicate implantation, but a cavity alone does not prove attachment.Practise with supportApply the explanationTry both questions.
