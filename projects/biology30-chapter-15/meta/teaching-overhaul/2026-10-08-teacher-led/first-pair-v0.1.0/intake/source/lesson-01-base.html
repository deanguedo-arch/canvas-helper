Fertilization and the zygote

How does the joining of two gametes begin a new developmental pathway?

Learning goal

Explain where fertilization normally occurs, how one sperm contributes its chromosomes, and how the diploid chromosome number is restored.

Before you begin

A gamete is a haploid sex cell. An oviduct transports the oocyte; the uterus is the usual site of later implantation.

Begin with the location and the cells

normally occurs in an oviduct, also called a uterine or Fallopian tube. Sperm must reach the released secondary oocyte. The ovary releases this cell; the uterus is not the usual place where sperm and oocyte meet. Keeping these locations separate will help you follow development.

Each gamete contributes one set of chromosomes. A typical human sperm has 23, and the oocyte has 23. Their contributions restore the number of 46. The single cell that results is a . It has a new combination of genetic information rather than being an identical copy of either parent.

Follow the events at the oocyte

The sperm head has an acrosome containing enzymes that help it interact with and pass through the oocyte's surrounding layers. Reaching these layers is not yet the same as successfully fertilizing the cell. Membranes must interact and the sperm must contribute its contents.

Fusion helps activate the oocyte. The oocyte completes meiosis II, and changes involving cortical granules modify the zona pellucida, the protective coat surrounding it. These changes help prevent additional sperm from entering. Do not imagine that many sperm are supposed to contribute chromosomes to one normal human zygote.

The two chromosome contributions become associated with the developing cell's first division. For this chapter, focus on the result: one diploid cell containing genetic information from both gametes. The detailed chromosome movements will return in Chapter 16.

A changed step has a specific consequence

An obstruction that prevents sperm and oocyte from meeting affects fertilization even when hormone release and ovulation are normal. A failure to prevent entry by additional sperm can create an abnormal chromosome contribution. These are different problems, so their explanations should identify different steps.

Worked example

Check the chromosome contribution

A typical sperm and oocyte successfully complete fertilization. How many chromosome sets does the resulting cell have?

  1. Identify the starting cells: each gamete is haploid, with one chromosome set.
  2. Add chromosome contributions, not the number of parents: n + n = 2n.
  3. For humans, 23 + 23 = 46 chromosomes in the zygote.
  4. Check the stage: this is one cell before cleavage, not two separate daughter cells.

Does the word diploid tell you that DNA has already replicated?

Show the explanation

No. Diploid describes the number of chromosome sets. DNA replication and DNA amount are separate ideas.
