3. Source conflicts and wording hazards
- Textbook p508 and slide06 say the egg membrane depolarizes to prevent further sperm entry. Native lesson01 uses cortical granules/zona changes. These are materially different mechanisms; do not silently copy the old mechanism over the existing human-fertilization explanation.
- Slide05 says the coating layers ensure only one sperm can successfully attach. The textbook diagram and narrative show multiple sperm interacting with the coating. Distinguish contact/attachment to surrounding layers from successful entry/fertilization.
- Slide06 and p509 Figure15.1 describe nuclei 'fusing.' Native wording instead centers the chromosome contributions and first division. The source figure retains its older fusion label; any use must reconcile that simplification with nearby prose.
- Slide07 says fetal stage 'weeks 9–12'; p508 says from week9 through birth. Do not reproduce the slide's truncated fetal range. This is overview content, not a reason to expand these lessons into later fetal development.
- Textbook p510 calls the endometrium the 'outer lining' of the uterus. Native language appropriately refers to the uterine lining; preserve that distinction when extracting text.
- Slide09 says trophoblast turns into 'chorion and placenta.' Textbook p510 says chorion forms part of the placenta; native lesson02 specifically says supporting tissues including the fetal part. Avoid implying the whole placenta is trophoblast-derived.
- Textbook p509 and slide08 specify a 16-cell morula. Native text correctly emphasizes compact organization instead of an exact count, but the optional imported OBJ_2131125 still explicitly uses 16 cells. Teach it as the source's typical landmark, without making cell count the sole identification criterion.
- Main text/slide09 places attachment at days5–7 and completion at days10–14. Slide10's diagram labels implantation days7–10. Treat these as approximate source conventions and keep the existing end-of-week1/into-week2 framing; avoid a new exact-day requirement.
- Textbook p511 begins later gastrulation material and calls the early two-layer disk ectoderm/endoderm. This is outside the present lesson boundary and should not be imported incidentally as a blastocyst explanation.
