Sperm must reach the released secondary oocyte. The ovary releases this cell; the uterus is not the usual place where sperm and oocyte meet. Keeping these locations separate will help you follow development.Each gamete contributes one set of chromosomes. A typical human sperm has 23, and the oocyte has 23. Their contributions restore the diploid number of 46. The single cell that results is a zygote. It has a new combination of genetic information rather than being an identical copy of either parent.Fertilization, cleavage and implantationView largerFollow the secondary oocyte from the ovary, then trace fertilization in the uterine tube and early cell divisions before implantation. Compare cell number with overall embryo size. Inquiry into Biology, Chapter 15, p. 510.Follow the events at the oocyteThe sperm head has an acrosome containing enzymes that help it interact with and pass through the oocyte's surrounding layers. Reaching these layers is not yet the same as successfully fertilizing the cell. Membranes must interact and the sperm must contribute its contents.Fusion helps activate the oocyte. The oocyte completes meiosis II, and changes involving cortical granules modify the zona pellucida, the protective coat surrounding it. These changes help prevent additional sperm from entering. Do not imagine that many sperm are supposed to contribute chromosomes to one normal human zygote.The two chromosome contributions become associated with the developing cell's first division. For this chapter, focus on the result: one diploid cell containing genetic information from both gametes. A failure to prevent entry by additional sperm can create an abnormal chromosome contribution. These are different problems, so their explanations should identify different steps.Use the fertilization figure to locate the sperm head, oocyte membrane and protective layers.
