Checked 8 October 2026. These are content-source findings, not clinical guidance or certification of the entire source book.

## Human fertilization: distinguish species and attachment from entry
Textbook printed p508 and actual teacher slide06 assert membrane depolarization prevents further sperm entry. The baseline native lesson already uses cortical granules and zona changes. This candidate preserves that correction and explains the visible source discrepancy briefly. It does not claim that human oocytes have no electrical activity.

Primary human evidence:
- Homa & Swann (1994), *A cytosolic sperm factor triggers calcium oscillations and membrane hyperpolarizations in human oocytes*, Human Reproduction 9:2356–2361, DOI 10.1093/oxfordjournals.humrep.a138452. Authoritative abstract inspected: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7714158/ . Human oocytes exposed to injected sperm extracts showed calcium oscillations with hyperpolarization. This is an experimental activation study, not a direct universal test of every polyspermy mechanism. It does not establish the textbook's rapid depolarization mechanism.
- Patrat et al. (2006), *Zona pellucida from fertilised human oocytes induces a voltage-dependent calcium influx and the acrosome reaction in spermatozoa, but cannot be penetrated by sperm*, BMC Developmental Biology 6:59, DOI 10.1186/1471-213X-6-59. Publisher article/abstract/results inspected: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-213X-6-59 . In the studied human material, fertilized zonae retained sperm binding but penetration was substantially reduced. The title's voltage-dependent calcium response is in sperm, not evidence for an egg-membrane fast block. The study supports separating binding from penetration; it does not establish an absolute impenetrable barrier or justify copying every mouse molecular mechanism into humans.

Species context: mammalian versus other-animal mechanisms are discussed in the research review *Sperm Meets Egg: The Genetics of Mammalian Fertilization*, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-genet-121415-121834 . Rapid depolarization blocks reported in several externally fertilizing species cannot simply be taught as the established human mechanism. Candidate wording deliberately says “should not be treated as the established human mechanism,” rather than making a universal claim of no electrical response.

Its own fast-block paragraph repeats the disputed generalization and is NOT used to verify that claim. No figures, problems or prose were copied from this external reference.

A rendered section omits some cells, so visible circles do not establish the whole embryo's exact count. “About 16 cells” preserves the source landmark used by an optional imported question without defining morula solely by number.

Approximate end-of-week-one/into-week-two timing reconciles text days5–7/10–14 with slide10's days7–10 diagram; no exact-day assessment was introduced.
