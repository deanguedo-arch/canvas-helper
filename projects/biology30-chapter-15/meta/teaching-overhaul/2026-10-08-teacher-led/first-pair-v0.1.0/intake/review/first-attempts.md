### Required written explanation 1: arrival to diploid zygote

My answer: Sperm reaching an oocyte in the oviduct must first interact with its outer coverings. The acrosome at the front of the sperm head contains enzymes that, along with sperm movement and layer interactions, help passage through the corona radiata and zona pellucida toward the oocyte's plasma membrane. Reaching the coating is not yet successful fertilization. One sperm fuses with the oocyte membrane and contributes its contents. This helps activate the oocyte, which completes meiosis II. Cortical granules release material that changes the zona pellucida and helps prevent extra sperm from entering. The sperm and oocyte each provide one chromosome set; in humans, 23 + 23 establishes 46 in one diploid zygote. The parental chromosome contributions come together for development as the nuclear envelopes break down and the chromosomes participate in the first division.

Step-by-step visible support: Usual site is explicit in "Where do the two cells meet?" Layer sequence, acrosome position and helper role are explicit in "Getting through the coverings and joining the cell" and visible in its JPEG. Membrane fusion, activation, meiosis II and cortical/zona changes are stated consecutively in the next paragraph. The paragraph beginning "Finally" supplies the careful chromosome/nuclear description. The earlier gamete paragraph supplies 23, 23, 46, haploidy and diploidy. The written task needs no imported mechanism.

### Required written explanation 2: normal ovulation but failure to meet

My answer: Hormones help coordinate oocyte maturation and ovulation, but releasing an oocyte is only one requirement. Sperm must still travel to the oocyte in the oviduct. If transport prevents their meeting, the later covering-interaction and membrane-fusion steps cannot occur, even though ovulation and its hormonal control were normal. Normal hormone production therefore cannot establish fertilization by itself.

Step-by-step visible support: "Releasing an egg and fertilizing it are separate events"; "Hormones help coordinate oocyte maturation and ovulation"; "Physical transport is another requirement"; and "Evidence of normal hormonal control cannot by itself prove that two cells met." The worked process requires reaching the oocyte before fusion. The practice obstruction case supplies a concrete transferable example.

### Required written explanation 1: more cells without large total growth

My answer: Cleavage consists of repeated mitotic divisions followed by division of the cytoplasm. These divide the starting material among more cells while the early conceptus adds relatively little new material overall. As cell number rises, each cell is therefore smaller on average, rather than regrowing to the original zygote's size between divisions. DNA is still copied and distributed and cells use energy; little change in total size does not mean no biological activity. Normal mitosis preserves the diploid chromosome complement in each daughter cell rather than adding new gamete contributions.

Step-by-step visible support: The lesson's opening paragraph defines mitosis and division of cytoplasm; the next gives the partitioning explanation and failure to regrow to zygote size. The clay analogy explicitly limits itself and mentions copying DNA and using energy. The paragraph after the SVG supplies chromosome preservation. The numerical worked example supports the inverse cell-number/average-size relation.

### Required written explanation 2: fertilization versus implantation

My answer: Fertilization normally happens in an oviduct and joins a sperm and oocyte contribution to form one diploid zygote. During subsequent travel toward the uterus, cleavage produces more cells and development produces a blastocyst. Implantation is the later attachment and embedding of that blastocyst in the endometrium, the inner uterine lining. Thus the events involve different stages and locations: formation of a one-cell zygote in the tube versus interaction of a blastocyst with the uterine lining.

Step-by-step visible support: Lesson 01 supplies the formation, single-cell stage and usual site. Lesson 02 explicitly repeats "Fertilization produces a zygote in the tube; cleavage begins while it travels; implantation involves a later blastocyst at the uterine lining." Its implantation definition supplies attachment/embedding and endometrium. No extra calendar or hormone knowledge is needed.
