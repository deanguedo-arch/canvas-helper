Worked example

Which conclusion does an image support?

Two illustrative images show early development. Image A shows a compact ball of small cells without a large internal cavity. Image B shows a fluid-filled cavity, an outer cell layer and a cluster at one side. Neither image includes uterine tissue. Identify each stage and decide whether implantation is established.

  1. Use organization to identify A. The compact, solid ball fits a morula. It cannot be a one-cell zygote, and there is no large cavity establishing the blastocyst stage.
  2. Interpret the separate features in B. The cavity plus the side cluster and outer layer identify a blastocyst. The side cluster is inner cell mass; the outer layer is trophoblast.
  3. Check the evidence needed for implantation. Neither image shows an interaction with the endometrium. Image B establishes blastocyst organization, but we cannot conclude from it alone that attachment has occurred.
  4. Keep development and location connected. A later image showing trophoblast attached to and embedding in the uterine lining would support implantation. It would add evidence that the cavity alone could not supply.

Now check a different kind of observation. Suppose an early embryo has a total volume of 80 arbitrary units divided among two equally sized cells, then among four, with little total-volume change. The average cell volume changes from 80 ÷ 2 = 40 to 80 ÷ 4 = 20 units. Cell number doubles while average cell size halves. These are illustrative values, not measured human embryo data. They show why counting cells and measuring overall growth answer different questions.
