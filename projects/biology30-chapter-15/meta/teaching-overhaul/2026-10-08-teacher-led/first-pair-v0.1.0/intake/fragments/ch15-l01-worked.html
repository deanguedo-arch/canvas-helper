Worked example

Read a chromosome record before adding

In an illustrative animal species, a typical body cell has 18 chromosomes. A normal sperm and egg complete fertilization. A laboratory record labels the immediate product “two cells, each with 18 chromosomes.” Does that description fit the stated stage?

  1. Identify the starting quantity. A typical body cell is diploid, so 2n = 18. Each normal gamete has one of the two sets: n = 9.
  2. Combine the two gamete contributions. The sperm's 9 chromosomes and egg's 9 chromosomes give 9 + 9 = 18 in the zygote. Adding 18 + 18 would wrongly use the body-cell count as the count in each gamete.
  3. Check the number of cells. Fertilization has produced one zygote. The statement about two cells describes a later division, not the immediate product asked for.
  4. State the corrected record. One diploid zygote with 18 chromosomes. The chromosome number matches the typical body-cell number, while the genetic combination comes from two gametes.

For humans, the same reasoning gives 23 + 23 = 46. Chromosome-set number and DNA copying are different descriptions: copying DNA in preparation for division does not by itself add another parental chromosome set. Detailed copying and separation return in the cell-division chapter.
