Perspectives on Sustainability

In this lesson you will investigate various views of sustainability and how it is affected by globalization. Can economic development and use of Earth’s resources continue at the current rate, or are there consequences? You will explore this issue question: How does globalization affect sustainability?

How does globalization affect sustainability?

Reena and TJ are Alberta students who just finished high school. Reena has inherited a little money, and she and TJ want to invest this it in their own small business. They know of a location in a local strip mall that they think would be perfect for a small restaurant. It’s on the freeway where there is lunch-hour traffic from local industries and easy access for people on their way home from work. There are no take-out restaurants nearby, as they know from their high school days.

Before you read on, think about what Reena and TJ might need to do to make their business sustainable. That is, what should they do to make sure their business lasts?

TJ learned about entrepreneurship in high school. He completed a market analysis and found that there was a demand for pizza in their neighbourhood. He and Reena researched local needs and food supply sources. They found a relative who had worked as a pizza cook and was looking for a location nearer to his home. They invested in equipment and supplies. They set up shop and, with only a few minor pitfalls, soon had a steady clientele.

In order to build up a client base, they provided gift certificates as awards at local schools. They offered their restaurant as a meeting place for community groups.  They acted on customer requests, created several vegetarian options to meet the cultural demands of their community, and constantly refined and updated their menu as well as their décor. They hired local students and stay-home parents by creating flexible work schedules. They used recycled materials in their packaging and did their best to purchase locally produced ingredients. Soon their pizzeria was flourishing. People loved the community-minded approach and the pizzas of the new restaurant.

However, just as they were considering a major expansion, a large pizza chain began to build on a vacant lot nearby. With its economies of scale, the large chain could sell pizzas far cheaper than Reena and TJ. It had an excellent marketing scheme and brand recognition, as well as room for many more customers. The small size of Reena and TJ‘s Pizzeria limited its bottom line. Do you think Reena and TJ’s Pizzeria can succeed?

What makes a business thrive or fail? How does globalization affect sustainability—economically, politically, socially, and environmentally?

What is sustainability?

What makes something “sustainable”? Sustainability is the ability of something to last or continue without causing a negative impact on the future.

There are many ways something can be sustainable, for example:

  • politically
  • environmentally
  • socially or culturally
  • economically

Think about Reena and TJ’s Pizzeria. In what ways was it sustainable?

  • Politically. The restaurant was popular and followed local laws.
  • Environmentally. The restaurant used environmentally friendly products.
  • Socially or Culturally. The restaurant considered local needs and values, including dietary considerations, the needs of students and parents, and community activities.
  • Economically. It was well located so that it had a steady clientele. Overhead was low, and at first there was not a lot of competition. But when a transnational corporation opened up nearby, with high volume of sales and more powerful marketing strategies, the smaller pizzeria was economically challenged.

Individual Versus Collective Impacts

With the pervious activity, you've had a chance to examine the impact that your individual actions may have on the environment. Building such an awareness is important to becoming an active, informed citizen and understanding your connection to the land. Just as the land shapes us, we shape the land around us, for good or ill.

One note to consider, however, is that individual actions are not solely responsible for environmental impact on the world. A report by The Guardian indicates that 71% of global emissions are caused by only 100 companies. While it should be noted that individual demand for their products do motivate many of these companies, this does raise the question of where the greatest impact on the environment comes from, and the best ways to address that impact.

Is global economic growth sustainable?

 As you saw in Unit 2, there were many factors behind the rise of historical imperialism. Two of those factors were industrialization and the rise of capitalism. In imperialist times, wealthy and industrial nations needed raw materials and labour to support the growth of industry. A great deal of those natural resources, and many labourers, came from the colonies. Capitalism and industrialization are still important forces in today’s world. As nations all over the world race to become more prosperous, economic growth through trade and industrial production continues. Are there limits to the natural resources on the planet? Can limitless economic growth continue at its current rate without damaging the environment and using up all the natural resources needed to sustain people in the future?

There is a great deal of inequality in the world. The vast majority of the people in the developed world are wealthier than almost all of the people in the developing world. As the footprint activities showed, people in countries such as Canada use up more resources than people in poorer countries. As the poorer nations struggle to catch up, resource depletion, or the using up of resources occurs. Environmental damage may result. How can we balance economic growth with sustainable resource use?

As the nations of the world become increasingly interconnected, economic development continues. No matter who benefits financially from economic development, the environment is always affected in one way or another.

Can Earth’s resources support continued economic growth? Can Earth support prosperity for all people and at the same time protect the environment and preserve enough natural resources for future generations? Can we find some kind of balance?

Should sustainability matter?

Only you can answer that question. How you feel about the future is a personal matter. You may believe that people should live the best life they can today and that the future will take care of itself. You may believe that new inventions and technology will solve any environmental problems that might occur. Or, you may feel strongly that today’s people need to act to preserve the world for future generations. No matter what you believe, in your study of the legacies of imperialism you have discovered that the actions of people today have an effect on the future.

Stewardship is another word for responsible care-giving. When it comes to the environment, stewardship means the responsible management and use of the land so that it can benefit future generations.

Vocabulary

sustainable: something that can be maintained

ecological or environmental footprint: the amount of land and water area a person or a human population needs to provide the resources required to sustain itself and to absorb its wastes, given prevailing technology

economies of scale: in industrial production, the idea that bigger is better. If large numbers of goods are produced, the average cost at which each unit makes a profit is lower because production costs can be spread over more units.

bottom line: in economics, the final profit or loss a company makes after expenses are paid

resource depletion: theprocess of reducing the total amount of a particular resource

stewardship: showing responsibility for or looking after something

sustainable development: economic development that can be maintained without depleting the world’s resources and polluting the environment

Lesson Summary

In this lesson on the background to economic globalization, you explored different kinds of sustainability and identified different perspectives on sustainability. You determined that some globalizing factors lead to a world that can be maintained for the future, while others contribute to the destruction of our natural environment and the reduction of resources that might be needed in the future. Finally, you considered your role in sustainable development.
