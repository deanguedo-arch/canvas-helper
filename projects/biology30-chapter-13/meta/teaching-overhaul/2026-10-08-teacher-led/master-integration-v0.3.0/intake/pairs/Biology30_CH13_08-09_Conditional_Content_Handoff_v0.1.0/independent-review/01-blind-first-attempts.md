### Stop and think
Prompt: Does an increase in calcium stored in bone necessarily mean an increase in blood calcium?
First answer: No. Bone calcium and blood calcium are different pools. Calcium moving into bone is removed from the circulating pool, so more stored in bone does not by itself show more in blood. We need to know where calcium moved from and to.
Visible support: “One element in different places”: “Blood calcium means the calcium level in that circulating pool”; “movement into bone removes calcium from the circulating pool.” “Before you begin” also explicitly distinguishes the two pools.
Imported knowledge: None.

### Required selection 1
Prompt: Low blood calcium most directly stimulates which response?
First answer: Increased PTH release.
Visible support: “PTH coordinates several responses to the same disturbance”: “When blood calcium falls, calcium-sensitive cells in the parathyroid glands increase PTH release.” The overview top box links low blood Ca2+ and parathyroid release.
Imported knowledge: None. The TSH and insulin distractors need no outside definitions because the positive answer is explicit.

### Required selection 2
Prompt: How does PTH indirectly increase intestinal calcium absorption?
First answer: By promoting activation of vitamin D.
Visible support: Same heading, “Through active vitamin D”: “PTH promotes activation of vitamin D in the kidneys”; “This active form, called calcitriol, increases calcium absorption across the intestine from its contents into the body.”
Imported knowledge: None.

### Required writing 1
Prompt: Trace the response to low blood calcium. Include PTH’s source and effects involving bone, kidney and intestine.
First response: When blood calcium falls, the parathyroid glands release more PTH into blood. PTH promotes processes that release calcium from bone mineral into blood. It increases kidney calcium reabsorption, returning more calcium from tubule fluid toward blood so less leaves in urine. PTH also promotes kidney activation of vitamin D. Active vitamin D increases absorption of available calcium from intestinal contents into the body, making the intestinal effect of PTH indirect. These three contributions help raise blood calcium. As it rises toward a suitable range, the low-calcium stimulus gets smaller and the drive for extra PTH release decreases. This is negative feedback because the response opposes the starting change.
Step-to-teaching map:
1. Falling blood calcium → parathyroid PTH release: “PTH coordinates several responses to the same disturbance,” opening sentence.
2. Bone reserve → blood: same heading, “At bone,” “PTH promotes processes that mobilize calcium from bone mineral into blood”; companion left panel.
3. Kidney tubule fluid → blood → reduced urine loss: same heading, “At the kidneys”; companion middle panel.
4. PTH → kidney activation of vitamin D → intestinal absorption: same heading, “Through active vitamin D”; companion right panel explicitly names active vitamin D, and the bottom text gives the PTH-to-kidney link.
5. Blood calcium rises → reduced stimulus for extra PTH: same heading, final paragraph, “As the combined responses raise blood calcium toward a suitable range, the original low-calcium stimulus becomes smaller and the drive for extra PTH release decreases.” Overview red return line and its text.
6. Meaning of negative feedback: same paragraph, “That return effect makes the response negative feedback.” Also “Name the movement and close the feedback loop” supplies the complete chain.
Imported knowledge: None.

### Required writing 2
Prompt: Explain how PTH and calcitonin are antagonistic without describing them as identical systems with reversed arrows.
First response: They have opposing effects on the same regulated variable, blood calcium. PTH raises it through calcium mobilization from bone, increased kidney reabsorption, and an indirect intestinal effect through active vitamin D. Calcitonin can lower blood calcium, including by reducing bone breakdown and allowing more calcium to leave in urine. This does not make the hormones equally important or their mechanisms exact reverses. PTH and active vitamin D have major roles in normal calcium regulation, while calcitonin has a relatively minor role in adult humans. In particular, the PTH–kidney–vitamin D–intestine pathway does not imply an equal reversed intestinal pathway for calcitonin.
Step-to-teaching map:
1. Opposing effects and shared variable: “Opposing effects do not require identical control systems,” “Hormones with opposing effects on a shared variable are called antagonistic hormones” and the preceding PTH/calcitonin sentence.
2. Calcitonin bone mechanism: same heading, “One mechanism is reduced bone breakdown: less stored calcium is released into blood, helping calcium remain in bone.”
3. Calcitonin kidney mechanism: same paragraph, “It can also reduce kidney calcium reabsorption, allowing more calcium to leave in urine.”
4. Unequal physiological importance and nonmirrored intestine: same heading, paragraph starting “The comparison has an important limit,” including “calcitonin has a relatively minor role in adult humans” and “does not justify drawing an equal reversed intestinal branch for calcitonin.”
Imported knowledge: None.

### Local optional question
Prompt: Explain absorption versus reabsorption using calcium.
First response: Absorption moves dietary calcium from the intestine’s contents across its wall into the body. Reabsorption returns calcium from fluid in kidney tubules toward blood after the calcium has entered that fluid. Absorption supplies calcium from food, while renal reabsorption conserves calcium that might otherwise be lost in urine. Active vitamin D increases the intestinal route; PTH increases renal calcium conservation and indirectly increases intestinal absorption through vitamin D activation.
Visible support: “Name the movement and close the feedback loop,” first two paragraphs; the middle and right companion panels show the distinct starting fluids and common blood destination.
Imported knowledge: None. The optional textbook figure itself was not required to answer this local question because the distinction is directly taught.

### Required selection 2

First answer: It makes glucose available to the blood.

### Required writing 1
Prompt: Trace the rapid adrenal stress pathway and explain two effects in terms of immediate action.
First response: During an acute stress response, brain circuits including the hypothalamus organize sympathetic nerve signals that reach the adrenal medulla. This neural input stimulates the medulla to release epinephrine and norepinephrine into blood. They travel as hormones to responsive tissues. Increased heart rate and contraction help circulate blood and deliver oxygen and fuels to tissues preparing for action. Liver glycogen breakdown makes glucose available for release into blood, providing fuel that responsive tissues can use for cellular respiration. The medulla is where the incoming neural message becomes a hormone output. This pathway does not require an ACTH step.
Step-to-teaching map:
1. Brain/hypothalamus → sympathetic signals: “Find the point where a neural message becomes hormonal,” “During an acute stress response, brain circuits, including the hypothalamus, organize sympathetic signals that reach the adrenal medulla.”
2. Medulla neural input → catecholamine secretion into blood: same heading, following paragraph.
3. Delivery to responsive tissues: same paragraph, “Circulation then carries these hormones to responsive tissues.”
4. Increased heart activity → transport oxygen/fuel: “Connect each response to oxygen or fuel delivery,” second paragraph, and table first row.
5. Liver glycogen → blood glucose → usable energy: same heading, liver paragraph and table third row. Glycogen is explicitly defined as linked stored glucose units.
6. Route change and no ACTH: “Find the point where a neural message becomes hormonal,” paragraph beginning “This is a neuroendocrine response.”
Imported knowledge: None.

### Required writing 2
Prompt: Compare the adrenal medulla with the adrenal cortex without yet listing every cortical hormone.
First response: The medulla is the inner region of an adrenal gland; the cortex is its outer region. The medulla receives direct sympathetic neural stimulation and releases catecholamines, including epinephrine and norepinephrine, into blood for the rapid action-ready response. The cortex produces steroid hormones made from cholesterol. In the cortisol example, the hypothalamus sends an upstream releasing signal, the anterior pituitary releases ACTH, and ACTH stimulates the cortex. That is a gland-to-gland hormonal route. The cortisol route can support more sustained responses, but ACTH should not be described as the main controller of every cortical hormone, and rapid and longer-lasting responses can occur together.
Step-to-teaching map:
1. Inner vs outer: “Locate the gland before choosing the pathway,” second paragraph; actual anatomical image cross-section labels.
2. Neural versus hormonal control: “Compare the two adrenal regions using the same questions,” first two paragraphs and comparison row “Control emphasized here.”
3. Catecholamine versus steroid: “Locate the gland before choosing the pathway,” paragraph following the image; comparison “Hormone class” row.
4. Rapid versus more sustained cortisol effects and overlapping responses: comparison “Role in this comparison” row and the paragraph after the table.
5. Limits on ACTH generalization: same heading, “Do not extend the cortisol example to say that ACTH is the main controller of every cortical hormone.”
Imported knowledge: None.

### Local optional question
Prompt: Explain why a rapid change in heart activity does not prove that only hormones were involved.
First response: Faster heart activity is an observed effect, not a measurement of the route that produced it. Sympathetic nerves can release norepinephrine directly near responsive heart cells. The adrenal medulla can also release catecholamine hormones into blood that reaches the heart. These routes can act together, and adrenal hormones can act rapidly. Therefore speed alone does not show that only hormones caused the change; evidence distinguishing the neural and blood-borne signals would be needed.
Visible support: “Find the point where a neural message becomes hormonal,” paragraph beginning “There is also a parallel neural route to some targets,” plus the next paragraph; “Connect each response to oxygen or fuel delivery,” final paragraph; worked example “Change the evidence: observe a heartbeat without measuring a pathway.”
Imported knowledge: None. The local teaching is sufficient without opening optional textbook Fig. 13.21.
