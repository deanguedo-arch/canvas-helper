## Authoritative external corroboration

Sources were opened on 2026-10-08. Only the narrow findings below are attributed to these pages; clinical treatment advice is outside this review.

- [NIDDK, Diabetes Insipidus](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/diabetes-insipidus), sections on causes and treatment: central DI involves inadequate vasopressin, made in the hypothalamus and released through the pituitary; nephrogenic DI involves impaired renal response despite an available hormone signal. Treatment is cause-dependent. This supports the lesson’s restricted replacement claim and the possibility of excess dilute urine with dehydration. It also distinguishes DI from glucose-regulation diabetes. **Pass.**
- [American Thyroid Association, Thyroid Function Tests](https://www.thyroid.org/thyroid-function-tests/), “How does the thyroid gland function?” and TSH/T4 test sections: T4 contains four iodine atoms, is converted to T3, and is controlled through TSH feedback. Low free T4 with elevated TSH supports primary failure; low free T4 with low TSH can accompany pituitary failure. This corroborates the corrected iodine unit and model distinction. Its own thermostat “off” language is a simplification, not evidence for permanent cessation. **Pass.**
- [Endocrine Society, Hormone Replacement in Hypopituitarism, recommendation1.6](https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/hormone-replacement-in-hypopituitarism): central hypothyroidism can involve low free T4 with low, normal or mildly elevated TSH in pituitary disease. This supports the candidate’s more careful “not elevated enough” phrasing rather than falsely requiring TSH always to be below a normal range. **Pass.**

## Misconceptions actually challenged

- More ADH means more urine: guided task, stop question, required selection and writing all reject this through permeability/reabsorption.
- Concentrated blood requires dilute urine, or dilute urine proves adequate body hydration: contrasting cases and local optional question distinguish compartments.
- Posterior pituitary makes ADH: main explanation and source pairing distinguish hypothalamic production from release.
- Hormone availability guarantees response; giving more hormone fixes every defect: lesson06’s contrast, DI comparison and required writing reject this.
- Negative feedback means harmful effect, backward hormone flow, permanent gland shutdown, or molecular conversion: visible explanation explicitly rejects these interpretations and writings require a return effect.
- TRH, TSH and T4 are interchangeable, or TSH is thyroid-produced: guided pathway and required writing require exact sources/signals.
- TSH and T4 must change in the same direction: reverse worked example and primary-failure case challenge this.
- Low T4 always implies high TSH: pituitary-failure comparison conditions the prediction on working controls.
- A large thyroid must make abundant hormone; extra stimulation supplies missing iodine: stop question and required selection explain the two causal branches.
- A symptom or a neck photograph identifies the cause: teaching expressly requires supplied conditions.
- All growth problems have the same missing hormone: local optional thyroid/hGH question requires signal-specific reasoning.
- All pituitary failures imply the same hormonal pattern: actual advanced question supplies excessive TSH resistant to feedback; required comparison supplies inadequate TSH. Content mapping correctly keeps those different.

## Nonblocking improvements and limits

1. Some wrong choices are very implausible: retina makes ADH, pancreas moves into neck, iodine becomes pituitary, TSH becomes oxytocin. Replacing one or two with plausible mechanism confusions would improve diagnostic power, but is not needed for correctness/teachability. The written tasks currently carry the stronger causal assessment.
2. The text explicitly says saved writing records a response without grading accuracy. A completed run should not be described as verified biological mastery. Actual saving, gating, timer, progress, persistence, keyboard operation, contrast and image zoom were not tested here.
3. No actual first-time human student was observed. This is an independent model-based learner simulation with a strict source boundary, not evidence of measured student learning gains.
4. The content specifies classroom assumptions and avoids offering a diagnosis from two values or a treatment recommendation. Real clinical tests distinguish free/total hormone and have confounders, beyond the intentionally simplified mechanism taught here.
5. This review covers the two supplied lessons, their local activities/figures and the stated sources. Separate optional textbook-practice destinations and the video are not comprehensively verified.
6. The advanced independent-first-attempt gap must be closed by the separate reader, not by relabelling this reviewer’s post-feedback inference as blind.
