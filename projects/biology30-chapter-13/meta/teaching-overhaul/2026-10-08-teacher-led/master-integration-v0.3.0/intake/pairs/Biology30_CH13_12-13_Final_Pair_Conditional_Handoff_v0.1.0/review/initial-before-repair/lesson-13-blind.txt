Vocabulary help
Key terms
Words for this lesson
Use these definitions when you need help with a term.
homeostasis
Maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions through ongoing regulation.
target cell
A cell able to respond to a particular hormone because it has a suitable receptor and response machinery.
negative feedback
A response that counteracts the original change and reduces the drive for further response.
tropic hormone
A hormone that acts on another endocrine gland to influence its secretion.

A target cell needs a suitable receptor and functioning response machinery. Blood carrying a hormone past a cell does not guarantee a response. Before predicting a failure, underline the stated defect and note which other parts still work. Do not silently replace those givens with “all glands are healthy.”
For negative feedback, the response opposes the starting disturbance and reduces the drive for the extra response. This is not a requirement that normal hormone secretion or target activity becomes zero. Use the reminders below to rebuild only the pathways you need, then attempt the preserved checks.
Keep the decisive differences visible
Pathway or comparison
Mechanism to retain
Water loss and ADH
More concentrated body fluids can increase ADH release from the
posterior pituitary
. Responsive kidney collecting ducts become more water-permeable; more water returns toward blood, leaving less, more concentrated urine. Correction reduces the original stimulus.
ADH versus
aldosterone
ADH changes water permeability. Aldosterone from the cortex increases sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion; sodium conservation supports water/volume retention when water can follow. Its principal controls are angiotensinII and potassium.
Insulin
and
glucagon
A meal-related glucose rise stimulates pancreatic beta-cell insulin: responsive uptake and liver storage reduce circulating glucose. A fall supports alpha-cell
glucagon
: the liver supplies glucose. Each opposes its own starting disturbance.
PTH and
calcitonin
Low
blood calcium
stimulates parathyroid PTH, promoting bone calcium mobilization, kidney conservation and indirect vitaminD-supported intestinal absorption. Calcitonin can lower the circulating level but has a smaller adult-human role; these are not equal mirrored systems.
Rapid threat response
Sympathetic neurons directly stimulate the
adrenal medulla
to release epinephrine/norepinephrine. Cardiac effects support transport, and liver glycogen breakdown supports fuel supply. This is neural control of endocrine output.
Cortisol route
Hypothalamic
CRH
→ anterior-pituitary
ACTH
→
adrenal cortex
→
cortisol
. Cortisol supports fuel availability and inhibits upstream stimulation. A failed cortex may remain low-output despite stronger stimulation from working control glands.
Primary thyroid versus upstream failure
Low thyroxine permits increased TSH when pituitary control works. If the pituitary cannot supply enough TSH, thyroid
hormone
can be low without an appropriate TSH increase.
Prolactin
and
oxytocin
Prolactin supports milk production; oxytocin supports milk ejection. Sharing a related organ does not make two signals interchangeable.
Negative versus
positive feedback
Negative feedback opposes the initiating change. In positive feedback, a response reinforces it, as cervical stretch and uterine contractions can do until delivery interrupts the loop.
The table compares mechanisms, not labels alone. Keep
aldosterone
's ion-handling controls separate from the cortisol pathway.
Insulin
deficiency and reduced insulin response can both impair glucose regulation; a person's age or an insulin prescription does not by itself identify the type. Likewise, glucagon's upward effect on glucose is still corrective when glucose first fell. PTH and
calcitonin
must be compared using the blood calcium compartment.
Reading clarification:
p.463 incorrectly assigns ACTH secretion to the hypothalamus. The source is the anterior pituitary; the hypothalamic releasing signal is CRH. Its organizer is not a claim that the pituitary directly controls every gland: pancreatic glucose responses and parathyroid calcium responses have their own triggers. Use the named pathway, not a broad connecting line, to justify a hormone prediction.
