## Source cautions and disposition

- Textbook449 describes calcitonin uptake/storage in bone. Candidate explains the net retention direction through reduced bone breakdown rather than imagining a hormone physically carrying ions into bone. Original source and protected bank definitions remain unchanged.
- Independent source review found textbook449's claim that the glands no longer secrete PTH could conflict with the candidate's graded feedback account. Added an explicit reading clarification in08 teaching02: restored calcium reduces the extra response and does not require zero PTH secretion. The reviewer rechecks that repair separately.
- Textbook449 PTH wording is not used to claim direct PTH action on every bone-resorbing cell. Candidate uses promotion of cellular mobilization processes without requiring detailed receptor biology.
- PTH/vitaminD mechanisms are not generalized to retention of every mineral. Existing08 caption explicitly omits phosphate regulation and detailed bone-cell signalling; exact caption is preserved.
- Generic teacher32 antagonism diagram is not evidence of identical targets or equal regulatory importance. Candidate qualifies adult-human calcitonin role and rejects a fabricated reverse vitaminD branch.
- Teacher39 says cortisol increases inflammation, conflicting with textbook453 reduced-inflammation account. This pair does not teach that erroneous statement; flag remains for lesson10 review.
- Teacher39/40 and textbook453 group ACTH over cortex broadly. Candidate names ACTH specifically for cortisol and warns against extending that principal control to aldosterone. Detailed aldosterone controls remain lesson11 work.
- Textbook452 timing ratio is not adopted as a universal fixed fact. Candidate uses qualitative overlapping neural/endocrine timing.
- Textbook emergency epinephrine examples are not converted into actionable treatment instructions.
- Source photographs/video thumbnails do not establish symptoms, causation, playback or availability. No actual video viewing claimed.
