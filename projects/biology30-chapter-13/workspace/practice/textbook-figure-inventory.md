### Chapter 12 science/diagram guardrails

- **Retina:** incoming light crosses the ganglion and bipolar layers before reaching rods/cones; neural information then travels back through bipolar → ganglion → optic nerve. Do not reverse these directions.
- **Accommodation:** near = ciliary contraction + reduced suspensory tension + rounder lens. Far = ciliary relaxation + increased suspensory tension + flatter lens.
- **Myopia/hyperopia:** myopia focuses distant images in front of the retina and is corrected with a concave/diverging lens; hyperopia tends to focus near images behind the retina and is corrected with a convex/converging lens.
- **Vestibular apparatus:** otoliths belong with the utricle/saccule, **not** the semicircular canals. Canal fluid/cupula sensing must be distinct from otolith sensing.
- **Hearing:** air pressure wave → tympanum → ossicles → oval window → cochlear fluid/basilar movement → hair-cell transduction → auditory nerve. Do not show sound waves themselves travelling down the nerve.

### Chapter 13 science/diagram guardrails

- **ADH and oxytocin:** produced in hypothalamic neurons, transported down axons, stored/released from posterior pituitary terminals. Do not call the posterior pituitary their site of synthesis.
- **Anterior pituitary:** synthesizes its own hormones (e.g., hGH, TSH, ACTH, PRL, FSH, LH) under hypothalamic control.
- **Cortisol:** the textbook identifies cortisol as **anti-inflammatory** and immune-suppressing. Do not reproduce the teacher-slide phrase that cortisol “increases inflammation.”
- **Aldosterone:** assess adrenal-cortex source and kidney Na+ reabsorption/water-retention effects. Avoid teaching ACTH as the sole/primary physiological aldosterone regulator.
- **Calcium:** PTH is the major human regulator emphasized for raising blood Ca2+; calcitonin can lower blood calcium but has a relatively minor regulatory role in adult humans.
- **Blood glucose:** beta cells → insulin → uptake/storage → glucose down; alpha cells → glucagon → liver glucose release → glucose up.

## What Codex should physically create

For every `P1` set:

1. Copy/extract the source visual into the Chapter 12 or 13 learner asset folder.
2. Create a **learner copy** with A/B/C… callouts and no biological answer words.
3. Keep or create an **author key** with the biological names visible.
4. Wire the learner copy and `labeling-manifest.json` answers into the chapter-specific labeling dropdown.
5. Check each letter individually and with “Check all labels.”
6. Verify the image at desktop and phone widths before accepting it.

Do not use photos/micrographs simply because they are present in the textbook. Include a visual when the learner can identify a meaningful biological structure, pathway, or comparison from it.
