## Lesson 4 — resting neuron membrane base illustration

Use the current Lesson 4 membrane model screenshot only as a composition reference. Do not copy a textbook, PowerPoint, publisher illustration, logo, or watermark.

### Prompt to paste into ChatGPT image creation

Create an original, scientifically accurate educational illustration of a small patch of a resting neuron's axon membrane for an Alberta Grade 12 Biology course. The image will be used as the unlabeled visual base beneath separately authored web labels, arrows, and accessibility text.

Composition and science requirements:

- Landscape 16:10 composition, ideally 2400 × 1500 pixels, with generous clear margins and no cropped structures.
- Show extracellular fluid across the upper third, a phospholipid bilayer across the middle, and axon cytoplasm across the lower third.
- Draw the membrane as two clear rows of hydrophilic phospholipid heads with hydrophobic tails facing inward. Do not represent the bilayer as a solid wall.
- Include one potassium leak channel spanning the entire bilayer and one clearly different sodium-potassium ATPase spanning the bilayer.
- Show many sodium particles outside and very few inside. Show many potassium particles inside and very few outside. Keep the two particle types visually distinct by both colour and shape, so meaning does not depend on colour alone.
- Leave open space above the channel, above and below the pump, at the left edge of the membrane, and at the right edge for later web labels and directional arrows.
- The image should communicate a maintained resting state, not an action potential, synapse, or whole neuron.

Visual direction:

- Clean scientific-editorial vector illustration, polished enough for a top-tier online Grade 12 course.
- Restrained flat colour, crisp edges, subtle depth only where it clarifies membrane structure, and no gradients or decorative effects.
- White or transparent background.
- Use this palette as a guide: dark ink #171B1B, sodium teal #146C60, potassium brick #A43F35, pump amber #A15C00, membrane neutral #EEF3EE.
- Avoid cartoon faces, mascots, stock-photo styling, dramatic lighting, glossy 3D rendering, medical-diagnostic styling, and ornamental cellular clutter.

Critical exclusions:

- Do not include any words, letters, ion symbols, numbers, equations, legends, arrows, captions, titles, logos, signatures, or watermarks.
- Do not add voltage-gated channels, neurotransmitters, dendrites, a nucleus, organelles, a synaptic cleft, or an action-potential graph.
- Do not imply that the sodium-potassium pump creates the rapid voltage phases of an action potential.

Return one finished high-resolution PNG and no mockup frame.

### Acceptance checklist before course integration

- The bilayer orientation is biologically correct.
- Both proteins cross the membrane and are visually distinct.
- Relative sodium and potassium distributions are correct.
- No unwanted text or invented structures appear.
- The image remains legible when reduced to the lesson's evidence lane.
- Clear spaces remain for exact HTML/SVG labels and arrows.
- The asset has a recorded generation date, model/tool, prompt, checksum, and rights status.
- The current adjacent explanation, equivalent table, alt text, and keyboard enlargement are preserved.

### Prompt to paste into ChatGPT image creation

Create an original, scientifically accurate educational illustration showing the locations of the principal endocrine structures studied in Alberta Grade 12 Biology Unit A. The image will be used as an unlabeled anatomical base beneath separately authored web labels, leader lines, hormone information, and accessibility text.

Composition and science requirements:

- Landscape 16:10 composition, ideally 2400 × 1500 pixels, on a white or transparent background, with generous clear space on both sides for later web labels.
- Show a centered, neutral human figure from the head to the upper thighs. Use a restrained transparent or cutaway treatment so the internal structures are easy to locate without making the image photorealistic or diagnostic.
- Place the hypothalamus inside the brain, directly superior to the pituitary. Show the pituitary connected below the hypothalamus by a short infundibulum or pituitary stalk. Do not merge the two structures or exaggerate the pituitary as a visual “master gland.”
- Show the thyroid as a butterfly-shaped gland wrapping around the anterior trachea below the larynx.
- Show the parathyroid glands accurately as several small glands on the posterior surface of the thyroid. Because they are not normally visible from an anterior view, include one small, clean posterior-thyroid inset without text or labels.
- Show paired adrenal glands resting on the superior poles of the kidneys, not inside the kidneys.
- Show the pancreas in the upper abdomen. Represent pancreatic islets as multiple small cell clusters within the pancreatic tissue, not as the entire pancreas and not as a separate abdominal organ.
- The brain, trachea, kidneys, and pancreas may be shown as subdued orientation landmarks. Keep these landmarks visually secondary to the endocrine structures.
- Preserve plausible anatomical scale and relative position. Do not enlarge glands so much that their locations become misleading.
- Include only the Unit A structures listed above. Do not add reproductive glands, pineal gland, thymus, unrelated organs, or target tissues.

Visual direction:

- Clean scientific-editorial vector or semi-flat illustration, polished enough for a top-tier online Grade 12 course.
- Restrained flat colour, crisp edges, and subtle depth only where it clarifies anatomy. Distinguish structures by silhouette and position as well as colour.
- Use this palette as a guide: dark ink #171B1B, science teal #146C60, amber #A15C00, brick #A43F35, and pale neutral #EEF3EE.
- Keep the body and orientation landmarks quiet and neutral so the endocrine structures carry the visual emphasis.
- Avoid gradients, glows, glass effects, dark dashboard styling, cartoon characters, dramatic lighting, glossy 3D rendering, stock-medical-poster styling, and diagnostic imagery.

Critical exclusions:

- Do not include any words, letters, gland names, hormone names, symbols, numbers, arrows, leader lines, legends, captions, titles, logos, signatures, or watermarks.
- Do not imply that every tissue responds to every circulating hormone.
- Do not place parathyroid glands visibly on the front of the thyroid without the separate posterior-view inset.
- Do not show pancreatic islets as one large structure or as an organ separate from the pancreas.
- Do not merge the hypothalamus and pituitary or create a size hierarchy that implies the pituitary acts independently.
- Do not create a full presentation slide, textbook page, labelled atlas plate, or decorative body-system collage.

Return one finished high-resolution PNG and no mockup frame.

## Prompt-use rule for future units

Do not paste one of these Unit A prompts unchanged into a B-D image request. For every target figure:

1. Start from the target unit's exact outcome and lesson purpose.
2. Write the causal mechanism or anatomical relationships before writing style language.
3. List exact labels and their spelling.
4. List known misconceptions and prohibited representations.
5. Specify composition, reading order, aspect ratio, white space, palette, and text-size constraints.
6. Generate one candidate, inspect the science, and issue a narrow correction prompt rather than accepting a visually attractive error.
7. Preserve the initial prompt, every correction prompt, selected full-resolution asset, checksum, model/provider record, rights decision, comparison evidence, teacher decision, and exact-build review.
