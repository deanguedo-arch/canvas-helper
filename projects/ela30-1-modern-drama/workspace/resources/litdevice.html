Literary Devices and Techniques

Why is it important to learn literary terms?

There is little point to simply memorizing definitions. Knowing terminology is of practical value only when you apply your knowledge to help you make meaning of what you read. Becoming comfortable with using these terms will help you communicate your ideas effectively and precisely.

Allusions make reference to something in history or previous literature. Allusions conjure up richly connotative words or symbols as a means of suggesting far more that it says.

Antagonists are those forces (usually other characters or circumstances) which work against the protagonist and may prevent the protagonist from achieving his/her goal.

Atmosphere or mood is the predominant feeling that is created by the literature. It usually sets up expectations in the reader about the outcome of a situation.

Asides are comments made only to a limited number of characters on the stage or, at times, only to the audience, and which other characters, although present on the stage, do not apparently hear.

Climax is the highest point of intensity in a story. It is the major crisis and at that point there is no turning back for the protagonist.

Conflict is the struggle between two opposing forces or characters.

Connotation refers to what is suggestion by a word beyond what it expresses - its overtones or meaning. Words acquire these meanings by past history and association. Connotation evolves by the way and the circumstances in which a word has been used.

Character Foils occur when a character, usually a minor one, is used for the purpose of emphasizing the essential nature of one of the central characters.

Denotation refers to the dictionary meaning of words.

Exposition is background information that is provided by the author. Also time and place, and some characters are generally introduced in the exposition.

External conflict is conflict that happens outside oneself. There are different types of external conflict: Man vs Man, Man vs Nature, Man vs Society.

Figurative Language used in prose helps to invigorate writing. The reader forms a mental picture from the image. Metaphors, similes, hyperboles, and personification are some of the more common figures of speech.

Flashback is a sudden switch in the plot from the past to the present.

Foils are usually minor characters or antagonists whose nature, actions, or motivations serve to highlight or emphasize the nature, actions, or motivations of the protagonist.

Foreshadowing: When an author builds anticipation by indicating or suggesting what will eventually happen, he is using foreshadowing. This device can create suspense or be used to comment indirectly on an event before it occurs.

Imagery: Effective descriptive images enable the reader to visualize setting, and action. Imagery appealing to different senses helps a reader to re-create a scene more fully. Writers may also use imagery to heighten the mood and the emotional effect they are seeking to create. Similar images that recur throughout a piece of writing are referred to as motifs. Tracing motifs often allows a reader to discover the significant themes being explored.

Internal conflict is conflict that happens within oneself.

Irony: Irony is the result of a discrepancy between appearance and reality: between what words seem to say and what they really mean (verbal irony); or between what logically may be expected to happen and what is the actual, yet unexpected outcome (situational irony). Dramatic irony occurs when at least one of the characters fails to perceive a discrepancy between appearance and reality but the reader is aware of the discrepancy. For example, when the reader knows that one character is telling a lie but another character believes what is being said, the author is employing dramatic irony. Such irony often serves as an effective way for the author to reveal the true nature of a character.

Juxtaposition is the technique of setting actions or characters side by side to emphasize similarities and differences.

Metaphors compare things that are essentially unlike. The comparison is implied rather than clearly stated.

Motifs are images that recur throughout a work. Motifs act as unifying forces and are often symbolically significant.

Nemesis refers to the central inner flaw in a character, the inner character flaw that ultimately causes the downfall of that character.

A Paradox is an apparent contradiction that is nevertheless somehow true. It may be either a situation or a statement.

Parallelism is a term used to describe the patterning of two characters or events in similar terms as a way of highlighting their differences and similarities. When one scene occurs immediately following a parallel scene, the writer is employing the technique of juxtaposition, a device that is particularly effective in emphasizing similarities or differences in the way characters respond to similar circumstances.

Personification consists of giving the attributes of a human being to an animal, an object or an idea.

Point of view is the perspective that the story is being told from. Point of view is usually one of the following:

  • First-person - The character is telling the story directly. The narrator uses the pronoun "I."
  • Third person or limited omniscient - The story is told from "outside" the characters but concentrates on the thoughts and actions of one character.
  • Omniscient - This point of view allows the author to peer into the hearts and mind of all the characters. He is able to interpret and comment on all their behavior, and even comment on the significance of the story.
  • Objective - No thought of any of the characters are revealed. The narrator refrains from making any comments. The literature is told mater-of-factly, void of any emotion.

The Protagonist is the central character who struggles to attain a certain goal or to resolve a conflict. A piece of literature may have more than one protagonist.

Setting is the time and place of a story.

Soliloquies are speeches delivered by characters standing alone on the stage. Such speeches allow the writer to reveal the inner thoughts of characters as if the characters are talking to themselves.

Symbolism: A symbol is a concrete image that also represents something else, usually an abstract quality or idea. The concrete image exists as part of the narrative, but to the perceptive reader, it suggests a figurative meaning, an idea that goes beyond the literal. Effective symbolism often illuminates major themes and acts as a unifying device when it recurs throughout a piece as a motif.

Theme is the central idea of the story. It is usually implied rather than stated directly.

Tone is the author's attitude toward his audience, characters, and topic.
