Position Paper Outline

HOW TO for the Social Studies Position Paper essay

  1. Read the source carefully, underline any key words/ideas
  2. Jot down key ideas and try to do a brief outline (if this is during the exam – give yourself 5 minutes to brainstorm and create a brief outline, it is easier to organize your ideas in a brief outline then when you are writing)
  3. You can do your analysis of the source in one paragraph and then do the essay or you can use the analysis of the source as your intro as long as you include a thesis statement (your position) at the beginning or end of your Intro/analysis.
  4. Write your essay – remember to be as specific as possible, provide analysis, and avoid overgeneralizing: Eg.“There have been many different ideas in the history of mankind about economics/politics.”

THESIS – the position taken by the writer (YOU!). “The perspective … (briefly say what it is) … taken by the source should (or should not) be embraced (fully, partly, not at all?)…(briefly write point 1, point 2 and point 3.)”

BODY … in 3 or more paragraphs, defend your position with arguments. Remember each paragraph is an argument and has only one main idea

Argument 1 – 1 or 2 sentence statement – This is your topic sentence: The first thing you should say in the paragraph is the argument the rest of the paragraph will explain and support.

Explanation – elaboration and explanation of argument. Be specific and analytical - theoretically based ‘what does it mean?’

Example – 1 or 2 pieces of specific evidence to back up the explanation and argument.

Argument 2 – 1 or 2 sentence statement – Your topic sentence.

Explanation – elaboration and explanation of argument

Example – 1 or 2 pieces of specific evidence to back up the explanation and argument

Argument 3 – 1 or 2 sentence statement – your topic sentence.

Explanation – elaboration and explanation of argument

Example – 1 or 2 pieces of specific evidence to back up the explanation and argument

CONCLUSION…in 1 paragraph

Summary of key points and Reassertion of thesis – you never put any new information into a Conclusion, if you have a new point/fact it should go into the body of your essay.
