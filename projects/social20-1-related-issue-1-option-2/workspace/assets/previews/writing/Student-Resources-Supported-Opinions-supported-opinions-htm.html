  • Support your opinion with evidence/examples
  • Include the premise of your argument

  • Position paper tips and tools

Dedicate at least 30 minutes to this lesson AND return to review before submitting future assignments to ensure you understand and include these critical thinking skills in your writing.

Support your opinion with evidence/examples

In all written work, you will be expected to state your opinion/position and defend it with arguments supported by historical or contemporary evidence and/or examples from the course content and your additional research. You will see this skills requirement in every rubric. So let's take a look at what this means before you attempt to demonstrate this skill in your assignments.

Open this PDF document from The Writing Center: Three Ways to Support an Argument. You can use statistics, examples and expert opinion. For the latter, beware of the Appeal to Authority logical fallacy (see Logical Fallacies page). Other valid support could be scientific research data, graphs and even historical or contemporary photographs.

What to avoid in your arguments

Arguments to Avoid!

See the Logical Fallacies page in the Student Tools section of content for the types of arguments to avoid in your writing.

For example, anecdotal evidence is typically not acceptable evidence. That would be something like a story about your personal experience: "This happened to me, therefore ..." Generally avoid quoting the opinions of others to support your opinions.

Position Paper Tips and Tools

Here's a general formula to follow for defending a position in all your written work:

  • Analyze the ideological perspective of the source
  • State your Opinion/Position
  • 2-3 Arguments to defend your opinion
  • Evidence/Examples to support your arguments (take the time to explain how they do that!)
  • All while making strong connections to librealism and the terms and concepts explored in the course.
