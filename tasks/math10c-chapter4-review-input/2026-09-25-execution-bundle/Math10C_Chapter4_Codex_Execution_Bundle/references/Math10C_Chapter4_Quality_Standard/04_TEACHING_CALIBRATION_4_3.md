## Student-facing teaching sample

### What you will learn

You will simplify products and quotients that contain powers of the same base. You will also explain why a nonzero number raised to the power zero equals 1.

The important decision is not simply “add or subtract.” First identify the operation connecting the powers. Then check whether their bases match.

You may keep your full working on paper. The course will sometimes ask you to enter one important decision or line so it can help you find a mistake. A direct independent check should remain available for learners who are already prepared.

### 1. Why the product rule works

The exponent counts the number of repeated factors:

\[
b^3=b\cdot b\cdot b.
\]

When we multiply \(b^3\) by \(b^4\), we join two groups of the **same factor**:

\[
b^3b^4=(b\cdot b\cdot b)(b\cdot b\cdot b\cdot b)=b^7.
\]

There are seven factors of \(b\), so the exponents add. The base stays \(b\).

\[
b^m b^n=b^{m+n}.
\]

This rule applies to multiplication of powers with the same base. It does not turn \(b^3+b^4\) into \(b^7\), because addition does not join repeated factors in the same way. Nor can we combine \(b^3c^4\) into one power of \(b\): its bases differ.

**Your decision:** Which expression can be simplified directly using this rule?

\[
p^2p^5\qquad p^2+p^5\qquad p^2q^5
\]

Choose the expression, then enter the resulting power. Do this before opening feedback. The course should explain an incorrect choice by referring to the operation or base, rather than simply displaying the answer.

### 2. Why the quotient rule works

Consider:

\[
\frac{a^7}{a^3}.
\]

Write the powers as repeated factors. Three factors of \(a\) in the numerator pair with the three in the denominator. Each pair divides to 1, leaving four factors in the numerator:

\[
\frac{a^7}{a^3}=a^{7-3}=a^4.
\]

We subtract because division removes matching factors. We do **not** divide 7 by 3.

The original denominator must not equal zero. Therefore the result retains the restriction \(a\ne0\), even though the simplified expression no longer displays a denominator.

**Try the decision before seeing the result:** For \(t^8/t^3\), which operation belongs between the exponents: \(8+3\), \(8-3\), or \(8\div3\)? Then simplify and record the restriction.

This first quotient example leaves a positive exponent. A quotient that leaves a negative exponent needs the reciprocal explanation developed in Lesson 4.5; it should not be an unexplained prerequisite here.

### 3. Why the zero exponent gives 1

There are two ways to simplify the same expression:

\[
\frac{a^4}{a^4}.
\]

Any nonzero quantity divided by itself equals 1. The quotient rule also gives \(a^{4-4}=a^0\). Therefore:

\[
a^0=1\qquad(a\ne0).
\]

A zero exponent does not mean “multiply by zero.” It is the result of removing all the matching factors in this quotient.

**Explain the connection:** Complete both routes from \(r^6/r^6\): one using “a quantity divided by itself,” and one using the exponent rule. Why do the results have to agree? Your written explanation can be retained for review; a keyword alone is not proof that you explained it.

### 4. Keep coefficient and variable decisions separate

Simplify:

\[
\frac{18p^7q^5}{6p^2q^3}.
\]

Start by separating the numerical coefficient from each matching base:

\[
\frac{18}{6}\cdot\frac{p^7}{p^2}\cdot\frac{q^5}{q^3}.
\]

The coefficient is an ordinary division: \(18\div6=3\). For the powers, subtract exponents of matching bases:

\[
3p^{7-2}q^{5-3}=3p^5q^2.
\]

The original denominator contains both \(p\) and \(q\), so retain \(p\ne0\) and \(q\ne0\).

Notice the different operations: we **divide coefficients** but **subtract exponents** in these quotients. Applying the same operation to all the numbers would change the mathematics.

### 5. Now do more of the organization yourself

Simplify the following and retain the restrictions:

\[
\frac{20m^6n^4}{5m^2n}.
\]

Write a useful intermediate line and your final expression. The course should not display the coefficient calculation and both exponent subtractions before you decide them. It may offer those prompts when you request guided support.

After checking, you should see which submitted relationship was correct and which needs another look. A coefficient error does not establish that your exponent work was wrong.

### 6. A focused repair example

A learner obtains \(4x^3\) for:

\[
\frac{12x^6}{3x^2}.
\]

The coefficient 4 is correct. The exponent needs examination. A final answer alone does not prove which mistaken method was used, so first ask: “Which operation did you use with 6 and 2?”

A learner who selected division needs a short cancellation demonstration: two denominator factors cancel two of the six numerator factors, leaving four. Then ask the learner to apply that decision to a different small quotient, such as \(z^7/z^2\), before returning to the original working.

The original response stays visible beside the correction. After the repair, provide a different problem for an independent demonstration. Correcting this revealed example is useful learning, not a fresh first attempt.

### 7. Remove the cues for independent evidence

A separate, clearly labelled demonstration should ask the learner to simplify a new expression and show a useful line, without displaying the method sequence or an already solved proposal. For example, a reviewed task family could combine a product and quotient so the learner must decide which operation applies where.

The checker must credit only the components actually demonstrated. A product question does not also prove zero-exponent reasoning. Where several targets are genuinely elicited in one problem, the same submitted work can support those specific targets without requiring four separate worksheets.

## Author-only implementation notes and answers

Do not insert this key above or alongside an independent response. It is for authoring/testing only. Any student who views a solution needs a different mathematical instance for an independent check.

- Product decision: \(p^2p^5=p^7\).
- Quotient decision: \(8-3\); \(t^5\), \(t\ne0\).
- Zero-exponent routes: \(r^6/r^6=1\) and \(r^{6-6}=r^0\), with \(r\ne0\).
- Reduced-support task: \(4m^4n^3\), with \(m,n\ne0\). Reordered factors such as \(4n^3m^4\) remain valid.
- Repair task: \(z^5\), \(z\ne0\).
- Possible independent family example, after mathematical review: \((15u^4v^2)(2u^3)/(5u^2v)\) gives \(6u^5v\), with \(u,v\ne0\). It can assess relevant product, quotient and coefficient decisions when the responses show them. It does not by itself establish the zero-exponent target.

### What makes this different from a longer summary

The writing explains the mechanism behind the rules, separates decisions, surfaces restrictions, avoids an untaught reciprocal step, and asks the learner to make a decision before seeing it completed. The repair is an actual sequence with a return to preserved work, not the label “focused repair” attached to a hint.

Do not implement every practice interaction as a mandatory multistep form. Prepared learners need the direct route; supported learners need prompts that can fade. Use a bounded structured reasoning component for reliable automatic feedback and retain unrestricted explanation or alternative paper working for review.
